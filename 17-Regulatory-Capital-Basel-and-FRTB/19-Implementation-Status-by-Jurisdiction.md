# Implementation Status by Jurisdiction

## TL;DR

- The Basel Committee set 1 January 2023 for the final Basel III package, with the output floor rising from 50% in 2023 to 72.5% from 1 January 2028 (RBC90.1); every jurisdiction then legislates its own dates and deviations.
- As of 2026-10: the EU applies CRR3 from 1 January 2025 with the floor phasing in from 50% (2025) to 72.5% (2030); its FRTB own-funds requirements were deferred twice to 1 January 2027 and then softened by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1221 (a multiplier of at most 1 until 31 December 2029).
- The UK PRA's Basel 3.1 rules (PS1/26, 20 January 2026) take effect on 1 January 2027, with the market-risk internal model approach from 1 January 2028.
- The US agencies proposed on 19 March 2026 that Category I and II banks compute one set of risk-based ratios under an "expanded risk-based approach" (no internal credit models); comments closed 18 June 2026 and no final rule had been issued as of 2026-10.
- Canada (OSFI) froze its floor at 67.5% "until further notice"; Japan applied the finalised framework from 31 March 2024 for internationally active banks and internal-model users.

## Learning objectives

- Map the implementation timelines and legislative instruments across the European Union (CRR3), the United States (Basel III Endgame), and the United Kingdom (Basel 3.1).
- Identify key jurisdictional deviations from the BCBS baseline, including EU corporate CVA exemptions and transitional output floor arrangements.
- Distinguish the March 2026 US proposals from the earlier 2023 NPR and 2024 re-proposal, including the proposed single-calculation framework and the absence of a stated first-application date.
- Quantify cross-border capital disparities for identical derivative and credit portfolios under divergent EU versus US regulatory treatments.
- Compare the US agencies' proposed single-calculation framework for the largest banks with the enacted EU and UK frameworks, while distinguishing proposals from effective rules.
- Evaluate the strategic implications of cross-border regulatory arbitrage on global bank booking models and legal entity structures.

### European Union

CRR3 has applied from 1 January 2025, and the EBA reflected it in the 2025 EU-wide stress test methodology.
On market risk the Commission used its empowerment twice to defer the FRTB own-funds requirements, to 1 January 2027 (Delegated Regulations (EU) 2024/2795 and 2025/1496).
Its deferral power exhausted, it adopted Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1221 on 4 June 2026 (published 11 September 2026, applying from 1 January 2027): temporary targeted operational relief and multipliers so that an institution may scale its market-risk own-funds requirement by a factor of at most 1 until 31 December 2029 (Article 495v(1) as inserted).
CRR3 recitals 63 and 64 keep the EU's CVA exemptions for certain client transactions while requiring banks to report the CVA charge the exempted transactions would have attracted.
Recital 12 allows IRB banks a favourable transitional treatment of investment-grade unrated corporates in the output-floor calculation (the EBA's monitoring instructions apply a 65% risk weight to them); the end date is subject to change.

### United Kingdom

PS1/26 (20 January 2026) finalises Basel 3.1 with effect from Friday 1 January 2027 and brings the FRTB internal model approach in on 1 January 2028, firms keeping their existing IMA permissions until then.
The PRA re-set the output-floor transitional factors for the 2027 start.
CP9/26 (June 2026) consults on adjustments to the IMA and keeps the 2028 date; until finalised it is a proposal.

### United States

On 19 March 2026 the Federal Reserve, FDIC and OCC issued three proposals.
For Category I and II banking organisations: one set of risk-based ratios under an expanded risk-based approach covering credit, operational, market and CVA risk; the standardized approach would no longer apply to them and the advanced approaches would be removed.
Market risk would apply to Category I and II holding companies and to banks with more than USD 5 billion of trading activity or trading activity of at least 10% of total assets, with an expected-shortfall models approach and a standardized fallback.
CVA requirements would apply to Category I and II firms and other banks with significant trading activity and at least USD 1 trillion of derivative notional, and client-facing derivative transactions would be exempt.
Comments were due by 18 June 2026; the Board's 2026 press-release list shows no final capital rule as of 2026-10.
The earlier 2023 proposal and its 2024 revisions are history: do not quote their dual-stack design or impact estimates as the current US position.

### Canada and Japan

OSFI's CAR guideline (2026) sets the capital-floor adjustment factor at 67.5% until further notice and commits to at least two years' notice before any increase (chapter 1, paragraph 33, published 11 September 2025); a 2024 schedule had planned 70% in 2026 and 72.5% in 2027.
Japan's FSA implemented the finalised Basel III framework (the December 2017 package plus the January 2019 market-risk standard) from 31 March 2024 for internationally active institutions and for others using internal models, and from 31 March 2025 for the rest (FSA Weekly Review No.582, 11 April 2024).

### Why the differences matter to a desk

- The output floor bites at different levels in different places in the same year, so the marginal value of an internal model differs by legal entity.
- CVA exemptions change the capital cost of uncollateralised corporate hedges by entity and counterparty type.
- FRTB timing and the EU multiplier change trading-book capital for identical positions until at least 2029.
- None of this alone justifies moving a booking: the entity's own rules, the counterparty's location and the supervisor's view of booking models all apply.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: CVA capital on an unrated airline hedge with and without an exemption

An uncollateralised swap netting set with an unrated airline: SA-CCR EAD of USD 18,500,000 and effective maturity 3.5 years.
Under the Basel reduced BA-CVA (MAR50.14 to MAR50.16), an unrated counterparty in "consumer goods and services, transportation and storage" takes the HY and NR risk weight of 8.5%.

1. Supervisory discount factor $DF = (1 - e^{-0.05 \times 3.5}) / (0.05 \times 3.5) = 0.91738845$ (0.9174 rounded).
2. Stand-alone CVA $SCVA = \frac{1}{\alpha} RW \cdot M \cdot EAD \cdot DF = \frac{1}{1.4} \times 0.085 \times 3.5 \times 18{,}500{,}000 \times 0.91738845 = 3{,}606{,}483$.
3. With one counterparty $K_{\text{reduced}} = \sqrt{(\rho\, SCVA)^2 + (1 - \rho^2) SCVA^2} = SCVA$, and capital is $DS \times K = 0.65 \times 3{,}606{,}483 = 2{,}344{,}214$.
4. RWA $= 12.5 \times 2{,}344{,}214.2 = 29{,}302{,}677$; at a 12% CET1 target that is 3,516,321 of CET1, costing 421,959 a year at a 12% hurdle.
5. If the supervisor approves mapping the airline's internal rating to investment grade (MAR50.16), the weight is 3.0% and capital falls to 827,370, a factor of 2.8333 lower.
6. If the trade sits in an EU entity and falls within the CRR exemptions kept by CRR3, CVA capital is zero (but must be reported); under the US proposal, a client-facing derivative would also be exempt.

The arithmetic shows the size of the gap; whether the exemption applies is a legal question about the entity and the counterparty, not a calculation.

### Example 2: one bank, several output-floor calibrations

A bank with pre-floor RWA of 485 and all-standardised RWA of 720 (USD billions):

| Calibration | Floor RWA | Binding RWA |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Basel 2026, 65% | 468 | 485 |
| Basel 2027, 70% | 504 | 504 |
| Basel from 2028, 72.5% | 522 | 522 |
| EU 2027, 60% (EBA dashboard path) | 432 | 485 |
| Canada, 67.5% until further notice | 486 | 486 |

The floor starts to bind at a factor of $485 / 720 = 67.36\%$, so Canada's paused factor binds by 1 and the EU's 2027 factor does not bind at all.
With 60 of CET1 the ratio is 12.37% at 485 and 11.49% at 522.
During the Basel phase-in a supervisor may cap the floor's increase at 25% of pre-floor RWA (RBC90.2): a bank at 400 pre-floor would be held at 500 instead of 522.

## Pitfalls

- Quoting a proposal as a rule: the US March 2026 package and the PRA's CP9/26 are proposals.
- Quoting a date without the regulator's own source: implementation dates moved repeatedly (the EU FRTB twice, the UK once, Canada's floor indefinitely).
- Using the Basel phase-in for an EU or Canadian entity: the floor percentage in a given year differs by jurisdiction.
- Assuming an exemption travels: an EU CVA exemption does not apply to the same trade booked in a UK or US entity.
- Treating the EU FRTB multiplier as permanent: it runs until 31 December 2029.
- Forgetting that the US proposal removes internal credit models for the largest banks, so an "output floor" comparison with the EU or UK is not like for like.

## Interview questions

> [!question]- reg-19-01 | How does the March 2026 US proposal change the calculation design?
> Category I and II banks would compute one set of risk-based ratios under an expanded risk-based approach covering credit, operational, market and CVA risk.
> The standardized approach would no longer apply to them and the advanced approaches would be removed, replacing the two-stack design of the 2023 proposal.
> It is a proposal: comments closed 18 June 2026 and no final rule or application date had been issued as of 2026-10.

> [!question]- reg-19-02 | How does the EU CRR3 treat unrated corporate exposures differently from the BCBS baseline?
> For the output floor of IRB banks, CRR3 gives investment-grade unrated corporates a favourable transitional treatment (recital 12), which the EBA's monitoring applies as a 65% risk weight.
> Basel has no such carve-out.
> The reason given is that most EU corporates have no external rating; the end date is set in the CRR transitional provisions and is subject to change.

> [!question]- reg-19-03 | Why did the European Union preserve CVA capital exemptions under CRR3?
> To avoid excessive costs for derivatives with clients that cannot exchange collateral, as when the exemptions were first introduced (CRR3 recital 63).
> CRR3 keeps them but requires banks to report the CVA charge that the exempted transactions would attract (recital 64), so supervisors can monitor the risk.

> [!question]- reg-19-04 | What is the Small Domestic Deposit Takers (SDDT) regime in the UK?
> A simpler prudential regime for small domestic UK deposit takers, with its own capital rules in PS4/26.
> PS1/26 aligns its dates with Basel 3.1 (1 January 2027), which is why the PRA's interim capital regime is no longer needed.

> [!question]- reg-19-05 | What is the status of the US capital proposals issued in March 2026?
> Proposed, not final.
> Three proposals of 19 March 2026 (large-bank expanded approach, standardized-approach revisions, and the Fed's G-SIB surcharge method), comments due 18 June 2026, and no final rule in the Board's press releases as of 2026-10.

> [!question]- reg-19-06 | How does the implementation timeline for FRTB market risk differ between the EU and the US?
> EU: own-funds requirements deferred to 1 January 2027 (Delegated Regulations 2024/2795 and 2025/1496), then applied with an optional multiplier of at most 1 until 31 December 2029 (Delegated Regulation 2026/1221).
> US: still a proposal (March 2026) with an ES-based models approach and a standardized fallback for banks with significant trading activity, and no application date.
> UK, for comparison: SA from 1 January 2027 and IMA from 1 January 2028 (PS1/26).

> [!question]- reg-19-07 | How do credit risk approaches differ across the EU, UK, and March 2026 US proposal?
> The EU and UK keep foundation and advanced IRB, subject to the Basel 3.1 input floors and the output floor.
> The US proposal would remove the advanced approaches, so Category I and II banks would use standardized credit risk weights in the expanded risk-based approach.

> [!question]- reg-19-08 | What is the phase-in schedule for the Basel III output floor?
> Six steps: 50% on 1 January 2023, 55% in 2024, 60% in 2025, 65% in 2026, 70% in 2027 and 72.5% from 1 January 2028 (RBC90.1).
> During the phase-in a supervisor may cap the floor's increase at 25% of pre-floor RWA (RBC90.2).
> Jurisdictions shifted the start: the EU runs 50% in 2025 to 72.5% in 2030, and Canada paused at 67.5%.

> [!question]- reg-19-09 | How does regulatory fragmentation lead to booking model restructuring in multinational investment banks?
> It changes the capital cost of the same trade by entity (CVA exemptions, floor level, FRTB timing), which feeds into where new business is booked.
> But booking decisions also depend on the counterparty's location, legal enforceability of netting, local supervisors' views on booking models and the cost of moving trades; a capital difference alone is not a reason.

> [!question]- reg-19-10 | What is the application date for the US proposals issued in March 2026?
> None: the agencies' release states no first-application date.
> Any date would come with a final rule, which had not been issued as of 2026-10.

> [!question]- reg-19-11 | A bank has pre-floor RWA of 485 and standardised RWA of 720. Under which of 65%, 67.5%, 70% and 72.5% does the floor bind, and what is binding RWA?
> It binds above 67.36% ($485 / 720$): 486 at 67.5%, 504 at 70% and 522 at 72.5%; at 65% the floor is 468 and binding RWA stays 485.

> [!question]- reg-19-12 | What is the reduced BA-CVA capital on a USD 18.5m EAD, 3.5-year netting set with an unrated airline?
> About USD 2.34m.
> RW 8.5% (HY and NR, transport sector, MAR50.16), $DF = 0.91738845$, $SCVA = 0.085 \times 3.5 \times 18.5\text{m} \times 0.91738845 / 1.4 = 3{,}606{,}483$, capital $0.65 \times SCVA = 2{,}344{,}214$.
> Mapping to investment grade (3%) cuts it to 827,370.

> [!question]- reg-19-13 | What did the EU do once it could no longer postpone FRTB?
> Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1221 (adopted 4 June 2026, applying from 1 January 2027) introduced temporary operational relief and targeted multipliers: banks may scale the market-risk own-funds requirement by a factor of at most 1 until 31 December 2029.
> The CRR empowerment allowed two years of postponement, already used by the 2024 and 2025 delegated acts.

> [!question]- reg-19-14 | When does the UK's FRTB internal model approach apply?
> 1 January 2028 (PS1/26), a year after the rest of Basel 3.1, with existing IMA permissions continuing until then.
> CP9/26 (June 2026) proposes adjustments to the IMA while keeping the 2028 date.

> [!question]- reg-19-15 | What is Canada's output floor as of 2026-10?
> 67.5% until further notice, with at least two years' notice before any increase (OSFI CAR 2026, chapter 1, paragraph 33).
> OSFI's 2024 schedule had planned 70% in 2026 and 72.5% in 2027.
