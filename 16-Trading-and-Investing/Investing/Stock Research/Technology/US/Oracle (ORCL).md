# Stock: Oracle (ORCL)

## Investment Thesis (Why it is a consistent compounder)
Oracle is the "data vault" of the world. Its database technology is so deeply integrated into the mission-critical systems of banks, governments, and global corporations that it is virtually impossible to remove. Recently, Oracle has successfully reinvented itself as a major Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) provider, specifically optimized for high-performance AI workloads.

## Financial Moat (Connect to Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers)
1. **Switching Costs:** "Database Lock-in." Migrating a global bank's core ledger from Oracle to a competitor is a "bet the company" risk that most CTOs won't take.
2. **Scale Economies:** Massive installed base allows for high R&D and aggressive acquisitions (e.g., Cerner).
3. **Counter-Positioning:** OCI (Oracle Cloud Infrastructure) counter-positioned against AWS by focusing on a "Generation 2" cloud designed specifically for security and database performance.

## Historical Performance (CAGR, key eras)
- **1980s-90s:** Dominance in relational databases.
- **2000s:** Growth through massive acquisitions (PeopleSoft, Siebel, Sun Microsystems).
- **2020s:** The Cloud and AI pivot.

## Risk Factors (Connect to Anti-Models)
- **Open Source:** Postgres and other open-source databases eroding the low-to-mid market.
- **Cloud Late-Mover:** AWS and Azure have a significant head start in general-purpose cloud.

## Architect Perspective (What would Buffett/Munger/Lynch say?)
- **Warren Buffett:** Generally avoids Oracle, but Larry Ellison's "ruthless efficiency" and the "toll bridge" nature of the database business align with Buffett's preference for monopolies.
- **Peter Lynch:** Would note that even if you don't see Oracle, every time you use a credit card or a hospital system, Oracle is likely in the background.
