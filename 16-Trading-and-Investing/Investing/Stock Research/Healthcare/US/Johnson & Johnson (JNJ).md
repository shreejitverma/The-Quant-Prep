# Stock: Johnson & Johnson (JNJ)

## Investment Thesis (Why it is a consistent compounder)
JNJ is a healthcare conglomerate (though recently spun off its consumer division Kenvue) that operates as a collection of high-quality "AAA" rated businesses. Its focus on Innovative Medicine (Pharmaceuticals) and MedTech (Surgical robots, implants) provides a balance of high-growth blockbuster drugs and steady, recurring medical device revenue.

## Financial Moat (Connect to Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers)
1. **Cornered Resource:** A massive portfolio of patents for life-saving drugs (e.g., Darzalex, Stelara).
2. **Scale Economies:** One of the world's largest R&D budgets, allowing it to acquire promising biotech startups and scale them globally.
3. **Switching Costs:** Surgeons are trained on JNJ medical devices and instruments; switching to a different system requires significant retraining and workflow disruption.
4. **Branding:** A century of trust in the medical community.

## Historical Performance (CAGR, key eras)
- **Dividend King:** Over 60 consecutive years of dividend increases.
- **Consistent Compounder:** Historically delivers steady returns with lower volatility than the broader market.

## Risk Factors (Connect to Anti-Models)
- **Litigation (Anti-Model: The Legal Quagmire):** Talc and opioid-related lawsuits have cost billions.
- **Patent Cliff:** Loss of exclusivity on major drugs (e.g., Stelara).
- **R&D Productivity:** The "Eroom's Law" problem where drug discovery becomes increasingly expensive.

## Architect Perspective (What would Buffett/Munger/Lynch say?)
- **Warren Buffett:** Historically held JNJ for its "bond-like" stability and defensive nature.
- **Peter Lynch:** Would love the "MedTech" side - products that are used every day in hospitals around the world.
