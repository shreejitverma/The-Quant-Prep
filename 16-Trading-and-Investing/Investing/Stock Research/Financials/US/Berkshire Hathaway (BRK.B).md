# Stock: Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B)

## Investment Thesis (Why it is a consistent compounder)
Berkshire Hathaway is the ultimate compounding machine. It is a massive conglomerate that uses the "float" from its insurance operations (GEICO, Berkshire Reinsurance) to acquire whole businesses and stocks with high returns on capital. It is built for resilience, holding a massive cash pile to deploy during market panics.

## Financial Moat (Connect to Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers)
1. **Scale Economies:** Access to immense capital allows it to make "elephant" sized acquisitions that competitors cannot.
2. **Cornered Resource:** The management duo of Buffett and Munger (historically) and the "Berkshire Culture" that attracts business owners looking for a permanent home.
3. **Process Power:** A decentralized management structure that minimizes bureaucracy and maximizes operational efficiency.
4. **Financial Strength:** AAA-rated stability (psychologically) allows it to be the "lender of last resort" during crises, extracting favorable terms (e.g., Goldman Sachs/BofA during 2008).

## Historical Performance (CAGR, key eras)
- **1965-Present:** Transformed from a failing textile mill into a $900B+ conglomerate.
- **CAGR:** ~20% annually since 1965, roughly double the S&P 500's return over the same period.

## Risk Factors (Connect to Anti-Models)
- **Succession Risk:** The transition from Warren Buffett to Greg Abel.
- **Size Drag:** Berkshire is so large that it is increasingly difficult to find "needle-moving" investments.
- **Complexity:** Hard for the average investor to value accurately.

## Architect Perspective (What would Buffett/Munger/Lynch say?)
- **Warren Buffett:** "Our favorite holding period is forever."
- **Charlie Munger:** "The first rule of compounding is to never interrupt it unnecessarily."
- **Peter Lynch:** Would love the "Diworsification" vs "Deworsification" aspect - Berkshire actually makes diversification work.
