# Stock: Goldman Sachs (GS)

## Investment Thesis (Why it is a consistent compounder)
Goldman Sachs is the premier "meritocracy" of Wall Street. It dominates M&A (Mergers & Acquisitions), IPO underwriting, and institutional trading. Its primary asset is "Human Capital" - the ability to attract the smartest minds in finance to work under the GS banner.

## Financial Moat (Connect to Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers)
1. **Branding:** The "Goldman Sachs" name is the ultimate prestige in finance, allowing them to win the most lucrative mandates.
2. **Cornered Resource:** The "GS Alumni Network." Former Goldman partners are everywhere in government, central banks, and Fortune 500 boardrooms.
3. **Scale Economies:** Their trading floor and data infrastructure provide a massive edge in market-making and risk management.

## Historical Performance (CAGR, key eras)
- **Pre-1999:** A private partnership.
- **Post-IPO:** Transitioned to a public company, surviving 2008 with a Buffett injection and becoming a Bank Holding Company.

## Risk Factors (Connect to Anti-Models)
- **Culture Decay:** If the "meritocracy" is replaced by "bureaucracy."
- **Regulatory Risk:** Volcker Rule and other restrictions on proprietary trading.
- **Earnings Volatility:** M&A and Trading are inherently more cyclical than retail banking.

## Architect Perspective (What would Buffett/Munger/Lynch say?)
- **Warren Buffett:** Famously saved GS in 2008 with a $5B investment, citing the firm's unique "talent density."
- **Charlie Munger:** Respected the intellectual caliber, though sometimes critical of Wall Street's "excesses."
