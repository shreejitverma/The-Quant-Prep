# Stock: Costco (COST)

## Investment Thesis (Why it is a consistent compounder)
Costco is a "Subscription Club" disguised as a retailer. It doesn't make its money on selling goods (which are sold at near cost); it makes its profit on membership fees. This aligns its interests perfectly with customers: provide the highest quality goods at the absolute lowest price to ensure membership renewal (which sits at ~90%+).

## Financial Moat (Connect to Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers)
1. **Scale Economies Shared:** As Costco gets bigger, it gets better prices, which it *passes on* to the customer, making it even bigger (The "Nick Sleep" Flywheel).
2. **Switching Costs:** The membership fee acts as a "sunk cost" that incentivizes members to shop exclusively at Costco to get their "money's worth."
3. **Branding:** Extreme trust in the "Kirkland Signature" private label.
4. **Process Power:** Extreme efficiency in warehouse layout and SKU management (Costco carries ~4,000 SKUs vs Walmart's ~100,000+).

## Historical Performance (CAGR, key eras)
- **Consistent Outperformer:** Has delivered massive CAGR over the last 30 years with very few periods of decline.

## Risk Factors (Connect to Anti-Models)
- **Valuation:** Often trades at a very high P/E ratio for a retailer.
- **Saturation:** Limited room for new warehouses in mature markets.

## Architect Perspective (What would Buffett/Munger/Lynch say?)
- **Charlie Munger:** "I love everything about Costco. I'm a total addict." He sat on the board for decades.
- **Nick Sleep:** Developed the "Scale Economies Shared" model based on Costco.
