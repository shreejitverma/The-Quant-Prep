# Stock: Procter & Gamble (PG)

## Investment Thesis (Why it is a consistent compounder)
P&G is the king of "small ticket" recurring purchases. If you brush your teeth (Crest), shave (Gillette), wash your clothes (Tide), or change a diaper (Pampers), you are likely a P&G customer. It owns the #1 or #2 spot in almost every category it competes in.

## Financial Moat (Connect to Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers)
1. **Branding:** A portfolio of "Trust" brands that allow for a premium over private labels.
2. **Scale Economies:** Unmatched advertising budget and data analytics on consumer behavior.
3. **Process Power:** Massive R&D that actually improves mundane products (e.g., Tide Pods, multi-blade razors).

## Historical Performance (CAGR, key eras)
- **The Turnaround (2015-Present):** Under David Taylor and Jon Moeller, P&G slimmed down from 170 brands to 65 core brands, leading to a massive resurgence in growth and margins.

## Risk Factors (Connect to Anti-Models)
- **Private Label Erosion:** Kirkland (Costco) and Amazon Basics taking share in essentials.
- **Direct-to-Consumer (DTC):** Startups (like Dollar Shave Club) attacking specific niches.

## Architect Perspective (What would Buffett/Munger/Lynch say?)
- **Warren Buffett:** Historically a major shareholder (via Gillette acquisition), though he later swapped PG for Duracell.
- **Peter Lynch:** A classic "Invest in what you use" stock.
