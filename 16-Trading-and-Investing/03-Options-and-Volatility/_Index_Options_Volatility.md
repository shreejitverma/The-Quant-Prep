# Options and Volatility Pillar Index

The Options and Volatility pillar covers non-linear derivatives pricing, Greeks sensitivities, volatility surface geometry, and institutional volatility trading strategies.
It approaches options not as directional lotteries, but as precise instruments for trading implied versus realized volatility, skew, kurtosis, and market-maker inventory positioning.

---

## Learning Objectives

- Master analytic and numerical option pricing under Black-Scholes-Merton, binomial trees, and local/stochastic volatility models (Heston, SABR).
- Quantify first-order (Delta, Theta, Vega, Rho) and second-order / cross Greeks (Gamma, Vanna, Volga, Charm, Speed).
- Reconstruct the implied volatility surface across moneyness and term structure to detect relative-value mispricings.
- Compute aggregate dealer Gamma Exposure (GEX) and predict market regime transitions (volatility suppression versus volatility acceleration).
- Design and backtest systematic volatility strategies including delta-neutral gamma scalping, calendar spreads, and dispersion trading.

## Core Topics

### 1. Greeks and Sensitivity Surfaces
- **First-Order Greeks**:
  - Delta ($\Delta$): Hedge ratio and directional sensitivity; drift and interest rate adjustments in dividend-paying assets.
  - Vega ($\nu$): Sensitivity to changes in implied volatility; term structure weighting across short-dated versus long-dated contracts.
  - Theta ($\Theta$): Calendar decay and non-linear acceleration as expiration approaches for at-the-money (ATM) strikes.
  - Rho ($\rho$): Sensitivity to the risk-free rate; impacts on deep in-the-money (ITM) leaps and long-dated interest rate options.
- **Second-Order and Cross Greeks**:
  - Gamma ($\Gamma$): Rate of change of Delta; the engine of delta-hedging convexity and gamma squeezes.
  - Vanna ($\frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial \sigma}$): Sensitivity of Delta to volatility changes; critical for skew risk and market maker hedging under sharp market drops.
  - Volga / Vomma ($\frac{\partial \nu}{\partial \sigma}$): Convexity of option price with respect to volatility; essential for pricing out-of-the-money (OTM) tail hedges.
  - Charm ($\frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial t}$): Delta decay over time; drives dealer rebalancing into weekend and expiration closes.

### 2. Volatility Surface Architecture
- **Implied Volatility Smile and Smirk**: Structural supply-demand imbalances, crash-phobia post-1987, and leverage hypothesis explaining persistent equity put skews.
- **Term Structure of Volatility**: Contango (calm regimes with upward-sloping IV) versus backwardation (crisis regimes with inverted near-term IV spikes).
- **Sticky Strike vs Sticky Moneyness (Delta)**: Practical delta-hedging implications when the underlying asset moves sharply.

### 3. Dealer Positioning and Gamma Exposure (GEX)
- **Market Maker Inventory Dynamics**: Long gamma regimes (dealers buy dips and sell rips, suppressing realized volatility) versus short gamma regimes (dealers sell dips and buy rips, amplifying market moves).
- **Zero Gamma Flip Point**: Quantitative estimation of the underlying price level where aggregate market maker gamma transitions from positive to negative.
- **Vanna and Charm Flows**: Systematic market-on-close flows generated by option dealers adjusting delta hedges as time passes and volatility shifts.

### 4. Advanced Volatility Trading Strategies
- **Gamma Scalping**: Mechanics of maintaining a delta-neutral book while monetizing realized market path oscillations against paid implied volatility.
- **Dispersion Trading**: Trading the spread between index implied volatility and the weighted average implied volatility of individual component stocks.
- **Variance Swaps and Volatility Carry**: Replicating log-contracts through continuous strips of OTM calls and puts; capturing the variance risk premium (VRP).
