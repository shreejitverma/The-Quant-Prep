# Jump Trading

> [!info] Draft
> Facts below were gathered from the sources listed at the end on 2026-09-28.
> Firms change their processes often; verify anything that matters with your recruiter.

## Snapshot

- Jump Trading describes itself as a research-driven global trading firm where "traders, engineers, and researchers solve hard problems" across asset classes and time horizons.
- Its site says it was established about 25 years ago; Wikipedia states it was founded in 1999 in Chicago by former CME pit traders Bill DiSomma and Paul Gurinas.
- It trades its own capital; Wikipedia describes it as privately funded throughout its existence.
- Its site lists 12 offices in 10 countries, including Chicago, New York, Austin, London, Paris, Amsterdam, Singapore, Hong Kong, Shanghai, Mumbai and Sydney.
- It describes technology spanning "raw silicon through order execution" and machine learning infrastructure for live inference.
- Related entities include Jump Crypto and Jump Capital, its venture arm.
- Wikipedia cites more than 2,000 employees; treat that as unverified.

## Roles

- Quantitative researcher: builds trading strategies from research; Jump recruits from mathematics, physics and computer science and says every strategy is "grounded in research, stress-tested in real markets".
- Quantitative trader: runs and improves strategies with researchers and engineers; campus postings combine researcher and trader internships.
- Software engineer and FPGA engineer: build the trading platform, low-latency systems and hardware.
- AI and machine learning: the experienced-hire pages list AI/ML, Technology, Trading and Signals as areas.

## Interview process

> [!warning] Commonly reported, verify with your recruiter
> Round counts and formats vary by role, office and year.
> Jump publishes little about its interview process.

- Jump's pages direct process questions to its recruiting teams rather than describing stages.
- Candidate reports commonly describe an online assessment or phone screen, one or more technical interviews, and a final round of several interviews.
- Researcher and trader reports mention probability, statistics and data questions, and discussion of past research for PhD candidates.
- Engineer reports stress C++ depth, systems knowledge and algorithms; FPGA candidates report hardware design questions.

## Culture and what they value

- Jump says constant innovation requires "fearlessness, creativity, intellectual honesty, and a relentless competitive streak".
- It describes winning together and "incenting collaboration and mutual respect".
- Its experienced-hire page asks for "curiosity, not complacency" and says "Our culture isn't a benefit. It's a foundation."
- It supports research through fellowship programmes and conference travel grants.

## Senior hire notes

- Jump has a dedicated experienced-candidates page and separate recruiting contacts for US, international and campus hiring.
- Experienced researchers should expect detailed questions on strategies and models they have built; do not disclose a prior employer's proprietary details.
- Experienced engineers should expect deep C++ and systems discussion grounded in systems they have shipped.
- Non-compete and garden-leave periods are common in HFT and vary by jurisdiction; check your contract.
- See [Senior-Level Expectations](../13-Interview-Playbook/12-Senior-Level-Expectations.md).
