# Akuna Capital

> [!info] Draft
> Facts below were gathered from the sources listed at the end on 2026-09-27.
> Firms change their processes often; verify anything that matters with your recruiter.

## Snapshot

- Akuna describes itself as a proprietary trading firm specialising in options market making.
- It began as an idea in Sydney and opened its Chicago headquarters in 2011.
- Offices are in Chicago, Sydney, Shanghai, Singapore and London.
- It builds its own trading systems, models and infrastructure.
- Headcount is not published on the pages consulted.

## Roles

- Trader: makes markets in options and manages the resulting risk; Akuna frames the role as helping shape the market.
- Quant: builds pricing, volatility and statistical models used by trading.
- Developer: builds the in-house trading systems and infrastructure; the firm organises around Technology, Trading, Quant and Operations.

## Interview process

> [!warning] Commonly reported, verify with your recruiter
> Round counts and formats vary by role, office and year.

- Candidate reports commonly describe online assessments first: mental maths and number sequences for traders, and a timed coding test in Python or C++ for developers.
- Some trader candidates report a betting-style game and recorded video answers after the online tests.
- Technical interviews reportedly cover probability, brainteasers, market making and options.
- Final rounds are reported as a longer block that revisits earlier topics plus fit questions.
- Speed and confidence under time pressure are repeatedly mentioned by candidates.

## Culture and what they value

- Akuna lists liquidity provision, rapid innovation through technology, collaborative flow of knowledge and individual growth as guiding principles.
- The name comes from an Aboriginal Australian term the firm glosses as "flowing water".
- It emphasises ownership and cross-functional collaboration.

## Senior hire notes

- Akuna hires experienced professionals through a separate pathway; expect options depth and risk-management discussion for trading roles.
- Experienced developers should expect detailed questions on systems they have built and their performance trade-offs.
- Check non-compete and garden-leave terms at your current employer before agreeing a start date.
- See [Senior-Level Expectations](../13-Interview-Playbook/12-Senior-Level-Expectations.md).
