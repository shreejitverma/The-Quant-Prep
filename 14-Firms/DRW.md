# DRW

> [!info] Draft
> Facts below were gathered from the sources listed at the end on 2026-09-27.
> Firms change their processes often; verify anything that matters with your recruiter.

## Snapshot

- DRW is a proprietary trading firm that trades its own capital and has no outside investors.
- It was founded in 1992 by Don Wilson and is headquartered in Chicago, with offices on five continents.
- It trades equities, fixed income and energy among other markets, and runs Cumberland DRW for crypto assets (launched 2014).
- Other units include DRW Venture Capital, Convexity Properties (real estate) and Artemeter DRW (carbon markets).
- The firm states more than 2,000 employees, including more than 800 technologists.

## Roles

- Quantitative trading analyst: per DRW, investigates trading opportunities through data analysis and model building, builds and backtests models, and contributes to risk systems.
- Trader: runs strategies across DRW's asset classes, from latency-sensitive trading to discretionary risk taking.
- Quantitative researcher: builds models and signals; DRW asks research candidates to discuss their research and publications in detail.
- Software engineer: builds trading and data systems; DRW says developer interviews go deep on advanced computer science.

## Interview process

> [!warning] Commonly reported, verify with your recruiter
> Round counts and formats vary by role, office and year.

- DRW describes a three-stage process for quantitative trading analysts: a remote quantitative challenge, a roughly 45-minute virtual interview on probability and statistics, and an in-person Superday.
- The Superday, per DRW, includes coding challenges and trading simulations; its final-round guide says it can be several back-to-back interviews.
- DRW advises trading candidates to be ready to discuss current market events.
- Candidate reports mention probability puzzles, mental maths and expectation and variance questions in early rounds.

## Preparation plan

1. Week 1: run `./qp firms drw`; work the [Probability Problem Set](../01-Probability/15-Probability-Problem-Set.md) and do `./qp drill arith` daily.
2. Week 2: statistics, then the [Statistics Problem Set](../02-Statistics-and-Econometrics/14-Statistics-Problem-Set.md), aimed at the virtual probability and statistics screen.
3. Week 3: Python coding under time pressure with [Numerical Python](../12-Quant-Development/11-Numerical-Python-NumPy-and-Numba.md) and [Coding Interviews for Quants](../13-Interview-Playbook/07-Coding-Interviews-for-Quants.md).
4. Week 4: trading simulations; study [Trading Games in Interviews](../13-Interview-Playbook/05-Trading-Games-in-Interviews.md) and run `./qp drill mm`.
5. Week 5: markets knowledge for your desk of interest, plus a daily market summary habit from [Macro and Market Awareness](../11-Risk-and-Trading/08-Macro-and-Market-Awareness.md).
6. Week 6: clear `./qp review` and rehearse [Behavioral and Fit](../13-Interview-Playbook/09-Behavioral-and-Fit.md).

## Culture and what they value

- DRW names curiosity as the top quality for a quant trader and stresses collaboration and communication.
- Its final-round guide describes a casual environment, business-casual dress, and treating interviewers as future coworkers.
- New analysts receive product orientation and desk-specific training before trading.

## Senior hire notes

- Experienced traders are commonly assessed on track record, risk management and the specific market they would trade; DRW's multi-asset spread means desk fit matters.
- Researchers should expect detailed discussion of prior research and publications, per DRW's own guidance.
- Check non-compete and garden-leave terms at your current employer before negotiating a start date.
- See [Senior-Level Expectations](../13-Interview-Playbook/12-Senior-Level-Expectations.md).
