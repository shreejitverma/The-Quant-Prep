# IMC Trading

> [!info] Draft
> Facts below were gathered from the sources listed at the end on 2026-09-28.
> Firms change their processes often; verify anything that matters with your recruiter.

## Snapshot

- IMC is a global market maker that trades its own capital using automated strategies.
- It was founded in 1989 by two floor traders on the Amsterdam Equity Options Exchange.
- It describes itself as built on "research, technology, trading", trading on more than 120 venues across asset classes.
- Its site describes IMC as a top-three liquidity provider by volume in listed options globally and a lead market maker in more than 150 US ETFs; it also runs a crypto trading team.
- It states more than 2,070 employees across 10 offices; Amsterdam, Chicago and Sydney are its main regional hubs.

## Roles

- Trader: trades a wide range of instruments and develops, maintains and improves trading strategies and algorithms; graduate traders start with a six-week Global Trainee Programme before placement on discretionary, systematic or execution-focused desks.
- Quantitative researcher and machine learning researcher: build pricing, signal and forecasting models; IMC lists ML research internships.
- Software engineer and hardware engineer: build the automated trading systems and infrastructure.
- IMC says trader candidates need no prior finance knowledge; programming (Python, Matlab or R) is listed as preferred for graduate traders.

## Interview process

> [!warning] Commonly reported, verify with your recruiter
> Round counts and formats vary by role, office and year.

- IMC's own preparation article describes four stages for campus hires: an online assessment, a behavioural interview with a recruiter, a technical interview with a trader or engineer, and an in-office assessment day with technical and behavioural stations.
- IMC says the technical interview is a problem you solve closely with the interviewer, and advises picking up on hints and asking questions when stuck.
- IMC publishes a downloadable preparation guide for the trading interview; read it before the online assessment.
- Trader candidates commonly report mental arithmetic, probability and numerical reasoning in the online assessment, and market-making or trading games later.
- Engineer candidates commonly report a coding assessment and technical interviews on algorithms and systems.

## Culture and what they value

- IMC says resilience is "a huge part of being a trader" and that interviews test how you learn, handle unfamiliar problems and work with others.
- It says it pays close attention to cultural fit alongside technical ability.
- It presents itself as an early adopter of new technology, with engineers building the systems that research-driven strategies run on.

## Senior hire notes

- Experienced hires apply to specific roles; the campus four-stage process may not apply, so ask the recruiter what the loop looks like.
- Experienced traders should expect questions on products they have traded, their risk management and how they improved strategies.
- Experienced engineers should expect deeper system design and performance discussion.
- Non-compete and garden-leave periods are common in market making and vary by jurisdiction; check your contract.
- See [Senior-Level Expectations](../13-Interview-Playbook/12-Senior-Level-Expectations.md).
