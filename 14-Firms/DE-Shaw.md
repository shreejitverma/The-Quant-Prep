# D. E. Shaw

> [!info] Draft
> Facts below were gathered from the sources listed at the end on 2026-09-28.
> Firms change their processes often; verify anything that matters with your recruiter.

## Snapshot

- The D. E. Shaw group is a global investment and technology development firm founded in 1988 by David E. Shaw, and describes itself as a pioneer in computational finance.
- It says it grew from six employees and $28 million in capital to more than 3,000 people.
- It manages client capital across quantitative, discretionary and hybrid strategies, including Systematic Equities, Systematic Futures, Discretionary Macro, Fundamental Equities, Fixed Income, Options, Energy, Reinsurance and private-markets strategies.
- Headquarters is in New York; it also has international offices, including in India.
- Assets under management are not stated on the pages consulted.

## Roles

- Quantitative analyst: per the firm, applies mathematical techniques and writes software to develop, analyse and implement statistical models for computerised trading strategies, aiming to increase profitability, decrease risk and reduce transaction costs.
- Quantitative researcher and systematic trading roles within the firm's quantitative strategies group.
- Software developer and systems roles: build the trading, research and data infrastructure.
- Discretionary and fundamental analyst roles: the firm says these assess comfort with statistical and logical reasoning, without assuming finance knowledge.
- A New York quantitative analyst listing stated base salaries of $275,000 (bachelor's or master's) and $300,000 (PhD) at the access date; posted figures change and exclude bonus.

## Interview process

> [!warning] Commonly reported, verify with your recruiter
> Round counts and formats vary by role, office and year.

- The firm's interviewing guide describes application review, phone and virtual interviews, references, and an offer, and warns the process can take several weeks.
- Per the firm, technical roles get questions on probability, statistics, algorithms, logic and programming, plus behavioural and situational questions.
- The firm says it looks at "what techniques, concepts, or algorithms you use", not only whether the final answer is right.
- It advises candidates to ask clarifying questions, "show your work" by stating assumptions, weigh trade-offs and discuss next steps.
- Candidate reports for quantitative analysts commonly describe several technical interviews mixing probability puzzles, statistics and coding.

## Culture and what they value

- The firm emphasises "creativity, entrepreneurship, and the spirit of discovery" and "a culture of collaboration across disciplines, geographies, and investment strategies".
- Its interviewing guide lists high standards, rigorous analysis, clear communication, collaboration and intellectual curiosity.
- It says candidates do not need prior finance knowledge, and it has no dress code for interviewees or employees.

## Senior hire notes

- Experienced candidates apply to specific groups; expect the same analytical bar plus detailed discussion of past work and results.
- Reference checks are an explicit step in the firm's published process; line up referees early.
- Keep a prior employer's proprietary models, data and code out of the conversation.
- Non-compete and garden-leave terms are common in this industry and vary by jurisdiction; review them with counsel.
