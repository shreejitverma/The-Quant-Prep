## Item set 1 (04-02): Marrowby Toys: Product Safety in an Earnings-Multiple Valuation

Source: [[IS-04-Corporate-Issuers-LM02-M06]], difficulty 3.

### Vignette

Ines Carvalho, CFA, is an equity analyst at Lindqvist Asset Management.
She covers Marrowby Toys plc, a listed manufacturer of children's toys that sells through large retailers in Europe and North America.
Over the past two years, national safety regulators have ordered three recalls of Marrowby products, including a set of magnetic building blocks that small children could swallow.
Carvalho concludes that product safety is Marrowby's most material ESG factor.

Before building her forecast, Carvalho discusses how to identify material factors with her colleague Owen Pryce.

Carvalho: "SASB publishes a materiality map of sector-specific ESG factors, which helps us decide which factors to research first for a toy manufacturer."

Pryce: "Environmental factors are the most material ESG factors in every industry, so we should start with the emissions from Marrowby's packaging plants."

Exhibit 1: Carvalho's 2027 forecast for Marrowby before ESG adjustments (GBP millions, except per-share data)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue | 2,400 |
| Cost of goods sold (COGS) as a percentage of revenue | 46.0% |
| Selling, general and administrative expense | 820 |
| Interest expense | 40 |
| Capital expenditure | 95 |
| Tax rate | 25% |
| Shares outstanding (millions) | 150 |
| Dividend per share (GBP) | 0.90 |

Carvalho makes three ESG adjustments to the 2027 forecast:

- Retailers are expected to reduce shelf space for Marrowby products, lowering revenue by 3.0%.
- Additional product testing and third-party factory audits raise COGS as a percentage of revenue by 1.5 percentage points.
- Litigation provisions and restructuring charges add non-operating expenses equal to 5.0% of adjusted operating income.

She holds selling, general and administrative expense, interest expense, and the tax rate at their Exhibit 1 values.

Carvalho values Marrowby's equity by applying a forward price-to-earnings multiple to her ESG-adjusted 2027 earnings per share.
Listed toy manufacturers trade at an average forward P/E of 18.0x, and Marrowby's shares currently trade at GBP 24.10, or 14.5x trailing earnings.
To reflect the risk of lasting damage to Marrowby's brand, she applies a multiple of 16.0x instead of the peer average.

Carvalho also runs a scenario analysis.
A consumer-safety agency is consulting on a ban of small high-powered magnets in toys.
She assigns a 25% probability to the ban, in which case she estimates Marrowby's equity value at GBP 19.50 per share.
In the remaining 75% of outcomes, she uses her multiple-based value as the base case.

Marlowe holds $22 million of protection on Portage Mills, a pulp producer whose five-year senior unsecured notes are the reference obligation.
Portage missed a scheduled interest payment on those notes, and the grace period expired last week without payment.
Portage has not filed for bankruptcy and says it is negotiating with a group of its lenders.
The regional Determinations Committee met, and its members voted 11 to 4 that a failure to pay had occurred.
Raman writes in his review: "Because the committee has voted that a failure to pay occurred, a credit event has been declared, and settlement of our Portage protection should follow in about 30 days."

Two weeks after the trade, Cormac announces a large acquisition financed with new debt, and its five-year credit spread jumps to 580 bps.

## Item set 5 (06-02): Halvorsen Re: Rebuilding a Vendor Lattice

### Vignette

Halvorsen Re is a reinsurer that holds a large portfolio of government and quasi-government bonds to back its claims reserves.
Leila Haddad, CFA, is the senior investment risk analyst, and she reports to Stefan Brandt, the chief risk officer.
A pricing vendor has delivered a lognormal binomial interest rate tree that Halvorsen will later use for its callable holdings, but a data-transfer error corrupted part of the file.

The vendor confirms that the tree was built with the one-factor lognormal model and calibrated to the annual-pay benchmark par curve in Exhibit 1.

| 2-year par rate | 3.70% |

Exhibit 2: Surviving entries of the vendor tree (one-year forward rates)

| Time 0 | Time 1 | Time 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1000% | 5.0603% | 6.7944% |
| | (corrupted) | (corrupted) |
| | | (corrupted) |

Halvorsen's model policy requires that any tree used for valuation reprice the benchmark par bonds exactly and that its volatility assumption be documented in the model inventory.
The vendor has promised to resend the Time 3 rates next week, so the four-year par rate is not needed for the current rebuild.
Haddad's plan is to rebuild the tree from the surviving entries rather than rely on an outside volatility estimate.

Brandt also questions whether the rebuild matters for valuation.
He makes the following comment.

"Suppose we ignore the vendor's volatility and recalibrate the whole tree at the 22% swaption volatility.
The wider tree gives more weight to low-rate outcomes, where the agency bond is worth more, so its value on the lattice will come out above what the vendor's tree gives."

Haddad replies that she will show the committee the Time 0 value from the rebuilt tree alongside the value from a recalibrated 22% tree before the bond is approved.

## Item set 6 (10-03): Solstice Crowd Markets: The Luma Listing

### Vignette

One applicant is Luma Mobility, which makes electric bicycles for teenagers and sells them through an app used mainly by 13- to 17-year-olds.
Luma plans to promote its crowdfunding round by sending in-app messages to all app users, inviting them to "tell your parents about owning a piece of Luma."
