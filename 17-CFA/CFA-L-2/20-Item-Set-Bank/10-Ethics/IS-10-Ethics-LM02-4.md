# Harrow Advisers: Brokerage, Referrals, and Side Payments

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Lucia Ferrante, CFA, manages institutional equity portfolios at Harrow Advisers.
Her largest account is the Pemberton Municipal Employees' Retirement Plan, whose investment policy statement restricts the account to large-capitalization equities.
Harrow also runs a long-short hedge fund that invests in small-cap biotechnology companies and is managed by a separate team.

Exhibit 1: Pemberton account brokerage, calendar year 2025

| Broker | Shares traded | Commission per share (USD) | Services |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oakline Securities | 3,200,000 | 0.040 | Execution plus proprietary small-cap biotechnology research |
| Delmont Brokerage | 800,000 | 0.030 | Execution only; directed by the Pemberton trustees |

Execution-only brokers of comparable execution quality quoted USD 0.015 per share throughout 2025.
The Oakline research is used only by Harrow's hedge fund team.
Harrow's head trader, Simon Kade, told Ferrante: "Pemberton picked Delmont, so best execution on those trades is their problem, not ours."
He added: "The Oakline arrangement is fine, because our soft dollar policy is described in our regulatory filings."

Since January 2025, Ferrante has recommended Lockridge Trust Company as custodian to her clients and prospective clients.
Lockridge pays Ferrante personally an annual fee of 0.04% of the assets of the clients she refers, paid quarterly at one-quarter of the annual rate on the quarter's average referred assets, plus a one-time payment of USD 3,000 for each new account opened.
Harrow's compliance department approved the arrangement in writing when it began.
Ferrante has not mentioned the arrangement to clients or prospects, reasoning that the payments are "trivial relative to custody balances in the hundreds of millions."
In the third quarter of 2025, average referred assets were USD 140 million, and two new referred accounts were opened.
Compliance has now asked Ferrante for the update on her referral compensation that the recommended procedures call for.

In November 2025, Marisol Aldana, whose USD 12 million family portfolio Ferrante manages at Harrow, offered to pay Ferrante personally 10% of the dollar amount by which the portfolio's annual return exceeds 8%, beginning in 2026.
Ferrante expects the portfolio to return 10.5% in 2026 and intends to accept the offer after following the recommended procedures.

In December 2025, Oakline sent Ferrante two courtside season tickets and a weekend at a ski resort, valued together at about USD 9,500, "in appreciation of our partnership."
She accepted them and told no one at Harrow.
In the same month, Aldana gave Ferrante a watch valued at about USD 1,200 to thank her for the family's 2025 results.
Ferrante reported the watch to her supervisor the next day.

Harrow's chief compliance officer, Grace Oyelaran, CFA, plans to review the firm's brokerage allocation, referral, and gift policies at the January 2026 compliance committee meeting.
She has asked Ferrante for a written account of each arrangement described above, including the amounts involved.

## Questions

1. The amount of the Pemberton plan's 2025 brokerage that paid for services not benefiting the plan is closest to: (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. USD 48,000.
   - B. USD 80,000.
   - C. USD 128,000.
2. Which assessment of Kade's two claims is most accurate? (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. Kade is correct about the Delmont trades but incorrect about the Oakline arrangement.
   - B. Kade is correct about the Oakline arrangement but incorrect about the Delmont trades.
   - C. Kade is incorrect about both.
3. The third-quarter 2025 referral compensation that Ferrante should report in the update is closest to: (LOS: los-10-02-b)
   - A. USD 14,000.
   - B. USD 20,000.
   - C. USD 62,000.
4. With respect to the Lockridge arrangement, Ferrante most likely: (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. complies with the Standards, because her employer approved the arrangement in writing.
   - B. violates Standard VI(C), because she must disclose the arrangement, its nature, and its estimated value to clients and prospective clients before they enter into an agreement for the service.
   - C. complies with the Standards, because the payments are immaterial relative to the custody balances.
5. Before accepting Aldana's offer, the approximate annual amount of additional compensation that Ferrante should state in her written report to her supervisor and compliance is closest to: (LOS: los-10-02-b)
   - A. USD 30,000.
   - B. USD 126,000.
   - C. USD 300,000.
6. Regarding the December 2025 gifts, Ferrante most likely: (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. violated Standard I(B) by accepting the Oakline gifts but not by accepting Aldana's watch.
   - B. violated Standard I(B) by accepting Aldana's watch but not by accepting the Oakline gifts, because client gifts require prior written consent.
   - C. violated no Standard, because the Oakline gifts were given after the business had already been placed.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** The plan could have obtained comparable execution at USD 0.015 per share, so the premium paid to Oakline bought research used only by the hedge fund.
> Excess $= 3{,}200{,}000 \times (0.040 - 0.015) = 3{,}200{,}000 \times 0.025 = 80{,}000$.
> Client brokerage is the client's asset, and paying up for research that does not benefit the paying client violates Standard III(A).
> A is wrong because $3{,}200{,}000 \times 0.015 = 48{,}000$ is the execution cost the plan would have paid anyway, the part that did benefit it.
> C is wrong because $3{,}200{,}000 \times 0.040 = 128{,}000$ is the gross Oakline commission, which counts the legitimate execution cost as misuse.
> The Delmont commissions, $800{,}000 \times 0.030 = 24{,}000$, are a decoy: they are directed brokerage, not soft dollars.
> 2. **C.** Directed brokerage does not violate loyalty, but the manager must still seek best execution for the directed trades and should tell the client that directing brokerage may mean best execution is not achieved (Standard III(A)).
> Disclosure of a soft dollar policy does not authorize using one client's commissions for research that benefits other accounts; the Oakline premium remains a III(A) violation.
> A is wrong because it treats client direction as ending the manager's duty; B is wrong because it treats disclosure as a cure for misuse of client assets.
> 3. **B.** The quarterly rate is $0.04\% / 4 = 0.01\%$, so the asset-based payment is $140{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0001 = 14{,}000$.
> One-time payments are $2 \times 3{,}000 = 6{,}000$, so total third-quarter compensation is $20{,}000$.
> The recommended procedure under Standard VI(C) is to give the employer at least quarterly updates on the amount and nature of referral compensation received.
> A is wrong because it omits the one-time account payments, which are also consideration for the recommendation.
> C is wrong because it applies the annual rate to a single quarter: $140{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0004 + 6{,}000 = 62{,}000$.
> 4. **B.** Standard VI(C) requires disclosure to the employer, clients, and prospective clients, before entry into any formal agreement for the service, of the nature of the consideration and its estimated value, so that they can evaluate partiality and the full cost of the service.
> A is wrong because employer approval satisfies only one of the three audiences.
> C is wrong because the duty contains no materiality exemption for payments that seem small to the member.
> 5. **A.** Expected excess return $= 10.5\% - 8.0\% = 2.5\%$, so the expected dollar excess is $0.025 \times 12{,}000{,}000 = 300{,}000$, and Ferrante's 10% share is $30{,}000$.
> Standard IV(B) requires written consent from all parties before accepting compensation that could compete with the employer's interest, and the recommended procedure is an immediate written report to the supervisor and compliance stating the nature, approximate amount, and duration of the arrangement, confirmed by the offering party.
> B is wrong because it ignores the 8% hurdle: $0.10 \times 0.105 \times 12{,}000{,}000 = 126{,}000$.
> C is wrong because it reports the whole dollar excess rather than Ferrante's 10% share.
> 6. **A.** Oakline is a broker seeking continued order flow, and lavish benefits from a party seeking influence could reasonably be expected to compromise objectivity, so accepting them undisclosed violates Standard I(B).
> A modest gift from a client after good results is supplementary compensation in an existing relationship; disclosing it to the employer, before acceptance where possible and otherwise promptly afterwards, satisfies the Standards.
> B is wrong because client gifts require disclosure to the employer, not prior written consent; written consent is the Standard IV(B) test for compensation arrangements that compete with the employer.
> C is wrong because a reward for past order flow is also an inducement for future order flow.

## Distractor design

1. A: execution cost that did benefit the client mislabelled as misuse; C: gross commission counted instead of the research premium.
2. A: client direction treated as removing the best execution duty; B: disclosure treated as curing misuse of client brokerage.
3. A: one-time referral payments omitted; C: annual rate applied to a quarter (period error).
4. A: employer consent treated as complete disclosure; C: a materiality exemption invented for referral fees.
5. B: hurdle rate ignored; C: whole excess dollar return reported instead of the member's share.
6. B: IV(B) written-consent test misapplied to a disclosed client gift; C: timing of the gift treated as removing the influence.
