# Tessaly Capital: Month-End Marks and Personal Trading

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Tessaly Capital is a small asset manager with eleven employees.
It manages two public funds, the Emerging Leaders Fund and the Microcap Opportunities Fund.
Callum Reyes, CFA, manages both funds; his annual bonus depends on each fund's peer ranking, which a data provider calculates from month-end net asset values.
Tessaly's chief compliance officer is Anouk Visser, CFA.

On 30 April 2026, Reyes e-mailed the trading desk: "Get Brisk up into the close; the ranking snapshot is taken tonight."
The desk then entered buy orders in the closing auction for Brisk Minerals, a thinly traded stock held by both funds: 60,000 shares for Emerging Leaders and 90,000 shares for Microcap.

Exhibit 1: Brisk Minerals trading data

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Closing price, 29 April | 3.98 |
| Last trade before the closing auction, 30 April | 4.10 |
| Closing auction price, 30 April | 4.60 |
| Opening price, 4 May | 4.08 |
| Average daily volume (shares) | 70,000 |
| Closing auction volume, 30 April (shares) | 170,000 |

Exhibit 2: Microcap Opportunities Fund immediately before the 30 April closing auction

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Brisk shares held | 1,200,000 |
| Net assets, with Brisk valued at 4.10 | 180,000,000 |
| Units outstanding | 12,000,000 |
| Emerging Leaders Fund Brisk shares held | 800,000 |

When Visser questioned the trades, Reyes replied: "Every order was a genuine purchase at the market price, and the unitholders of both funds benefited from the higher valuation."

Tessaly's personal trading policy requires preclearance of every personal trade and prohibits employees from buying or selling a security from five business days before through three business days after any client trade in that security.
Purchases and sales are treated alike, and weekends are not business days.
In May 2026, the funds traded Kovac Rail on Wednesday 13 May and Lumen Grid on Monday 18 May.
The analyst Jonas Whitcombe obtained preclearance from the trading desk for the personal trades in Exhibit 3; the desk did not check the funds' trade calendar.

Exhibit 3: Whitcombe's personal trades, May 2026

| Trade | Security | Date | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Buy Kovac Rail | Thursday 7 May | |
| T2 | Sell Kovac Rail | Monday 18 May | Sale to pay university tuition |
| T3 | Buy Lumen Grid | Friday 8 May | |
| T4 | Buy Lumen Grid | Thursday 21 May | |

Tessaly's board is also selecting a subadviser for a new global small-cap sleeve.
The investment committee approximates each candidate's net return by deducting all fees from its five-year annualized gross return.
Aldermoor charges a management fee of 0.75% plus a performance fee of 10% of the gross annual return in excess of 8%.
One committee member proposes Brightfold "because it has the highest net return," and another proposes Castine "because fees are the only thing we can control."

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Brightfold Capital | 13.4% | 0.95% | 30% of assets in large-cap stocks | Returns not verified |
| Castine Global | 10.9% | 0.60% | Consistent | Unremediated 2025 regulatory finding on trade allocation controls |

## Questions

1. The increase in Microcap's 30 April NAV per unit caused by the closing auction price, relative to valuing Brisk at the last trade before the auction, is closest to: (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. 0.050.
   - B. 0.054.
   - C. 0.062.
2. Which statement about the 30 April Brisk trades is most accurate? (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. Reyes violated no Standard, because the purchases were genuine trades at market prices that benefited unitholders.
   - B. Reyes violated Standard II(B), because the orders were intended to raise the closing price and mislead market participants about Brisk's value.
   - C. Reyes violated only Standard VI(A), because he did not disclose that his bonus depends on month-end rankings.
3. The number of Whitcombe's personal trades that breach Tessaly's blackout policy is: (LOS: los-10-02-b)
   - A. 1.
   - B. 2.
   - C. 3.
4. Applying standardized selection criteria consistent with the Standards, the approximate net return of the candidate Tessaly should select is: (LOS: los-10-02-b)
   - A. 9.87%.
   - B. 10.67%.
   - C. 12.45%.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Only the shares held before the auction are revalued: $1{,}200{,}000 \times (4.60 - 4.10) = 600{,}000$.
> Per unit: $600{,}000 / 12{,}000{,}000 = 0.0500$, raising NAV per unit from 15.0000 to 15.0500.
> The 90,000 shares bought in the auction cost 4.60 and are valued at 4.60, so they add nothing to NAV.
> B is wrong because it revalues the post-trade holding: $1{,}290{,}000 \times 0.50 / 12{,}000{,}000 = 0.05375$.
> C is wrong because it measures from the prior day's close: $1{,}200{,}000 \times (4.60 - 3.98) / 12{,}000{,}000 = 0.0620$.
> The Emerging Leaders holding of 800,000 shares is a decoy; the question concerns Microcap.
> 2. **B.** Reyes's e-mail shows intent to lift the closing price for the ranking snapshot; the funds supplied 150,000 of the 170,000 auction shares (88.24%), more than twice average daily volume, and the price fell back to 4.08 at the next open.
> Trading to distort a price with intent to mislead is transaction-based manipulation under Standard II(B), and trading between funds he manages to raise the price violates even when fund participants appear to benefit.
> A is wrong because intent to mislead, not the genuineness of each order or the benefit to unitholders, decides II(B); the 0.05 per unit from Question 1 is exactly the distortion.
> C is wrong because the bonus is a conflict that should be disclosed, but nondisclosure does not describe the manipulation itself.
> 3. **C.** T1 (7 May) is 4 business days before the 13 May Kovac trade (12, 11, 8, and 7 May), inside the five-day window.
> T2 (18 May) is the third business day after 13 May (14, 15, and 18 May), inside the three-day window; the policy covers sales, whatever the reason.
> T3 (8 May) is 6 business days before the 18 May Lumen trade, outside the window.
> T4 (21 May) is the third business day after 18 May, inside the window.
> Breaches: T1, T2, and T4, so 3.
> A is wrong because it counts calendar days: T1 is 6 calendar days before and T2 is 5 calendar days after, so only T4 is caught.
> B is wrong because it excuses the tuition sale; Standard VI(B) permits a personal trade contrary to a recommendation when clients are not disadvantaged, but that does not override a blackout period, which is a recommended procedure in a small firm to prevent front-running.
> The weakness in Tessaly's procedure is that preclearance was granted without checking the client trade calendar.
> 4. **B.** Standard V(A) requires selecting external advisers with the same diligence as selecting securities: review of the adviser's code of ethics, compliance and internal controls, quality of published returns, and adherence to its stated strategy.
> Brightfold fails on strategy drift and unverified returns; Castine fails on its unremediated control finding; only Aldermoor passes.
> Aldermoor's net return: performance fee $= 0.10 \times (11.8\% - 8.0\%) = 0.38\%$, so net $= 11.8\% - 0.75\% - 0.38\% = 10.67\%$.
> A is wrong because it charges the performance fee on the whole return: $11.8\% - 0.75\% - 1.18\% = 9.87\%$.
> C is wrong because it selects Brightfold on net return alone ($13.4\% - 0.95\% = 12.45\%$), a selection shortcut the guidance rejects.
> Castine's net return of $10.9\% - 0.60\% = 10.30\%$ reflects the lowest-fee shortcut, which also fails.

## Distractor design

1. B: post-trade holding revalued, including shares bought at the auction price; C: price move measured from the prior day's close instead of the last pre-auction trade.
2. A: outcome and genuineness of orders substituted for the intent test; C: nearest-neighbour conflict-disclosure standard chosen instead of market manipulation.
3. A: calendar days counted instead of business days; B: VI(B) contrary-trade permission misapplied to override a firm blackout period.
4. A: performance fee applied to the whole return rather than the excess over the hurdle; C: selection on highest net return alone, ignoring strategy drift and unverified returns.
