# Castellan Securities: Chemicals Research Under Pressure

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Ines Moreau, CFA, is a specialty chemicals analyst at Castellan Securities, a broker-dealer with investment banking, sales and trading, and research divisions.
Castellan's research policy states that rating changes are approved by a research committee and then released to all clients simultaneously through the firm's research platform.

In September 2026, Moreau downgraded Pellam Resins from buy to sell.
Her file contains Pellam's public filings, a paid subscription to rail car-load data showing resin shipments down 14% over two quarters, and notes of conversations with two independent distributors who described orders as "noticeably softer" but who had no access to Pellam's internal figures.
Moreau archived all of this material with her valuation model.

The research committee approved the downgrade at 09:00 on 8 September, with release scheduled for 13:00.
At 09:20, the director of research, Julien Faure, CFA, e-mailed the approved report to the sales and trading desk "so the desk is ready for client calls."
Castellan has no written policy restricting what sales and trading staff may do with research before its release.
At 10:05, a sales trader, Nico Brandt, sold short 4,000 Pellam shares in his personal account.
Pellam fell 9% after the 13:00 release.

From 14 to 25 September, compliance brought Moreau over the wall to help Castellan's investment bankers model synergies for a client, Orvane Chemicals, which intends to make a tender offer for Lysett Coatings, a company Moreau covers.
The bid has not been announced.
On 28 September, compliance returned Moreau to her research role.
That afternoon, a hedge fund client that holds Lysett asked her whether it should trim its position.
Moreau replied, "I would not sell Lysett right now," without mentioning Orvane.

An investor relations firm acting for Quarry Polymers, a small company with no analyst coverage, offered Castellan's research division USD 25,000 to initiate coverage of Quarry, plus a bonus of USD 10,000 if the initiation carried a buy rating.
Faure asked Moreau how the engagement could be accepted.

Moreau is also drafting an initiation report on Ardent Chemical.
Castellan makes a market in Ardent shares and was a co-manager of Ardent's 2025 bond offering; Moreau personally owns no Ardent securities.
The draft includes three sentences.

Sentence 1: "Ardent's operating margin will expand to 18% in 2027 when the Tavira plant upgrade is completed."

Sentence 2: "Our target price is derived from a two-stage dividend discount model; the model's inputs, and its sensitivity to the terminal growth rate, are shown in the appendix."

Sentence 3: "The author owns no Ardent securities; accordingly, there are no conflicts of interest to disclose."

Faure reviews the draft and asks Moreau to finish it before the end of the month, noting that Castellan's banking division hopes to lead Ardent's next bond offering.

## Questions

1. Moreau's Pellam downgrade is most likely: (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. a violation of Standard II(A), because the distributor conversations were nonpublic and the resulting conclusion moved the price.
   - B. consistent with the Standards, because the conclusion was assembled from public information and nonmaterial nonpublic information.
   - C. a violation of Standard V(A), because conversations with two distributors are not a reasonable basis for a sell rating.
2. Regarding the events of 8 September, which statement is most accurate? (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. Only Brandt violated the Standards, by trading ahead of the release.
   - B. Brandt violated the Standard on priority of transactions, and Faure violated the Standard on responsibilities of supervisors.
   - C. Only Faure violated the Standards, by circulating the report internally before its release.
3. Which procedure would most likely have prevented Brandt's trade? (LOS: los-10-02-b)
   - A. Requiring employees to report their personal holdings to compliance on hiring and at least annually.
   - B. Limiting knowledge of an approved rating change to those who need it before release and prohibiting anyone who knows of it from trading until clients have had an opportunity to act.
   - C. Disclosing Castellan's personal trading policies to clients in plain language.
4. Moreau's reply to the hedge fund client on 28 September most likely: (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. violates Standard II(A), because she caused a client to act on material nonpublic information.
   - B. violates Standard III(B) only, because she gave one client guidance that other clients did not receive.
   - C. does not violate the Standards, because she did not disclose the Orvane bid.
5. Castellan can accept the Quarry engagement consistently with the Standards only if: (LOS: los-10-02-b)
   - A. the USD 10,000 bonus is disclosed prominently in the initiation report.
   - B. the fee is a flat amount agreed in advance, not contingent on the rating or conclusions, and the compensation is disclosed in the report.
   - C. the initiation is assigned to an analyst who is not told about the bonus.
6. Which sentence in the Ardent draft is least likely to violate the Standards? (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. Sentence 1.
   - B. Sentence 2.
   - C. Sentence 3.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Public filings and purchased car-load data are public or legitimately obtained, and the distributors' opinions are nonpublic but nonmaterial because they reflect no inside knowledge of Pellam; a conclusion assembled this way is permitted under the mosaic theory even though it moved the price.
> Moreau's archive of the research trail is exactly the documentation Standard V(C) recommends for a mosaic conclusion.
> A is wrong because it tests the materiality of the conclusion rather than of the individual nonpublic inputs.
> C is wrong because the channel checks are one part of a basis that also includes filings and quantitative shipment data.
> 2. **B.** Brandt traded his own account before clients had an opportunity to act on the change, violating Standard VI(B).
> Faure circulated an approved rating change before release when Castellan had no procedures restricting trading by those with prior knowledge, which is a failure of reasonable supervisory effort under Standard IV(C).
> A is wrong because it clears the supervisor who sent the report without any trading restrictions in place.
> C is wrong because internal circulation is not itself tipping of issuer inside information; the violation lies in the absent supervisory procedures, and Brandt's own trade is not excused.
> 3. **B.** The recommended procedures are to limit the number of people who know a recommendation is coming and to restrict trading by anyone with prior knowledge until wide distribution (fair dealing and priority-of-transactions procedures); this device operates before the conduct.
> A is wrong because periodic holdings reports detect a trade after the fact and would not have stopped a same-morning short sale.
> C is wrong because disclosing the policy to clients is recommended but prevents nothing on its own.
> 4. **A.** Moreau learned of the unannounced Orvane bid while over the wall, and that information is material and nonpublic; advising a Lysett holder not to sell causes the client to act on it, which violates Standard II(A) even without naming the bidder.
> The recommended procedure is that an analyst brought over the wall stays there until the information is public, and that Lysett be placed on a restricted list.
> B is wrong because the problem is the tainted basis of the advice, not its selective timing.
> C is wrong because causing others to act on the information is prohibited whether or not the information itself is revealed.
> 5. **B.** Issuer-paid research is permitted only when the analysis is independent and thorough, the compensation is a flat fee negotiated in advance and not contingent on the conclusions, and the compensation arrangement is disclosed (Standard I(B)).
> A is wrong because disclosure does not cure compensation tied to a buy rating; the contingent bonus must be refused.
> C is wrong because shielding the analyst from knowing the terms leaves the conflict in the firm's pay structure.
> 6. **B.** Sentence 2 identifies the process and points readers to the model's inputs and sensitivity, which is what Standard V(B) requires for quantitative work.
> Sentence 1 states a forecast margin as fact, failing V(B)'s duty to distinguish fact from opinion.
> Sentence 3 is false and incomplete: Castellan's market making and its role in Ardent's bond offering are firm-level conflicts that Standard VI(A) requires to be disclosed even when the analyst owns nothing.
> A and C are therefore the sentences that do violate, which is the trap in a "least likely" stem.

## Distractor design

1. A: conclusion materiality confused with input materiality (mosaic theory misapplied); C: sound multi-source basis mislabelled as inadequate.
2. A: supervisor cleared because he did not trade; C: internal circulation mislabelled as tipping, and the trader cleared.
3. A: after-the-fact reporting chosen over a preventive barrier; C: disclosure to clients treated as prevention.
4. B: fair dealing substituted for the material nonpublic information standard; C: "did not reveal the information" treated as a defence against causing others to act.
5. A: disclosure treated as a cure for contingent compensation; C: concealment within the firm treated as independence.
6. A: opinion stated as fact picked as compliant; C: firm-level conflicts ignored because the analyst owns nothing.
