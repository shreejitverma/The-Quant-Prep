# Corrigan & Pell Wealth Partners: Accountants, a Consultant, and a New Hire

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Corrigan & Pell Wealth Partners is a registered investment adviser in Dublin with 310 private clients.
Its managing partner is Declan Corrigan, CFA, and its planning partner is Harriet Pell, CFA.
The firm's brochure, given to every prospect, states the annual advisory fee in Exhibit 1.

Exhibit 1: Corrigan & Pell fee schedule and referred-client terms

| Item | Terms |
|---|---|
| Fee on the first EUR 2 million of assets | 1.00% per year |
| Fee on assets above EUR 2 million | 0.75% per year |
| Clients paying the full brochure schedule | 218 of 310 |
| Discount for clients referred by Beaumont Tax Advisors | 25% off the brochure fee |
| Payment by Corrigan & Pell to Beaumont for referrals | None |

Beaumont Tax Advisors is an accounting firm whose partners refer clients to Corrigan & Pell.
Beaumont receives no payment or benefit from Corrigan & Pell, and the discount is explained to each referred prospect before signing.
In March, Beaumont refers Aoife Brennan, who invests EUR 3.2 million with Corrigan & Pell.

Pell coordinates tax planning with Beaumont for 40 clients who use both firms.
She emails each client's quarterly account statements to Beaumont, and Beaumont emails back the client's tax returns.
Every client has signed a separate engagement letter with each firm, and each letter contains a confidentiality clause.
Pell says: "These clients see us and Beaumont as one team, and both firms are bound to confidentiality, so sharing is part of the service."

Exhibit 2: Consent status of the 40 shared clients

| Status | Clients |
|---|---|
| Signed Corrigan & Pell's information-sharing consent before any file was sent | 14 |
| Gave consent by phone after their files had already been exchanged | 8 |
| No consent beyond the two engagement letters | 18 |

Gerald Whitcombe is an independent pension consultant who recommends investment advisers to the trustees of small company pension plans.
He is not a Corrigan & Pell client.
Whitcombe chairs the Whitcombe Children's Foundation.
Corrigan tells him that, for each pension plan that hires Corrigan & Pell on his recommendation, the firm will donate 5% of the plan's first-year fees to the foundation.
Three plans hire the firm during the year, and the donations total EUR 11,400.

In September, Corrigan & Pell hires Yara Okonjo as an associate.
Okonjo learned in August that she passed the Level III exam; she has not yet completed the work experience required for the charter.
Corrigan's client newsletter introduces her as "our newest CFA charterholder."
Okonjo reads the newsletter and says nothing.
Earlier, while waiting for her result, Okonjo posted three comments in an online candidate forum.

Exhibit 3: Okonjo's forum comments

| Comment | Text |
|---|---|
| 1 | "The Level III morning session ran long for me; I barely finished." |
| 2 | "Expect heavy weighting on individual tax drag. I had three questions on after-tax accumulation, one on a trust." |
| 3 | "I think the pass rate this year will be low." |

## Questions

1. The annual fee Brennan will pay on EUR 3.2 million, and the most accurate evaluation of the referred-client discount, are: (LOS: los-10-03-a)
   - A. EUR 21,750; the discount does not violate the Code and Standards.
   - B. EUR 21,750; the discount violates Standard I(C) Misrepresentation, because the brochure states a fee that referred clients do not pay.
   - C. EUR 18,000; the discount does not violate the Code and Standards.
2. For how many of the 40 shared clients does Pell's exchange of information with Beaumont most likely violate Standard III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality? (LOS: los-10-03-a)
   - A. 18
   - B. 26
   - C. 0
3. Corrigan's donations to the Whitcombe Children's Foundation most likely: (LOS: los-10-03-b)
   - A. violate Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity, because they are a benefit tied to Whitcombe's recommendations that could reasonably be expected to compromise his objectivity.
   - B. violate Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements, because they are compensation paid to Whitcombe that Corrigan & Pell must consent to in writing.
   - C. do not violate the Code and Standards, because the money goes to a charity rather than to Whitcombe.
4. Which conduct most likely violates Standard VII? (LOS: los-10-03-b)
   - A. Comment 1 and Comment 3 by Okonjo; the newsletter description is acceptable because Okonjo has passed all three levels.
   - B. Comment 2 by Okonjo and the newsletter description, which Okonjo should correct.
   - C. Comment 2 by Okonjo only, because Corrigan, not Okonjo, wrote the newsletter.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Brochure fee $= 2{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0100 + 1{,}200{,}000 \times 0.0075 = 20{,}000 + 9{,}000 = 29{,}000$.
> Referred-client fee $= 29{,}000 \times (1 - 0.25) = 21{,}750$, an effective rate of $21{,}750 / 3{,}200{,}000 = 0.6797\%$.
> Charging referred clients less than the brochure fee is not unfair dealing, because Standard III(B) concerns investment recommendations and investment action, not fee levels, and it is not a misrepresentation, because 218 clients pay the brochure schedule, which is the maximum any client pays (V10 p.250, p.251).
> Beaumont receives nothing, so no referral fee arises, and the discount is explained to prospects before signing.
> B is wrong because a stated fee that is genuinely charged and never exceeded is not misleading.
> C is wrong because it applies 0.75% to all EUR 3.2 million before the discount, $3{,}200{,}000 \times 0.0075 \times 0.75 = 18{,}000$, ignoring the 1.00% tier on the first EUR 2 million.
> 2. **B.** Standard III(E) requires advance client approval, ideally written, before client information is shared with another firm (V10 p.251, p.252).
> The 14 clients who signed consent before any file was sent are covered.
> The 8 clients who consented only after their files had been exchanged were not asked in advance, and the 18 with only engagement letters never consented to sharing between firms.
> Violations $= 8 + 18 = 26$.
> A is wrong because it treats consent given after the files were sent as a cure; approval must come before the disclosure.
> C is wrong because separate confidentiality clauses with each firm are not consent to share between the firms, and a client's view of the firms as a team is not consent either.
> 3. **A.** Donations promised in proportion to the fees of plans that Whitcombe steers to the firm give the gatekeeper an incentive to recommend Corrigan & Pell and at least create the appearance that his independence and objectivity are compromised, violating Standard I(B) (V10 p.253).
> The EUR 11,400 total is not what matters; the arrangement is an inducement tied to each recommendation.
> B is wrong because IV(B) covers benefits a member receives from outside parties that may conflict with the employer's interest; here the firm is the payer and Whitcombe is not its employee.
> C is wrong because routing the benefit through a charity the consultant chairs does not remove the influence on him.
> 4. **B.** Standard VII(A) prohibits disclosing confidential exam content, including the broad topic areas tested and specific question detail; Comment 2 reveals the topic weighting and the number and type of questions (V10 p.254, p.255).
> Under Standard VII(B), a candidate who has passed Level III but has not completed the work experience requirement is not a charterholder, so "our newest CFA charterholder" misrepresents her status; Corrigan violates VII(B) by writing it, and Okonjo should correct the record rather than stay silent (V10 p.255).
> A is wrong because impressions of time pressure and predictions about the pass rate disclose no confidential exam content, while passing all three levels does not confer the charter.
> C is wrong because it misses the newsletter violation; Corrigan is bound by VII(B), and Okonjo has a duty to correct a misstatement of her own designation.

## Distractor design

1. B: a genuinely charged maximum fee mistaken for a misrepresentation; C: flat 0.75% applied to all assets, ignoring the 1.00% first tier (misread fee schedule).
2. A: after-the-fact consent treated as advance approval; C: bilateral confidentiality clauses treated as consent to share between firms (the character's rationalization).
3. B: I(B) inducement relabeled as IV(B) additional compensation; C: charity routing accepted as removing the influence.
4. A: harmless comments flagged and the charter confused with passing Level III; C: VII(B) violation missed because a colleague wrote the statement.
