# Nordlys Public Pension: A Candidate Before the Professional Conduct Program

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Henrik Aas is an investment analyst in the Nordic equity team of Nordlys Public Pension, an Oslo public pension fund with NOK 410 billion in assets.
Aas sat the Level II exam in May and is a CFA candidate.
His supervisor, Solveig Brekke, CFA, heads the equity team of 14 analysts.
In November, Brekke receives a letter from CFA Institute's Professional Conduct staff stating that an inquiry into Aas has been opened.
She reconstructs the year's events from Aas's file.

Exhibit 1: Events involving Aas

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 10 January | A former employer, a Bergen stockbroker, names Aas as a defendant in a civil lawsuit alleging he took client lists when he resigned |
| 22 January | A national financial newspaper publishes an article about the lawsuit that names Aas |
| 15 March | Aas completes his annual Professional Conduct Statement and answers "no" to the question on civil litigation |
| 18 May | At the exam centre, a proctor files a report that Aas continued writing after time was called |
| 3 June | CFA Institute's monitoring of social media identifies a post by Aas describing the topics and one vignette from his exam |
| 9 September | A former colleague at the Bergen stockbroker mails a written complaint about Aas to CFA Institute |
| 30 September | The Norwegian regulator publishes a routine notice renewing Nordlys's licence; the notice does not mention Aas |

Brekke and Aas discuss the letter on 12 November.

Brekke: "The lawsuit is about a former employer, not a client, so you had no duty to mention it on your Professional Conduct Statement."

Aas: "I agree I had no duty, but for a different reason: CFA Institute can only open an inquiry when someone files a written complaint, so there was nothing to disclose until September."

Brekke then says: "Once staff have investigated, they have two choices: impose a sanction or refer you straight to the Disciplinary Review Committee."

Aas asks Brekke what the investigation will involve.
Brekke says she expects staff to contact her.
She lists four actions she believes staff may take before deciding how to proceed:

- Action 1: ask Aas for a written explanation of each event;
- Action 2: interview Brekke, the former colleague who complained, and the exam-centre proctor;
- Action 3: collect Aas's exam-day records and a copy of the social media post;
- Action 4: convene a panel of Disciplinary Review Committee members to hear Aas's side before any charges are proposed.

Aas tells Brekke that if staff propose a sanction he will reject it.
He says that the worst outcome for a candidate is a public censure, because charter revocation applies only to charterholders.

Brekke reminds Aas that Nordlys's own staff code does not mention exam conduct.
She tells him that the Code and Standards nevertheless apply to him as a candidate regardless of his job function or local law, and that each member and candidate carries a personal responsibility to uphold them.
Brekke adds that she herself has a responsibility under the Standards because Aas works under her supervision.

## Questions

1. Based on Exhibit 1, the number of distinct sources of Professional Conduct inquiry named in the curriculum that are present in Aas's case is: (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. 3.
   - B. 4.
   - C. 5.
2. Regarding the lawsuit and the Professional Conduct Statement, which 12 November statement is most likely correct? (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. Brekke's statement only.
   - B. Aas's statement only.
   - C. Neither statement.
3. Of the four actions Brekke lists, the one least consistent with the Professional Conduct investigation process is: (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. Action 1.
   - B. Action 2.
   - C. Action 4.
4. Brekke's description of the choices available to staff after the investigation is: (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. correct.
   - B. incorrect, because staff may also conclude the inquiry with no disciplinary sanction or issue a cautionary letter.
   - C. incorrect, because staff may only issue a cautionary letter and all sanctions are imposed by the Board of Governors.
5. If a DRC panel finds that Aas violated the Code and Standards or testing policies, the sanction most appropriate to his status is: (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. public censure, which is the most severe sanction available for a candidate.
   - B. suspension or prohibition from further participation in the CFA Program.
   - C. revocation of the CFA charter, which applies once he completes the program.
6. Aas's 3 June social media post most directly violates: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. Standard III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality.
   - B. Standard VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs.
   - C. Standard VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** The curriculum names five inquiry sources: self-disclosure on the annual Professional Conduct Statement, written complaints, the media or regulatory notices or other public sources, proctor reports, and post-exam analyses and social media monitoring (V10 p.7).
> Mapping Exhibit 1: the 22 January article is the media source, the 18 May report is the proctor source, the 3 June finding is post-exam social media monitoring, and the 9 September letter is a written complaint.
> Self-disclosure is absent because Aas answered "no" on 15 March, and the 30 September regulatory notice does not concern Aas.
> Count $= 4$.
> A is wrong because it drops the newspaper article on the belief that media cannot trigger an inquiry, giving $4 - 1 = 3$.
> C is wrong because it counts self-disclosure, which requires Aas actually to disclose the matter rather than answer "no", giving $4 + 1 = 5$; the 30 September notice cannot add a fifth source, because regulatory notices belong to the same public-source category as the newspaper article and the notice does not concern Aas.
> 2. **C.** Members and candidates must self-disclose on the annual Professional Conduct Statement all matters that question their professional conduct, and the curriculum names involvement in civil litigation explicitly; the duty is not limited to client disputes (V10 p.7).
> Brekke is wrong because the lawsuit alleging misuse of client lists plainly questions professional conduct, whoever the plaintiff is.
> Aas is wrong because written complaints are only one of five sources, and the self-disclosure duty exists independently of whether anyone complains.
> A and B each accept one of the two wrong rationales.
> 3. **C.** Investigations may include requesting a written explanation from the member or candidate, interviewing the member or candidate, complaining parties, and third parties, and collecting relevant documents and records, so Actions 1, 2, and 3 are all within the toolkit (V10 p.7).
> A DRC panel is convened only if the candidate rejects charges and a proposed sanction, which has not yet happened, so Action 4 is inconsistent with the process (V10 p.8).
> A is wrong because a written explanation is the first item in the investigation toolkit.
> B is wrong because interviews of the complainant and third parties such as a supervisor or proctor are expressly permitted.
> 4. **B.** After reviewing the material, staff may conclude the inquiry with no disciplinary sanction, issue a cautionary letter, or continue proceedings to discipline the member or candidate (V10 p.7).
> Brekke's version omits two of the three dispositions and wrongly implies automatic referral to the DRC.
> A is wrong for that reason.
> C is wrong because staff can continue proceedings and propose sanctions, contested cases go to a DRC panel, and the Board of Governors has oversight of the Professional Conduct Program rather than imposing individual sanctions (V10 p.7, p.8).
> 5. **B.** Candidates who violate the Code and Standards or testing policies are subject to suspension or prohibition from further participation in the CFA Program (V10 p.8).
> A is wrong because public censure is listed among member sanctions, and it is not the ceiling for a candidate, since prohibition from the program is more severe; this is the error in Aas's own claim.
> C is wrong because charter revocation applies to members who hold the charter, and Aas holds none.
> 6. **B.** VII(A) prohibits conduct that compromises the integrity, validity, or security of CFA Institute programs, and disclosing exam content is the curriculum's example of that conduct (V10 p.17).
> A is wrong because III(E) protects information about current, former, and prospective clients, not exam content (V10 p.15).
> C is wrong because VII(B) concerns misrepresenting or exaggerating what membership, the designation, or candidacy implies; it is tempting because it also names the CFA Program.

## Distractor design

1. A: media wrongly excluded as an inquiry source (count 3); C: the undisclosed civil lawsuit counted as a self-disclosure source (count 5).
2. A: self-disclosure duty wrongly limited to client matters; B: written complaint treated as the only inquiry source.
3. A and B: legitimate investigation tools misidentified as improper; the correct answer is the panel convened before any charge (stage sequence error).
4. A: two of three staff dispositions omitted and DRC referral made automatic; C: wrong actor (Board of Governors) given the sanctioning role.
5. A: member sanction applied to a candidate and ranked as the maximum; C: sanction for charterholders applied to a candidate.
6. A: client confidentiality confused with exam confidentiality; C: VII(A) conduct confused with VII(B) references because both name the CFA Program.
