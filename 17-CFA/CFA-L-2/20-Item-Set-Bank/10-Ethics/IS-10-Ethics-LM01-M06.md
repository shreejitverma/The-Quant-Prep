# Qasr Capital: An Oversubscribed IPO Allocation

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Faisal Rahman, CFA, is a portfolio manager at Qasr Capital, a Dubai asset manager with AED 8.4 billion of discretionary equity mandates for institutional clients.
Rahman reports to Mariam Al Suwaidi, CFA, head of equities, who signs off every new-issue allocation before settlement.
Qasr's written allocation policy states that when a new issue is oversubscribed, shares are allocated to client accounts pro rata to order size, and that accounts in which an employee is a beneficial owner are filled only after every client order has been filled in full.

In March, Qasr placed orders for the initial public offering of Zahra Logistics, which was heavily oversubscribed.
The mandates of all three client accounts in Exhibit 1 permit investment in new issues.

**Exhibit 1: Zahra Logistics IPO orders and Rahman's allocation**

| Account | Description | Shares ordered | Shares allocated by Rahman |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Public-sector pension fund, discretionary; Qasr's largest fee payer | 120,000 | 75,000 |
| 2 | Family office, discretionary | 60,000 | 0 |
| 3 | Corporate treasury, discretionary | 45,000 | 0 |
| 4 | Rahman's personal account | 15,000 | 15,000 |
| Total | | 240,000 | 90,000 |

**Exhibit 2: Selected data**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| IPO price | AED 12.00 |
| Zahra closing price, first trading day | AED 15.60 |
| Qasr management fee on Account 3 | 0.50% per year |
| Shares received by Qasr in the offering | 90,000 |

Al Suwaidi signed off Rahman's allocation two minutes after receiving it.
For the past two years she has signed off every allocation without comparing it with the policy, and Qasr has no procedure for doing so.

When Account 3's treasurer learns of the first-day rise, she complains to Qasr.
Rahman tells Al Suwaidi: "The treasurer never asked for this issue by name, so nobody was harmed, and my personal account paid exactly the same price as the clients."

Separately, Nadia Karam, an analyst on Rahman's team and a CFA Level II candidate, resigns in April to launch her own research firm.
In her final month she uses Qasr's proprietary sector models during office hours to prepare a presentation for her new firm's investors, and on her last day she copies the models to a personal drive.

In June, the Dubai regulator publishes an enforcement notice that fines Qasr for the allocation and names Rahman.
After reading the notice, CFA Institute Professional Conduct staff open an inquiry into Rahman.
No client has complained to CFA Institute.
Rahman writes to staff: "The regulator has already fined my firm and no client has complained to you, so CFA Institute has no basis to pursue me."
After their investigation, staff charge Rahman with violating the Code and Standards and propose a public censure, and Rahman must decide whether to accept.

## Questions

1. Had Rahman followed Qasr's written allocation policy, the first-day gain on the Zahra shares that Account 3 would have received is closest to: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. AED 60,750.
   - B. AED 64,800.
   - C. AED 280,800.
2. Rahman's allocation most directly violates: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. Standard III(B) Fair Dealing only, because Rahman's personal account paid the same price as the clients.
   - B. Standard III(B) Fair Dealing and Standard VI(B) Priority of Transactions.
   - C. Standard VI(B) Priority of Transactions and Standard III(C) Suitability, because Accounts 2 and 3 were denied an investment their mandates permit.
3. Al Suwaidi's approval practice and Karam's conduct before her departure most directly engage, respectively: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. Standard IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors, and Standard IV(A) Loyalty.
   - B. Standard VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs, and Standard IV(A) Loyalty.
   - C. Standard IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors, and Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care.
4. Regarding the Professional Conduct proceedings against Rahman, which statement is most accurate? (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. The regulator's notice is a valid source of an inquiry, and if Rahman accepts the charges and the proposed public censure, the matter is not referred to a Disciplinary Review Committee panel.
   - B. Without a written complaint the inquiry is valid only if Rahman also self-discloses the matter, and any sanction must be confirmed by a Disciplinary Review Committee panel even if Rahman accepts it.
   - C. Because the regulator has already fined Qasr, the Code and Standards defer to local enforcement, and any further sanction would be decided by the Board of Governors.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Under the policy, Rahman's personal account is excluded until every client order is filled, so the 90,000 shares are prorated over client orders only.
> Client orders $= 120{,}000 + 60{,}000 + 45{,}000 = 225{,}000$, and the fill rate is $90{,}000/225{,}000 = 0.4000$.
> Account 3's allocation $= 45{,}000 \times 0.4000 = 18{,}000$ shares (Accounts 1 and 2 receive $48{,}000$ and $24{,}000$, and Account 4 receives none because client orders are not filled in full).
> First-day gain per share $= 15.60 - 12.00 = 3.60$, so the forgone gain $= 18{,}000 \times 3.60 = 64{,}800$ AED.
> A is wrong because it includes Rahman's 15,000-share order in the proration: $90{,}000/240{,}000 = 0.3750$, $45{,}000 \times 0.3750 = 16{,}875$ shares, and $16{,}875 \times 3.60 = 60{,}750$.
> C is wrong because it values the 18,000 shares at the closing price instead of measuring the gain: $18{,}000 \times 15.60 = 280{,}800$.
> The 0.50% management fee and Qasr's AED 8.4 billion of assets are decoys.
> 2. **B.** III(B) requires dealing fairly and objectively with all clients when taking investment action (V10 p.15); giving the largest fee payer 75,000 shares, well above its 48,000 pro rata share, while Accounts 2 and 3 received nothing, favours one client over others.
> VI(B) requires client and employer transactions to have priority over transactions in which the member is the beneficial owner (V10 p.17); Rahman filled his own 15,000-share order in full while client orders were unfilled.
> A is wrong because paying the same price does not cure the priority breach; the harm is that Rahman took scarce shares ahead of clients.
> C is wrong because III(C) concerns whether recommendations and actions are consistent with a client's objectives and constraints (V10 p.15); being denied a permitted investment is an allocation-fairness issue under III(B), not a suitability failure.
> 3. **A.** IV(C) requires members to make reasonable efforts to ensure that anyone subject to their supervision or authority complies with applicable laws, rules, regulations, and the Code and Standards (V10 p.16); signing off allocations for two years without comparing them with the policy, and with no checking procedure, is not a reasonable effort.
> IV(A) requires members and candidates, in matters related to their employment, to act for the benefit of the employer and not deprive it of their skills and abilities, divulge confidential information, or otherwise cause it harm (V10 p.15, p.16); Karam used Qasr's proprietary models on Qasr's time for a competing venture and took them when she left.
> B is wrong because Standard VII contains no supervisory requirement; VII(A) concerns conduct that compromises the integrity, validity, or security of CFA Institute programs (V10 p.17).
> C is wrong because the loyalty Karam owed in this matter runs to her employer under IV(A), not to clients under III(A); both sub-standards contain the word "loyalty", which is the trap.
> 4. **A.** Professional Conduct staff may open an inquiry when they become aware of questionable conduct through the media, regulatory notices, or another public source, so no written complaint is needed (V10 p.7).
> Once staff charge a violation, the member may accept or reject the charges and proposed sanction, and a panel of DRC members is convened only if the member does not accept (V10 p.7, p.8).
> The Code and Standards apply to members regardless of local laws and regulations (V10 p.13), so a regulatory fine on Qasr does not displace them.
> B is wrong because self-disclosure is only one of five inquiry sources and is not a precondition, and DRC review is triggered by rejection, not by acceptance.
> C is wrong because the Code and Standards do not defer to local enforcement, and the Board of Governors has oversight of the Professional Conduct Program rather than deciding individual sanctions; contested sanctions are decided by DRC panels (V10 p.7, p.8).

## Distractor design

1. A: beneficial-owner order included in the pro rata denominator (VI(B) policy ignored); C: position value at the close used instead of the gain over the offer price.
2. A: equal price treated as curing the priority breach (VI(B) missed); C: allocation fairness mislabelled as suitability (III(B) versus III(C)).
3. B: supervision placed in Standard VII (wrong roman numeral); C: loyalty to employer confused with loyalty to clients (IV(A) versus III(A)).
4. B: self-disclosure treated as a required trigger and DRC review made automatic (stage sequence error); C: Code scope wrongly subordinated to local enforcement and the oversight body given the panel's job.
