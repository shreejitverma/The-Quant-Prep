# Kestrel Equity Research: Platinum Tier and a Mine Visit

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Leona Marsh, CFA, is managing director of Kestrel Equity Research, an independent firm in Johannesburg that sells subscription research to 140 asset managers and pension funds.
In January, Marsh launches a Platinum tier that adds a monthly call with the lead analyst for an annual fee of ZAR 180,000, compared with ZAR 60,000 for the standard subscription.
She emails the Platinum offer to the 25 subscribers she believes have budgets large enough to pay.
The tier is not mentioned on Kestrel's website or in its standard client materials.

Tomas Brandt, CFA, Kestrel's head of mining research, is preparing an initiation report on Karoo Platinum Mines.
Last year Brandt's wife inherited a 9% stake in Karoo, worth about ZAR 42 million.
Brandt has told Marsh about the stake but no one else.
Marsh tells him: "Your valuation work is independent of your family's holdings, so there is nothing to disclose."

Marsh also issues three procedures for reports that use third-party material.

- Procedure 1: When a report quotes an outside economist, the report names the economist and the publication.
- Procedure 2: When a report uses a central bank inflation forecast, analysts cite the central bank, show the point forecast, and omit its published confidence bands and conditioning assumptions "so that our charts stay clean."
- Procedure 3: Charts reproduced from industry associations carry a source line beneath each chart.

Karoo's investor relations team invites Brandt to a three-day site visit and investor forum at its Limpopo mine.
Scheduled commercial flights and hotels near the mine are readily available.
Karoo offers Kestrel three arrangements.

Exhibit 1: Karoo site visit arrangements

| Arrangement | Paid by Karoo | Paid by Kestrel |
|---|---|---|
| Option 1 | Return airfare (ZAR 9,800), three hotel nights (ZAR 7,200), meals, forum dinner | Nothing |
| Option 2 | Return airfare and three hotel nights | Meals and forum dinner |
| Option 3 | Meals and forum dinner at the mine | Return airfare and three hotel nights |

Brandt brings Nadia Okafor, a junior analyst registered for the Level II CFA exam.
At the forum's registration desk, Okafor sees a printout that Karoo's staff left behind listing every registered investor attendee with private mobile numbers and assets under management.
She photographs the list and gives it to Kestrel's sales team, which starts calling the attendees to sell Kestrel subscriptions.

On 3 March at 08:15, Brandt completes the Karoo initiation with a Buy rating.
Kestrel's written policy is to release all new ratings to every subscriber through its research portal at 11:00.
At 08:40, Marsh phones Pieter Vos, portfolio manager at Vos Capital, Kestrel's largest subscriber and one of the 25 Platinum clients, and tells him the rating.
Vos Capital buys Karoo shares at 09:30.
When Kestrel's compliance officer questions the call, Marsh replies: "Vos pays for Platinum, and Platinum clients pay for early access to our analysts."

## Questions

1. Does Marsh most likely violate the Code and Standards with respect to the Platinum tier? (LOS: los-10-03-a)
   - A. No, because clients who choose Platinum pay a higher fee for the added service.
   - B. Yes, because the tier is offered only to clients Marsh selects and is not disclosed to all subscribers.
   - C. Yes, because Fair Dealing requires every subscriber to receive the same level of service.
2. Marsh's statement to Brandt about the Karoo report is most likely: (LOS: los-10-03-b)
   - A. correct, because Brandt's valuation work is independent of his wife's holding.
   - B. incorrect, because Standard VI(A) prohibits an analyst from covering a company in which a family member holds shares.
   - C. incorrect, because the stake is a conflict that must be disclosed prominently and in plain language in the report, or coverage avoided, whatever the objectivity of the analysis.
3. With respect to the Code and Standards, Marsh's three research procedures most likely: (LOS: los-10-03-b)
   - A. violate Standard I(C), because Procedure 2 strips the forecaster's qualifying information.
   - B. comply, because each third-party source is cited.
   - C. violate Standard I(C), because Procedure 1 names outside economists without their written permission.
4. To preserve his independence and objectivity, Brandt should most likely: (LOS: los-10-03-a)
   - A. accept Option 2, because Kestrel paying for meals shows the visit is not a gift.
   - B. accept Option 3.
   - C. decline the visit entirely, because any benefit provided by an issuer compromises objectivity.
5. By photographing the attendee list and giving it to Kestrel's sales team, Okafor most likely: (LOS: los-10-03-a)
   - A. did not violate the Code and Standards, because as a Level II candidate she is not yet a member.
   - B. violated Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information.
   - C. violated Standard I(D) Misconduct.
6. Marsh's justification for her call to Vos is most likely: (LOS: los-10-03-b)
   - A. invalid, because giving one client a new rating before it is disseminated to all subscribers violates Standard III(B), whatever tier that client pays for.
   - B. valid, because the Platinum tier entitles its clients to enhanced analyst access.
   - C. invalid, because the rating is material nonpublic information about Karoo, so Vos Capital's purchase violates Standard II(A).

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Standard III(B) Fair Dealing permits premium service levels only if they are disclosed and made available to every client willing to pay, and do not disadvantage other clients (V10 p.227).
> Marsh offers Platinum only to the 25 clients she privately judges able to afford it and keeps it off the website and standard materials, so the tier is selective and undisclosed.
> A is wrong because the higher fee is the permitted part; the violation is the selective, undisclosed offer.
> C is wrong because III(B) does not require identical service; differentiated service is allowed when disclosed and available to all.
> 2. **C.** A 9% family stake worth ZAR 42 million is a conflict that could reasonably impair Brandt's objectivity, so Standard VI(A) requires full, prominent, plain-language disclosure in the report, or avoidance by dropping coverage (V10 p.227, p.228).
> Telling Marsh alone does not reach the readers who rely on the report.
> A is wrong because VI(A) is triggered by the conflict itself; the analyst's belief that he is independent does not remove it.
> B is wrong because VI(A) is an avoid-or-disclose standard, not an outright prohibition on coverage.
> 3. **A.** Standard I(C) Misrepresentation requires that others' forecasts be presented with their qualifying statements; cutting the confidence bands and conditioning assumptions presents the forecast as more certain than its author stated (V10 p.228).
> Procedures 1 and 3 are the correct anti-plagiarism practice.
> B is wrong because citing the source does not cure removing the source's caveats.
> C is wrong because naming a quoted economist is what I(C) requires; no written permission rule applies to properly attributed quotations.
> 4. **B.** Under Standard I(B), best practice when evaluating an issuer is to pay for one's own commercial travel and lodging, especially when commercial options are readily available, as they are here (V10 p.230, p.231).
> Accepting modest meals and a forum dinner provided at the event would not reasonably be expected to compromise objectivity.
> A is wrong because it pays the small costs while accepting the two largest benefits, airfare and hotel, which are the ones that threaten independence.
> C is wrong because the Standard does not bar all issuer hospitality; modest, customary event meals are acceptable.
> 5. **C.** Photographing and exploiting a confidential list that Okafor knew was not hers is dishonest conduct under Standard I(D) Misconduct (V10 p.231, p.232).
> The Code and Standards bind candidates in the CFA Program as well as members.
> A is wrong because candidates are subject to the Code and Standards from the time they register.
> B is wrong because a list of attendees' contact numbers and assets under management would not affect the price of any security, so it is confidential but not material nonpublic information.
> 6. **A.** Standard III(B) requires that a new rating be disseminated so that all clients have a fair opportunity to act; Marsh gave Vos the Buy at 08:40, 2 hours and 20 minutes before the 11:00 release, and Vos Capital traded at 09:30, ahead of every other subscriber (V10 p.233).
> A premium tier may add service, but it may not disadvantage other clients by giving some of them investment recommendations first.
> B is wrong because early receipt of a rating is not a permissible premium service; Platinum's advertised benefit is a monthly analyst call, and even a disclosed tier could not include pre-release ratings.
> C is wrong because it names the wrong sub-standard; the duty engaged by a firm's own pre-release recommendation is fair dissemination under III(B).

## Distractor design

1. A: the permitted feature (a higher fee) mistaken for the whole analysis; C: overstatement that fair dealing means equal service.
2. A: the character's own rationalization (independence claimed as a cure); B: disclosable conflict treated as a prohibition.
3. B: attribution confused with preserving qualifications; C: invented permission requirement applied to a compliant procedure.
4. A: paying the minor cost while accepting the major benefits; C: over-restrictive reading that bans all issuer hospitality.
5. A: scope error, candidates excluded from the Standards; B: wrong sub-standard sharing the keyword "nonpublic" with the fact pattern.
6. B: the character's rationalization that a premium tier buys early recommendations; C: wrong sub-standard, II(A) substituted for III(B).
