# Aurelian Private Bank: Three Jurisdictions, One Desk

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Aurelian Private Bank is headquartered in Esterra and serves wealthy families in Esterra, Valdoria, and Mirovia.
Under the bank's policy and the rules of all three countries, the applicable law for any client relationship is the law of the country where the client resides.
Relationship manager Hugo Lenz, CFA, has clients in Valdoria and Esterra.
Relationship manager Amara Diallo, CFA, has clients in Esterra and Mirovia.
Both report to Rosa Klein, CFA, head of the private client desk.

Exhibit 1: Rules by country of client residence

| Practice | Valdoria | Esterra | Mirovia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posting market commentary on personal social media | Prohibited | Allowed | Allowed |
| Confirming executed trades by messaging app | Allowed | Allowed | Prohibited |
| Recording client telephone calls | Required | Required | Allowed |
| Disclosing referral benefits to clients | Not required | Not required | Not required |

Lenz posts weekly market commentary on his personal social media account, which many of his clients follow.
At the March desk meeting, Klein praises the posts as "the model for the desk" and asks Diallo to start posting, which Diallo does in April.
Both managers confirm executed trades to all of their clients by messaging app.
Klein reviews and signs the desk's monthly communications log, which lists every confirmation, the client's country of residence, and the channel used.
The desk records all client calls.
Aurelian's compliance manual states: "Employees must follow the laws of each client's country of residence."

A Valdorian accounting firm, Brenner & Voss, refers new clients to Lenz.
Each season Lenz gives two Brenner & Voss partners seats at the national rugby final, at EUR 1,400 per seat.
In March, after the most recent referred client had signed his advisory agreement, Lenz emails Klein describing the arrangement.
No client has been told.
Lenz tells Klein: "None of our three jurisdictions requires disclosure of referral benefits, so we are compliant."

Aurelian publishes rating changes on its client portal at 07:00 and simultaneously emails an alert to every client.
On 14 May, the research committee approves a downgrade of Nordhavn Shipping to Sell at 06:15.
Lenz sees the approval and at 06:40 phones his largest client, Selma Ek, because she is about to board a flight and "would miss the email."
Ek enters a sell order that executes at the 07:00 market open, ahead of any order from a client who first learned of the downgrade from the 07:00 portal posting and alert.
At 07:30, Diallo phones her three largest clients to discuss the downgrade in detail.

Diallo's largest client, Viktor Rahn, lives in Mirovia.
Rahn has offered Diallo's family a week at his villa in every year that his account beats its benchmark by at least 2 percentage points.
The arrangement is written into Rahn's investment policy statement, which Rahn has countersigned.
Diallo mentions it to Klein in a hallway conversation, and Klein replies, "Fine."
Last year Rahn's account returned 9.4% against a benchmark return of 7.1%.

Lenz joined Aurelian 18 months ago from a competitor.
Aurelian pays him EUR 2,000 for each client who follows him from his former employer, and 11 clients have done so.
None of those clients has been told about the payment.

## Questions

1. With respect to Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law, which statement about the managers' social media posts and messaging-app confirmations is most accurate? (LOS: los-10-03-a)
   - A. Both managers violate the Standard with respect to both practices.
   - B. Neither manager violates the Standard, because Aurelian is headquartered in Esterra, which allows both practices.
   - C. Lenz violates the Standard through his social media posts, and Diallo violates it through her messaging-app confirmations.
2. Does Klein most likely violate Standard IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors? (LOS: los-10-03-a)
   - A. No, because the compliance manual requires employees to follow the law of each client's residence.
   - B. Yes, with respect to both Lenz and Diallo.
   - C. Yes, with respect to Lenz only.
3. Lenz's statement that the Brenner & Voss arrangement is compliant is most likely: (LOS: los-10-03-b)
   - A. incorrect, because the Code and Standards are stricter than local law here and require disclosure of the benefit to his employer, clients, and prospective clients before they engage him.
   - B. correct, because Standard I(A) requires members to follow applicable law, and no applicable law requires disclosure.
   - C. incorrect, because Standard VI(C) prohibits members from providing benefits to sources of client referrals.
4. Which of the telephone calls about the Nordhavn downgrade most likely violate Standard III(B) Fair Dealing? (LOS: los-10-03-a)
   - A. Neither Lenz's call nor Diallo's calls
   - B. Lenz's call only
   - C. Both Lenz's call and Diallo's calls
5. Before Diallo's family may use Rahn's villa, Diallo must most likely: (LOS: los-10-03-b)
   - A. do nothing further, because the arrangement is documented in the IPS and Rahn has consented in writing.
   - B. refuse the arrangement, because performance-contingent benefits from clients are prohibited.
   - C. obtain written consent to the arrangement from all parties involved, including Aurelian.
6. By not disclosing the EUR 2,000 payment per transferred client to those clients, Lenz most likely: (LOS: los-10-03-a)
   - A. does not violate the Code and Standards.
   - B. violates Standard VI(C) Referral Fees.
   - C. violates Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** Fix applicable law by client residence, then apply the stricter of that law and the Code and Standards, practice by practice (V10 p.239, p.240).
> Lenz has Valdorian clients, and Valdoria prohibits market commentary on personal social media, so his posts violate I(A); his messaging-app confirmations are lawful because Valdoria and Esterra allow them.
> Diallo has Mirovian clients, and Mirovia prohibits messaging-app confirmations, so her confirmations violate I(A); her posts are lawful because Esterra and Mirovia allow them.
> A is wrong because it applies the strictest rule found anywhere in the exhibit to both managers instead of each manager's own client countries.
> B is wrong because the bank's headquarters is not the applicable law; the vignette fixes applicable law at client residence.
> 2. **B.** Standard IV(C) requires reasonable efforts that actually prevent and detect violations (V10 p.239, p.240, p.258).
> Klein praised and promoted Lenz's posts, which are illegal for his Valdorian clients, and she reviewed and signed logs showing Diallo confirming trades by messaging app to Mirovian clients, where it is prohibited, without acting.
> A is wrong because a manual that states the right rule is no defense when the supervisor endorses the conduct and ignores the evidence she reviews.
> C is wrong because it stops at the conduct Klein praised; the signed logs gave her knowledge of Diallo's violation, which she failed to address.
> 3. **A.** Rugby final seats given to referring accountants are consideration paid to others for the recommendation of services, which Standard VI(C) requires be disclosed to the employer, clients, and prospective clients before they enter into an agreement (V10 p.241, p.251).
> When local law requires no disclosure, the Code and Standards are the stricter rule and govern, so I(A) points to the Code, not away from it.
> Emailing Klein after the latest referred client signed is too late, and no client has been told.
> B is wrong because it applies the local rule even though the Code is stricter.
> C is wrong because VI(C) is a disclosure standard; the benefit is permitted once properly disclosed.
> 4. **B.** Lenz gave Ek the downgrade at 06:40, 20 minutes before the 07:00 release to all clients, and her order executed at the open, so she acted ahead of clients who had not yet received it; that is selective pre-release dissemination under III(B) (V10 p.233).
> Diallo called at 07:30, after the downgrade had been posted and emailed to every client, which is permitted added service for large clients (V10 p.241, p.242).
> A is wrong because a client's travel plans do not justify pre-release disclosure; Lenz could have waited until 07:00.
> C is wrong because extra service to the largest clients is permitted once the information has been disseminated to all.
> 5. **C.** The villa week is a benefit contingent on account performance that could bias Diallo toward Rahn's account, so Standard IV(B) requires written consent from all parties involved, including her employer, before she accepts (V10 p.242, p.243).
> Klein's hallway "Fine" is verbal, and Rahn's countersigned IPS is client-side documentation, not the employer's written consent.
> A is wrong because client documentation does not substitute for the employer's written consent.
> B is wrong because IV(B) does not prohibit such arrangements; written consent makes them permissible.
> 6. **A.** The EUR 2,000 per transferred client is compensation paid by Lenz's own employer for services, not a benefit received from or paid to a third party for recommending services, and it creates no conflict with Aurelian's interests (V10 p.254).
> The Code and Standards do not require him to tell clients how his employer pays him; the EUR 22,000 he has earned on 11 clients does not change that.
> B is wrong because it relabels employer compensation as a referral fee.
> C is wrong because IV(B) covers benefits from outside parties that may conflict with the employer's interest, and here the employer is the payer.

## Distractor design

1. A: strictest rule in the exhibit applied to every manager (grid not read by client residence); B: headquarters law used instead of client-residence law.
2. A: paper-policy defense; C: supervisor liability limited to the conduct she praised, ignoring the logs she reviewed.
3. B: local rule applied although the Code is stricter; C: disclosable benefit treated as prohibited.
4. A: client convenience accepted as a reason for pre-release disclosure; C: post-dissemination premium service mistaken for unfair dealing.
5. A: client-side IPS documentation substituted for employer written consent; B: consent-cured arrangement treated as prohibited.
6. B: employer compensation relabeled as a referral fee; C: employer-paid bonus relabeled as outside additional compensation.
