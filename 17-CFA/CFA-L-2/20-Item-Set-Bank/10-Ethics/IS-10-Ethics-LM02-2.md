# Amberly Wealth Partners: The Global Dividend Marketing Deck

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Daniel Osei, CFA, is chief compliance officer of Amberly Wealth Partners, a registered investment adviser serving families and foundations.
Amberly does not claim compliance with the GIPS standards.
In January 2026, Tomasz Brenner, CFA, who joined Amberly in March 2025 from Caldera Capital, submits a draft marketing deck for Amberly's Global Dividend strategy.
Brenner reports to Amberly's chief investment officer, Helen Marsh, CFA.

Amberly's written composite definition states that the Global Dividend composite includes all fee-paying discretionary accounts above USD 3 million managed to the strategy for the full calendar year.

Exhibit 1: Global Dividend accounts, calendar year 2025 (no external cash flows during the year)

| Account | Assets 1 Jan 2025 (USD m) | 2025 return | Status at deck date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harlow Trust | 42.0 | 9.8% | Active |
| Ngata Family | 25.0 | 11.2% | Active |
| Ruiz Foundation | 18.0 | 7.6% | Active |
| Pell Endowment | 30.0 | 1.4% | Terminated 15 January 2026 |
| Osric Holdings | 5.0 | 24.5% | Active |

All five accounts met the composite definition throughout 2025.
The deck reports a 2025 composite return of 10.57%, which Brenner computed from the four accounts still at Amberly on the date he prepared the deck.
A footnote states: "Past performance is no guarantee of future results."

A page titled "Our Record" shows an annualized return of 12.3% for 2019 through 2024.
Brenner earned that record at Caldera as the lead of a three-person team; the page does not mention Caldera or the team.

The team page contains two statements written by Brenner.

Statement 1: "All three of our portfolio managers passed each level of the CFA Program on their first attempt."

Statement 2: "Our disciplined dividend process will deliver at least 8% a year, as it has in every year since 2016."

The deck's fee page announces: "We cut our fee rate from 1.00% to 0.90%."
Under the old methodology, Amberly charged 1.00% annually on the average of beginning-of-year and end-of-year assets.
Under the new methodology, effective for 2025, Amberly charges 0.90% annually on end-of-year assets.
Amberly did not notify existing clients of the change because, as Marsh put it, "the rate went down, so no client can be hurt."

When Osei tells Marsh that the draft deck omitted the Pell Endowment, Marsh speaks with Brenner, accepts his assurance that it was an oversight that will not recur, and forwards a short note to the chief executive.
She takes no further action, and Brenner continues to prepare all Global Dividend marketing material without additional review.

Osei must decide which changes to require before the deck is released and what procedures Amberly should adopt for future performance materials.
He notes that several prospects in the deck's target audience are unsophisticated investors who will rely on the headline numbers.

## Questions

1. The 2025 Global Dividend composite return that is fair, accurate, and complete under Amberly's written composite definition is closest to: (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. 8.27%.
   - B. 10.57%.
   - C. 10.90%.
2. For the Ngata Family account, the change in the 2025 annual fee caused by the new methodology, and its treatment under the Standards, is best described as: (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. a decrease of about USD 13,800, and the change must be communicated to clients.
   - B. a decrease of about USD 13,800, and no communication is required because the fee fell.
   - C. a decrease of about USD 26,400, and the change must be communicated to clients.
3. Which of Brenner's statements violates the Code and Standards? (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both Statement 1 and Statement 2.
4. To present the 2019 through 2024 record consistently with the Standards, Osei should require that the deck: (LOS: los-10-02-b)
   - A. omit the record entirely, because performance earned at a prior firm cannot be shown.
   - B. identify the record as earned at Caldera by a three-person team, describe Brenner's role, and keep it separate from Amberly's composite.
   - C. link the record to Amberly's composite as one continuous track record, retaining the past-performance footnote.
5. Which procedure would most likely have prevented the problem with the composite return in the draft deck? (LOS: los-10-02-b)
   - A. Presenting the Osric Holdings account as a representative account, with its return and size fully disclosed.
   - B. Expanding the footnote to state that accounts no longer at Amberly are excluded from reported results.
   - C. Calculating composites under the written definition, including terminated accounts through their final period of management, and retaining the underlying data.
6. Marsh's response after learning that the Pell Endowment was omitted most likely: (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. complies with Standard IV(C), because she obtained Brenner's assurance and reported the matter to the chief executive.
   - B. violates Standard IV(C), because she did not investigate the matter or restrict or increase review of Brenner's activities pending the outcome.
   - C. violates Standard III(D) only, because supervisors are accountable for published numbers but not for the conduct of those they supervise.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Pell met the definition for all of 2025, so excluding it because it left in January 2026 is survivorship bias, and Standard III(D) requires terminated accounts to be included for the periods they were managed.
> Asset-weighted contributions: $42.0(9.8) = 411.6$, $25.0(11.2) = 280.0$, $18.0(7.6) = 136.8$, $30.0(1.4) = 42.0$, $5.0(24.5) = 122.5$; sum $= 992.9$ on total assets of 120.0.
> Composite return $= 992.9 / 120.0 = 8.2742\%$.
> B is wrong because it is the deck's figure, which drops Pell: $(992.9 - 42.0)/90.0 = 950.9/90.0 = 10.5656\%$.
> C is wrong because it equal-weights all five accounts, $(9.8 + 11.2 + 7.6 + 1.4 + 24.5)/5 = 10.9000\%$, which lets the small Osric account's 24.5% carry the same weight as the 42.0 million Harlow Trust.
> 2. **A.** End-of-year assets $= 25.0 \times 1.112 = 27.8$ million; average assets $= (25.0 + 27.8)/2 = 26.4$ million.
> Old fee $= 0.0100 \times 26{,}400{,}000 = 264{,}000$; new fee $= 0.0090 \times 27{,}800{,}000 = 250{,}200$; change $= -13{,}800$.
> Standard V(B) requires disclosure of the nature of services and all associated costs and prompt disclosure of changes; a change in fee calculation methodology must be communicated even when fees fall, and the client's sophistication is no excuse.
> B is wrong because it applies a no-harm test that the Standards reject.
> C is wrong because it applies the new rate to the old base, $0.0090 \times 26{,}400{,}000 - 264{,}000 = -26{,}400$, ignoring the switch to end-of-year assets.
> 3. **B.** Statement 2 promises a minimum return on a volatile equity strategy, which is a guarantee prohibited by Standard I(C); the historical record does not convert an opinion about future returns into fact.
> Statement 1 is a factual statement about exam results that does not imply superior performance, which Standard VII(B) permits.
> A is wrong because it punishes a permitted factual claim and misses the guarantee; C is wrong for the same reason about Statement 1.
> 4. **B.** Standard III(D) permits prior-firm performance only with disclosure of where it was earned and the member's specific role; a team record presented as one person's record, or blended into the new firm's history, is not fair, accurate, and complete.
> A is wrong because the cure is disclosure, not exclusion.
> C is wrong because linking the record to Amberly's composite misstates who earned it and where, and a generic footnote cures nothing.
> 5. **C.** The recommended procedures under III(D) (absent GIPS compliance) are to present weighted composites under a consistent definition, include terminated accounts through their final period, and maintain the data and records that support the results.
> A is wrong because a single representative account, especially the best-performing small one, is exactly what the weighted-composite procedure replaces.
> B is wrong because disclosing the exclusion does not make a result that contradicts the firm's own written definition fair and complete for an audience that relies on the headline number.
> 6. **B.** Once a violation is discovered, Standard IV(C) requires the supervisor to investigate its extent promptly, not rely on the wrongdoer's assurances, and limit the employee's activities or increase monitoring pending the outcome; reporting up the chain and a warning are not enough.
> A is wrong because it accepts assurance and escalation as sufficient, which the guidance rejects explicitly.
> C is wrong because supervisors are responsible for making reasonable efforts to ensure that those they supervise comply with the Code and Standards.

## Distractor design

1. B: survivorship bias, terminated account dropped (misread exhibit status column as a reason to exclude); C: equal weighting instead of asset weighting.
2. B: correct number but a no-harm test applied to V(B) cost disclosure; C: new rate applied to the old fee base.
3. A: factual candidacy claim punished and the guarantee missed; C: both statements treated as violations.
4. A: exclusion offered as the cure instead of disclosure; C: prior-firm record linked to the current firm, with a footnote treated as a cure.
5. A: single representative account instead of a weighted composite; B: disclosure treated as a cure for a result that contradicts the written definition.
6. A: assurance plus escalation treated as reasonable supervision; C: supervisory duty wrongly limited to the numbers rather than the people.
