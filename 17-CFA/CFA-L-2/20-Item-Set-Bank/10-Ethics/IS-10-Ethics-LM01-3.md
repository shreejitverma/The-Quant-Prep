# Karoo Securities: Initiating Coverage of Lesedi Platinum

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Thandi Mokoena, CFA, is a mining analyst at Karoo Securities, a Johannesburg broker-dealer with an institutional sales desk and a proprietary trading book.
She is preparing to initiate coverage of Lesedi Platinum, a listed platinum group metals producer.
Over three months Mokoena visited Lesedi's two operating shafts, interviewed management and suppliers, and built a discounted cash flow model that Karoo's head of research reviewed and approved.
Her key inputs and outputs appear in Exhibit 1.

Exhibit 1: Lesedi Platinum initiation, selected data

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Share price at publication | ZAR 150.00 |
| Mokoena's 12-month target price | ZAR 180.00 |
| Expected dividend per share, next 12 months | ZAR 4.50 |
| 52-week high share price | ZAR 195.00 |
| Average daily trading volume | 1.6 million shares |
| Weighted average cost of capital used in the model | 11.5% |

The first line of the draft report reads: "Lesedi's share price will rise to ZAR 180 within 12 months."
The report does not describe the target price as an estimate, and it contains no discussion of the assumptions behind it.

One week before publication, Mokoena has dinner with a university friend who works at Lesedi's external audit firm.
The friend tells her that Lesedi will announce a large impairment of its Kwena shaft the following week, and asks her to keep it quiet.
Mokoena does not trade Lesedi shares.
The next morning she calls Pieter Botha, CFA, Karoo's head of institutional sales, explains what the auditor said, and asks him to "keep it between us".
Botha immediately phones his largest clients and recommends that they sell their Lesedi shares before the end of the week.

On the day the report is published, Sipho Dlamini, CFA, a trader on Karoo's proprietary desk, proposes a plan to Botha.
Dlamini would buy and sell 400,000 Lesedi shares repeatedly between two accounts controlled by Karoo at the same price, so that Lesedi "shows up on every screen as the most active mining stock of the day" and attracts buyers to the initiation.
The plan would leave Karoo's net position unchanged.

Karoo does not claim compliance with the CFA Institute Code and Standards, and its internal research policy is silent on how target prices must be presented.
South African regulation, as described by Karoo's legal team, also contains no specific wording requirement for target prices.
When Mokoena's editor, Anele Khumalo, questions the report's first line, Mokoena replies:

"Karoo has not adopted the CFA Institute Code, and local rules are silent, so the only standard that applies to my report is Karoo's research policy."

## Questions

1. The price return implied by Mokoena's target price, and the Standard most directly engaged by the first line of the draft report, are: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. 16.67%, and Standard V(B) Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients.
   - B. 20.00%, and Standard V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis.
   - C. 20.00%, and Standard V(B) Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients.
2. Regarding the auditor's information about the Kwena impairment, which statement is most accurate? (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. Only Botha violated Standard II(A), because only his conduct led to trades.
   - B. Both Mokoena and Botha violated Standard II(A).
   - C. Mokoena violated Standard II(A), and Botha violated Standard II(B), because client selling distorted Lesedi's price.
3. Dlamini's proposed plan, if carried out, would most directly violate: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information, because the plan is known only to Karoo.
   - B. Standard II(B) Market Manipulation.
   - C. Standard III(B) Fair Dealing, because the trades are made in firm-controlled accounts.
4. Mokoena's reply to Khumalo is best described as: (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. correct, because the Code and Standards bind a member only through an employer that has adopted them.
   - B. correct, because where laws and firm policy are silent, members may apply their own judgment in place of the Standards.
   - C. incorrect, because the Code and Standards apply to her as an individual member regardless of her employer's policy or local law.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** Implied price return $= 180.00/150.00 - 1 = 1.2000 - 1 = 0.2000$, or $20.00\%$.
> V(B) requires members to distinguish between fact and opinion in the presentation of investment analysis and recommendations (V10 p.16).
> "Will rise to ZAR 180" presents a model-based forecast as certain fact, and the report omits the assumptions, so V(B) is engaged.
> A is wrong on the number because it divides the gain by the target price instead of the current price: $(180.00 - 150.00)/180.00 = 0.1667$, or $16.67\%$.
> B is wrong on the Standard because the facts establish a reasonable and adequate basis, supported by site visits, interviews, and a reviewed model, so V(A) is satisfied; the defect is in how the conclusion is communicated.
> The dividend of ZAR 4.50 and the 52-week high are decoys; including the dividend would give a total return of $(180.00 + 4.50)/150.00 - 1 = 0.2300$, which is not the price return asked for.
> 2. **B.** II(A) prohibits a member who possesses material nonpublic information that could affect the value of an investment from acting or causing others to act on it (V10 p.14).
> An impending large impairment disclosed by the auditor is material and nonpublic.
> Mokoena caused others to act by passing it to the sales head, and Botha, knowing its source, caused his clients to act by recommending sales.
> A is wrong because the "cause others to act" wording means a tipper who never trades still violates II(A).
> C is wrong because II(B) requires practices that distort prices or artificially inflate volume with intent to mislead; selling on inside information is a II(A) matter even though it moves the price.
> 3. **B.** II(B) prohibits practices that distort prices or artificially inflate trading volume with the intent to mislead market participants (V10 p.14).
> Repeated same-price trades between accounts Karoo controls add volume with no change in ownership, and the stated purpose is to make the stock look active to attract buyers, which supplies the intent element.
> A is wrong because the plan is not information about the value of Lesedi that Karoo would act on; knowledge of one's own planned trades is not the material nonpublic information that II(A) addresses.
> C is wrong because III(B) governs fair and objective treatment among clients, and the victims here are market participants at large; the 400,000-share size and the 1.6 million average volume do not change the classification.
> 4. **C.** The Code and Standards apply to members and candidates regardless of job function, cultural differences, or local laws and regulations, and every member carries a personal responsibility to uphold them (V10 p.13).
> They bind individuals, so whether Karoo has adopted them is irrelevant to Mokoena's obligations (V10 p.8).
> Under I(A), where the Standards are stricter than applicable law or policy, the member follows the stricter rule (V10 p.14).
> A is wrong because it reverses the scope: firm adoption is voluntary and encouraged, while individual members are bound directly.
> B is wrong because silence in law or firm policy leaves the Standards as the binding rule; it does not replace them with personal judgment.

## Distractor design

1. A: wrong denominator (target price instead of current price); B: communication failure mislabelled as a research-basis failure (V(A) versus V(B)).
2. A: tipper without a trade wrongly cleared (the "cause others to act" clause ignored); C: price effect of insider selling mislabelled as manipulation (II(A) versus II(B)).
3. A: firm's knowledge of its own trades confused with material nonpublic information; C: market-wide harm confused with unfair treatment among clients.
4. A: individual scope of the Code confused with firm adoption; B: silence in law misread as releasing the member from the Standards rather than making them the stricter rule.
