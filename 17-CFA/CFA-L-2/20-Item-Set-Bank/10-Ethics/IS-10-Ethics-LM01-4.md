# Meridian Straits Asset Management: Implementing the 2023 Revisions

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Lim Wei Ling, CFA, is head of compliance at Meridian Straits Asset Management, a Singapore asset manager that runs discretionary mandates for family offices and corporate treasuries.
All eleven of Meridian's portfolio managers are CFA charterholders.
Lim is leading a project to align Meridian's client documents and internal code of ethics with the 2023 revisions to the Standards of Professional Conduct.
She works with Arjun Nair, CFA, head of product.

Exhibit 1: Meridian discretionary mandate fee schedule

| Fee | Terms |
|---|---|
| Management fee, first SGD 5 million | 0.80% per year |
| Management fee, next SGD 10 million | 0.60% per year |
| Management fee, above SGD 15 million | 0.45% per year |
| Custody fee, charged by Meridian's custodian and passed through to the client | 0.05% of assets per year |
| Performance fee | 10% of annual return above an 8% hurdle |
| Minimum mandate size | SGD 2 million |

Meridian's current client disclosure describes the investment process, promises prompt notice of material changes to it, and sets out the principal risks and limitations of the strategy.
It does not state what the services cost.
Nair argues that the disclosure already meets every element of Standard V(B).
Lim disagrees and asks him to prepare a worked cost figure for a new client, Tan Holdings, which is opening a SGD 12 million mandate.
Tan Holdings' mandate returned 6.2% in a trial period last year.

Lim also reviews Meridian's bonus plan, under which portfolio managers receive a bonus linked to client inflows into Meridian's in-house money market fund.
Nair proposes adding a footnote about the bonus on page 38 of the client agreement.
Lim instead proposes to remove the link between the bonus and in-house fund flows, noting that this can be done at little cost.

Meridian recently hired Chen Jia Hui, CFA, to manage structured credit mandates, and Nair has drafted a competence policy for her.

Nair (4 March): "Standard I(E) sets one competence benchmark for all members, so Chen needs exactly the same level of competence as our junior equity associates, and completing 20 hours of continuing education a year satisfies it."

Lim (4 March): "Competence under I(E) depends on the nature and complexity of each member's duties, and the Standard does not prescribe a particular continuing-education program."

Meridian's draft internal code quotes the Code of Ethics.
Its sixth principle is quoted as: "Maintain and improve their professional competence."

Lim is also considering whether Meridian should adopt the CFA Institute Asset Manager Code.
In a briefing memo, Nair writes that the Asset Manager Code covers six areas: "representative of high standards, relevant to the changing profession, globally applicable, sufficiently comprehensive and practical and specific, enforceable, and testable".

Finally, Nair's memo adds: "The 2023 revisions were written and approved by the Disciplinary Review Committee, which will also certify Meridian's compliance once our code is finished."

## Questions

1. Using Exhibit 1 and excluding the performance fee, the total annual cost that Tan Holdings will bear for the services, which the revised V(B) disclosure should communicate, is closest to: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. SGD 78,000.
   - B. SGD 82,000.
   - C. SGD 88,000.
2. Lim's response to the bonus plan, compared with Nair's footnote, is best described as: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. unnecessary, because any written disclosure of the bonus satisfies Standard VI(A).
   - B. consistent with revised Standard VI(A), which favours avoiding a conflict when reasonably possible and requires disclosure to be prominent, in plain language, and effective when it is not avoided.
   - C. required only because Standard III(A) prohibits portfolio managers from receiving any bonus tied to firm products.
3. Regarding Standard I(E), which 4 March statement is most likely correct? (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. Nair's statement.
   - B. Lim's statement.
   - C. Neither statement, because competence is addressed in the Code of Ethics but not in the Standards.
4. The draft internal code's version of the sixth Code principle is incomplete because it omits the words: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. "and strive to maintain and improve the competence of other investment professionals".
   - B. "for the ultimate benefit of society".
   - C. "through a program of continuing education approved by CFA Institute".
5. Nair's description of the Asset Manager Code is: (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. correct.
   - B. incorrect, because he has listed the Standards of Practice Council's criteria for evaluating the Code and Standards rather than the Asset Manager Code's areas.
   - C. incorrect, because the Asset Manager Code applies to individual members rather than to firms.
6. The final sentence of Nair's memo is best corrected to state that the 2023 revisions were: (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. approved by the Board of Governors, and CFA Institute does not verify a firm's claim of compliance.
   - B. approved by the Standards of Practice Council, which will certify Meridian's compliance.
   - C. approved by the Board of Governors, which will certify Meridian's compliance through the Professional Conduct Program.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** The 2023 revision to V(B) requires disclosure of the nature of the services provided and the costs to the client associated with those services (V10 p.6, p.16).
> Tiered management fee: $5{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0080 = 40{,}000$ plus $7{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0060 = 42{,}000$, total $82{,}000$; the 0.45% tier is not reached.
> Custody pass-through: $12{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0005 = 6{,}000$.
> Total $= 82{,}000 + 6{,}000 = 88{,}000$, an effective rate of $88{,}000/12{,}000{,}000 = 0.7333\%$.
> A is wrong because it applies the 0.60% marginal rate to the entire balance: $12{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0060 + 6{,}000 = 78{,}000$.
> B is wrong because it stops at Meridian's own management fee and omits the custody fee that the client also bears for the service: $82{,}000$.
> The 6.2% trial return and the minimum mandate size are decoys; the performance fee is excluded by the stem and would in any case be zero below the 8% hurdle.
> 2. **B.** Revised VI(A), renamed "Avoid or Disclose Conflicts", codifies that members should avoid conflicts whenever reasonably possible, and when avoidance is not reasonable must make full and fair disclosure that is prominent, in plain language, and effective (V10 p.6, p.7, p.17).
> Lim's change is cheap and removes the conflict at its source, which is the approach the revision favours.
> A is wrong because a footnote on page 38 fails the prominence and effectiveness requirement, and the standard now expressly contemplates avoidance.
> C is wrong because III(A) requires placing client interests first when acting, not a blanket ban on product-linked pay; the pay structure itself is a VI(A) matter.
> 3. **B.** I(E) requires acting with and maintaining the competence necessary to fulfil professional responsibilities; the expected level varies with the nature and complexity of each member's duties, and I(E) does not require any specific program of professional development or continuing education (V10 p.5, p.6).
> A is wrong on both counts: there is no single benchmark, and a set number of hours neither is required nor, by itself, demonstrates the competence a complex structured credit role demands.
> C is wrong because I(E) was added to the Standards in 2023 precisely to make the Code's competence principle explicit (V10 p.5).
> 4. **A.** The sixth component reads "Maintain and improve their professional competence and strive to maintain and improve the competence of other investment professionals" (V10 p.14).
> B is wrong because "for the ultimate benefit of society" ends the fifth component, not the sixth.
> C is wrong because neither the Code nor I(E) prescribes a continuing-education program (V10 p.6).
> 5. **B.** The six areas of the Asset Manager Code are loyalty to clients, the investment process, trading, compliance, performance, and disclosure (V10 p.8).
> Nair's list is the six criteria the SPC uses to evaluate the Code and Standards: representative of high standards, relevant, globally applicable, sufficiently comprehensive and practical and specific, enforceable, and testable (V10 p.9).
> A is wrong for that reason.
> C is wrong because the Asset Manager Code is the firm-level document; it is the Code and Standards that bind individual members (V10 p.8).
> 6. **A.** The Board of Governors added I(E) and revised V(B) and VI(A) in 2023, and a firm that claims compliance must state that the claim has not been verified by CFA Institute (V10 p.5, p.8).
> The DRC enforces the Code and Standards through panels in contested cases; it neither revises the Standards nor certifies firms (V10 p.7).
> B is wrong because the SPC maintains and interprets the Code and Standards, and no body certifies a firm's compliance.
> C is wrong because the Board of Governors oversees the Professional Conduct Program, which enforces conduct by individual members and candidates; it does not certify firms.

## Distractor design

1. A: marginal tier rate applied to the whole balance (wrong formula variant); B: pass-through cost omitted from the cost disclosure (incomplete cost base).
2. A: pre-2023 disclose-only reading of VI(A) and the prominence requirement ignored; C: III(A) action duty misread as a ban on a compensation structure.
3. A: fact-specific competence test replaced by a single benchmark and an hours rule; C: 2023 addition of I(E) forgotten.
4. B: fifth component's ending grafted onto the sixth; C: a continuing-education requirement invented from I(E).
5. A: SPC evaluation criteria swapped in for the Asset Manager Code areas; C: firm-level and individual-level documents reversed.
6. B: interpreting body (SPC) given the approving role plus a certification that does not exist; C: correct approver but an invented certification role for the PCP.
