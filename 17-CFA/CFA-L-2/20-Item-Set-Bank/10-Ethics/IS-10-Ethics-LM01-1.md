# Tavora Wealth Partners: Mapping a Compliance Log

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Beatriz Tavora, CFA, is chief compliance officer of Tavora Wealth Partners, a Lisbon wealth manager with EUR 2.3 billion in client assets.
The firm employs 41 investment professionals, of whom 19 are CFA charterholders and 6 are CFA candidates.
Each quarter Tavora maps every entry in the firm's compliance log to the provision of the CFA Institute Standards of Professional Conduct it most directly engages.
She is training Rui Almeida, a junior analyst and Level II candidate, to do the mapping.

Exhibit 1: Compliance log, third quarter

| Entry | Person | Facts recorded |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paulo Seixas, CFA, equity analyst | Accepted a seven-night resort holiday for himself and his spouse, paid by a listed retailer he covers; his next report upgraded the retailer to Buy |
| 2 | Ines Farto, CFA, portfolio manager | Moved a discretionary client from an external bond fund into Tavora's in-house bond fund, which pays the firm a higher fee; the external fund had the same mandate, lower cost, and a better fit with the client's written objectives; the fee arrangement is described in every client agreement |
| 3 | Tomas Reis, CFA, portfolio manager | Bought shares of a utility for his own account at 09:05 on 14 August; Tavora's Buy recommendation on the utility was sent to clients at 11:30 the same day |
| 4 | Ines Farto, CFA | Named a current client and described its bond holdings to a prospective client as a reference, without asking the current client |
| 5 | Paulo Seixas, CFA | Recommended a concentrated position to an advisory client after reviewing the account held at Tavora, without asking about the client's holdings at two other banks |
| 6 | Rui Almeida, candidate | Accepted EUR 3,000 from a client of the firm for building a private tax spreadsheet over weekends, a service Tavora sells; Almeida told nobody at the firm |

Reviewing Entry 2, Tavora and Almeida disagree.

Tavora (2 October): "Because Farto's fee arrangement is disclosed in every client agreement, Entry 2 does not violate any Standard."

Almeida (2 October): "Entry 2 is a violation of Standard VI(A), because the higher fee is a conflict of interest and the revised standard now requires members to avoid every conflict."

Tavora also asks Almeida how Entry 6 could have been handled properly.
Almeida suggests that he could have kept the fee if Tavora's managing partner had approved it in writing.

The final item on Tavora's agenda is the firm's website.
The marketing team has drafted the following sentence for the home page:
"Tavora Wealth Partners complies with the CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct, and our compliance has been verified by CFA Institute."
Tavora notes that the firm's internal code of ethics was rewritten last year to meet every principle of the Code and Standards.

Almeida closes the meeting by asking what would happen to Farto if the client in Entry 2 sent a written complaint to CFA Institute.
Tavora explains that Professional Conduct staff would investigate, and she asks Almeida to research what happens if staff propose a sanction that Farto refuses to accept.

## Questions

1. Entry 1 most directly engages which Standard? (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity
   - B. Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements
   - C. Standard VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts
2. Regarding Entry 2, which of the 2 October statements is most likely correct? (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. Tavora's statement, because full disclosure of the fee arrangement satisfies the Standards.
   - B. Almeida's statement, because the fee differential is a conflict that the revised VI(A) required Farto to avoid.
   - C. Neither statement, because placing the client in the higher-fee fund subordinated the client's interest to the firm's, which violates Standard III(A).
3. Of the six log entries in Exhibit 1, the number that most directly engage a sub-standard of Standard III Duties to Clients is: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. 2.
   - B. 3.
   - C. 4.
4. For Entry 6, the condition under which Almeida could have accepted the EUR 3,000 is: (LOS: los-10-01-a)
   - A. written consent from Tavora's managing partner, as Almeida suggests.
   - B. written consent from all parties involved.
   - C. disclosure of the payment to the client as a referral fee under Standard VI(C).
5. The home page sentence is best revised to read: (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. "Tavora Wealth Partners claims compliance with the CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct. This claim has not been verified by CFA Institute."
   - B. "Tavora Wealth Partners employs individual CFA charterholders; as a firm it may not adopt the CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct."
   - C. "Tavora Wealth Partners complies with the CFA Institute Asset Manager Code, as verified by CFA Institute."
6. If Professional Conduct staff propose a sanction and Farto rejects both the charges and the sanction, the next step is that: (LOS: los-10-01-b)
   - A. the Standards of Practice Council interprets the Standards and issues a binding ruling.
   - B. a panel of Disciplinary Review Committee members decides whether a violation occurred and, if so, the sanction.
   - C. staff must close the matter with a cautionary letter because the sanction was not accepted.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** A lavish holiday paid by a company Seixas covers is a benefit that could reasonably be expected to compromise his independence and objectivity, which is the wording of I(B) (V10 p.14).
> B is wrong because IV(B) protects the employer from compensation that competes with its interest; the threat here is to the objectivity of research, not to Tavora's business (V10 p.16).
> C is wrong because the sub-standard that names the specific behaviour (gifts and benefits) wins over the general conflicts standard.
> 2. **C.** III(A) requires placing client interests before the employer's or one's own; Farto chose the higher-fee product even though the external fund was cheaper and better suited, so the action itself breached the duty of loyalty, prudence, and care (V10 p.15).
> Disclosure addresses VI(A), but disclosure does not cure an action taken against the client, so Tavora's statement is wrong.
> A is wrong for that reason: disclosure satisfies only the disclosure obligation, not the duty to act for the client's benefit.
> B is wrong because the revised VI(A) prefers avoidance only when that is reasonably possible and still permits prominent, plain-language, effective disclosure otherwise, so it imposes no absolute duty to avoid (V10 p.6, p.7); the harm in the facts is the subordinated client interest.
> 3. **B.** Mapping each entry: 1 is I(B), 2 is III(A), 3 is VI(B), 4 is III(E), 5 is III(C), 6 is IV(B).
> Entries 2, 4, and 5 fall under Standard III, so the count is $3$.
> Entry 4 is III(E) because none of the three exceptions (illegal activity, disclosure required by law, client permission) applies (V10 p.15).
> Entry 5 is III(C) because in an advisory relationship suitability must be judged in the context of the client's total portfolio (V10 p.15).
> Entry 3 is VI(B), which gives client transactions priority over those in which the member is beneficial owner (V10 p.17).
> A is wrong because it sends Entry 2 to VI(A) on the word "conflict", giving $3 - 1 = 2$.
> C is wrong because it sends Entry 6 to Standard III because the payer was a client, giving $3 + 1 = 4$; the protected party in Entry 6 is the employer.
> 4. **B.** IV(B) prohibits accepting compensation that competes with or might reasonably create a conflict with the employer's interest unless written consent is obtained from all parties involved (V10 p.16).
> A is wrong because consent from the employer alone is the "employer only" misreading that the curriculum's practice problem 8 tests.
> C is wrong because VI(C) concerns compensation for recommending products or services, and Almeida was paid to perform a service, not to refer one (V10 p.17).
> 5. **A.** The Code and Standards bind individual members and candidates, but firms are encouraged to adopt them; a firm whose code meets their principles should claim compliance with the prescribed statement whose second sentence is "This claim has not been verified by CFA Institute" (V10 p.8).
> B is wrong because adoption by firms is encouraged, not prohibited.
> C is wrong because CFA Institute does not verify the claim, and the Asset Manager Code is a separate firm-level document that the firm has not adopted in the facts.
> 6. **B.** When a member rejects the charges and proposed sanction, the matter is referred to a panel composed of DRC members, which reviews materials and presentations from staff and the member and determines whether a violation occurred and what sanction to impose (V10 p.7, p.8).
> A is wrong because the SPC maintains and interprets the Code and Standards and has no enforcement role (V10 p.8).
> C is wrong because a cautionary letter is one of the staff's three dispositions before charges, not a required consequence of rejection (V10 p.7).

## Distractor design

1. B: gift threatening independence sent to the employer-protection standard (I(B) versus IV(B)); C: specific sub-standard passed over for the general conflicts standard.
2. A: disclosure treated as curing an action against the client; B: III(A) harm relabelled VI(A) because the vignette uses the word "conflict", plus the revised VI(A) misread as a mandatory avoidance rule.
3. A: Entry 2 miscounted as VI(A) (count 2); C: Entry 6 miscounted as a client duty because a client paid (count 4).
4. A: "employer only" consent in place of all parties; C: service compensation confused with a referral fee.
5. B: individual scope of the Code misread as a ban on firm adoption; C: verification claimed and the firm-level Asset Manager Code substituted.
6. A: the interpreting body (SPC) given the enforcing body's job; C: a pre-charge staff disposition treated as the post-rejection outcome.
