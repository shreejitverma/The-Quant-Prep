# Kestrel Bay Private Wealth: A Manager Between Two Jurisdictions

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Wen Hao Lim, CFA, is a portfolio manager at Kestrel Bay Private Wealth, an adviser registered in Veldria that manages discretionary accounts for families resident in Veldria and in neighbouring Orsenia.
Veldrian securities law prohibits an adviser from recommending to clients any security in which the adviser holds a personal position.
The same law states that services provided to clients resident outside Veldria are governed by the law of the client's country of residence.
Orsenia has no law or regulation addressing advisers' personal holdings.

Lim personally owns shares of Corvane Therapeutics, which he believes are undervalued.
One of his clients, Farida Qasim, is resident in Orsenia and holds a discretionary account at Kestrel Bay.
Her written IPS sets the ranges and limits shown in Exhibit 1 for the Kestrel Bay account.

Exhibit 1: Qasim account at 4 May 2026 (EUR)

| Asset class | Market value | IPS range (percent of account) |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | 1,200,000 | 5% to 20% |
| Bonds | 2,300,000 | 25% to 45% |
| Equities (including Corvane, 300,000) | 4,500,000 | 40% to 60% |
| Total | 8,000,000 | Single-issuer limit: 10% |

Qasim's equities are benchmarked to a regional index in which Corvane has a weight of 2.4%.
Kestrel Bay's policy classifies an unsolicited client trade as having a material impact if, after the trade, any IPS range or limit would be breached, and as having a minimal impact otherwise.
On 4 May, Qasim telephoned Lim and, without any recommendation from him, instructed him to buy EUR 450,000 of Corvane for her account, funded from cash.

On 11 May, Lim gave notice that he would resign, effective 29 May, to join Tidemark Advisers, a competitor.
On Saturday 16 May he signed a lease on an office for his Tidemark team, using his own funds.
On 20 May he e-mailed to his personal address a client risk-scoring spreadsheet that he had built at home over several weekends and had used for all his Kestrel Bay clients.
He did not ask Kestrel Bay for permission.
Lim has no noncompete or nonsolicitation agreement.
On 3 June, from Tidemark, he contacted six former clients whose details he found in a public professional directory.

After Lim's departure, Kestrel Bay's compliance officer, Oriana Vasquez, CFA, prepared a records purge for 30 June 2026.
Veldrian regulation requires advisers to retain records supporting investment recommendations and client communications for six years from the date each record is created.
Kestrel Bay has no retention rule of its own.
A colleague argued that "the CFA Institute seven-year period overrides the local rule," and another argued that "a review that changed nothing is not an investment record."

Exhibit 2: Records proposed for destruction on 30 June 2026

| Record | Content | Date created |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Research file supporting a buy recommendation | 12 March 2020 |
| R2 | Notes of an annual IPS review that led to no change | 14 September 2021 |
| R3 | Model input parameters and output supporting a sell decision | 3 August 2020 |
| R4 | E-mails to a client containing a recommendation | 15 January 2019 |
| R5 | Criteria used to select an external subadviser | 23 April 2019 |

## Questions

1. Applying Kestrel Bay's policy, Qasim's Corvane order is best described as having: (LOS: los-10-02-b)
   - A. a minimal impact, because Corvane would be about 9.4% of the account; Lim should discuss the order with Qasim, document her acknowledgment, and execute it under the firm's approval policy.
   - B. a material impact, because equities would be about 61.9% of the account; the IPS should be updated before the order is executed, and if Qasim declines, the trade should be placed in a separate unmanaged account.
   - C. a material impact, because equities would be about 61.9% of the account; Lim must refuse the order because it is inconsistent with the written IPS.
2. With respect to recommending Corvane to Qasim, Lim is most likely required to: (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. refrain from recommending Corvane, because Veldrian law is stricter than the Code and Standards.
   - B. recommend Corvane only with full and fair disclosure of his personal holding.
   - C. recommend Corvane without any disclosure, because Orsenian law imposes no requirement.
3. Which of Lim's actions after giving notice most likely violate the Standards? (LOS: los-10-02-a)
   - A. Only e-mailing the risk-scoring spreadsheet to his personal address.
   - B. E-mailing the spreadsheet and contacting former clients on 3 June.
   - C. Signing the office lease and e-mailing the spreadsheet.
4. Under a retention procedure consistent with the Standards, the number of Exhibit 2 records that Kestrel Bay may destroy on 30 June 2026 is: (LOS: los-10-02-b)
   - A. 2.
   - B. 3.
   - C. 4.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** After the trade, cash is $1{,}200{,}000 - 450{,}000 = 750{,}000$, or $750{,}000 / 8{,}000{,}000 = 9.3750\%$, inside its 5% to 20% range.
> Corvane is $300{,}000 + 450{,}000 = 750{,}000$, or $9.3750\%$, inside the 10% single-issuer limit.
> Equities are $4{,}500{,}000 + 450{,}000 = 4{,}950{,}000$, or $4{,}950{,}000 / 8{,}000{,}000 = 61.8750\%$, which breaches the 60% ceiling, so the trade has a material impact.
> Under Standard III(C), for an unsolicited request with a material impact the manager should update the IPS before executing; if the client will not update it but insists, the trade can be executed in a separate unmanaged account, or the relationship reconsidered.
> A is wrong because it checks only the single-issuer limit and misses the asset-class breach.
> C is wrong because the Standards do not require refusing a client's instruction; the recommended procedure is an IPS update or a separate account.
> The 2.4% benchmark weight is a decoy; IPS limits are measured against the account.
> 2. **B.** Veldrian law contains a choice-of-law rule sending services for non-resident clients to the law of the client's country, so Orsenian law applies.
> Orsenia has no rule, so the applicable law is less strict than the Code and Standards, and under Standard I(A) Lim must follow the Code and Standards.
> Standard VI(A) requires, at a minimum, full and fair disclosure of any beneficial interest in a recommended security.
> A is wrong because it applies the Veldrian prohibition to a client whom Veldrian law itself assigns to Orsenian law.
> C is wrong because the absence of local law does not remove the Code's disclosure duty.
> 3. **A.** Records and work product created for the employer belong to the employer even when built on the member's own time; taking the spreadsheet without written permission violates Standard IV(A), and the records are the firm's property under Standard V(C).
> Signing a lease on his own time and money is permitted preparation before resignation.
> Contacting former clients after departure, using public information and with no nonsolicitation agreement, is permitted.
> B is wrong because post-departure contact from a public directory is not a violation.
> C is wrong because preparation that does not solicit clients or compete before departure is allowed.
> 4. **B.** Where local regulation sets a retention period, meeting it satisfies Standard V(C); the seven-year recommendation applies only absent regulatory or firm guidance.
> Six-year anniversaries: R1 on 12 March 2026 (age 6.2998 years on 30 June 2026), R4 on 15 January 2025 (7.4552 years), and R5 on 23 April 2025 (7.1869 years) have all passed, so these three may be destroyed.
> R2 is 4.7912 years old and R3 is 5.9055 years old, so both must be kept.
> A is wrong because it applies a seven-year period, which leaves only R4 and R5.
> C is wrong because it treats R2 as outside the Standard; records supporting reviews that lead to no change must also be retained, so R2 is kept until 14 September 2027.

## Distractor design

1. A: only the single-issuer limit checked, asset-class breach missed (incomplete multi-limit test); C: correct number but refusal substituted for the IPS update or separate-account procedure.
2. A: choice-of-law clause ignored, so the wrong country's law applied; C: absence of local law treated as absence of any duty.
3. B: permitted post-departure public-source contact treated as solicitation; C: permitted preparation treated as competition.
4. A: seven-year default applied despite a governing local rule (wrong period); C: no-change reviews excluded from the records the Standard covers (misread scope).
