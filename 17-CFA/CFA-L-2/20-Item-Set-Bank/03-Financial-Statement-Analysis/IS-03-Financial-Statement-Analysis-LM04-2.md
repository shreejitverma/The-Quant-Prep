# Meridian Pacific Banking Group: Liquidity and Earnings Quality

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Hana Okafor is an equity analyst at a brokerage in Singapore.
She is updating her valuation of Meridian Pacific Banking Group, a diversified universal bank with retail, corporate, and markets businesses in eleven countries.
Meridian reports in $ millions except where stated, and its year ends in December.
Okafor starts with liquidity, because her discount rate for the shares depends on her view of funding risk.

Exhibit 1: Meridian liquidity disclosures, 31 December 2025 ($ billions)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Total assets | 1,184.0 |
| High-quality liquid assets (unencumbered) | 171.6 |
| Expected net cash outflows over a 30-day stress scenario | 132.0 |
| Required stable funding | 520.0 |

Exhibit 2: Meridian funding structure, 31 December 2025 ($ billions)

| Funding source | Amount |
|---|---|
| Regulatory capital | 62 |
| Long-term debt with residual maturity over one year | 88 |
| Stable retail deposits, maturity under one year | 310 |
| Less stable retail deposits, maturity under one year | 140 |
| Non-financial corporate deposits, maturity under one year | 120 |
| Interbank funding, maturity under six months | 75 |
| Trade-date payables | 15 |

Okafor applies the Basel III available stable funding factors: 100% for regulatory capital and liabilities of one year or more, 95% for stable retail deposits under one year, 90% for less stable retail deposits, 50% for non-financial corporate funding under one year, and 0% for all other liabilities.

Exhibit 3: Meridian interest and earnings data ($ millions)

| Item | 2021 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Interest income | | 38,640 |
| Interest expense | | 15,960 |
| Average interest-earning assets | | 912,000 |
| Average interest-bearing liabilities | | 798,000 |
| Total revenue | 38,500 | 42,000 |
| Trading revenue | 2,310 | 5,040 |
| Provision for credit losses | 5,420 | 2,150 |
| Pretax income | 9,850 | 14,210 |
| One-day 99% trading VaR, year end | 64 | 71 |

Meridian's reported net interest margin for 2024 was 2.61%.

Okafor's manager, Wei Tan, asks her to comment on an item in the notes.
Meridian's notional amount of freestanding credit derivatives, which do not qualify for hedge accounting, rose from $12.4 billion at the end of 2023 to $96.0 billion at the end of 2025.
Tan also notes that Meridian's board approved a new equity-based compensation plan for its markets division that pays above the peer average.

Before closing her model, Okafor drafts an introduction for clients who are new to bank shares.
She considers three sentences and will keep only the accurate one.

Sentence A: "Meridian's customer deposits are its largest asset category, and they expose the bank mainly to credit risk."

Sentence B: "Relative to a large property and casualty insurer, Meridian likely poses greater global systemic risk, because more of its business is cross-border and insurers' foreign branches generally must hold local assets against local policy liabilities."

Sentence C: "Because Meridian's deposits are government-insured, it faces no risk of a bank run, so systemic importance is mainly a concern for unregulated lenders."

## Questions

1. Based on Exhibit 1, the number of days that Meridian could withstand stress-level net cash outflows is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-d)
   - A. 9.0 days.
   - B. 23.1 days.
   - C. 39.0 days.
2. Based on Exhibits 1 and 2, Meridian's net stable funding ratio is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-d)
   - A. 121.3%.
   - B. 128.5%.
   - C. 155.8%.
3. Meridian's 2025 net interest margin, and its direction relative to 2024, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-d)
   - A. 2.24%, a decline.
   - B. 2.49%, a decline.
   - C. 2.84%, an increase.
4. The proportion of the 2021 to 2025 increase in pretax income that is explained by the decline in the provision for credit losses is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-d)
   - A. 23.0%.
   - B. 33.2%.
   - C. 75.0%.
5. The change in Meridian's freestanding credit derivatives position from 2023 to 2025 most likely: (LOS: los-03-04-e)
   - A. reduced earnings volatility, because derivatives that do not qualify as hedges are recognised in other comprehensive income.
   - B. increased earnings volatility, because fair value changes on freestanding derivatives are recognised in net income each period.
   - C. had no effect on earnings or collateral, because only the notional amount changed.
6. Which of Okafor's three sentences is accurate? (LOS: los-03-04-a)
   - A. Sentence A.
   - B. Sentence B.
   - C. Sentence C.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** $LCR = 171.6 / 132.0 = 1.3000 = 130\%$.
> Days covered $= 1.30 \times 30 = 39.0$ days of stress-level outflows; equivalently, outflows could run 30% above the stress assumption.
> A is wrong because it multiplies only the excess over 100% by 30: $(1.30 - 1) \times 30 = 9.0$.
> B is wrong because it inverts the ratio: $132.0 / 171.6 = 0.7692$ and $0.7692 \times 30 = 23.1$.
> 2. **A.** $ASF = 62 \times 1.00 + 88 \times 1.00 + 310 \times 0.95 + 140 \times 0.90 + 120 \times 0.50 + 75 \times 0 + 15 \times 0$.
> $ASF = 62 + 88 + 294.5 + 126 + 60 + 0 + 0 = 630.5$.
> $NSFR = 630.5 / 520.0 = 1.2125 = 121.3\%$.
> B is wrong because it gives short interbank funding a 50% factor as if it were six-to-twelve-month wholesale funding: $(630.5 + 37.5) / 520.0 = 1.2846 = 128.5\%$.
> C is wrong because it uses unweighted funding: $810 / 520.0 = 1.5577 = 155.8\%$.
> 3. **B.** Net interest revenue $= 38{,}640 - 15{,}960 = 22{,}680$.
> $NIM = 22{,}680 / 912{,}000 = 0.024868 = 2.49\%$, down from 2.61% in 2024.
> A is wrong because it computes the spread, yield on assets less cost of liabilities: $38{,}640 / 912{,}000 - 15{,}960 / 798{,}000 = 4.2368\% - 2.0000\% = 2.24\%$.
> C is wrong because it divides net interest revenue by average interest-bearing liabilities: $22{,}680 / 798{,}000 = 2.84\%$.
> 4. **C.** Increase in pretax income $= 14{,}210 - 9{,}850 = 4{,}360$.
> Decrease in provisions $= 5{,}420 - 2{,}150 = 3{,}270$.
> Share explained $= 3{,}270 / 4{,}360 = 0.7500 = 75.0\%$, so most of the profit growth came from a lower discretionary estimate, not from the business; trading also rose from $2{,}310 / 38{,}500 = 6.0\%$ to $5{,}040 / 42{,}000 = 12.0\%$ of revenue, so earnings quality is weak.
> A is wrong because it divides the provision decline by the 2025 level of pretax income: $3{,}270 / 14{,}210 = 23.0\%$.
> B is wrong because it divides by the 2021 level of pretax income: $3{,}270 / 9{,}850 = 33.2\%$.
> 5. **B.** A derivative that qualifies as a cash flow or net investment hedge has fair value changes recorded in OCI, but a freestanding derivative is marked to fair value through net income each period, so a nearly eightfold increase in notional exposure most likely raised earnings volatility and could also require more posted collateral.
> A is wrong because it assigns cash flow hedge (OCI) treatment to derivatives that do not qualify for hedge accounting.
> C is wrong because a larger freestanding book exposes earnings to larger fair value changes and can raise collateral requirements.
> 6. **B.** Global commercial banks have the largest share of cross-border business, while insurers conduct less of it and their foreign branches must generally hold local assets against local policy liabilities, so an insurer's global systemic risk is lower.
> A is wrong because deposits are liabilities, making up the bulk of a bank's liabilities; a bank's assets are mainly loans and securities.
> C is wrong because deposit insurance covers deposits only up to a limit, and the mere expectation that a bank might not pay can still trigger a run.

## Distractor design

1. A: only the excess over 100% converted to days (wrong formula variant); B: LCR inverted (numerator and denominator swapped).
2. B: short interbank funding given the 50% wholesale factor (wrong factor bucket); C: ASF factors ignored (unweighted funding).
3. A: interest spread computed instead of margin (wrong formula variant); C: liabilities used as the denominator (misread exhibit).
4. A: provision decline divided by the ending level of pretax income (change versus level); B: divided by the beginning level (change versus level).
5. A: OCI hedge treatment applied to freestanding derivatives (wrong accounting category); C: notional changes treated as irrelevant to earnings and collateral.
6. A: deposits called assets (balance-sheet side error); C: deposit insurance treated as unlimited (overstated protection).
