# Tarrant Logic: Soft Assets, Bundled Contracts, and a Warranty Reserve

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Rohan Mehta, CFA, is a technology analyst at Sable Point Asset Management, which holds a large position in Tarrant Logic Inc.
Tarrant sells logistics software under multi-year contracts that bundle a licence with implementation and support services.
It reports under US GAAP with a December year end, and all amounts are in $ millions.
Mehta is reviewing the quality of Tarrant's 2025 financial reports before a portfolio review.

He begins by reading industry reports on logistics software and by working through Tarrant's revenue recognition and warranty notes, which he identifies as the areas requiring the most management judgment.
He then reads the proxy statement, which shows that the CEO's bonus doubles if annual revenue growth exceeds 8%, and the Form 4 filings, which show that the CFO sold 40% of her shares in November 2025.

Exhibit 1: Tarrant Logic, selected balance sheet data at 31 December ($ millions)

| Item | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Current assets | 820.0 | 905.0 |
| Property, plant, and equipment, net | 410.0 | 430.0 |
| Capitalized software development costs | 160.0 | 270.0 |
| Goodwill | 250.0 | 375.0 |
| Total assets | 1,640.0 | 1,980.0 |

Exhibit 2: Tarrant Logic, 2025 quarterly revenue ($ millions)

| Quarter | Licence revenue | Services revenue | Total revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 118.0 | 58.0 | 176.0 |
| Q2 | 124.0 | 61.0 | 185.0 |
| Q3 | 128.0 | 63.0 | 191.0 |
| Q4 | 150.0 | 50.0 | 200.0 |

On 1 October 2025, Tarrant signed a $32.0 million contract with a freight operator and recorded the full amount as Q4 licence revenue.
Mehta reads the contract and concludes that, on a standalone selling price basis, $20.0 million relates to the licence, delivered in October 2025, and $12.0 million relates to implementation and support services delivered evenly over the 12 months beginning 1 October 2025.
He finds no other misallocated contracts.

Exhibit 3: Tarrant Logic, selected income data ($ millions)

| Item | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | 612.0 | 690.0 | 752.0 |
| Warranty expense | 12.24 | 25.80 | 3.04 |
| Pre-tax income | 118.0 | 140.0 | 151.0 |

Warranty claims paid have run at 2.0% of revenue in every year since 2019, and management states that product quality has not changed.
In 2024, a year of strong demand, Tarrant raised its warranty accrual rate, explaining that it was being prudent.
In 2025, when demand softened, Tarrant released part of the warranty reserve into income, describing the 2024 accrual as more than was needed.
The auditor accepted both estimates as falling within a reasonable range.

Mehta's colleague, Grace Whitlow, reviews his file and comments: "Tarrant's reported pre-tax income rose again in 2025, so the business is still growing."

Mehta plans to finish his review by computing Beneish model indices for 2025.

## Questions

1. Tarrant's 2025 asset quality index (AQI) is closest to: (LOS: los-03-05-d)
   - A. 0.77.
   - B. 1.30.
   - C. 1.40.
2. After Mehta reallocates the freight operator contract, services revenue as a percentage of Tarrant's Q4 2025 total revenue is closest to: (LOS: los-03-05-h)
   - A. 26.5%.
   - B. 27.7%.
   - C. 31.0%.
3. If warranty expense in 2024 and 2025 is restated to the historical claims rate of 2.0% of reported revenue, the growth in Tarrant's 2025 pre-tax income is closest to: (LOS: los-03-05-b)
   - A. $-8.6\%$.
   - B. $-0.7\%$.
   - C. $+7.9\%$.
4. Mehta's review of the proxy statement and the Form 4 filings is most closely associated with which step in evaluating the quality of financial reports? (LOS: los-03-05-c)
   - A. Develop an understanding of the company and its industry.
   - B. Make comparisons with prior years and with peers.
   - C. Learn about management.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** The AQI numerator and denominator are each year's share of assets that are neither current assets nor PP&E.
> 2024: $1 - (410.0 + 820.0)/1{,}640.0 = 1 - 0.7500 = 0.2500$.
> 2025: $1 - (430.0 + 905.0)/1{,}980.0 = 1 - 0.6742 = 0.3258$.
> $AQI = 0.3258/0.2500 = 1.3030$; the rising share of soft assets (capitalized software and goodwill) is consistent with excessive capitalization of expenditures.
> A is wrong because it puts the prior year in the numerator ($0.2500/0.3258 = 0.7674$); AQI has the current year on top, unlike GMI and DEPI.
> C is wrong because it treats only capitalized software as soft assets and ignores goodwill ($(270.0/1{,}980.0)/(160.0/1{,}640.0) = 0.1364/0.0976 = 1.3977$); the formula captures every asset outside current assets and PP&E.
> 2. **B.** Only the licence portion of 20.0 belongs in Q4 licence revenue, so Q4 licence revenue becomes $150.0 - 32.0 + 20.0 = 138.0$.
> The services portion of 12.0 is earned evenly over 12 months, so Q4 recognizes $12.0 \times 3/12 = 3.0$ and services revenue becomes $50.0 + 3.0 = 53.0$.
> Corrected Q4 total revenue $= 138.0 + 53.0 = 191.0$, and the services share $= 53.0/191.0 = 27.75\%$.
> The reported Q4 share of 25.0% broke sharply from roughly 33.0% in each of Q1 to Q3 (for example, $63.0/191.0 = 32.98\%$ in Q3); an erratic licence and services mix under a multiple-deliverable policy is the cue to question the allocation.
> A is wrong because it adds the 3.0 of earned services but leaves Q4 total revenue at 200.0, ignoring the 9.0 of services revenue that must be deferred ($53.0/200.0 = 26.50\%$).
> C is wrong because it moves the whole 12.0 of services into Q4 services revenue with no deferral ($62.0/200.0 = 31.00\%$).
> 3. **A.** Normal warranty expense is $0.02 \times 690.0 = 13.80$ in 2024 and $0.02 \times 752.0 = 15.04$ in 2025.
> 2024 expense was overstated by $25.80 - 13.80 = 12.00$, and 2025 expense was understated by $15.04 - 3.04 = 12.00$.
> Restated pre-tax income: 2024 $= 140.0 + 12.0 = 152.0$; 2025 $= 151.0 - 12.0 = 139.0$.
> Growth $= 139.0/152.0 - 1 = -8.55\%$, so the reported increase is manufactured by smoothing: the reserve built in the strong year was released in the weak year, and Whitlow's conclusion does not hold.
> B is wrong because it removes the 2025 release but leaves the excess 2024 accrual in place ($139.0/140.0 - 1 = -0.71\%$); a timing choice affects both periods.
> C is wrong because it is the reported growth ($151.0/140.0 - 1 = +7.86\%$), which accepts the smoothed figures.
> 4. **C.** Evaluating management's incentives to misreport, including compensation tied to reported results and sales of shares by insiders, is the "learn about management" step.
> The incentive is live: reported revenue growth is $752.0/690.0 - 1 = 8.99\%$, just above the 8% bonus hurdle, while removing the 9.0 of deferred services revenue gives $743.0/690.0 - 1 = 7.68\%$, below it.
> A is wrong because understanding the company and industry concerns how the business makes money and which performance measures matter, which Mehta did from the industry reports.
> B is wrong because comparisons with prior years and peers concern changes in accounting policies and financial ratios, not management's pay or share sales.

## Distractor design

1. A: prior and current year inverted (wrong formula variant, confusion with GMI and DEPI); C: goodwill left out of soft assets (misread exhibit).
2. A: earned services added but deferred revenue left in the total (incomplete adjustment); C: services reclassified into Q4 without deferral (timing error).
3. B: only the release year adjusted (one-sided timing adjustment); C: reported figures accepted (smoothing missed).
4. A: company and industry step confused with management step (wrong step); B: comparison step confused with management step (wrong step).
