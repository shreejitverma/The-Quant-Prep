# Calder Fluid Systems Australian Subsidiary

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Calder Fluid Systems Inc. is a US manufacturer of industrial pumps that reports under US GAAP with the US dollar (USD) as its presentation currency.
On 1 January 2025, Calder formed Calder Pacific Pty Ltd in Perth to serve Australian mining customers.
Calder Pacific was capitalised with AUD2,800 thousand of common stock and a AUD2,200 thousand loan from an Australian bank, and it immediately bought plant and equipment for AUD3,200 thousand.

Calder Pacific sells pumps under contracts priced in Australian dollars (AUD), and its prices are set by competition with other Australian suppliers.
It pays its engineers and assembly staff in AUD, retains its operating cash in an Australian bank account, and services its own debt.
About 40% of its components are bought from Calder's US plant and invoiced in USD.

Nadia Okafor, CFA, the group reporting manager, is preparing the 2025 consolidation.
The subsidiary controller, Grant Ellery, sends her a note dated 12 January 2026.

Ellery: "Because a large share of our components is invoiced in US dollars, our functional currency should be the US dollar, and we should be remeasured with the temporal method."

To show management the effect of the designation, Okafor translates Calder Pacific's 2025 statements under both methods.
The AUD strengthened steadily against the USD during 2025.
Inventory is measured using FIFO, and depreciation is straight-line over ten years on plant acquired on 1 January 2025.
A dividend of AUD400 thousand was declared and paid on 1 October 2025.
The tax rate is 30%.

Management wants to report Calder Pacific's share of consolidated sales with the effect of the AUD's appreciation since the 1 January 2025 formation rate removed.

Okafor tells Ellery that she will present the current rate and temporal figures side by side to the audit committee, together with the translated fixed asset turnover and return on equity.

## Questions

1. The most appropriate response to Ellery's note is that Calder Pacific's functional currency is the: (LOS: los-03-03-a)
   - A. USD, so the temporal method applies, because the currency of a significant share of inputs determines the functional currency.
   - B. AUD, so the current rate method applies, because the currency that mainly influences sales prices takes priority among the indicators.
   - C. AUD, but the temporal method applies, because the subsidiary buys components from its parent.
2. If the AUD is the functional currency, the cumulative translation adjustment reported for Calder Pacific at 31 December 2025 is closest to (USD thousands): (LOS: los-03-03-e)
   - A. 160.0
   - B. 168.0
   - C. 195.3
3. If the USD is the functional currency, the remeasurement result included in Calder Pacific's 2025 translated net income is closest to (USD thousands): (LOS: los-03-03-e)
   - A. a loss of 20.6.
   - B. a loss of 59.9.
   - C. a gain of 2.5.
4. Compared with the current rate method, the temporal method would report Calder Pacific's 2025 translated net income and 31 December 2025 translated total equity as: (LOS: los-03-03-d)
   - A. higher net income and lower total equity.
   - B. lower net income and lower total equity.
   - C. higher net income and higher total equity.
5. If the USD is the functional currency, Calder Pacific's 2025 fixed asset turnover (sales divided by ending net plant and equipment) in USD is closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-f)
   - A. 3.19
   - B. 3.33
   - C. 3.49
6. With the effect of the AUD's appreciation since the formation rate removed, Calder Pacific's share of Calder's 2025 consolidated sales is closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-c)
   - A. 12.40%
   - B. 12.47%
   - C. 13.00%

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Calder Pacific prices in AUD against Australian competitors, pays its staff in AUD, keeps its cash in AUD and services its own debt, so it is a self-contained operation (V3 p.135, p.136).
> When indicators are mixed, the currency that mainly influences sales prices and the currency of the country whose competitive forces set those prices take priority, and both point to the AUD.
> A local functional currency means the current rate method with the adjustment in equity (V3 p.137).
> A is wrong because input currency is a secondary indicator and cannot override the sales-price indicators.
> C is wrong because the method follows the functional currency: an AUD functional currency cannot be remeasured with the temporal method.
> 2. **C.** Translate the income statement first: net income $1{,}176 \times 0.65 = 764.40$; dividend $400 \times 0.66 = 264.00$; retained earnings $764.40 - 264.00 = 500.40$.
> Assets $6{,}496 \times 0.68 = 4{,}417.28$; liabilities $(720 + 2{,}200) \times 0.68 = 1{,}985.60$; common stock $2{,}800 \times 0.62 = 1{,}736.00$.
> CTA $= 4{,}417.28 - 1{,}985.60 - 1{,}736.00 - 500.40 = 195.28$.
> Check by exposure: $2{,}800 \times (0.68 - 0.62) + 1{,}176 \times (0.68 - 0.65) - 400 \times (0.68 - 0.66) = 168.00 + 35.28 - 8.00 = 195.28$ (V3 p.134, p.145).
> A is wrong because it translates net income at the closing rate: retained earnings $1{,}176 \times 0.68 - 264 = 535.68$, CTA $160.00$.
> B is wrong because it translates retained earnings directly at the closing rate ($776 \times 0.68 = 527.68$), which leaves only the opening exposure $2{,}800 \times 0.06 = 168.00$.
> 3. **B.** Translate the balance sheet first.
> Monetary assets $(1{,}376 + 1{,}200) \times 0.68 = 1{,}751.68$; inventory $1{,}040 \times 0.67 = 696.80$; plant $2{,}880 \times 0.62 = 1{,}785.60$; total assets $4{,}234.08$.
> Retained earnings plug $4{,}234.08 - 1{,}985.60 - 1{,}736.00 = 512.48$, so net income must be $512.48 + 264.00 = 776.48$.
> Income before remeasurement: sales $6{,}240.00$ less COGS $6{,}240 \times 0.64 = 3{,}993.60$, selling and administrative $769.60$, depreciation $320 \times 0.62 = 198.40$, interest $114.40$, tax $327.60$, giving $836.40$.
> Remeasurement result $776.48 - 836.40 = -59.92$, a loss, consistent with a net monetary liability position and a strengthening AUD (V3 p.139, p.145).
> A is wrong because it multiplies the ending net monetary liability $(1{,}376 + 1{,}200 - 720 - 2{,}200 = -344)$ by the full-year rate change $(0.06)$, ignoring when each balance arose.
> C is wrong because it translates COGS at the average rate: income before remeasurement becomes $774.00$ and the plug-based result flips to a gain of $2.48$.
> 4. **A.** Temporal net income is $776.48$ against $764.40$ under the current rate method, because COGS and depreciation carry the older, lower rates and more than offset the $59.92$ remeasurement loss.
> Temporal equity is $1{,}736.00 + 512.48 = 2{,}248.48$ against $1{,}736.00 + 500.40 + 195.28 = 2{,}431.68$, lower because the temporal method has no positive CTA and carries plant and inventory at historical rates (V3 p.147, p.157).
> B is wrong because it assumes the textbook pattern that a translation loss always leaves temporal net income below the current rate figure; here income before remeasurement is high enough to reverse that.
> C is wrong because equity under the temporal method is lower when the foreign currency strengthens and nonmonetary assets are held at historical rates.
> 5. **C.** Temporal method: sales at the average rate $6{,}240.00$; plant at its historical rate $1{,}785.60$; turnover $6{,}240.00 / 1{,}785.60 = 3.4946$ (V3 p.149).
> A is wrong because it uses the current rate method's closing rate for plant: $6{,}240.00 / (2{,}880 \times 0.68) = 6{,}240.00 / 1{,}958.40 = 3.1863$.
> B is wrong because it reports the AUD ratio $9{,}600 / 2{,}880 = 3.3333$, assuming a mixed ratio is preserved on translation.
> 6. **B.** Sales are translated at the average rate, so reported Calder Pacific sales are $9{,}600 \times 0.65 = 6{,}240$; at the formation rate they would be $9{,}600 \times 0.62 = 5{,}952$, a currency effect of $288$ (V3 p.140, p.166).
> Removing the effect from both the segment and the consolidated total: $5{,}952 / (48{,}000 - 288) = 5{,}952 / 47{,}712 = 12.47\%$.
> A is wrong because it removes the currency effect from the segment but not from the consolidated total: $5{,}952 / 48{,}000 = 12.40\%$.
> C is wrong because it is the reported share with the currency effect still included: $6{,}240 / 48{,}000 = 13.00\%$.

## Distractor design

- Q1: A lets a secondary indicator (input currency) override the sales-price indicators; C breaks the link between functional currency and method.
- Q2: A translates net income at the closing rate instead of the average rate; B translates retained earnings directly at the closing rate instead of rolling it forward.
- Q3: A uses ending net monetary position times the full-year rate change (timing error); C uses the average rate for COGS instead of the historical inventory rate.
- Q4: B assumes the Canadaco pattern without checking income before remeasurement; C gets the equity direction wrong for a strengthening currency under the temporal method.
- Q5: A applies the current rate method's closing rate to plant; B assumes a mixed ratio equals the local currency ratio.
- Q6: A removes the currency effect from the numerator only; C leaves the currency effect in (reported share); the 0.70 budget rate is a decoy.
