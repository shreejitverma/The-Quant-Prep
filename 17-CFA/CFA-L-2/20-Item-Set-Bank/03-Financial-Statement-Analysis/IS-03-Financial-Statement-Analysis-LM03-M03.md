# Valtara Industrie Brazilian Subsidiary

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Valtara Industrie SE is a German maker of agricultural irrigation controls that reports under IFRS with the euro (EUR) as its functional and presentation currency.
Its fiscal year ends on 31 December.
Lena Brandauer, CFA, a credit analyst at Oberkamp Bank, is reviewing Valtara's 2025 statements before renewing a revolving credit facility.

In late 2025 Valtara entered into two US dollar (USD) transactions that were still open at year end.
On 1 November 2025 it sold controllers to a Texas distributor for USD2,400,000, collectable on 15 February 2026.
On 1 December 2025 it bought circuit boards from a California supplier for USD1,500,000, payable on 20 January 2026.
Valtara did not hedge either position.

**Exhibit 1: Exchange rates (EUR per USD)**

| Date or period | Rate |
|---|---|
| 1 November 2025 | 0.9200 |
| 1 December 2025 | 0.9350 |
| Average, fourth quarter 2025 | 0.9300 |
| 31 December 2025 | 0.9500 |

Valtara's only foreign subsidiary, Valtara do Brasil Ltda, was formed years ago when the parent subscribed BRL40,000 thousand of common stock at EUR0.2000 per BRL and the subsidiary bought its plant.
It prices, produces and borrows in Brazilian reais (BRL) and operates autonomously, so the BRL is its functional currency and Valtara applies the current rate method.
At 31 December 2024 the subsidiary's net assets were BRL60,000 thousand, its translated retained earnings were EUR3,350 thousand and the cumulative translation adjustment (CTA) was a debit balance of EUR1,150 thousand.
In 2025 it earned net income of BRL9,000 thousand on sales of BRL120,000 thousand, and on 30 September 2025 it declared and paid a dividend of BRL3,000 thousand.
The BRL weakened against the EUR throughout 2025.

**Exhibit 2: Valtara do Brasil balance sheet, 31 December 2025 (BRL thousands)**

| Assets | | Liabilities and equity | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | 14,000 | Accounts payable | 16,000 |
| Accounts receivable | 20,000 | Bank loan | 68,000 |
| Inventory (FIFO) | 26,000 | Common stock | 40,000 |
| Plant, net | 90,000 | Retained earnings | 26,000 |
| Total | 150,000 | Total | 150,000 |

**Exhibit 3: Exchange rates (EUR per BRL)**

| Date or item | Rate |
|---|---|
| Common stock issued, plant acquired | 0.2000 |
| 31 December 2024 | 0.1700 |
| Average 2025 | 0.1620 |
| Inventory on hand at 31 December 2025 | 0.1580 |
| 30 September 2025 (dividend) | 0.1600 |
| 31 December 2025 | 0.1550 |

Brandauer's covenant model uses the subsidiary's net profit margin and its return on assets, defined as net income divided by ending total assets, both measured in EUR.

On 9 February 2026 Brandauer meets Valtara's treasurer, Jonas Ferreira.
Ferreira says the board is debating whether the subsidiary's growing reliance on components invoiced by the parent should lead to a change of functional currency to the EUR.

Ferreira: "If we had used the temporal method in 2025, the weaker real would have hurt us twice: a remeasurement loss would have hit net income, and our translated assets would have been smaller."

## Questions

1. The total foreign currency transaction result from the two USD positions that Valtara recognizes in its 2025 net income is closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-b)
   - A. a gain of EUR27,000.
   - B. a gain of EUR49,500.
   - C. a gain of EUR94,500.
2. The cumulative translation adjustment for Valtara do Brasil reported in Valtara's equity at 31 December 2025 is closest to (EUR thousands): (LOS: los-03-03-e)
   - A. -948
   - B. -2,140
   - C. -2,098
3. Valtara do Brasil's 2025 net profit margin and return on assets in Brandauer's EUR-based covenant model are closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-f)
   - A. 7.50% and 6.27%.
   - B. 7.50% and 6.00%.
   - C. 7.18% and 6.00%.
4. Relative to the current rate method, had Valtara applied the temporal method to Valtara do Brasil in 2025, it would most likely have reported: (LOS: los-03-03-d)
   - A. a remeasurement gain in net income and higher translated total assets.
   - B. a remeasurement loss in net income and lower translated total assets.
   - C. a remeasurement gain in net income and lower translated total assets.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Each transaction is recorded at the spot rate on its own date, and only the open receivable or payable is remeasured at the balance sheet date, with the result in net income even though unrealized (V3 p.119, p.120, p.121).
> Receivable (asset exposure, USD strengthened): $2{,}400{,}000 \times (0.9500 - 0.9200) = 72{,}000$ gain.
> Payable (liability exposure, USD strengthened): $-1{,}500{,}000 \times (0.9500 - 0.9350) = -22{,}500$ loss.
> Net result $72{,}000 - 22{,}500 = 49{,}500$ gain.
> A is wrong because it nets the two positions into a USD900,000 asset and remeasures it from the 1 November rate: $900{,}000 \times 0.0300 = 27{,}000$, ignoring that the payable arose on 1 December at 0.9350.
> C is wrong because it gives the payable the receivable's sign, treating a stronger USD as a gain on a liability: $72{,}000 + 22{,}500 = 94{,}500$.
> 2. **C.** Current rate method: translate the income statement and dividends first, roll retained earnings forward, then take the CTA as the balance sheet plug (V3 p.145).
> Retained earnings: $3{,}350 + 9{,}000 \times 0.1620 - 3{,}000 \times 0.1600 = 3{,}350 + 1{,}458 - 480 = 4{,}328$.
> Net assets: $(150{,}000 - 16{,}000 - 68{,}000) \times 0.1550 = 66{,}000 \times 0.1550 = 10{,}230$; common stock $40{,}000 \times 0.2000 = 8{,}000$.
> CTA $= 10{,}230 - 8{,}000 - 4{,}328 = -2{,}098$.
> Check by exposure: $60{,}000 \times (0.1550 - 0.1700) + 9{,}000 \times (0.1550 - 0.1620) - 3{,}000 \times (0.1550 - 0.1600) = -900 - 63 + 15 = -948$ for the year, added to the opening $-1{,}150$ gives $-2{,}098$ (V3 p.134).
> A is wrong because it reports only the 2025 adjustment of $-948$, omitting the opening cumulative balance of $-1{,}150$.
> B is wrong because it applies the full-year rate change to ending net assets, $-1{,}150 + 66{,}000 \times (0.1550 - 0.1700) = -2{,}140$, ignoring that income accrued at the average rate and the dividend left at the 30 September rate.
> 3. **A.** Under the current rate method every income statement item is at the average rate, so a pure income statement ratio is preserved: net profit margin $9{,}000 / 120{,}000 = 7.50\%$ (V3 p.149).
> Return on assets mixes an average-rate numerator with a closing-rate denominator: $9{,}000 \times 0.1620 / (150{,}000 \times 0.1550) = 1{,}458 / 23{,}250 = 6.27\%$, which is the BRL ratio of $6.00\%$ scaled by $0.1620 / 0.1550 = 1.0452$.
> With a weakening BRL the closing rate is below the average rate, so translated turnover and return ratios rise above their local currency values.
> B is wrong because it assumes a mixed ratio is preserved on translation, reporting the BRL return on assets of $6.00\%$.
> C is wrong because it translates net income at the closing rate while sales stay at the average rate: margin $1{,}395 / 19{,}440 = 7.18\%$ and return on assets $1{,}395 / 23{,}250 = 6.00\%$.
> 4. **A.** Temporal exposure is net monetary assets: $14{,}000 + 20{,}000 - 16{,}000 - 68{,}000 = -50{,}000$, a net monetary liability (V3 p.139).
> A weakening BRL shrinks the EUR value of that net liability, so the temporal method produces a remeasurement gain in net income (V3 p.134, p.157).
> Inventory at its historical rate 0.1580 and plant at 0.2000 both exceed the 0.1550 closing rate, so temporal total assets are $34{,}000 \times 0.1550 + 26{,}000 \times 0.1580 + 90{,}000 \times 0.2000 = 5{,}270 + 4{,}108 + 18{,}000 = 27{,}378$ against $150{,}000 \times 0.1550 = 23{,}250$ under the current rate method (V3 p.153).
> Ferreira is therefore wrong on both counts.
> B is wrong because it carries the current rate method's net asset exposure (a weaker BRL hurts) over to the temporal method, whose exposure is a net monetary liability.
> C is wrong because it assumes historical rates are lower than the closing rate, which holds only when the foreign currency has strengthened.

## Distractor design

- Q1: A nets two positions that arose on different dates into one exposure measured from the earlier rate (period error); C applies the receivable sign to the payable (sign error); the fourth-quarter average rate is a decoy.
- Q2: A reports the period adjustment as the cumulative balance (omits the opening CTA); B applies the full-year rate change to ending net assets (wrong formula variant, timing).
- Q3: B assumes a mixed ratio is preserved under the current rate method; C translates net income at the closing rate instead of the average rate (spot vs average).
- Q4: B imports the current rate method's net asset exposure into the temporal method; C reverses the ordering of historical and closing rates for a weakening currency.
