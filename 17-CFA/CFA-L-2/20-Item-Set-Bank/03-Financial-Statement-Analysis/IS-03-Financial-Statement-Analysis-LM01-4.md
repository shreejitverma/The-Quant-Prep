# Kestrel Retail Group: Covenant Review of Off-Balance-Sheet Structures

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Tomasz Wierzbicki, CFA, works in the credit risk team of a bank that lends to Kestrel Retail Group plc, a UK consumer electronics retailer that reports under IFRS.
Kestrel's revolving credit agreement requires total liabilities to total equity of no more than 1.60, measured on the IFRS consolidated statements.
Wierzbicki is reviewing three arrangements that Kestrel's treasurer described in a compliance certificate.
All amounts are in £ millions.

Exhibit 1: Kestrel consolidated balance sheet before the receivables transaction

| Assets | | Liabilities and equity | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | 80 | Current liabilities | 350 |
| Trade receivables | 300 | Long-term debt | 450 |
| Inventory | 420 | Shareholders' equity | 700 |
| Other non-current assets | 700 | | |
| Total | 1,500 | Total | 1,500 |

Arrangement 1, a receivables trust.
On 31 December 2026 Kestrel sold its 300 of trade receivables to Harrow Receivables Trust for 310 in cash.
The trust was created for this purpose only.
An unrelated investor contributed 30 for all of the trust's voting equity, and the trust borrowed 280 from banks.
Kestrel holds no equity in the trust, services the receivables, and has guaranteed the trust's bank debt, so it absorbs any credit losses on the receivables.
The treasurer's certificate shows the receivables removed, the cash received, and a gain of 10 on the sale.
The certificate states that the transaction improves Kestrel's covenant headroom.

Arrangement 2, a minority stake.
Kestrel owns 16% of the voting shares of Wren Logistics and holds warrants, exercisable at any time, that would raise its stake to 24%.
Kestrel has no board seat and no other involvement with Wren.

Arrangement 3, an associate.
Kestrel has owned 35% of Brightline Components since 2021 and accounts for it using the equity method.
The investment had a carrying amount of 245 on 1 January 2026, and amortization of the excess purchase price is 4 per year.
Brightline reported 2026 net income of 80 and paid dividends of 30.
At 31 December 2025, Brightline held inventory bought from Kestrel carrying 12 of unrealized intra-group profit in total, before applying Kestrel's ownership share; Brightline sold all of it to outside customers during 2026.
During 2026 Kestrel sold further inventory costing 60 to Brightline for 90, and one-third of those goods remained in Brightline's inventory at 31 December 2026.

On 31 December 2026, after recording its 2026 share of Brightline's results and dividends, Kestrel sold 20 percentage points of Brightline for cash of 160.
It retained a 15% stake, lost significant influence, and measured the retained stake at its fair value of 120.

Wierzbicki's colleague, Aisha Rahman, notes that a US GAAP lender had asked how the same arrangements would be assessed by a company reporting under US GAAP.
She believes the treatment of the trust and of the Wren warrants may differ between the two frameworks.

## Questions

1. Regarding Harrow Receivables Trust, which statement is most accurate? (LOS: los-03-01-b)
   - A. Kestrel keeps the trust off its balance sheet because it holds none of the trust's voting equity.
   - B. Kestrel consolidates the trust under IFRS, but a US GAAP reporter would keep it off balance sheet as a qualifying special purpose entity.
   - C. Kestrel consolidates the trust under IFRS, and a US GAAP reporter would consolidate it as the primary beneficiary of a variable interest entity.
2. After properly reflecting Arrangement 1, Kestrel's consolidated total assets at 31 December 2026 and the gain on the receivables sale in its consolidated income statement are closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-c)
   - A. total assets 1,510 and gain 10.
   - B. total assets 1,810 and gain 0.
   - C. total assets 1,810 and gain 10.
3. Regarding Wren Logistics, the most likely treatment by Kestrel and by a company in the same position reporting under US GAAP is: (LOS: los-03-01-b)
   - A. equity method for Kestrel; financial asset for the US GAAP reporter.
   - B. equity method for both.
   - C. financial asset for both.
4. Kestrel's 2026 equity income from Brightline is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-a)
   - A. 20.5.
   - B. 24.7.
   - C. 26.0.
5. The gain Kestrel recognizes in profit or loss on losing significant influence over Brightline is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-a)
   - A. 10.3.
   - B. 11.9.
   - C. 20.8.
6. After properly reflecting Arrangement 1, Kestrel's covenant ratio of total liabilities to total equity at 31 December 2026, before the effects of Arrangements 2 and 3, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-c)
   - A. 1.13.
   - B. 1.48.
   - C. 1.54.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** Under IFRS 10, control means power over the entity plus exposure or rights to variable returns; Kestrel services the receivables and absorbs their credit losses through its guarantee, so it controls the trust despite holding no votes.
> Under US GAAP the trust's equity at risk (30 against 310 of assets) is insufficient and its equity holder does not absorb losses, so it is a variable interest entity, and Kestrel, which absorbs the majority of expected losses, is the primary beneficiary and consolidates.
> A is wrong because consolidation follows economic control and exposure to losses, not voting interest.
> B is wrong because the qualifying special purpose entity exemption has been eliminated under US GAAP and never existed under IFRS.
> 2. **B.** On consolidation the intra-group sale is eliminated: the receivables stay at 300, the gain of $310 - 300 = 10$ is reversed, and the trust's 280 bank debt and the investor's 30 of equity (presented as non-controlling interest) come onto the group statements.
> Cash $= 80 + 310 = 390$, because the trust paid out all 310 it raised.
> Total assets $= 390 + 300 + 420 + 700 = 1{,}810$; liabilities and equity $= 350 + (450 + 280) + (700 + 30) = 1{,}810$.
> A is wrong because it is the treasurer's derecognition view: receivables removed and cash added ($1{,}500 - 300 + 310 = 1{,}510$) with the gain retained.
> C is wrong because it consolidates the trust but keeps the gain on a sale made to a consolidated entity.
> 3. **A.** IFRS considers currently exercisable potential voting rights when assessing significant influence, so Kestrel's effective 24% gives presumed significant influence and the equity method.
> US GAAP considers only voting shares outstanding, so 16% with no board seat or other indicators leaves the US GAAP reporter with a financial asset.
> B is wrong because it applies the IFRS potential voting rights rule to the US GAAP reporter.
> C is wrong because it ignores the exercisable warrants under IFRS.
> 4. **B.** Unrealized profit on the 2026 downstream sale $= (90 - 60) \times \tfrac{1}{3} = 10.0$; Kestrel's elimination $= 0.35 \times 10.0 = 3.5$.
> The 2025 deferral realized in 2026 $= 0.35 \times 12 = 4.2$.
> Equity income $= 0.35 \times 80 - 4 - 3.5 + 4.2 = 28.0 - 4 - 3.5 + 4.2 = 24.7$.
> A is wrong because it omits the add-back of the prior-year deferral that is realized in 2026 ($28.0 - 4 - 3.5 = 20.5$).
> C is wrong because it eliminates and restores 100% of the intra-group profit rather than the 35% ownership share ($28.0 - 4 - 10.0 + 12 = 26.0$).
> 5. **C.** Carrying amount at 31 December 2026 $= 245 + 24.7 - 0.35 \times 30 = 245 + 24.7 - 10.5 = 259.2$.
> Gain $= (\text{proceeds} + \text{fair value of retained stake}) - \text{carrying amount} = (160 + 120) - 259.2 = 20.8$.
> A is wrong because it does not deduct the 10.5 share of dividends from the carrying amount ($280 - (245 + 24.7) = 10.3$).
> B is wrong because it computes the gain only on the portion sold and leaves the retained 15% at its old carrying amount instead of remeasuring it to fair value ($160 - \tfrac{20}{35} \times 259.2 = 160 - 148.1 = 11.9$).
> 6. **B.** Consolidated liabilities $= 350 + 450 + 280 = 1{,}080$; consolidated equity $= 700 + 30 = 730$, including the non-controlling interest and without the reversed gain.
> Ratio $= 1{,}080 / 730 = 1.4795$, within the 1.60 covenant, although much higher than the certificate implies.
> A is wrong because it is the treasurer's derecognition view ($800 / (700 + 10) = 1.1268$).
> C is wrong because it leaves the non-controlling interest out of total equity ($1{,}080 / 700 = 1.5429$).

## Distractor design

1. A: voting interest used as the control test; B: obsolete US GAAP qualifying SPE exemption invoked (IFRS vs US GAAP).
2. A: derecognition treated as a true sale (off-balance-sheet view); C: gain on an intra-group sale left in consolidated income.
3. B: IFRS potential voting rights rule applied under US GAAP (IFRS vs US GAAP); C: exercisable warrants ignored under IFRS.
4. A: prior-period deferred profit not realized (period error); C: 100% rather than ownership share eliminated and restored.
5. A: dividends omitted from the carrying amount; B: retained interest not remeasured to fair value.
6. A: derecognition view with the gain retained; C: NCI excluded from equity (misclassified presentation).
