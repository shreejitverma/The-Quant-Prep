# Orrin Photonics Equity Award Accounting

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Hannah Okafor, CFA, is a credit and equity analyst at Brightwater Advisors.
She is reviewing Orrin Photonics Inc., a US maker of laser components that reports under US GAAP and pays no dividends.
Orrin relies heavily on equity awards to retain its engineers, and Okafor wants to reconcile the FY2025 share-based compensation note to the income statement, the tax line, and the share count.

All Orrin RSUs are equity-settled and vest one third on 31 December of each of the three years after the grant, with shares delivered on the vesting date.
Orrin recognises the grant-date fair value of each grant straight-line over three years and has experienced no forfeitures.
The option grant is also equity-settled and cliff-vests on 31 December 2026.

**Exhibit 1: Orrin Photonics equity award grants**

| Grant date | Award | Number granted | Grant-date fair value per award (USD) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 January 2023 | RSUs | 2,400,000 | 52.00 |
| 1 January 2024 | RSUs | 1,800,000 | 64.00 |
| 1 January 2025 | RSUs | 2,100,000 | 78.00 |
| 1 January 2024 | At-the-money options, strike USD 64.00, 7-year life | 3,000,000 | 21.50 |

**Exhibit 2: Selected FY2025 data**

| Item | 31 Dec 2024 | 31 Dec 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Unvested RSUs (millions) | 2.0 | 2.0 |
| Unrecognised RSU expense (USD millions) | 118.4 | 147.6 |
| Unrecognised option expense (USD millions) | 43.0 | 21.5 |
| Share price at year end (USD) | 70.00 | 91.00 |

Additional FY2025 information: weighted average basic shares outstanding 148,000,000; net income positive; average share price USD 84.00; statutory income tax rate 21%.
Orrin's market capitalisation at 31 December 2025 was about USD 13.5 billion.

Orrin also pays its engineers an annual cash bonus.
The FY2025 bonus of USD 38 million was approved by the board in December 2025 and paid on 15 February 2026.

Okafor's associate, Leon Marsh, prepares two scenarios for discussion.
In the first, he asks how the FY2025 figures would differ if Orrin's share price had fallen steadily after 1 January 2025, to an FY2025 average of USD 45.00 and a year-end price of USD 40.00, with all other data unchanged.
In the second, he assumes that during 2027, after the options have vested, employees exercise 1,200,000 options when the share price is USD 110.00.

Marsh also notes that a peer of Orrin reporting under IFRS showed an effective tax rate close to the statutory rate in FY2025 despite a similar share price rise, and asks Okafor whether the difference reflects the accounting standards or the peer's award mix.

## Questions

1. Orrin's share-based compensation expense for FY2025 is closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-b)
   - A. USD 134.6 million.
   - B. USD 156.1 million.
   - C. USD 212.6 million.
2. The effect of the RSUs that vested in FY2025 on Orrin's FY2025 income tax expense is closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-b)
   - A. a reduction of USD 11.9 million.
   - B. a reduction of USD 40.1 million.
   - C. no effect, with USD 11.9 million recognised directly in equity.
3. Using the treasury stock method, Orrin's FY2025 diluted weighted average shares outstanding are closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-b)
   - A. 148.75 million.
   - B. 150.71 million.
   - C. 153.00 million.
4. Under Marsh's first scenario, which is most accurate about Orrin's FY2025 share-based compensation expense and diluted shares? (LOS: los-03-02-b)
   - A. Expense unchanged; diluted shares 145.06 million.
   - B. Expense lower; diluted shares 148.00 million.
   - C. Expense unchanged; diluted shares 148.00 million.
5. Under Marsh's second scenario, the 2027 exercise increases Orrin's cash flow from financing activities and its paid-in capital, respectively, by closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-b)
   - A. USD 132.0 million and USD 157.8 million.
   - B. USD 76.8 million and USD 76.8 million.
   - C. USD 76.8 million and USD 102.6 million.
6. Which statement best contrasts Orrin's accounting for the FY2025 cash bonus with its accounting for the 1 January 2025 RSU grant? (LOS: los-03-02-a)
   - A. The bonus is expensed in FY2026 when it is paid, while the RSU grant is expensed over three years at grant-date fair value with a credit to equity.
   - B. Both are credited to liabilities and remeasured at the year-end share price or payment amount until settled.
   - C. The bonus is expensed in FY2025 at its undiscounted amount with a credit to accrued compensation, while the RSU grant is expensed over three years at grant-date fair value with a credit to equity.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Each grant's aggregate grant-date fair value is spread straight-line over three years, and overlapping grants stack.
> 2023 RSUs: $2.4 \times 52.00 / 3 = 41.6$; 2024 RSUs: $1.8 \times 64.00 / 3 = 38.4$; 2025 RSUs: $2.1 \times 78.00 / 3 = 54.6$; options: $3.0 \times 21.50 / 3 = 21.5$ (USD millions).
> Total $= 41.6 + 38.4 + 54.6 + 21.5 = 156.1$.
> A is wrong because it omits the options, as under pre-2005 intrinsic value accounting, where an at-the-money option has zero intrinsic value and therefore zero expense: $41.6 + 38.4 + 54.6 = 134.6$.
> C is wrong because it remeasures every RSU grant at the USD 91.00 year-end price: $(2.4 + 1.8 + 2.1) \times 91.00 / 3 + 21.5 = 191.1 + 21.5 = 212.6$; grant-date fair value is fixed and never remeasured.
> 2. **A.** RSUs vesting on 31 December 2025: 800,000 (2023 grant), 600,000 (2024 grant), and 700,000 (2025 grant), a total of 2.1 million.
> Tax deduction at settlement $= 2.1 \times 91.00 = 191.1$; cumulative book expense at grant-date values $= 0.8 \times 52.00 + 0.6 \times 64.00 + 0.7 \times 78.00 = 41.6 + 38.4 + 54.6 = 134.6$.
> Windfall $= 21\% \times (191.1 - 134.6) = 21\% \times 56.5 = 11.8650$, which reduces income tax expense under US GAAP.
> B is wrong because it applies the tax rate to the whole deduction ($21\% \times 191.1 = 40.1310$) without subtracting the expense already deducted for book purposes.
> C is wrong because it is the IFRS treatment, which books the windfall in equity; Orrin reports under US GAAP.
> 3. **A.** RSUs: average unrecognised expense $= (118.4 + 147.6)/2 = 133.0$; assumed repurchases $= 133.0 / 84.00 = 1.583333$ million; dilutive shares $= 2.0 - 1.583333 = 0.416667$ million.
> Options: average unrecognised expense $= (43.0 + 21.5)/2 = 32.25$; assumed proceeds $= 3.0 \times 64.00 + 32.25 = 224.25$; repurchases $= 224.25 / 84.00 = 2.669643$ million; dilutive shares $= 3.0 - 2.669643 = 0.330357$ million.
> Diluted shares $= 148.0 + 0.416667 + 0.330357 = 148.747024$ million, or 148.75 million.
> B is wrong because it leaves the unrecognised expense out of assumed proceeds: RSUs add the full 2.0 million and options add $3.0 - 192.0/84.00 = 0.714286$, giving $150.714286$.
> C is wrong because it adds the gross 5.0 million awards with no repurchase offset.
> 4. **C.** Grant-date fair value is measured once, so a share price fall after the grant leaves the FY2025 expense at 156.1.
> RSUs: repurchases $= 133.0 / 45.00 = 2.955556$ million exceed the 2.0 million awards, so net dilution is $-0.955556$ million and the RSUs are anti-dilutive (zero).
> Options: the USD 64.00 strike exceeds the USD 45.00 average price, and $3.0 - 224.25/45.00 = -1.983333$ million, so they are anti-dilutive (zero).
> Diluted shares therefore equal basic shares of 148.00 million.
> A is wrong because it counts anti-dilutive instruments as negative dilution: $148.0 - 0.955556 - 1.983333 = 145.0611$.
> B is wrong because it remeasures existing grants for the price fall.
> 5. **C.** Financing inflow $=$ options exercised $\times$ strike $= 1.2 \times 64.00 = 76.8$.
> Reserve transferred $= (1.2 / 3.0) \times (3.0 \times 21.50) = 0.4 \times 64.5 = 25.8$; paid-in capital increases by $76.8 + 25.8 = 102.6$.
> The USD 110.00 share price explains why exercise occurred but is not booked.
> A is wrong because it uses the exercise-date share price instead of the strike: $1.2 \times 110.00 = 132.0$ and $132.0 + 25.8 = 157.8$.
> B is wrong because it omits the transfer of 25.8 from the share-based compensation reserve to paid-in capital.
> 6. **C.** Both follow the rule that compensation is expensed as the employee provides service.
> The bonus is a short-term benefit earned in FY2025, measured undiscounted, credited to accrued compensation, and settled in operating cash flow in February 2026.
> The RSU grant is measured at grant-date fair value, credited to the share-based compensation reserve in equity, expensed over the three-year vesting period, and settled in shares with no operating cash flow.
> A is wrong because it applies cash-basis timing to the bonus; the service was provided in FY2025.
> B is wrong because it applies liability and remeasurement accounting, which fits cash-settled awards, to equity-settled RSUs.

## Distractor design

1. A applies intrinsic value measurement to at-the-money options (wrong measurement basis); C remeasures grants at the year-end price (spot instead of grant date).
2. B taxes the full deduction instead of the excess over book expense; C applies the IFRS treatment to a US GAAP reporter.
3. B omits unrecognised expense from assumed proceeds (wrong formula variant); C adds gross awards with no treasury stock offset.
4. A subtracts anti-dilutive instruments as negative dilution (sign); B remeasures grants after a price move.
5. A uses the exercise-date price instead of the strike (misread exhibit); B omits the reserve-to-paid-in-capital transfer.
6. A uses cash-basis timing for a short-term benefit; B confuses equity-settled with cash-settled awards.
