# Halvorsen Foods Growth and Currency Forecast

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Diego Marchetti, CFA, is an equity analyst at Larkspur Capital, a long-only asset manager.
He is building a 2026 earnings forecast for Halvorsen Foods Inc., a US packaged-food company that reports under US GAAP in US dollars (USD).
Halvorsen operates through four segments: United States, Eurozone, Mexico and Japan.
Each foreign subsidiary uses its local currency as its functional currency.

Halvorsen de Mexico produces snacks in Mexico with peso-denominated labour and raw material costs.
It sells about 40% of its output to US retailers under contracts priced in USD and the rest in Mexico in pesos at prices set by Mexican competition.
The Eurozone and Japan subsidiaries both produce and sell locally.
The US plants buy flavourings from a Swiss supplier under contracts priced in Swiss francs (CHF).

Eurozone segment sales were EUR1,250 million in 2025, and Marchetti forecasts EUR1,310 million for 2026.
The 2025 average rate was USD1.1340 per EUR, and his base case holds that rate for 2026.

**Exhibit 1: 2025 sales growth by segment (%)**

| Segment | Volume | Price and mix | Foreign exchange | Acquisitions | Reported growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | 2.0 | 3.0 | 0.0 | 4.0 | 9.0 |
| Eurozone | 1.0 | 2.5 | 5.0 | 0.0 | 8.5 |
| Mexico | 6.0 | 4.0 | -7.0 | 0.0 | 3.0 |
| Japan | -1.0 | 2.0 | -3.0 | 0.0 | -2.0 |

Halvorsen's 2024 consolidated sales were USD6,500 million and its 2025 consolidated sales grew 6.4% as reported.
A footnote states that currency movements reduced 2025 consolidated sales by USD26 million and that the March 2025 acquisition of Brava Snacks contributed USD117 million.

**Exhibit 2: Sensitivity of 2026 operating profit to a 10% strengthening of the USD (USD millions)**

| Currency | Effect on operating profit |
|---|---|
| EUR | -24 |
| MXN | +9 |
| JPY | -4 |
| CHF | +7 |

Halvorsen states that the effects are approximately linear and symmetric for moves of up to 10%.

**Exhibit 3: Marchetti's 2026 pretax profit forecast by jurisdiction**

| Jurisdiction | Pretax profit (USD millions) | Statutory tax rate |
|---|---|---|
| United States | 600 | 21% |
| Eurozone | 240 | 25% |
| Mexico | 130 | 30% |
| Japan | 30 | 30% |

Marchetti's colleague, Keiko Tanabe, reviews the model on 20 January 2026 and makes two comments.

Tanabe: "Because Halvorsen de Mexico is translated with the current rate method, its operating margin in dollars will be the same as its operating margin in pesos, so currency moves cannot change that margin."

Tanabe: "Your stress scenario should have the dollar strengthen 10% against the euro and the peso while weakening 10% against the Swiss franc, with the yen unchanged."

Marchetti also wants a downside case in which the euro depreciates 8% against the dollar from the base-case rate in 2026.
He plans to value Halvorsen on sustainable growth, so he wants to separate growth management controls from growth driven by currency and acquisitions.

## Questions

1. Under Marchetti's downside case, Eurozone segment sales translated into USD for 2026 are closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-c)
   - A. USD1,304.1 million
   - B. USD1,366.7 million
   - C. USD1,375.5 million
2. Based on Exhibit 1, the segment with the most sustainable 2025 sales growth is: (LOS: los-03-03-i)
   - A. United States.
   - B. Eurozone.
   - C. Mexico.
3. Halvorsen's 2025 consolidated organic sales growth is closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-i)
   - A. 4.2%
   - B. 5.0%
   - C. 6.8%
4. Using Exhibit 2, the effect of Tanabe's stress scenario on Halvorsen's 2026 operating profit is closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-j)
   - A. -USD8 million
   - B. -USD22 million
   - C. -USD26 million
5. Regarding Tanabe's first comment, the currency movement most likely to reduce the Mexico segment's operating margin as reported in USD is: (LOS: los-03-03-j)
   - A. an appreciation of the peso against the USD.
   - B. a depreciation of the peso against the USD.
   - C. neither, because the current rate method preserves income statement ratios.
6. Based on Exhibit 3, Halvorsen's forecast 2026 effective tax rate is closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-h)
   - A. 21.0%
   - B. 23.4%
   - C. 26.5%

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Sales are translated at the average rate, so a weaker euro lowers translated sales (V3 p.140, p.166).
> An 8% euro depreciation from USD1.1340 per EUR gives $1.1340 \times 0.92 = 1.04328$; translated sales are $1{,}310 \times 1.04328 = 1{,}366.70$, down $118.84$ from the base case of $1{,}485.54$.
> A is wrong because it applies the downside rate to 2025 local sales instead of the 2026 forecast: $1{,}250 \times 1.04328 = 1{,}304.10$.
> C is wrong because it treats the move as an 8% dollar appreciation, $1.1340 / 1.08 = 1.05$, which is only a 7.41% euro depreciation: $1{,}310 \times 1.05 = 1{,}375.50$.
> 2. **C.** Sustainable growth is volume plus price and mix, the components management controls (V3 p.172, p.173).
> Organic growth: United States $2.0 + 3.0 = 5.0\%$, Eurozone $1.0 + 2.5 = 3.5\%$, Mexico $6.0 + 4.0 = 10.0\%$, Japan $-1.0 + 2.0 = 1.0\%$.
> A is wrong because it ranks by reported growth (9.0%), which includes 4 points from an acquisition that will not recur organically.
> B is wrong because 5 of its 8.5 points come from a euro tailwind outside management's control.
> 3. **B.** Foreign exchange effect $-26 / 6{,}500 = -0.40\%$; acquisition effect $117 / 6{,}500 = 1.80\%$.
> Organic growth $= 6.4\% - (-0.4\%) - 1.8\% = 5.0\%$ (V3 p.173, p.174).
> A is wrong because it treats the currency effect as a tailwind and subtracts it: $6.4 - 0.4 - 1.8 = 4.2\%$.
> C is wrong because it is constant-currency growth, removing foreign exchange but not the acquisition: $6.4 + 0.4 = 6.8\%$.
> 4. **B.** The exhibit gives the effect of a USD strengthening; with linear, symmetric effects a USD weakening against the CHF reverses the sign of the CHF line (V3 p.174, p.175, p.176).
> EUR $-24$, MXN $+9$, CHF $-7$, JPY $0$: total $-22$.
> A is wrong because it keeps the CHF line at $+7$, ignoring that the scenario has the dollar weakening against the franc: $-24 + 9 + 7 = -8$.
> C is wrong because it also includes the yen line, which the scenario holds unchanged: $-24 + 9 - 7 - 4 = -26$.
> 5. **A.** Halvorsen de Mexico's costs are in pesos while 40% of its revenue is fixed in USD, so its peso-measured margin itself depends on the exchange rate.
> A stronger peso lowers the peso value of USD revenue without lowering peso costs, compressing the margin in pesos and therefore in USD, which is why Exhibit 2 shows a dollar strengthening helping through the MXN line (V3 p.174, p.175).
> Translation at the average rate preserves whatever margin exists in pesos; it does not shield the peso margin from the economic exposure created by selling in USD and producing in pesos.
> B is wrong because a weaker peso raises the peso value of USD-priced sales relative to peso costs, widening the margin.
> C is wrong because ratio preservation under the current rate method removes only translation effects; it does not remove the transaction and economic exposure built into the subsidiary's own results.
> 6. **B.** Tax: $600 \times 0.21 + 240 \times 0.25 + 130 \times 0.30 + 30 \times 0.30 = 126 + 60 + 39 + 9 = 234$ on pretax profit of $1{,}000$, an effective rate of $23.4\%$ (V3 p.169, p.170, p.171).
> The 2.4-point excess over the 21% US rate is the effect of tax rates in foreign jurisdictions; a mix shift toward Mexico or the Eurozone would raise it.
> A is wrong because it applies the home statutory rate to all profit, ignoring the foreign jurisdictions.
> C is wrong because it takes a simple average of the four statutory rates, $(21 + 25 + 30 + 30) / 4 = 26.5\%$, instead of weighting by pretax profit.

## Distractor design

- Q1: A applies the right rate to the wrong period's local-currency sales; C converts an 8% euro depreciation as an 8% dollar appreciation (wrong quote direction).
- Q2: A ranks by reported growth including acquisitions; B ranks by growth inflated by a currency tailwind.
- Q3: A is the sign error on a negative currency effect; C is constant-currency growth mistaken for organic growth.
- Q4: A misses the sign flip for a currency moving the other way; C includes a currency the scenario holds constant.
- Q5: B reverses the direction of the economic exposure; C overextends ratio preservation under the current rate method to economic exposure.
- Q6: A ignores the profit mix; C uses an unweighted average of statutory rates.
