# Kestrel Valley Bank: Buying the Tier 2 Notes

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Tomas Reinholt, CFA, manages a credit portfolio for a public pension plan.
He is deciding whether to buy newly issued subordinated Tier 2 notes of Kestrel Valley Bank, a regional commercial bank that reports in $ millions and has a December year end.
Kestrel lends mainly to households and agricultural businesses in a single farming region, and about 40% of its commercial loans are to crop and livestock producers.

Kestrel's 2025 annual report does not show risk-weighted assets for 2025 in its summary table, so Reinholt rebuilds them from the exposure note using the risk weights that Kestrel's national regulator applies.

Exhibit 1: Kestrel exposures at 31 December 2025 ($ millions)

| Exposure | Amount | Risk weight |
|---|---|---|
| Cash and central bank balances | 4,200 | 0% |
| Government bonds | 9,800 | 0% |
| Residential mortgages | 22,400 | 50% |
| Commercial and corporate loans | 18,600 | 100% |
| High-volatility commercial real estate loans | 2,100 | 150% |
| Loans more than 90 days past due | 700 | 150% |
| Other assets | 1,900 | 100% |
| Total on-balance-sheet assets | 59,700 | |
| Undrawn loan commitments (credit-equivalent amount, off balance sheet) | 3,500 | 100% |

Exhibit 2: Kestrel regulatory capital ($ millions)

| | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common Equity Tier 1 capital | 3,420 | 3,700 | 4,020 |
| Additional Tier 1 capital | 450 | 540 | 650 |
| Total regulatory capital | 5,160 | 5,300 | 5,480 |
| Risk-weighted assets | 36,000 | 37,300 | not disclosed |

Exhibit 3: Kestrel loan-loss data ($ millions)

| | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provision for loan losses | 520 | 450 | 380 |
| Gross charge-offs | 540 | 580 | 610 |
| Recoveries | 70 | 75 | 90 |
| Allowance for loan losses, end of year | 1,295 | 1,240 | not disclosed |
| Non-accrual loans, end of year | 720 | 810 | 880 |

Reinholt assigns Kestrel the following CAMELS component ratings, where 1 is best and 5 is worst: capital adequacy 2, asset quality 3, management 2, earnings 2.5, liquidity 1.5, and sensitivity to market risk 2.
Because he is a creditor, his firm's policy is to weight capital adequacy and liquidity twice as heavily as each of the other four components.
His colleague Sofia Lindgren, who covers Kestrel's shares, weights asset quality and earnings twice as heavily instead.

Lindgren makes two statements about the regulatory framework.

Statement 1: "Kestrel's home country is a member of the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, so Basel III applies to Kestrel directly and the Committee can sanction Kestrel if it breaches a Basel III minimum."

Statement 2: "Kestrel's 2025 Pillar 1 capital charge at the 8% total capital floor is about $3.15 billion, comfortably below its total regulatory capital."

Before committing, Reinholt lists three concerns about Kestrel for the investment committee.

Concern 1: The share of Kestrel's revenue coming from trading income rose from 3% to 7% over three years.

Concern 2: Kestrel's lending is concentrated in one regional industry because of its mission as a community agricultural lender.

Concern 3: Kestrel's liquidity coverage ratio fell from 138% to 117% over two years.

## Questions

1. Kestrel's Tier 1 capital ratio at 31 December 2025 is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-d)
   - A. 11.85%.
   - B. 12.29%.
   - C. 13.01%.
2. Based on Exhibits 1 and 2, Kestrel's capital adequacy from 2023 to 2025, as measured by its three capital ratios, is best described as: (LOS: los-03-04-d)
   - A. mixed.
   - B. declining.
   - C. improving.
3. Kestrel's ratio of the allowance for loan losses to non-accrual loans at 31 December 2025 is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-d)
   - A. 1.15.
   - B. 1.25.
   - C. 1.41.
4. Reinholt's weighted composite CAMELS rating for Kestrel is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-c)
   - A. 2.06.
   - B. 2.17.
   - C. 2.31.
5. Which of Lindgren's statements is correct? (LOS: los-03-04-b)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both statements.
6. Which of Reinholt's concerns relates to a limitation of the CAMELS approach rather than to one of its components? (LOS: los-03-04-c)
   - A. Concern 1.
   - B. Concern 2.
   - C. Concern 3.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Risk-weighted assets include weighted on-balance-sheet and off-balance-sheet exposures.
> $RWA = 4{,}200 \times 0 + 9{,}800 \times 0 + 22{,}400 \times 0.50 + 18{,}600 \times 1.00 + 2{,}100 \times 1.50 + 700 \times 1.50 + 1{,}900 \times 1.00 + 3{,}500 \times 1.00$.
> $RWA = 0 + 0 + 11{,}200 + 18{,}600 + 3{,}150 + 1{,}050 + 1{,}900 + 3{,}500 = 39{,}400$.
> Tier 1 capital $= 4{,}020 + 650 = 4{,}670$, so the Tier 1 ratio $= 4{,}670 / 39{,}400 = 0.1185 = 11.85\%$.
> B is wrong because it weights the high-volatility commercial real estate loans and the past-due loans at 100% rather than 150%, giving $RWA = 38{,}000$ and $4{,}670 / 38{,}000 = 12.29\%$.
> C is wrong because it omits the off-balance-sheet commitments, giving $RWA = 35{,}900$ and $4{,}670 / 35{,}900 = 13.01\%$.
> 2. **A.** Tier 2 is backed out as total capital less CET1 less Additional Tier 1: $1{,}290$, $1{,}060$, and $810$ for 2023, 2024, and 2025.
> CET1 ratio: $3{,}420 / 36{,}000 = 9.50\%$, $3{,}700 / 37{,}300 = 9.92\%$, $4{,}020 / 39{,}400 = 10.20\%$, rising.
> Tier 1 ratio: $3{,}870 / 36{,}000 = 10.75\%$, $4{,}240 / 37{,}300 = 11.37\%$, $4{,}670 / 39{,}400 = 11.85\%$, rising.
> Total capital ratio: $5{,}160 / 36{,}000 = 14.33\%$, $5{,}300 / 37{,}300 = 14.21\%$, $5{,}480 / 39{,}400 = 13.91\%$, falling.
> Two ratios improve while the third declines as Tier 2 capital is run down, so the signal is mixed, which matters directly to a buyer of Tier 2 notes.
> B is wrong because it looks only at the total capital ratio.
> C is wrong because it looks only at CET1 and Tier 1, or at the dollar amount of total regulatory capital, which rose from 5,160 to 5,480 even though the total capital ratio fell.
> 3. **B.** Net charge-offs $= 610 - 90 = 520$.
> Ending allowance $= 1{,}240 + 380 - 520 = 1{,}100$.
> Allowance to non-accrual loans $= 1{,}100 / 880 = 1.25$.
> The same roll-forward ties 2024: $1{,}295 + 450 - (580 - 75) = 1{,}240$.
> The cushion is thinning (2023: $1{,}295 / 720 = 1.80$; 2024: $1{,}240 / 810 = 1.53$), and provision to net charge-offs fell from $520 / 470 = 1.11$ to $450 / 505 = 0.89$ to $380 / 520 = 0.73$, so provisioning now trails actual losses.
> A is wrong because it deducts gross charge-offs and ignores recoveries: $1{,}240 + 380 - 610 = 1{,}010$ and $1{,}010 / 880 = 1.15$.
> C is wrong because it uses the beginning allowance without the roll-forward: $1{,}240 / 880 = 1.41$.
> 4. **A.** With capital adequacy and liquidity weighted 2 and the others 1, the weights sum to 8.
> Composite $= (2 \times 2 + 3 \times 1 + 2 \times 1 + 2.5 \times 1 + 1.5 \times 2 + 2 \times 1) / 8 = 16.5 / 8 = 2.0625 \approx 2.06$.
> B is wrong because it is the unweighted mean: $(2 + 3 + 2 + 2.5 + 1.5 + 2) / 6 = 13 / 6 = 2.1667$.
> C is wrong because it applies Lindgren's equity weights (asset quality and earnings doubled): $(2 + 6 + 2 + 5 + 1.5 + 2) / 8 = 18.5 / 8 = 2.3125$.
> 5. **B.** Statement 2 is correct: the Pillar 1 charge is $0.08 \times 39{,}400 = 3{,}152$, about $3.15 billion, below total regulatory capital of 5,480.
> Statement 1 is incorrect: global bodies such as the Basel Committee set and harmonise standards, but only national regulators have legal authority over a bank, so Basel III reaches Kestrel through its national regulator.
> A is wrong because it accepts legal authority for a global standard setter and would reject a correct calculation; a candidate who used RWA without the off-balance-sheet commitments ($0.08 \times 35{,}900 = 2{,}872$) might think Statement 2 overstates the charge.
> C is wrong because Statement 1 misstates who has legal authority.
> 6. **B.** Concentration arising from the bank's mission, such as a community bank tied to one regional industry, is a bank-specific factor that CAMELS does not address, alongside government support and corporate culture.
> A is wrong because the composition of revenue, including a rising trading share, is part of the earnings component.
> C is wrong because the liquidity coverage ratio is part of the liquidity component.

## Distractor design

1. B: special risk weights above 100% ignored (wrong formula variant); C: off-balance-sheet exposures omitted from RWA (misread exhibit).
2. B: only the total capital ratio computed (partial evidence); C: only CET1 and Tier 1, or dollar capital instead of the ratio (numerator-only error).
3. A: gross rather than net charge-offs in the roll-forward (recoveries omitted); C: beginning allowance used (period error).
4. B: weights ignored (unweighted mean); C: equity analyst's weights applied instead of the creditor's (wrong weighting scheme).
5. A: global standard setter treated as a legal authority, calculation rejected on RWA without off-balance-sheet items; C: accepts the legal-authority error alongside the correct calculation.
6. A: earnings composition treated as outside CAMELS (component confusion); C: liquidity ratio treated as outside CAMELS (component confusion).
