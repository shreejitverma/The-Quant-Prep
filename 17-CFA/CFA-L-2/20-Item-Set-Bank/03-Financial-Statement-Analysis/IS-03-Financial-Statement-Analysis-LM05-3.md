# Banca Adriatica: Cash Flow Quality of Three Retail Borrowers

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Luca Ferretti, CFA, is a senior credit analyst at Banca Adriatica Commerciale.
Three established apparel retailers have applied for five-year revolving credit facilities, and Ferretti must recommend which applications the credit committee should approve.
All three report under IFRS with a December year end, and all amounts are in € millions.

The first applicant is Tessaro Retail Group SpA.
Ferretti notices that the FY2024 comparative figures in Tessaro's FY2025 annual report differ from those in its FY2024 annual report.
The FY2025 report reclassified proceeds from sales of short-term investments from investing activities into "changes in working capital" within operating activities.
In addition, beginning in FY2025 Tessaro presents interest paid in financing activities; it had presented interest paid in operating activities in all earlier reports and did not restate FY2024 for this change.

Exhibit 1: Tessaro Retail Group, selected cash flow data (€ millions)

| Item | FY2024 as originally reported | FY2024 as restated in FY2025 report | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash flow from operating activities | 142.6 | 171.0 | 158.3 |
| Proceeds from sales of short-term investments included in operating activities | 0.0 | 28.4 | 35.9 |
| Interest paid | 16.2 (operating) | 16.2 (operating) | 18.5 (financing) |
| Depreciation and amortization | 57.4 | 57.4 | 61.0 |

In FY2025 Tessaro spent 96.0 on capital expenditures, paid dividends of 30.0, and made scheduled debt repayments of 25.0.
Tessaro's FY2025 net income was 88.6, and its tax rate is 24%.
A note discloses that Tessaro capitalized 26.0 of store pre-opening costs in FY2025, a practice its peers do not follow; FY2025 depreciation and amortization includes 2.6 of amortization of those capitalized costs.
Ferretti's junior colleague writes that "Tessaro's operating cash flow exceeds its net income, so its earnings are clearly of high quality."

Exhibit 2 contains Ferretti's research notes on the other two applicants, based on the last five fiscal years.

Exhibit 2: Research notes on Brunetti Moda and Castellan Outdoor

| Company | Notes |
|---|---|
| Brunetti Moda | Note 1: OCF has been positive every year, close to net income, and less volatile than peers' OCF. Note 2: OCF has been sufficient, with a small margin, to cover capital expenditures, dividends, and debt repayments. Note 3: Return on invested capital has averaged 5.1%, against a weighted average cost of capital of 8.4%. Note 4: The auditor's opinion is unqualified and the notes are detailed and transparent. |
| Castellan Outdoor | Note 5: OCF has been well above operating income in each of the last two years. Note 6: Over the last two years, days of payables rose from 48 to 71, while days sales outstanding fell from 22 to 14 and days of inventory fell from 118 to 96. |

Ferretti plans to present the committee with a view on each applicant's position on the spectrum of financial reporting quality.
For Brunetti, the unqualified opinion and his own review of the notes convince him that its reports comply with IFRS and are decision-useful.

## Questions

1. Measured on the basis Tessaro used in its FY2024 annual report for short-term investment proceeds, and ignoring the interest reclassification, Tessaro's FY2025 OCF growth is closest to: (LOS: los-03-05-i)
   - A. $-14.2\%$.
   - B. $-7.4\%$.
   - C. $+11.0\%$.
2. Using an FY2025 OCF that is fully comparable with the classifications in Tessaro's FY2024 annual report, the ratio of OCF to capital expenditures, dividends, and scheduled debt repayments is closest to: (LOS: los-03-05-i)
   - A. 0.69.
   - B. 0.81.
   - C. 1.05.
3. Which applicant would most likely be described as having high-quality operating cash flow? (LOS: los-03-05-l)
   - A. Tessaro Retail Group.
   - B. Brunetti Moda.
   - C. Castellan Outdoor.
4. Which of Ferretti's notes about Castellan Outdoor describes a warning sign that operating cash flow is being managed? (LOS: los-03-05-l)
   - A. Note 5 only.
   - B. Note 6 only.
   - C. Both Note 5 and Note 6.
5. If Tessaro expensed its store pre-opening costs as its peers do, its FY2025 net income would be closest to: (LOS: los-03-05-h)
   - A. €65.2 million.
   - B. €68.8 million.
   - C. €70.8 million.
6. Brunetti Moda's financial reports are best placed on the quality spectrum as: (LOS: los-03-05-a)
   - A. GAAP-compliant and decision-useful, with sustainable and adequate returns.
   - B. GAAP-compliant and decision-useful, but with earnings that do not provide an adequate return.
   - C. within GAAP, but reflecting biased accounting choices.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** The restatement moved $171.0 - 142.6 = 28.4$ of investment proceeds into operating activities for FY2024, and FY2025 OCF includes 35.9 of the same proceeds.
> FY2025 OCF on the original basis $= 158.3 - 35.9 = 122.4$; growth $= 122.4/142.6 - 1 = -14.17\%$.
> A classification shift changes only the split between sections, so removing it reveals a much steeper decline than reported.
> B is wrong because it uses both years on the new, flattered basis ($158.3/171.0 - 1 = -7.43\%$); the comparison is consistent but hides the size of operating cash generation from sustainable sources.
> C is wrong because it compares FY2025 on the new basis with FY2024 on the original basis ($158.3/142.6 - 1 = +11.01\%$), mixing classifications.
> 2. **A.** Comparable FY2025 OCF removes the investment proceeds and puts interest paid back into operating activities: $158.3 - 35.9 - 18.5 = 103.9$.
> Required outflows $= 96.0 + 30.0 + 25.0 = 151.0$; coverage $= 103.9/151.0 = 0.6881$.
> On a consistent basis Tessaro's OCF is not adequate to cover capital expenditures, dividends, and debt repayments.
> B is wrong because it removes the investment proceeds but leaves interest paid in financing ($122.4/151.0 = 0.8106$).
> C is wrong because it uses reported OCF with both favorable classification choices intact ($158.3/151.0 = 1.0483$).
> 3. **B.** For an established company, high-quality OCF is positive, from sustainable sources, adequate to cover capital expenditures, dividends, and debt repayments, and low in volatility relative to peers; Brunetti meets all four (Notes 1 and 2).
> A is wrong because Tessaro's comparable OCF covers only about 69% of its commitments and has been flattered by classification choices.
> C is wrong because Castellan's OCF is boosted by the working-capital pattern in Note 6, so it is not from sustainable sources.
> 4. **B.** Payables rising while receivables and inventory fall substantially is the warning sign that OCF has been managed through the timing of working capital flows, for example by stretching supplier payments; the effect reverses when payables normalize.
> A is wrong because the warning sign runs the other way: OCF much lower than operating income is the flag, and OCF above operating income is not.
> C is wrong because Note 5 is not a warning sign in itself.
> 5. **C.** Expensing the costs would add 26.0 of pre-tax expense but remove the 2.6 of amortization already recognized on them, a net pre-tax reduction of $26.0 - 2.6 = 23.4$.
> After tax: $23.4 \times (1 - 0.24) = 17.784$; adjusted net income $= 88.6 - 17.784 = 70.816$.
> The same capitalization places the 26.0 outflow in investing rather than operating activities, which is why OCF above net income is no proof of earnings quality here.
> A is wrong because it ignores the tax shield ($88.6 - 23.4 = 65.2$).
> B is wrong because it ignores the amortization already recognized, so the 2.6 is expensed twice ($88.6 - 26.0 \times 0.76 = 68.84$).
> 6. **B.** The first framework question (are the reports GAAP-compliant and decision-useful?) is answered yes; the second (are the results adequate and sustainable?) fails because a 5.1% return on invested capital is below the 8.4% cost of capital.
> High reporting quality, and even high cash flow quality, do not guarantee high earnings quality.
> A is wrong because earnings that do not cover the cost of capital are not adequate, so Brunetti cannot sit at the top of the spectrum.
> C is wrong because nothing in the notes points to aggressive or conservative choices; the problem lies in the economics, not in the reporting.

## Distractor design

1. B: both years measured on the flattered basis (classification shift left in place); C: FY2025 new basis compared with FY2024 original basis (mixed classification).
2. B: interest reclassification ignored (IFRS classification choice missed); C: reported OCF used with both reclassifications intact (misread exhibit).
3. A: reported rather than comparable OCF used to judge adequacy; C: OCF above operating income mistaken for quality.
4. A: warning sign direction reversed (OCF above rather than below operating income); C: accepts the reversed sign alongside the correct one.
5. A: tax shield ignored (before-tax vs after-tax error); B: amortization already recognized expensed twice (double count).
6. A: reporting quality confused with earnings quality; C: poor economics attributed to biased accounting (wrong rung).
