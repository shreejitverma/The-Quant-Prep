# Thornbury Casualty: A Rating Agency Underwriting Review

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Priya Raman, CFA, is a senior analyst at a credit rating agency.
She is conducting the annual rating review of Thornbury Casualty, a property and casualty (P&C) insurer that writes commercial property, motor, and small-business liability cover.
She benchmarks Thornbury against Castellan Mutual, a P&C competitor of similar size.
Both insurers report on a statutory basis in $ millions, with December year ends.

Exhibit 1: Underwriting data for 2025 ($ millions)

| Item | Thornbury | Castellan |
|---|---|---|
| Losses and loss adjustment expenses | 4,212 | 2,960 |
| Net premiums earned | 6,120 | 4,480 |
| Underwriting expenses | 1,964 | 1,540 |
| Net premiums written | 6,380 | 4,410 |
| Dividends to policyholders | 214 | 62 |

Exhibit 2: Thornbury net loss reserve roll-forward for 2025 ($ millions)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Net reserves, beginning of year | 9,480 |
| Estimated claims incurred, current accident year | 4,390 |
| Revision of prior-year estimates (decrease) | (178) |
| Claims paid, current accident year | (1,720) |
| Claims paid, prior accident years | (2,430) |
| Net reserves, end of year | not shown |
| Gross reserves, end of year | 11,860 |
| Reinsurance recoverables on unpaid losses, end of year | 2,318 |

Thornbury's 2025 pretax income was $1,046 million.
Its invested assets (cash and investments) were $14,200 million at the start of 2025 and $15,000 million at year end.
For 2025 it reported net investment income of $468 million, realised gains of $52 million, and unrealised gains of $210 million recognised in other comprehensive income.

Raman's rating committee memo includes her colleague Luca Benedetti's observations.

Benedetti's Observation 1: "Thornbury's 2025 reserve release is small relative to total claims incurred and should not affect our view of earnings."

Benedetti's Observation 2: "Thornbury's modest equity allocation and large short-term holdings fit the P&C business model, because claims arise from unpredictable events and policies are short-term."

Thornbury's parent is considering an acquisition of Halden Life, a life and health (L&H) insurer, and the committee asks Raman how regulation of insurers compares with banking regulation.
Benedetti drafts two statements for the memo.

Statement 1: "The International Association of Insurance Supervisors works with the Basel Committee and IOSCO in the Joint Forum, but it cannot legally require Thornbury to hold any particular level of capital; that authority rests with Thornbury's national supervisor."

Statement 2: "Reinsurance is the segment of the insurance industry with the least cross-border business, so a reinsurer failure is less likely to spread internationally than a primary insurer failure."

Raman notes that Castellan's net premiums written fell below its net premiums earned in 2025, while Thornbury's continued to grow.

## Questions

1. Thornbury's 2025 combined ratio after dividends is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-f)
   - A. 99.6%.
   - B. 103.1%.
   - C. 104.4%.
2. Based on Exhibit 1, which conclusion about the two insurers' 2025 underwriting is most accurate? (LOS: los-03-04-f)
   - A. Castellan was more successful in estimating the risks it insured, while Thornbury was more efficient in obtaining new premiums.
   - B. Thornbury was more successful in estimating the risks it insured, while Castellan was more efficient in obtaining new premiums.
   - C. Castellan had the more efficient underwriting operation overall, because its loss and loss adjustment expense ratio was lower.
3. Thornbury's net loss reserves at the end of 2025 are closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-f)
   - A. 9,542.
   - B. 9,898.
   - C. 11,262.
4. Regarding Benedetti's Observation 1, the reserve release as a percentage of Thornbury's 2025 pretax income, and the most appropriate response, is: (LOS: los-03-04-f)
   - A. 4.2%, so the observation is correct.
   - B. 17.0%, so the observation is incorrect, and the release could reflect conservative initial reserving or earnings management.
   - C. 17.0%, so the observation is incorrect, and the release confirms that initial reserves were set conservatively.
5. Thornbury's 2025 investment return on average invested assets, excluding unrealised gains, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-f)
   - A. 3.47%.
   - B. 3.56%.
   - C. 5.00%.
6. Which of Benedetti's statements for the memo is correct? (LOS: los-03-04-b)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both statements.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Loss and LAE ratio $= 4{,}212 / 6{,}120 = 0.6882 = 68.82\%$ (over net premiums earned).
> Underwriting expense ratio $= 1{,}964 / 6{,}380 = 0.3078 = 30.78\%$ (over net premiums written).
> Combined ratio $= 68.82\% + 30.78\% = 99.61\%$.
> Dividend ratio $= 214 / 6{,}120 = 0.0350 = 3.50\%$.
> Combined ratio after dividends $= 99.61\% + 3.50\% = 103.11\%$, so a small underwriting profit before dividends becomes a loss after dividends.
> A is wrong because it stops at the combined ratio before dividends (99.6%).
> C is wrong because it divides underwriting expenses by net premiums earned: $1{,}964 / 6{,}120 = 32.09\%$, giving $68.82\% + 32.09\% + 3.50\% = 104.41\%$.
> 2. **A.** Castellan's loss and LAE ratio is $2{,}960 / 4{,}480 = 66.07\%$, below Thornbury's 68.82%, and a lower loss ratio indicates more success in estimating insured risks.
> Castellan's underwriting expense ratio is $1{,}540 / 4{,}410 = 34.92\%$, above Thornbury's 30.78%, so Thornbury spends less to obtain each dollar of new premiums.
> B is wrong because it reverses both comparisons.
> C is wrong because it judges overall efficiency on the loss ratio alone; Castellan's combined ratio is $66.07\% + 34.92\% = 100.99\%$, an underwriting loss, against Thornbury's 99.61%.
> 3. **A.** Ending net reserves $= 9{,}480 + 4{,}390 - 178 - 1{,}720 - 2{,}430 = 9{,}542$.
> The incurred losses of $4{,}390 - 178 = 4{,}212$ tie to Exhibit 1.
> B is wrong because it adds the prior-year decrease instead of subtracting it: $9{,}480 + 4{,}390 + 178 - 4{,}150 = 9{,}898$.
> C is wrong because it deducts only claims paid on prior accident years: $9{,}480 + 4{,}390 - 178 - 2{,}430 = 11{,}262$.
> 4. **B.** Release relative to pretax income $= 178 / 1{,}046 = 0.1702 = 17.0\%$, which is material to profit even though it is small against claims incurred.
> A downward revision of prior-year estimates flows straight to pretax income and may indicate conservative initial reserving, but it may also be a tool for managing earnings, so it warrants scrutiny rather than a definitive conclusion.
> A is wrong because it measures the release against incurred losses ($178 / 4{,}212 = 4.2\%$), the wrong benchmark for its effect on earnings.
> C is wrong because a single release cannot confirm conservatism; it is equally consistent with earnings management.
> 5. **B.** Average invested assets $= (14{,}200 + 15{,}000) / 2 = 14{,}600$.
> Return excluding unrealised gains $= (468 + 52) / 14{,}600 = 520 / 14{,}600 = 0.0356 = 3.56\%$.
> A is wrong because it uses the ending balance: $520 / 15{,}000 = 3.47\%$.
> C is wrong because it includes the unrealised gains: $(468 + 52 + 210) / 14{,}600 = 730 / 14{,}600 = 5.00\%$.
> 6. **A.** Statement 1 is correct: the IAIS, the Basel Committee, and IOSCO form the Joint Forum, but global bodies only set and harmonise standards; legal authority over a given insurer rests with its national regulator.
> Statement 2 is incorrect: reinsurance is the exception within insurance, because it is largely international and links insurers around the world, so a reinsurer failure is more, not less, likely to spread across borders.
> B is wrong because it reverses the cross-border character of reinsurance and denies the national-authority principle.
> C is wrong because Statement 2 misstates the cross-border character of reinsurance.

## Distractor design

1. A: dividends to policyholders omitted (before-dividend ratio); C: premiums earned used as the expense ratio denominator (wrong denominator, earned versus written).
2. B: the two component ratios assigned to the wrong attribute (loss ratio versus expense ratio swapped); C: overall efficiency judged on the loss ratio alone.
3. B: favourable prior-year development added rather than subtracted (sign); C: current-year payments omitted (misread exhibit).
4. A: release scaled by incurred losses instead of pretax income (wrong benchmark); C: correct number but over-claims conservatism (one-sided interpretation).
5. A: ending rather than average invested assets (period); C: unrealised gains included when the question excludes them (wrong variant).
6. B: reinsurance treated as local and global bodies as authorities (reversed facts); C: accepts the reinsurance error alongside the correct statement.
