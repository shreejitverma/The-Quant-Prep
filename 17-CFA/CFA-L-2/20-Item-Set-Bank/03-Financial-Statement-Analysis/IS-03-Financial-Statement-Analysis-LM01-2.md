# Carrow Logistics: Acquiring 70% of Bexley Cold Chain

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Marcus Oyelaran, CFA, is a credit analyst at a fixed-income fund that holds the senior notes of Carrow Logistics Inc., a US company that reports under US GAAP.
On 1 January 2026 Carrow acquired 70% of the voting shares of Bexley Cold Chain Ltd for $616 million in cash, gaining control.
Oyelaran compares Carrow's reporting with that of a European peer that reports under IFRS and elects the partial goodwill method for every acquisition.
All amounts are in $ millions, and taxes are ignored.

Exhibit 1: Balance sheets immediately before the acquisition

| Item | Carrow | Bexley (book) |
|---|---|---|
| Total assets | 3,400 | 1,240 |
| Total liabilities | 1,900 | 700 |
| Shareholders' equity | 1,500 | 540 |

The purchase price allocation team identified the following differences at the acquisition date.
Bexley's refrigerated warehouses are worth 120 more than book value and have a remaining life of 10 years.
Bexley has customer relationships that it never recognized, with a fair value of 60 and a remaining life of 5 years.
Bexley is the defendant in a customer lawsuit arising from a 2025 temperature failure.
The claim is non-contractual, is a present obligation arising from past events, and has a fair value that can be measured reliably at 20.
Counsel estimates only a 40% likelihood that the claim will result in a liability.
All other Bexley assets and liabilities have fair values equal to book values.
The price Carrow paid per share is representative of the fair value of all of Bexley's shares.

Carrow paid $9 million in cash to advisers for legal and valuation work on the deal.
Carrow's management also plans to close two Bexley depots during 2027 at an estimated cost of $15 million; no obligation to do so existed at the acquisition date.

Oyelaran's forecasts for 2026 are as follows.
Carrow's stand-alone net income is 180, after the advisory fees and before any contribution from Bexley.
Bexley's own reported net income is 90, before any consolidation adjustments.
No goodwill impairment is expected in 2026.

For stress testing, Oyelaran also sketches a downside case for the goodwill impairment test at 31 December 2027, assuming Bexley is a single reporting unit and goodwill is unchanged until then.

Exhibit 2: Bexley reporting unit, downside case at 31 December 2027

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Carrying amount, including goodwill | 1,300 |
| Fair value of the unit | 1,180 |
| Costs to sell the unit | 15 |
| Value in use | 1,150 |
| Fair value of identifiable net assets | 1,080 |

Oyelaran's junior colleague, Hana Petrov, drafts two notes on the deal.

Note 1: "The $9 million of advisory fees is part of the cost of the acquisition, and the $15 million depot closure plan is a liability assumed at the acquisition date."

Note 2: "Neither the advisory fees nor the depot closure costs belong in the purchase price allocation; the fees are expensed in 2026 and the closure costs are expensed when they are incurred."

## Questions

1. Had the IFRS peer made the same acquisition, the goodwill it would recognize is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-a)
   - A. 126.
   - B. 135.
   - C. 180.
2. The goodwill and the non-controlling interest that Carrow recognizes at the acquisition date are closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-b)
   - A. goodwill 112 and non-controlling interest 216.
   - B. goodwill 160 and non-controlling interest 264.
   - C. goodwill 180 and non-controlling interest 264.
3. Carrow's consolidated total liabilities to total equity ratio immediately after the acquisition is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-c)
   - A. 1.27.
   - B. 1.48.
   - C. 1.54.
4. In the downside case, Carrow's goodwill impairment loss at 31 December 2027 is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-b)
   - A. 60.
   - B. 100.
   - C. 135.
5. Regarding Petrov's notes, which is most accurate? (LOS: los-03-01-a)
   - A. Note 1 is correct.
   - B. Note 1 is correct for the fees only, and Note 2 is correct for the closure costs only.
   - C. Note 2 is correct.
6. Carrow's forecast 2026 net income attributable to its own shareholders is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-a)
   - A. 219.0.
   - B. 226.2.
   - C. 243.0.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Under IFRS the contingent liability is recognized because it is a present obligation with a reliably measurable fair value, whatever the probability of outflow.
> Fair value of identifiable net assets $= 540 + 120 + 60 - 20 = 700$.
> Partial goodwill $= 616 - 0.70 \times 700 = 616 - 490 = 126$.
> B is wrong because it capitalizes the 9 of advisory fees into the consideration ($625 - 490 = 135$).
> C is wrong because it is full goodwill measured on IFRS net assets ($616 / 0.70 = 880$; $880 - 700 = 180$), not the partial method the peer elects.
> 2. **B.** US GAAP requires the full goodwill method.
> The lawsuit is a non-contractual contingency that is not more likely than not to result in a liability, so US GAAP does not recognize it and identifiable net assets are $540 + 120 + 60 = 720$.
> Fair value of Bexley $= 616 / 0.70 = 880$.
> Goodwill $= 880 - 720 = 160$; non-controlling interest $= 0.30 \times 880 = 264$.
> A is wrong because it applies partial goodwill, which US GAAP does not permit ($616 - 0.70 \times 720 = 112$; $0.30 \times 720 = 216$).
> C is wrong because it applies the IFRS contingent liability rule, which lowers net assets to 700 and raises goodwill to 180.
> 3. **B.** Consolidated assets $= 3{,}400 - 616 - 9 + (1{,}240 + 120 + 60) + 160 = 4{,}355$.
> Consolidated liabilities $= 1{,}900 + 700 = 2{,}600$, with no liability for the lawsuit under US GAAP.
> Consolidated equity $= (1{,}500 - 9) + 264 = 1{,}755$, including the non-controlling interest; $2{,}600 + 1{,}755 = 4{,}355$ balances.
> Ratio $= 2{,}600 / 1{,}755 = 1.4815$.
> A is wrong because it is Carrow's stand-alone ratio ($1{,}900 / 1{,}500 = 1.2667$), as if Bexley's liabilities stayed off the balance sheet.
> C is wrong because it applies the IFRS peer's treatment: liabilities $2{,}620$ including the lawsuit, and equity $1{,}491 + 0.30 \times 700 = 1{,}701$, giving $2{,}620 / 1{,}701 = 1.5403$.
> 4. **A.** Step 1: fair value of the unit $1{,}180$ is below its carrying amount $1{,}300$, so goodwill is impaired.
> Step 2: implied goodwill $= 1{,}180 - 1{,}080 = 100$; loss $= 160 - 100 = 60$, which is below the cap of 160 of carrying goodwill.
> B is wrong because it reports implied goodwill itself as the loss.
> C is wrong because it applies the IFRS one-step test, with recoverable amount $= \max(1{,}150,\ 1{,}180 - 15) = 1{,}165$ and loss $= 1{,}300 - 1{,}165 = 135$, which is not the US GAAP measure.
> 5. **C.** Acquisition-related costs such as legal and valuation fees are expensed as incurred, and expected restructuring costs are not liabilities at the acquisition date; both rules apply under IFRS and US GAAP.
> A is wrong because it capitalizes the fees and recognizes a restructuring provision at acquisition, which overstates both goodwill and liabilities.
> B is wrong because it capitalizes the fees into the purchase price.
> 6. **B.** Extra annual depreciation and amortization on the step-ups $= 120 / 10 + 60 / 5 = 12 + 12 = 24$.
> Bexley's adjusted net income $= 90 - 24 = 66$.
> Consolidated net income $= 180 + 66 = 246$; the non-controlling interest's share $= 0.30 \times 66 = 19.8$.
> Net income attributable to Carrow $= 246 - 19.8 = 226.2$, which equals $180 + 0.70 \times 66$.
> A is wrong because it deducts the NCI's share of Bexley's unadjusted income ($246 - 0.30 \times 90 = 219.0$), although the NCI bears its share of the step-up expense.
> C is wrong because it ignores the step-up depreciation and amortization ($180 + 0.70 \times 90 = 243.0$).

## Distractor design

1. B: transaction costs capitalized into the consideration; C: full goodwill reported where the partial method was elected (wrong formula variant).
2. A: partial goodwill applied under US GAAP (IFRS vs US GAAP); C: IFRS contingent liability recognition applied to a US GAAP reporter (IFRS vs US GAAP).
3. A: subsidiary left unconsolidated (stand-alone ratio); C: IFRS partial goodwill and IFRS contingent liability used for a US GAAP reporter.
4. B: implied goodwill reported as the loss (misread of step 2); C: IFRS one-step test with recoverable amount used for a US GAAP reporter.
5. A: both items capitalized into the acquisition; B: fees capitalized (acquisition cost rule misapplied).
6. A: NCI share computed on unadjusted subsidiary income; C: fair value step-up expense omitted.
