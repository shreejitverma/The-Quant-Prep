# Brenmark Analytics Tax Rate and Valuation Bridge

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Tomasz Wieczorek, CFA, is a portfolio manager at Harlan Peak Investors.
He is valuing Brenmark Analytics Inc., a US maker of laboratory instruments and data software that reports under US GAAP and is profitable.
His associate, Aiko Thibodeau, has prepared the inputs below from the FY2025 annual report.

All Brenmark equity awards are equity-settled.
The US statutory income tax rate is 21%, and the tax deduction for an award equals the share price at settlement for RSUs and the intrinsic value at exercise for options.

**Exhibit 1: Equity awards settled in FY2025**

| Award | Number settled | Grant-date fair value per award (USD) | Share price at settlement (USD) |
|---|---|---|---|
| RSUs from the 2022 grant, vested March 2025 | 1,500,000 | 42.00 | 58.00 |
| RSUs from the 2023 grant, vested September 2025 | 900,000 | 66.00 | 51.00 |
| Options from the 2021 grant, strike USD 30.00, exercised | 600,000 | 11.00 | 63.50 |

The FY2025 average share price was USD 55.00, and the 31 December 2025 price was USD 60.00.

**Exhibit 2: FY2025 income statement extracts (USD millions)**

| Item | FY2025 |
|---|---|
| Revenue | 3,200.0 |
| Income before tax | 480.0 |
| Income tax expense | 104.2 |

Brenmark's only material item in income tax expense that Thibodeau considers non-recurring is the tax effect of share-based awards settled in the year.

Brenmark sponsors two defined benefit plans, which are presented separately on the balance sheet.

**Exhibit 3: Defined benefit plans (USD millions)**

| Item | 31 Dec 2024 | 31 Dec 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| US plan: net pension liability | (412.0) | (538.0) |
| UK plan: net pension asset (other non-current assets) | 150.0 | 164.0 |

FY2025 employer contributions were USD 95.0 million to the US plan and USD 12.0 million to the UK plan, and benefits paid by the plans totalled USD 260.0 million.
Combined FY2025 pension expense in profit or loss was USD 131.0 million: service cost 84.0, interest cost 203.0, expected return on plan assets (236.0), and amortisation of net actuarial loss 80.0.

Wieczorek's FCFF forecast deducts share-based compensation as an operating cost, keeps pension service cost in operating costs, and excludes the other pension components.
Discounting that forecast gives an enterprise value of USD 14,860 million at 31 December 2025.
Brenmark has USD 2,300 million of debt and USD 940 million of cash.

**Exhibit 4: Share data at 31 December 2025 (millions)**

| Item | Shares |
|---|---|
| Basic shares outstanding | 212.0 |
| Incremental shares from unvested RSUs and in-the-money options (treasury stock method) | 3.4 |
| Options excluded from diluted shares (strike USD 68.00, above the average and year-end share prices) | 2.1 |

At a review on 10 March 2026, Thibodeau makes two proposals.

First: "Brenmark's reported effective tax rate is the best forecast, because the share price has risen over time and the tax benefit from awards will recur."

Second: "To be conservative, we should add the 6.5 million shares we expect Brenmark to grant over the next three years to the diluted share count used for the per-share value."

Wieczorek rejects both proposals and asks Thibodeau to rebuild the tax rate and per-share value on the curriculum basis.

## Questions

1. The net effect of the FY2025 award settlements in Exhibit 1 on Brenmark's FY2025 income tax expense is closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-b)
   - A. a reduction of USD 5.04 million.
   - B. a reduction of USD 7.88 million.
   - C. a reduction of USD 8.82 million.
2. The effective tax rate Wieczorek should use in his FCFF forecast, assuming the FY2025 rate excluding the share-based award tax effect is sustainable, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-c)
   - A. 20.7%.
   - B. 21.7%.
   - C. 22.8%.
3. The FY2025 net amount recognised in other comprehensive income for Brenmark's two plans combined is closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-d)
   - A. a loss of USD 88.0 million.
   - B. a loss of USD 102.0 million.
   - C. a gain of USD 24.0 million.
4. Brenmark's equity value per share on Wieczorek's basis is closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-e)
   - A. USD 58.41.
   - B. USD 60.18.
   - C. USD 60.94.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Each settlement's tax effect is $t \times$ (tax deduction at settlement $-$ cumulative book expense at grant-date fair value), and under US GAAP windfalls and shortfalls both run through income tax expense.
> 2022 RSUs: $1.5 \times (58.00 - 42.00) = 24.0000$ (windfall).
> 2023 RSUs: $0.9 \times (51.00 - 66.00) = -13.5000$ (shortfall).
> Options: deduction $= 0.6 \times (63.50 - 30.00) = 20.1000$; book expense $= 0.6 \times 11.00 = 6.6000$; excess $= 13.5000$.
> Net excess $= 24.0 - 13.5 + 13.5 = 24.0000$; tax effect $= 21\% \times 24.0 = 5.0400$, a reduction in income tax expense.
> B is wrong because it ignores the shortfall on the 2023 RSUs: $21\% \times (24.0 + 13.5) = 7.8750$.
> C is wrong because it uses the full exercise-date share price rather than the intrinsic value as the option deduction: $0.6 \times (63.50 - 11.00) = 31.5000$, so $21\% \times (24.0 - 13.5 + 31.5) = 8.8200$.
> 2. **C.** Add back the non-recurring net excess tax benefit from Question 1: normalised tax expense $= 104.2 + 5.04 = 109.2400$; rate $= 109.24 / 480.0 = 22.7583\%$.
> A windfall depends on the share price at settlement exceeding grant-date values and should not be extrapolated; using a rate that contains it overstates FCFF and enterprise value.
> A is wrong because it subtracts the benefit a second time (sign error): $(104.2 - 5.04) / 480.0 = 20.6583\%$.
> B is wrong because it extrapolates the reported rate, $104.2 / 480.0 = 21.7083\%$, which is Thibodeau's first proposal.
> 3. **A.** Total periodic pension cost $=$ contributions $-$ change in funded status, and it splits into profit or loss and OCI.
> Combined funded status: opening $= -412.0 + 150.0 = -262.0$; closing $= -538.0 + 164.0 = -374.0$; change $= -112.0$.
> Total cost $= 107.0 - (-112.0) = 219.0$; OCI $= 219.0 - 131.0 = 88.0$, a loss (net of the 80.0 amortisation reclassified to profit or loss).
> Benefits paid of 260.0 reduce plan assets and the obligation equally and are irrelevant.
> B is wrong because it uses only the US plan liability line and drops the UK plan presented in assets: total cost $= 107.0 - (-538.0 + 412.0) = 233.0$, OCI $= 233.0 - 131.0 = 102.0$.
> C is wrong because it treats contributions as the total cost (defined contribution logic): $107.0 - 131.0 = -24.0$, a gain.
> 4. **B.** Equity value $= 14{,}860 - 2{,}300 + 940 - 538 = 12{,}962$; the US deficit is debt, and the UK surplus is ignored because plan assets cannot be distributed to shareholders.
> Because share-based compensation is already deducted in FCFF, future grants are captured in enterprise value, so the divisor is current diluted shares: $212.0 + 3.4 = 215.4$ million.
> Value per share $= 12{,}962 / 215.4 = 60.1764$.
> The 2.1 million out-of-the-money options are correctly excluded; Brenmark is profitable, so they are anti-dilutive because of moneyness, not a net loss.
> A is wrong because it follows Thibodeau's second proposal and inflates the share count for future grants after deducting share-based compensation from FCFF, which double counts: $12{,}962 / 221.9 = 58.4137$.
> C is wrong because it nets the UK surplus against the US deficit: $(12{,}962 + 164) / 215.4 = 60.9378$.

## Distractor design

1. B ignores the shortfall on RSUs that settled below grant-date value; C uses the share price instead of intrinsic value as the option deduction (wrong formula variant).
2. A adds the windfall adjustment with the wrong sign; B extrapolates a reported rate that contains a non-recurring windfall.
3. B drops the plan presented in assets (misread exhibit, no-netting rule); C treats contributions as the pension cost.
4. A double counts dilution (share count inflated after deducting share-based compensation); C nets a surplus against a deficit.
