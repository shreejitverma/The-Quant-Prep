# Pinecrest Packaging: Critiquing a Credit Upgrade

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Tamsin Okafor, CFA, is a senior credit analyst at a fixed-income manager that holds the senior unsecured notes of Pinecrest Packaging Inc., a US manufacturer of corrugated and flexible packaging that reports under US GAAP.
In January 2026 a rating agency upgraded Pinecrest by one notch, citing "record earnings and improving earnings quality."
Okafor's chief investment officer asks her to decide within two weeks, using public filings only, whether the upgrade is justified and whether the fund should add to its position.

Okafor plans to test whether Pinecrest's earnings are backed by cash.
She defines operating assets as total assets less cash and short-term investments, and operating liabilities as total liabilities less all interest-bearing debt.

Exhibit 1: Pinecrest Packaging, selected balance sheet data at 31 December (USD millions)

| Item | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash and equivalents | 1,240 | 980 | 1,110 |
| Short-term investments | 360 | 420 | 390 |
| Total assets | 24,600 | 22,300 | 21,000 |
| Accounts payable | 2,480 | 1,960 | 1,890 |
| Short-term bank borrowings | 900 | 350 | 300 |
| Current portion of long-term debt | 650 | 600 | 550 |
| Long-term debt | 7,900 | 7,400 | 7,300 |
| Total liabilities | 15,100 | 13,900 | 13,300 |

Exhibit 2: Pinecrest Packaging, selected income and cash flow data (USD millions)

| Item | 2025 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating income (EBIT) | 2,390 | 2,150 |
| Net income | 1,380 | 1,210 |
| Cash flow from operating activities | 1,720 | 1,840 |
| Cash flow from investing activities | (1,610) | (1,150) |
| Cash flow from financing activities | 150 | (820) |
| Income taxes paid | 410 | 380 |
| Interest paid | 480 | 450 |

Pinecrest classifies interest paid within operating activities.
During 2025 Pinecrest entered a supplier finance program under which a bank pays Pinecrest's suppliers early and Pinecrest settles with the bank 150 days after the invoice date, compared with 60 days previously.
At 31 December 2025, USD300 million of the accounts payable in Exhibit 1 was owed under this program.

Okafor computes that Pinecrest's 2024 balance-sheet-based accruals ratio was 6.7% and its 2024 cash-flow-based accruals ratio was 3.5%.

Okafor discusses the file with a junior analyst, Deshawn Pryor, who makes two statements.

Statement 1: "An IFRS peer that classifies interest paid in financing activities will report higher operating cash flow than Pinecrest for identical operations, so comparing the two companies' raw operating cash flow to operating income ratios flatters the peer."

Statement 2: "The supplier finance program raises operating liabilities and therefore raises Pinecrest's net operating assets, which increases the balance-sheet-based accruals ratio and makes Pinecrest's earnings quality look worse than it is."

## Questions

1. Okafor's first step, agreeing with her chief investment officer on the question to be answered, the two-week deadline and the use of public filings only, belongs to which phase of the financial statement analysis framework? (LOS: los-03-06-a)
   - A. Define the purpose and context of the analysis.
   - B. Collect input data.
   - C. Follow up.
2. Pinecrest's 2025 balance-sheet-based accruals ratio is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-e)
   - A. 9.2%.
   - B. 11.5%.
   - C. 12.2%.
3. Pinecrest's 2025 cash-flow-based accruals ratio is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-e)
   - A. $-2.1\%$.
   - B. 7.8%.
   - C. 9.8%.
4. Pinecrest's 2025 ratio of operating cash flow before interest and taxes to operating income is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-e)
   - A. 0.72.
   - B. 0.89.
   - C. 1.09.
5. Based on her analysis, Okafor's most appropriate conclusion about the agency's reasoning is that: (LOS: los-03-06-a)
   - A. the upgrade rationale is supported, because operating cash flow before interest and taxes still exceeds operating income.
   - B. the upgrade rationale is not supported, because both accruals ratios rose and cash support of operating income weakened.
   - C. no conclusion is possible, because the two accruals ratios measure different things.
6. Which of Pryor's statements is correct? (LOS: los-03-06-b)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Neither statement.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Articulating the question the analysis must answer, its deliverable, the time frame and the resource constraints is phase 1, define the purpose and context.
> B is wrong because collecting input data means gathering the filings themselves, which happens after the purpose is set.
> C is wrong because follow-up is the periodic repetition of the analysis after conclusions have been communicated.
> 2. **C.** Interest-bearing debt includes short-term bank borrowings, the current portion of long-term debt and long-term debt.
> 2025: operating assets $= 24{,}600 - 1{,}240 - 360 = 23{,}000$; operating liabilities $= 15{,}100 - 7{,}900 - 650 - 900 = 5{,}650$; NOA $= 17{,}350$.
> 2024: operating assets $= 22{,}300 - 980 - 420 = 20{,}900$; operating liabilities $= 13{,}900 - 7{,}400 - 600 - 350 = 5{,}550$; NOA $= 15{,}350$.
> Ratio $= (17{,}350 - 15{,}350) / [(17{,}350 + 15{,}350) / 2] = 2{,}000 / 16{,}350 = 0.1223 = 12.2\%$.
> A is wrong because it omits short-term bank borrowings from debt, leaving them in operating liabilities (NOA 16,450 and 15,000): $1{,}450 / 15{,}725 = 0.0922$.
> B is wrong because it divides the change by ending rather than average NOA: $2{,}000 / 17{,}350 = 0.1153$.
> 3. **B.** $NI - (CFO + CFI) = 1{,}380 - (1{,}720 - 1{,}610) = 1{,}380 - 110 = 1{,}270$.
> Ratio $= 1{,}270 / 16{,}350 = 0.0777 = 7.8\%$.
> A is wrong because it drops investing cash flow: $(1{,}380 - 1{,}720) / 16{,}350 = -0.0208$.
> C is wrong because it uses the investing outflow alone: $1{,}610 / 16{,}350 = 0.0985$.
> 4. **C.** Under US GAAP interest paid reduces operating cash flow, so both taxes and interest are added back: $(1{,}720 + 410 + 480) / 2{,}390 = 2{,}610 / 2{,}390 = 1.0921$.
> A is wrong because it compares raw operating cash flow with operating income: $1{,}720 / 2{,}390 = 0.7197$.
> B is wrong because it adds back taxes only, the treatment for an IFRS filer that classifies interest in financing: $(1{,}720 + 410) / 2{,}390 = 0.8912$.
> 5. **B.** The balance-sheet-based ratio rose from 6.7% to 12.2% and the cash-flow-based ratio rose from 3.5% to 7.8%, so both measures show a larger accrual component of earnings.
> Operating cash flow before interest and taxes to operating income fell from $(1{,}840 + 380 + 450) / 2{,}150 = 1.2419$ to $1.0921$.
> Operating cash flow before interest and taxes also fell in absolute terms, from 2,670 to 2,610, while net income rose, and USD300 million of year-end payables sat in a program that stretched payment terms from 60 to 150 days, which flatters 2025 operating cash flow.
> Rising accruals plus weakening cash support contradict "improving earnings quality."
> A is wrong because a ratio above 1 in one year does not offset a deteriorating trend in every quality measure.
> C is wrong because the two ratios moving in the same direction is itself a conclusion.
> 6. **A.** Statement 1 is correct: the IFRS peer's operating cash flow is not reduced by interest paid, so its raw ratio is higher for the same operations, and interest must be added back to Pinecrest's CFO before comparison.
> Statement 2 is incorrect: higher operating liabilities lower, not raise, net operating assets, so the program reduces the balance-sheet-based accrual and also lifts operating cash flow, making earnings quality look better, not worse.
> B is wrong because it accepts the reversed direction in Statement 2.
> C is wrong because Statement 1 correctly identifies the classification choice that breaks comparability.

## Distractor design

1. B: phase 2 confused with phase 1; C: phase 6 confused with phase 1.
2. A: short-term borrowings left in operating liabilities (misread exhibit); B: ending NOA as denominator (wrong formula variant).
3. A: investing cash flow dropped (wrong formula variant); C: investing outflow alone used (misread exhibit).
4. A: no add-backs (raw CFO); B: taxes only, the IFRS-interest-in-financing treatment applied to a US GAAP filer (IFRS vs US GAAP).
5. A: level read instead of trend; C: treats two consistent signals as contradictory.
6. B: sign error on the effect of operating liabilities on NOA; C: misses the interest classification difference.
