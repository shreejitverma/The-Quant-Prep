# Kestrelmoor Paper Compensation Cost Review

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Claire Dufresne, CFA, is a sell-side analyst at Laurentide Securities in Montreal.
She covers Kestrelmoor Paper Corp., a Canadian producer of containerboard and specialty paper that reports under IFRS.
Dufresne is reviewing how Kestrelmoor's labour and benefit costs flow through its FY2025 financial statements before she updates her FY2026 forecast.

Kestrelmoor pays all employees in cash every two weeks.
Every employee is also a member of a defined contribution plan to which Kestrelmoor contributes 5% of wages and salaries; Kestrelmoor has no obligation beyond the contribution.
Mill production workers' pay and the related plan contributions are included in the cost of paper produced.
Dufresne's working assumption is that 80% of FY2025 production was sold during FY2025, with the remainder in closing inventory, and that opening inventory can be ignored.

**Exhibit 1: FY2025 wages and salaries (CAD millions)**

| Employee group | Wages and salaries | DC contribution rate |
|---|---|---|
| Mill production workers | 84.0 | 5% |
| Head office and sales staff | 46.0 | 5% |

Kestrelmoor also sponsors a closed defined benefit plan for salaried employees hired before 2008.
On 31 December 2025, Kestrelmoor amended the plan to remove an early retirement subsidy, which reduced the obligation for past service.
The plan is in surplus, and Kestrelmoor's actuary confirms that no asset ceiling limits the net pension asset.
Kestrelmoor made no contributions in FY2025.

**Exhibit 2: Salaried defined benefit plan, FY2025 (CAD millions)**

| Obligation | Amount | Plan assets | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening obligation | 2,450.0 | Opening fair value | 2,730.0 |
| Current service cost | 38.0 | Actual return on plan assets | 60.0 |
| Past service cost (credit) from amendment | (25.0) | Employer contributions | 0.0 |
| Interest cost | 98.0 | Benefits paid | (140.0) |
| Benefits paid | (140.0) | | |
| Actuarial gain (discount rate increase) | (90.0) | | |
| Closing obligation | 2,331.0 | Closing fair value | 2,650.0 |

The discount rate was 4.0% at 1 January 2025 and 4.6% at 31 December 2025.
The asset allocation note discloses a long-term expected rate of return on plan assets of 6.25%.
The actuary estimates FY2026 current service cost at CAD 36.0 million and expects no plan amendments in FY2026.

Kestrelmoor grants equity-settled options and RSUs to senior managers each January.
Exhibit 3 shows the option valuation assumptions.

**Exhibit 3: Option valuation assumptions**

| Assumption | 2025 grants | 2026 grants (planned) |
|---|---|---|
| Expected volatility | 34% | 29% |
| Expected dividend yield | 1.2% | 1.8% |
| Expected life (years) | 5.5 | 6.0 |
| Risk-free rate | 3.1% | 3.1% |

Kestrelmoor's share price has risen about 20% since the January 2025 grants.

At a team meeting on 20 February 2026, Dufresne and her associate, Marc Ouellet, discuss the 2026 awards.

Dufresne states: "The lower volatility assumption will reduce the grant-date fair value of the 2026 RSU grants, and the 20% share price rise will increase the remaining expense on the 2025 grants."

Ouellet replies: "Holding the other inputs constant, the longer expected life raises the grant-date fair value of the 2026 options, while the higher dividend yield lowers it."

## Questions

1. The mill production workers' wages and plan contributions recognised in Kestrelmoor's FY2025 cost of sales are closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-a)
   - A. CAD 67.2 million.
   - B. CAD 70.6 million.
   - C. CAD 88.2 million.
2. The amount recognised in profit or loss for the salaried defined benefit plan in FY2025 is closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-d)
   - A. an expense of CAD 1.8 million.
   - B. an expense of CAD 24.2 million.
   - C. an expense of CAD 26.8 million.
3. Dufresne's forecast of the FY2026 amount recognised in profit or loss for the salaried defined benefit plan is closest to: (LOS: los-03-02-e)
   - A. an expense of CAD 21.3 million.
   - B. an expense of CAD 23.2 million.
   - C. an expense of CAD 36.0 million.
4. Regarding the statements made at the team meeting, which is most likely correct? (LOS: los-03-02-b)
   - A. Dufresne's statement only.
   - B. Ouellet's statement only.
   - C. Both statements.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Short-term benefits and defined contribution costs are measured undiscounted and recognised as the employee provides service, but when the employee makes inventory the cost is capitalised and reaches the income statement only as cost of sales when the goods are sold.
> Mill labour cost capitalised $= 84.0 \times (1 + 0.05) = 88.2000$; cost of sales $= 0.80 \times 88.2000 = 70.5600$, with $0.20 \times 88.2000 = 17.6400$ remaining in inventory.
> A is wrong because it leaves out the 5% defined contribution cost, as if contributions to the plan bypassed the sponsor's statements: $0.80 \times 84.0 = 67.2000$; the contribution is part of the employee's compensation cost.
> C is wrong because it expenses the full mill cost as incurred, as for head office staff, ignoring capitalisation into the 20% of production still in inventory.
> 2. **A.** Under IFRS, profit or loss contains service cost (current and past) plus net interest on the opening net pension liability or asset at the discount rate.
> Opening funded status $= 2{,}730.0 - 2{,}450.0 = 280.0$ (net asset), so net interest is income of $4.0\% \times 280.0 = 11.2000$.
> Profit or loss $= 38.0 - 25.0 - 11.2 = 1.8000$, an expense.
> B is wrong because it treats net interest on a surplus as an expense (sign error): $38.0 - 25.0 + 11.2 = 24.2000$.
> C is wrong because it leaves out the past service credit, which is the US GAAP treatment (past service cost goes to OCI first and is amortised); IFRS recognises it immediately in profit or loss: $38.0 - 11.2 = 26.8000$.
> 3. **A.** The FY2026 amount is FY2026 service cost plus net interest on the closing FY2025 net pension asset at the closing discount rate.
> Closing funded status $= 2{,}650.0 - 2{,}331.0 = 319.0$; net interest income $= 4.6\% \times 319.0 = 14.6740$.
> Forecast profit or loss $= 36.0 - 14.674 = 21.3260$, an expense.
> B is wrong because it applies the old 4.0% discount rate: $36.0 - 4.0\% \times 319.0 = 36.0 - 12.76 = 23.2400$.
> C is wrong because it drops net interest income, confusing the valuation rule that a surplus is ignored in the enterprise value bridge with the accounting, which still recognises net interest on a net pension asset when no asset ceiling applies.
> 4. **B.** Ouellet is correct: option fair value rises with a longer expected life and falls with a higher dividend yield, so the two changes pull in opposite directions.
> Dufresne is incorrect on both points: an RSU's fair value is the share price adjusted for dividends not received, so volatility does not enter; and grant-date fair value is measured once, so a later share price rise does not change the expense on the 2025 grants.
> A and C are wrong because each accepts Dufresne's statement.

## Distractor design

1. A omits the defined contribution cost from compensation; C ignores capitalisation into inventory and expenses production labour as incurred.
2. B reverses the sign of net interest on a surplus; C applies the US GAAP past service cost treatment to an IFRS reporter.
3. B uses the opening instead of the closing discount rate (period); C applies the valuation rule for surpluses to the income statement.
4. A and C apply option pricing inputs to RSUs and remeasure existing grants after a share price move.
