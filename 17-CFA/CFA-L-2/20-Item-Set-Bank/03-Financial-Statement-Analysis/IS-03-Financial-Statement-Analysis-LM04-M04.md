# Veradal Bank Acquires Osterholm Savings: The Pro Forma Balance Sheet

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Elin Marsh, CFA, is a fixed-income analyst at an insurance company that holds senior unsecured bonds of Veradal Bank, a listed commercial bank that reports in € millions.
Veradal has agreed to acquire 100% of Osterholm Savings Bank, a regional savings bank, for €2,690 million in cash.
The deal is expected to close on 31 December 2025, and Marsh must decide whether to keep the bonds.

Veradal will pay the purchase price from its central bank balances, which count as high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) and carry a 0% risk weight.
Management states that the fair value of Osterholm's identifiable net assets equals their book value.
Under Veradal's national regulator, goodwill and other intangible assets are deducted in full from Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital and carry no risk weight.
Osterholm has no Additional Tier 1 or Tier 2 instruments.

Exhibit 1: Standalone data at 31 December 2025, before the acquisition (€ millions)

| Item | Veradal | Osterholm |
|---|---|---|
| Total assets | 142,000 | 24,300 |
| Total shareholders' equity | 12,480 | 2,150 |
| Intangible assets (software and licences) | 1,720 | 120 |
| CET1 capital | 9,840 | 2,030 |
| Additional Tier 1 capital | 1,200 | 0 |
| Tier 2 capital | 1,650 | 0 |
| Risk-weighted assets (RWA) | 78,400 | 17,600 |
| HQLA | 21,300 | 4,100 |
| Expected net cash outflows over a 30-day stress scenario | 16,800 | 3,900 |

Veradal's board has set a minimum CET1 ratio target of 10.0% for the combined group, well above the 4.5% Basel III floor, to absorb its capital buffers.
If the combined group falls short of the target at closing, the chief financial officer, Rafael Quint, plans to sell residential mortgages carrying a 40% risk weight.
The sale would be at carrying amount with no gain or loss, and the proceeds would be held as central bank balances.

Marsh's colleague, Jonas Berg, notes that Veradal's standalone CET1 ratio is about 12.6% and Osterholm's about 11.5%.
He argues that the combined ratio must land between those two figures and so will exceed the board's target without any action.

Marsh then reviews the qualitative factors that the CAMELS framework does not capture.

Factor 1: Osterholm has twice restated its loan-loss provisions in the past five years, each time after provisioning slowly for problem loans and then recording a large write-down.

Factor 2: About 45% of Osterholm's loans are to fishing and fish-processing companies in a single coastal region, reflecting its founding mission as a community lender.

Factor 3: The national central bank has said that the combined group will be designated a domestic systemically important bank after the merger, making it the country's second-largest bank.

## Questions

1. Veradal's pro forma CET1 ratio at closing, before any mortgage sale, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-d)
   - A. 9.56%.
   - B. 9.69%.
   - C. 10.25%.
2. The minimum carrying amount of residential mortgages that Quint must sell to bring the pro forma CET1 ratio to the board's 10.0% target is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-d)
   - A. €4,200 million.
   - B. €7,500 million.
   - C. €10,500 million.
3. Veradal's pro forma liquidity coverage ratio at closing, before any mortgage sale, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-04-d)
   - A. 109.7%.
   - B. 116.0%.
   - C. 122.7%.
4. Which of Marsh's factors is most likely favourable for holders of Veradal's senior unsecured bonds? (LOS: los-03-04-e)
   - A. Factor 1.
   - B. Factor 2.
   - C. Factor 3.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** In consolidation, Osterholm's equity is eliminated against Veradal's investment, so group equity is unchanged, but group intangibles rise.
> Goodwill $= 2{,}690 - 2{,}150 = 540$, and Osterholm's own intangibles of 120 also enter the consolidated balance sheet.
> Pro forma CET1 $= 9{,}840 - 540 - 120 = 9{,}180$.
> Cross-check: $9{,}840 + 2{,}030 - 2{,}690 = 9{,}180$, because Osterholm's CET1 already excludes its 120 of intangibles.
> Pro forma RWA $= 78{,}400 + 17{,}600 = 96{,}000$; the cash paid carried a 0% weight and the deducted goodwill carries none, so RWA does not change for either.
> CET1 ratio $= 9{,}180 / 96{,}000 = 0.095625 = 9.56\%$, below the 10.0% target.
> Berg's reasoning fails because the purchase premium and the acquired intangibles are deducted from capital while all of Osterholm's RWA is added.
> B is wrong because it deducts goodwill but forgets Osterholm's intangibles: $9{,}300 / 96{,}000 = 9.69\%$.
> C is wrong because it makes no deduction at all: $9{,}840 / 96{,}000 = 10.25\%$, the error behind Berg's view.
> 2. **C.** RWA consistent with the target $= 9{,}180 / 0.10 = 91{,}800$.
> Required RWA reduction $= 96{,}000 - 91{,}800 = 4{,}200$.
> Replacing a 40%-weighted mortgage with a 0%-weighted central bank balance cuts RWA by 40% of the amount sold, so the sale must be $4{,}200 / 0.40 = 10{,}500$.
> Check: $9{,}180 / (96{,}000 - 0.40 \times 10{,}500) = 9{,}180 / 91{,}800 = 10.00\%$.
> A is wrong because it treats the required RWA reduction as the carrying amount of loans to sell, ignoring the 40% risk weight.
> B is wrong because it carries forward the goodwill-only CET1 of 9,300 from Question 1: $9{,}300 / 0.10 = 93{,}000$, reduction $3{,}000$, and $3{,}000 / 0.40 = 7{,}500$.
> 3. **A.** The cash paid leaves the group, so pro forma HQLA $= 21{,}300 - 2{,}690 + 4{,}100 = 22{,}710$.
> Pro forma net outflows $= 16{,}800 + 3{,}900 = 20{,}700$.
> $LCR = 22{,}710 / 20{,}700 = 1.0971 = 109.7\%$, still above 100% but equal to only about $1.0971 \times 30 = 32.9$ days of stress-level outflows.
> B is wrong because it averages the two standalone ratios: $(21{,}300 / 16{,}800 + 4{,}100 / 3{,}900) / 2 = (1.2679 + 1.0513) / 2 = 116.0\%$.
> C is wrong because it adds the two banks' HQLA without deducting the cash paid: $25{,}400 / 20{,}700 = 122.7\%$.
> 4. **C.** Factor 3 concerns government support, a bank-specific factor that CAMELS does not address.
> A bank's systemic importance, which rises with its size, makes government support in distress more likely, which favours senior creditors.
> A is wrong because slow provisioning followed by large write-downs, with restatements, is a corporate-culture warning sign and is unfavourable.
> B is wrong because a mission that concentrates lending in one regional industry exposes the bank to that industry's cycle and is unfavourable.

## Distractor design

1. B: goodwill deducted but the target's acquired intangibles overlooked (incomplete deduction); C: no intangible deduction at all (numerator not adjusted for the acquisition).
2. A: required RWA reduction taken as the loan amount (exposure versus risk-weighted amount); B: correct method on the goodwill-only CET1 from Question 1 (carried-forward error).
3. B: simple average of standalone ratios instead of pooled numerator and denominator (wrong aggregation); C: cash consideration not deducted from HQLA (misread transaction).
4. A: culture red flag read as favourable (direction error); B: mission-driven concentration read as favourable (direction error).
