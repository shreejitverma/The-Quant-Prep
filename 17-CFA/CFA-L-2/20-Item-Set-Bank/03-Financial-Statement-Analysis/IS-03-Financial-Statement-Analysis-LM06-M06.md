# Pellucid Optics: Testing Management's ROE Story

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Tomasz Wrona, CFA, is an equity analyst at a London family office that owns shares in Pellucid Optics plc, a UK manufacturer of spectacle and contact lenses that reports under IFRS.
In its 2025 management commentary, Pellucid states: "Return on equity rose to 18.9% from 15.4%, driven by improved operating efficiency, with operating margin up two percentage points to 15.0%."
The family office's chief investment officer asks Wrona to evaluate that statement before the office decides whether to add to its holding.
The office would hold any additional shares for at least five years, so it cares about the sustainable level of returns rather than the result of a single year.
Pellucid's annual executive bonus is paid on reported return on equity, with no adjustment for gains on disposals or one-off tax items.

Exhibit 1: Pellucid Optics plc, income statement data (GBP millions)

| Item | 2025 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 5,200 | 4,800 |
| Operating profit (EBIT) | 780 | 624 |
| Net finance costs | (60) | (64) |
| Profit before tax | 720 | 560 |
| Income tax expense | (144) | (140) |
| Profit for the year | 576 | 420 |
| Dividends paid | 230 | 210 |
| Number of retail optician outlets | 1,310 | 1,240 |

Operating profit for 2025 includes a GBP104 million gain on the sale of a distribution warehouse, presented within other operating income.
The gain is taxable at Pellucid's statutory rate of 25%.
The 2025 income tax expense also includes a one-off credit of GBP36 million from recognizing deferred tax assets on prior-year losses.
Pellucid reported no unusual items in 2024.

Exhibit 2: Pellucid Optics plc, balance sheet data at 31 December (GBP millions)

| Item | 2025 | 2024 (restated) | 2023 (as published) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total assets | 6,400 | 6,000 | 5,700 |
| Total equity | 3,200 | 2,900 | 2,860 |

In 2025 Pellucid changed its accounting policy for its defined benefit pension plan and applied the change retrospectively, restating 2024 comparatives.
At 1 January 2024, the change increased the net pension liability by GBP400 million and increased deferred tax assets by GBP100 million.
The 2023 column in Exhibit 2 is taken from Wrona's database, which holds the figures as originally published.
Pellucid has no non-controlling interests and no associates.
Wrona uses average balances for all balance sheet denominators.

Wrona's colleague, Harriet Blake, makes two statements while reviewing his work.

Statement 1: "Pellucid's 2023 balance sheet as originally published is not comparable with the restated 2024 balance sheet, so any 2024 ratio built on averages of the two years must first bring the 2023 figures onto the new pension policy."

Statement 2: "Pellucid presents the warehouse gain within operating profit, while its main peer presents disposal gains below operating profit; because both companies follow IFRS, their reported 2025 EBIT margins are directly comparable without adjustment."

## Questions

1. Pellucid's 2024 financial leverage, measured as average total assets divided by average total equity on a consistent accounting basis, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-d)
   - A. 2.03.
   - B. 2.07.
   - C. 2.16.
2. Pellucid's 2025 return on average equity, normalized to exclude the warehouse gain and the one-off tax credit, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-e)
   - A. 14.3%.
   - B. 15.1%.
   - C. 16.3%.
3. Wrona's most appropriate evaluation of the statement in Pellucid's management commentary is that: (LOS: los-03-06-a)
   - A. it is supported, because the reported EBIT margin rose from 13.0% to 15.0% while leverage fell.
   - B. it is not supported, because the rise in ROE came from non-recurring items, the normalized EBIT margin was unchanged and normalized ROE fell slightly.
   - C. it is not supported, because the rise in ROE came mainly from higher financial leverage.
4. Which of Blake's statements is correct? (LOS: los-03-06-b)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both statements.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** Impose the 1 January 2024 adjustments on the 31 December 2023 balances.
> Revised 2023 total assets $= 5{,}700 + 100 = 5{,}800$; revised 2023 equity $= 2{,}860 - 400 + 100 = 2{,}560$, so equity falls by the after-tax 300.
> Average total assets $= (6{,}000 + 5{,}800) / 2 = 5{,}900$; average equity $= (2{,}900 + 2{,}560) / 2 = 2{,}730$.
> Leverage $= 5{,}900 / 2{,}730 = 2.1612$.
> A is wrong because it averages the restated 2024 balances with the unrevised 2023 balances: $5{,}850 / 2{,}880 = 2.0312$, mixing two accounting policies in one denominator.
> B is wrong because it uses year-end rather than average balances: $6{,}000 / 2{,}900 = 2.0690$.
> 2. **B.** After-tax warehouse gain $= 104 \times (1 - 0.25) = 78$.
> Normalized profit $= 576 - 78 - 36 = 462$.
> Average 2025 equity $= (3{,}200 + 2{,}900) / 2 = 3{,}050$.
> Normalized ROE $= 462 / 3{,}050 = 0.1515 = 15.1\%$, against reported ROE of $576 / 3{,}050 = 18.89\%$.
> A is wrong because it removes the gain before tax, $576 - 104 - 36 = 436$ and $436 / 3{,}050 = 0.1430$, although the gain raised tax expense by 26.
> C is wrong because it removes the after-tax gain but leaves the one-off tax credit in profit: $(576 - 78) / 3{,}050 = 498 / 3{,}050 = 0.1633$.
> 3. **B.** Normalized 2025 EBIT $= 780 - 104 = 676$; EBIT margin $= 676 / 5{,}200 = 13.00\%$, identical to 2024's $624 / 4{,}800 = 13.00\%$.
> Normalized 2025 five-way DuPont: EBIT margin 0.1300, interest burden $616 / 676 = 0.9112$, tax burden $462 / 616 = 0.7500$, asset turnover $5{,}200 / 6{,}200 = 0.8387$, leverage $6{,}200 / 3{,}050 = 2.0328$; product $= 0.1515$.
> 2024: EBIT margin 0.1300, interest burden $560 / 624 = 0.8974$, tax burden $420 / 560 = 0.7500$, turnover $4{,}800 / 5{,}900 = 0.8136$, leverage 2.1612 from Question 1; product $= 420 / 2{,}730 = 0.1538$.
> Normalized ROE fell from 15.38% to 15.15%: better turnover and interest burden were more than offset by lower leverage, and the whole rise in reported ROE to 18.89% came from the warehouse gain and the tax credit.
> A is wrong because it reads the reported EBIT margin, which is inflated by a non-recurring gain the company chose to present inside operating profit.
> C is wrong because leverage fell from 2.1612 to 2.0328, so it reduced ROE.
> 4. **A.** Statement 1 is correct: the retrospective policy change reduced equity at 1 January 2024 by 300 and raised assets by 100, so averaging the published 2023 figures with restated 2024 figures mixes two policies, which is why the curriculum revises the earlier balance sheet before computing averages.
> Statement 2 is incorrect: IAS 1, which governs Pellucid's 2025 presentation, does not define operating profit, so companies following the same standards can place a disposal gain differently, and Pellucid's 2025 EBIT margin of 15.0% must be reduced to 13.0% before comparison with a peer that presents such gains below operating profit.
> B is wrong because it treats a common set of standards as a guarantee of comparable line items.
> C is wrong because Statement 2 ignores a presentation choice that inflates Pellucid's margin by two percentage points.

## Distractor design

1. A: unrevised 2023 balances averaged with restated 2024 balances (accounting policy mix); B: year-end instead of average balances (period error).
2. A: gain removed before tax instead of after tax (before-tax vs after-tax); C: one-off tax credit left in profit (incomplete normalization).
3. A: reported EBIT margin read without normalizing (non-recurring item accepted as operating); C: misread direction of leverage, which fell.
4. B: same standards assumed to give comparable presentation; C: accepts both, missing the presentation choice.
