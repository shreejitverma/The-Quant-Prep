# Solenne Energy: Reporting the Mistral Offshore Joint Venture

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Clara Duval is an equity analyst covering European utilities for a Paris-based investment bank.
She is comparing Solenne Energy SA with a Danish competitor, Nordhavn Vind A/S, ahead of a sector rating review.
Both companies report under IFRS.

Solenne owns 50% of Mistral Offshore, a wind farm operator.
A contractual agreement gives Solenne and its partner, a sovereign infrastructure fund, joint control over Mistral; unanimous consent is required for all relevant decisions.
Solenne accounts for Mistral using the equity method.
Solenne's investment in Mistral equals 50% of Mistral's book value of equity, and there is no excess purchase price.
There were no transactions between Solenne and Mistral in 2025.

Nordhavn owns 50% of a similar offshore venture and, citing the rare circumstances allowed under its reporting framework, presents it using proportionate consolidation.
To put Solenne on the same footing, Duval restates Solenne's 2025 statements as if it had used proportionate consolidation.
She also runs a third case in which the partner fund sells its governance rights to Solenne, giving Solenne control of Mistral while it keeps a 50% economic interest.
In that case the fair values of Mistral's identifiable net assets equal their book values and no goodwill arises.

Exhibit 1: 2025 financial data (€ millions)

| Item | Solenne (equity method, as reported) | Mistral Offshore (100%) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 2,400 | 500 |
| Operating income | 312 | 140 |
| Net income | 180 | 60 |
| Dividends paid | 70 | 20 |
| Total assets | 3,000 | 2,000 |
| Total liabilities | 1,800 | 1,200 |
| Shareholders' equity | 1,200 | 800 |

Solenne's net income includes €30 million of equity income from Mistral, and its total assets include the €400 million investment in Mistral.
Duval computes all ratios on year-end balances.
Net profit margin is net income attributable to Solenne's shareholders divided by revenue, and return on assets is net income attributable to Solenne's shareholders divided by total assets.
Leverage is measured as total liabilities divided by total equity.

Duval's manager, Étienne Roux, reviews her draft and adds two comments about the joint venture.

Comment 1: "Under both IFRS and US GAAP, the equity method is the required treatment for an interest in a joint venture; proportionate consolidation is acceptable only in rare circumstances, so Nordhavn's presentation is the exception, not Solenne's."

Comment 2: "If Solenne ever impaired its investment in Mistral and Mistral's value later recovered, Solenne could reverse the impairment, and a US GAAP venturer in the same position could reverse it too."

Roux asks Duval to state clearly in the report which of Solenne's ratios would move, and in which direction, if Solenne adopted Nordhavn's presentation.
He emphasizes that Mistral's net profit margin is well above Solenne's own operating business, and that Mistral turns over its assets much more slowly than Solenne does.

## Questions

1. Solenne's 2025 net profit margin, restated to proportionate consolidation, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-c)
   - A. 6.21%.
   - B. 6.79%.
   - C. 7.50%.
2. Solenne's 31 December 2025 ratio of total liabilities to total equity, restated to proportionate consolidation, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-c)
   - A. 1.50.
   - B. 1.88.
   - C. 2.00.
3. Which of Roux's comments is correct? (LOS: los-03-01-b)
   - A. Comment 1 only.
   - B. Comment 2 only.
   - C. Both comments.
4. Solenne's 2025 return on assets, restated to proportionate consolidation, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-c)
   - A. 4.50%.
   - B. 5.00%.
   - C. 6.00%.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Proportionate consolidation adds 50% of Mistral's revenue, so revenue is $2{,}400 + 0.50 \times 500 = 2{,}650$.
> Net income is unchanged at 180, because the equity income of $0.50 \times 60 = 30$ is replaced by 50% of the same revenues and expenses.
> Net profit margin $= 180 / 2{,}650 = 0.0679 = 6.79\%$.
> A is wrong because it adds 100% of Mistral's revenue, which is the control case, not proportionate consolidation ($180 / 2{,}900 = 6.21\%$).
> C is wrong because it is the equity method margin as reported ($180 / 2{,}400 = 7.50\%$).
> Even though Mistral's own margin ($60 / 500 = 12.0\%$) exceeds that of Solenne's own business ($150 / 2{,}400 = 6.25\%$), the margin falls, because net income is fixed while revenue rises.
> 2. **C.** Liabilities $= 1{,}800 + 0.50 \times 1{,}200 = 2{,}400$; equity is unchanged at 1,200 because proportionate consolidation adds equal net amounts of assets and liabilities.
> Ratio $= 2{,}400 / 1{,}200 = 2.00$.
> A is wrong because it is the equity method ratio ($1{,}800 / 1{,}200 = 1.50$).
> B is wrong because it is the control case, with 100% of Mistral's liabilities and a non-controlling interest of $0.50 \times 800 = 400$ added to equity ($3{,}000 / 1{,}600 = 1.875$).
> 3. **A.** Comment 1 is correct: both IFRS and US GAAP require the equity method for joint ventures and permit proportionate consolidation only in rare circumstances.
> Comment 2 is incorrect: IFRS allows reversal of an impairment of an equity method investment, but US GAAP prohibits reversal.
> B is wrong because it accepts the reversal claim and rejects the correct statement on method.
> C is wrong because Comment 2 misstates US GAAP.
> 4. **B.** Assets under proportionate consolidation $= 3{,}000 - 400 + 0.50 \times 2{,}000 = 3{,}600$, because the investment line is replaced by 50% of Mistral's assets.
> Return on assets $= 180 / 3{,}600 = 0.0500 = 5.00\%$.
> A is wrong because it adds 50% of Mistral's assets without removing the 400 investment line ($180 / 4{,}000 = 4.50\%$).
> C is wrong because it is the equity method ratio ($180 / 3{,}000 = 6.00\%$).

## Distractor design

1. A: full consolidation used instead of proportionate (wrong method variant); C: the as-reported equity method margin (no restatement).
2. A: the as-reported equity method ratio; B: control case with the NCI added to equity (wrong method variant).
3. B: US GAAP impairment reversal treated as allowed (IFRS vs US GAAP); C: accepts both comments.
4. A: investment line not eliminated (double counting of the venture's net assets); C: the as-reported equity method ROA.
