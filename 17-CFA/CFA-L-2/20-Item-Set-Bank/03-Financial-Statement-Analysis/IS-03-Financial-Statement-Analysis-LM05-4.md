# Nordvik Packaging: Balance Sheet Quality Before an Audit Committee Meeting

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Sofia Lindahl, CFA, is a stewardship analyst at Oresund Pension Fund, which owns 3% of Nordvik Packaging ASA.
Nordvik manufactures corrugated and folding-carton packaging, reports under IFRS with a December year end, and presents its statements in € millions.
Lindahl is preparing for a meeting with Nordvik's audit committee and wants to raise specific concerns about the quality of the 2025 balance sheet.

Exhibit 1: Nordvik Packaging, selected data at and for the year ended 31 December 2025 (€ millions unless noted)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Total assets | 7,400.0 |
| Total liabilities | 4,020.0 |
| Total equity | 3,380.0 |
| Goodwill | 3,050.0 |
| Other intangible assets | 910.0 |
| Sales | 6,200.0 |
| Pre-tax income | 580.0 |
| Net income | 434.0 |
| Shares outstanding (millions) | 84.5 |
| Share price, 31 December 2025 (€) | 31.20 |
| Share price, 12 March 2026 (€) | 27.40 |

Nordvik owns 48% of Fjordline Cartons AS and accounts for it using the equity method.
Fjordline reported 2025 sales of €1,450 million and net income of €112 million.
Nordvik's two closest peers hold similar stakes in their joint ventures but consolidate them.

A commitments note discloses a take-or-pay contract under which Nordvik must pay €120 million at the end of each year from 2026 to 2030 for containerboard, whether or not it takes delivery.
The obligation is not recognized on the balance sheet.
Lindahl uses Nordvik's incremental borrowing rate of 6% to value such commitments.

On 1 January 2025, Nordvik acquired Hallsberg Kartong AB.
Management allocated €120 million of the purchase price to customer relationships, amortized over 8 years, and the remainder of the excess over identifiable net assets to goodwill.
An independent appraisal commissioned by a group of shareholders valued the same customer relationships at €360 million with the same 8-year life.
No goodwill impairment was recognized in 2025, and Nordvik's tax rate is 22%.

Lindahl lists three further concerns and classifies each by the balance sheet quality attribute it affects:

- Concern 1: The take-or-pay commitment is not recognized on the balance sheet; she classifies this as a lack of completeness.
- Concern 2: A deferred tax asset of €64 million relates to tax losses at a German subsidiary that has reported losses for three consecutive years, and the full amount remains recognized with no assessment of whether future taxable profit is probable; she classifies this as a lack of clear presentation.
- Concern 3: A single line, "Other operating assets and liabilities, net," equals 18% of total assets and is not broken down in the notes; she classifies this as a lack of clear presentation.

Nordvik replaced its external auditor in 2023 and again in 2025.
The new auditor issued an unqualified opinion dated 12 March 2026.
Lindahl's colleague Henrik Aas makes two statements.

Statement 1: "The unqualified opinion is our most timely assurance that Nordvik has no reporting problem, so we should not dwell on the balance sheet at the meeting."

Statement 2: "Two auditor changes in three years are in themselves a warning sign that we should raise with the committee."

## Questions

1. The value that the market implicitly assigns to all of Nordvik's assets other than goodwill at 31 December 2025 is closest to: (LOS: los-03-05-k)
   - A. $-1{,}323.6$ million.
   - B. $-413.6$ million.
   - C. $+330.0$ million.
2. Nordvik's 2025 return on sales, adjusted to be comparable with its peers, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-05-k)
   - A. 5.67%.
   - B. 6.29%.
   - C. 7.00%.
3. After constructively capitalizing the take-or-pay contract, Nordvik's ratio of total liabilities to total assets is closest to: (LOS: los-03-05-k)
   - A. 57.2%.
   - B. 57.8%.
   - C. 61.2%.
4. Which of Lindahl's classifications of Concerns 1, 2, and 3 are correct? (LOS: los-03-05-j)
   - A. Concern 1 only.
   - B. Concerns 1 and 3 only.
   - C. Concerns 1, 2, and 3.
5. Which of Aas's statements is correct? (LOS: los-03-05-m)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Both statements.
   - C. Statement 2 only.
6. If the appraisal's allocation to customer relationships is correct, Nordvik's 2025 net income is: (LOS: los-03-05-b)
   - A. overstated by €23.4 million.
   - B. overstated by €30.0 million.
   - C. understated by €23.4 million.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Market capitalization $= 84.5 \times 31.20 = 2{,}636.4$ million; implied value of all non-goodwill assets $= 2{,}636.4 - 3{,}050.0 = -413.6$ million.
> A negative value means the market assigns less than nothing to every other asset, so goodwill appears overvalued and an impairment is likely; goodwill plus other intangibles is also $(3{,}050.0 + 910.0)/7{,}400.0 = 53.5\%$ of total assets.
> A is wrong because it deducts other intangible assets as well ($2{,}636.4 - 3{,}050.0 - 910.0 = -1{,}323.6$); intangibles are among the non-goodwill assets whose implied value is being measured.
> C is wrong because it uses book value of equity instead of market capitalization ($3{,}380.0 - 3{,}050.0 = 330.0$), which cannot reveal what the market thinks goodwill is worth.
> 2. **B.** Nordvik's net income includes 48% of Fjordline's profit but its sales exclude any of Fjordline's sales, so the reported margin is overstated.
> Share of investee sales $= 0.48 \times 1{,}450.0 = 696.0$; adjusted return on sales $= 434.0/(6{,}200.0 + 696.0) = 434.0/6{,}896.0 = 6.29\%$.
> A is wrong because it adds 100% of Fjordline's sales ($434.0/7{,}650.0 = 5.67\%$) while income includes only 48% of Fjordline's profit.
> C is wrong because it is the reported margin ($434.0/6{,}200.0 = 7.00\%$), which is exactly the overstated figure.
> 3. **A.** $PV = 120 \times [1 - 1.06^{-5}]/0.06 = 120 \times 4.2124 = 505.48$.
> Adjusted ratio $= (4{,}020.0 + 505.48)/(7{,}400.0 + 505.48) = 4{,}525.48/7{,}905.48 = 57.25\%$, against a reported $4{,}020.0/7{,}400.0 = 54.32\%$.
> B is wrong because it adds the undiscounted payments of $5 \times 120 = 600$ ($4{,}620.0/8{,}000.0 = 57.75\%$).
> C is wrong because it adds the present value to liabilities but not to assets ($4{,}525.48/7{,}400.0 = 61.16\%$); constructive capitalization recognizes a right-type asset and a liability of the same amount.
> 4. **B.** Concern 1 is correctly classified: an unrecognized obligation is a completeness failure.
> Concern 3 is correctly classified: a large aggregated line that is not disaggregated in the notes is a presentation failure.
> Concern 2 is misclassified: a deferred tax asset carried in full despite recurring losses (under IFRS it is recognized only to the extent future taxable profit is probable; under US GAAP a valuation allowance would be required) is a failure of unbiased measurement, not presentation.
> A is wrong because Concern 3 is also correctly classified.
> C is wrong because Concern 2 is a measurement problem.
> 5. **C.** Auditor changes, especially repeated ones, are a warning sign worth investigating, so Statement 2 is correct.
> Statement 1 is incorrect because the audit opinion is among the least timely sources of information about risk: it relates to historical statements and is issued well after the period, and serious problems have often surfaced before any audit opinion reflected them.
> A is wrong because it treats the audit opinion as timely assurance.
> B is wrong because Statement 1 is incorrect.
> 6. **A.** Allocating 120 instead of 360 to customer relationships moves 240 into goodwill, which is not amortized.
> Amortization is understated by $(360 - 120)/8 = 30.0$ pre-tax; after tax $30.0 \times (1 - 0.22) = 23.4$, so net income is overstated by 23.4 for as long as goodwill is not impaired.
> B is wrong because it ignores the tax effect.
> C is wrong because it reverses the sign: understating amortizable intangibles lowers expense and raises reported earnings.

## Distractor design

1. A: other intangibles deducted along with goodwill (wrong formula variant); C: book equity used instead of market capitalization (misread exhibit, decoy number).
2. A: 100% of investee sales added instead of the ownership share (proportion error); C: reported margin with no gross-up (adjustment omitted).
3. B: undiscounted payments added (present value omitted); C: liability added without the matching asset (one-sided adjustment).
4. A: presentation failure missed for the aggregated line (wrong attribute); C: measurement failure called presentation (wrong attribute).
5. A: audit opinion treated as timely (timeliness error); B: accepts the timeliness error alongside the correct warning sign.
6. B: tax shield ignored (before-tax vs after-tax error); C: direction of the earnings effect reversed (sign error).
