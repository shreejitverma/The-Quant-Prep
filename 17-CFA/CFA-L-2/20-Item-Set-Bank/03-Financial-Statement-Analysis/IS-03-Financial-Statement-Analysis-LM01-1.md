# Veldhoven Chemie: Securities Portfolio and an Associate

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Ingrid Maas, CFA, covers specialty chemicals for a Rotterdam asset manager.
She is preparing the 2025 earnings review of Veldhoven Chemie NV, which reports under IFRS and presents its statements in € thousands.
Veldhoven has a December year end and bought every position described below on 1 January 2025.

Veldhoven holds three securities in its treasury portfolio.
Security P is a block of listed equity shares held for trading.
Security Q is a debt instrument held within a business model whose objective is achieved both by collecting contractual cash flows and by selling; its cash flows are solely payments of principal and interest.
Security R is a five-year bond with a 6% annual coupon, held to collect contractual cash flows that are solely payments of principal and interest.
The market yield on bonds of R's risk was 5.0% on the purchase date.

Exhibit 1: Treasury portfolio (€ thousands)

| Security | Par value | Price paid 1 Jan 2025 | Fair value 31 Dec 2025 | 2025 cash received |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | n/a | 18,000 | 21,400 | Dividend 600 |
| Q | 30,000 | 30,000 | 28,900 | Coupon 1,200 (4%) |
| R | 40,000 | 41,732 | 41,100 | Coupon 2,400 (6%) |

On the same date Veldhoven paid €105,000 thousand for 30% of Lindqvist Polymer AB and obtained two of seven board seats.
Exhibit 2 summarizes Lindqvist at acquisition.

Exhibit 2: Lindqvist Polymer AB at 1 January 2025 (€ thousands)

| Item | Book value | Fair value | Remaining life |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant and equipment | 310,000 | 350,000 | 8 years |
| Land | 60,000 | 80,000 | Indefinite |
| Other net assets | (120,000) | (120,000) | n/a |
| Net assets | 250,000 | 310,000 | |

Lindqvist reported 2025 revenue of €410,000 thousand, net income of €36,000 thousand, and paid dividends of €12,000 thousand.
During 2025 Lindqvist sold resin to Veldhoven for €20,000 thousand; the resin had cost Lindqvist €14,000 thousand.
At 31 December 2025, 30% of that resin remained in Veldhoven's inventory.
The quoted market value of Veldhoven's 30% stake was €112,500 thousand at 31 December 2025.

Maas discusses the associate with her colleague Pieter Bakker, who covers a US GAAP peer.
Bakker makes two statements.

Statement 1: "If Veldhoven ever impairs the Lindqvist investment and the recoverable amount later recovers, Veldhoven may reverse the impairment, whereas my US GAAP company could not reverse an impairment of an equity method investment."

Statement 2: "Veldhoven could elect to measure the Lindqvist stake at fair value through profit or loss, exactly as my US GAAP company can for any of its equity method investments."

Maas also wants to show clients how much Veldhoven's classification choices matter.
She asks what 2025 pre-tax profit would have looked like had Veldhoven's board seats not existed, so that it lacked significant influence, and had it irrevocably elected fair value through other comprehensive income for the Lindqvist shares.
She ignores taxes throughout.

## Questions

1. The carrying value of Veldhoven's treasury portfolio (Securities P, Q and R) at 31 December 2025, in € thousands, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-a)
   - A. 91,400.
   - B. 91,719.
   - C. 92,032.
2. The pre-tax profit recognized in 2025 from Securities P, Q and R combined, in € thousands, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-a)
   - A. 6,187.
   - B. 7,287.
   - C. 7,600.
3. The goodwill embedded in Veldhoven's investment in Lindqvist, in € thousands, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-a)
   - A. 12,000.
   - B. 18,000.
   - C. 30,000.
4. The carrying amount of Veldhoven's investment in Lindqvist at 31 December 2025, in € thousands, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-01-a)
   - A. 108,900.
   - B. 110,160.
   - C. 111,660.
5. Which of Bakker's statements is correct? (LOS: los-03-01-b)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both statements.
6. Relative to the equity method as reported, Veldhoven's 2025 pre-tax profit under Maas's FVOCI scenario would have been: (LOS: los-03-01-c)
   - A. lower by 7,200.
   - B. lower by 5,160.
   - C. higher by 2,340.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** FVPL and FVOCI securities are carried at fair value; the amortized cost bond is carried at cost adjusted by effective interest amortization.
> For R, interest income $= 0.05 \times 41{,}732 = 2{,}086.6$, the coupon is $0.06 \times 40{,}000 = 2{,}400$, so premium amortization is $2{,}400 - 2{,}086.6 = 313.4$ and the carrying value is $41{,}732 - 313.4 = 41{,}418.6$.
> Portfolio $= 21{,}400 + 28{,}900 + 41{,}418.6 = 91{,}718.6$.
> A is wrong because it marks R to its fair value of 41,100, which applies only to FVPL and FVOCI assets ($21{,}400 + 28{,}900 + 41{,}100 = 91{,}400$).
> C is wrong because it leaves R at its purchase price and ignores premium amortization ($21{,}400 + 28{,}900 + 41{,}732 = 92{,}032$).
> 2. **B.** P (FVPL): dividend plus fair value change $= 600 + (21{,}400 - 18{,}000) = 4{,}000$.
> Q (FVOCI debt): interest of $0.04 \times 30{,}000 = 1{,}200$ in profit or loss; the $28{,}900 - 30{,}000 = -1{,}100$ fair value change goes to OCI.
> R (amortized cost): effective interest of $2{,}086.6$.
> Total $= 4{,}000 + 1{,}200 + 2{,}086.6 = 7{,}286.6$.
> A is wrong because it runs Q's 1,100 fair value loss through profit or loss ($7{,}286.6 - 1{,}100 = 6{,}186.6$).
> C is wrong because it records R's 2,400 coupon rather than effective interest ($4{,}000 + 1{,}200 + 2{,}400 = 7{,}600$).
> 3. **A.** Excess purchase price $= 105{,}000 - 0.30 \times 250{,}000 = 105{,}000 - 75{,}000 = 30{,}000$.
> Step-ups: plant $0.30 \times 40{,}000 = 12{,}000$; land $0.30 \times 20{,}000 = 6{,}000$.
> Goodwill $= 30{,}000 - 12{,}000 - 6{,}000 = 12{,}000$, which equals $105{,}000 - 0.30 \times 310{,}000 = 12{,}000$.
> B is wrong because it allocates only the plant step-up and leaves the land step-up in goodwill, confusing "not amortized" with "not allocated".
> C is wrong because it reports the whole excess over book value as goodwill.
> 4. **B.** Plant step-up amortization $= 12{,}000 / 8 = 1{,}500$; land and goodwill are not amortized.
> Unrealized profit on the upstream sale $= (20{,}000 - 14{,}000) \times 0.30 = 1{,}800$; Veldhoven eliminates its share, $0.30 \times 1{,}800 = 540$.
> Equity income $= 0.30 \times 36{,}000 - 1{,}500 - 540 = 10{,}800 - 1{,}500 - 540 = 8{,}760$.
> Carrying amount $= 105{,}000 + 8{,}760 - 0.30 \times 12{,}000 = 105{,}000 + 8{,}760 - 3{,}600 = 110{,}160$.
> Check: $0.30 \times (250{,}000 + 36{,}000 - 1{,}800 - 12{,}000) + (30{,}000 - 1{,}500) = 81{,}660 + 28{,}500 = 110{,}160$.
> A is wrong because it eliminates 100% of the unrealized profit rather than the 30% share ($105{,}000 + 10{,}800 - 1{,}500 - 1{,}800 - 3{,}600 = 108{,}900$).
> C is wrong because it omits the 1,500 amortization of the plant step-up ($105{,}000 + 10{,}800 - 540 - 3{,}600 = 111{,}660$).
> 5. **A.** Statement 1 is correct: IFRS permits reversal of an impairment of an equity method investment up to the recoverable amount, and US GAAP prohibits reversal.
> Statement 2 is incorrect: US GAAP makes the fair value option available to all entities, but IFRS restricts it to venture capital organizations, mutual funds, unit trusts and similar entities, which does not describe a chemicals producer.
> B is wrong because it accepts the unrestricted fair value option under IFRS and rejects a correct statement about reversal.
> C is wrong because Statement 2 misstates the IFRS restriction.
> 6. **B.** Under the FVOCI election only the dividend reaches profit or loss: $0.30 \times 12{,}000 = 3{,}600$.
> The equity method recognized $8{,}760$ of equity income (Question 4).
> Difference $= 3{,}600 - 8{,}760 = -5{,}160$, so pre-tax profit would have been 5,160 lower.
> A is wrong because it compares the dividend with the unadjusted 30% share of net income and ignores amortization and the unrealized profit elimination ($3{,}600 - 10{,}800 = -7{,}200$).
> C is wrong because it runs the fair value gain through profit or loss, which is the FVPL treatment, not FVOCI ($3{,}600 + (112{,}500 - 105{,}000) - 8{,}760 = 2{,}340$).

## Distractor design

1. A: misread category (amortized cost asset marked to fair value); C: premium amortization omitted (period error on the effective interest roll-forward).
2. A: OCI item run through profit or loss (FVOCI treated as FVPL); C: coupon used instead of effective interest (wrong formula variant).
3. B: land step-up left in goodwill (partial allocation); C: whole excess over book value called goodwill.
4. A: 100% rather than ownership share of unrealized profit eliminated; C: step-up amortization omitted.
5. B: IFRS fair value option treated as unrestricted (IFRS vs US GAAP swap); C: accepts the unrestricted option alongside the correct reversal rule.
6. A: naive share of net income used as equity income (amortization and elimination omitted); C: FVOCI fair value change placed in profit or loss (category error).
