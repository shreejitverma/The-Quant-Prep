# Solvang Maritime: ROE With and Without the Port Associate

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Henrik Aas, CFA, is an equity analyst at a Norwegian public pension fund.
His portfolio manager, Marte Solberg, is considering Solvang Maritime ASA, a container shipping and logistics group that reports under IFRS, as a long-term core holding.
Solberg notes that Solvang's reported return on equity rose sharply in 2025 and asks Aas whether the improvement comes from Solvang's own operations and whether it is sustainable.

Aas describes his work plan in four steps.

Step 1: He downloads Solvang's annual reports for 2023 to 2025 and the annual report of Solvang's listed associate.
Step 2: He removes the associate from Solvang's profit and total assets and recomputes the DuPont ratios on the adjusted figures.
Step 3: He will present a written recommendation to the investment committee.
Step 4: He will update the analysis each quarter after Solvang reports.

Solvang owns 31% of Fjordhavn Terminal ASA, a port operator, and accounts for it using the equity method.
In 2025, Fjordhavn acquired control of a terminal in which it previously held a joint interest, and remeasured its original interest to fair value.
Solvang's share of that remeasurement gain was NOK1,300 million and is included in Solvang's 2025 income from associates.
Solvang presents income from associates below income tax expense.

Exhibit 1: Solvang Maritime ASA, selected income statement data (NOK millions)

| Item | 2025 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 42,600 | 40,100 |
| Operating profit (EBIT) | 4,260 | 4,410 |
| Profit before tax and associates | 3,780 | 3,990 |
| Income tax expense | (945) | (998) |
| Income from associates | 2,150 | 610 |
| Profit for the year | 4,985 | 3,602 |
| Dividends paid | 1,650 | 1,420 |

Exhibit 2: Solvang Maritime ASA, selected balance sheet data at 31 December (NOK millions)

| Item | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investment in associates | 9,900 | 7,600 | 7,100 |
| Total assets | 61,800 | 57,400 | 55,200 |
| Total equity | 29,300 | 26,900 | 25,500 |
| Number of vessels operated | 118 | 112 | 109 |

Solvang has no non-controlling interests, and equity in Exhibit 2 is attributable entirely to Solvang's shareholders.
Aas uses average balances for all balance sheet denominators.
When he removes the associate, he leaves the leverage ratio at its consolidated value.

Aas reviews his preliminary findings with a colleague, Ingrid Haugen, who makes two statements.

Statement 1: "Income from associates is an after-tax figure with no revenue behind it on Solvang's income statement, so leaving it in the DuPont analysis overstates Solvang's net profit margin, and leaving the investment in total assets understates its asset turnover."

Statement 2: "Solvang appoints two of Fjordhavn's nine directors, so Fjordhavn's returns are effectively under Solvang management's control and belong in any assessment of Solvang's core operating performance."

Solberg adds that, for her decision, the key question is whether the 2025 increase in reported return on equity reflects better operating performance or the associate.

## Questions

1. Aas's Step 2 belongs to which phase of the financial statement analysis framework? (LOS: los-03-06-a)
   - A. Collect input data.
   - B. Process data.
   - C. Develop and communicate conclusions.
2. Solvang's 2025 net profit margin excluding the associate is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-c)
   - A. 6.65%.
   - B. 8.65%.
   - C. 11.70%.
3. Solvang's 2025 return on equity excluding the associate is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-c)
   - A. 10.09%.
   - B. 11.83%.
   - C. 14.65%.
4. In the five-way DuPont decomposition excluding the associate, Solvang's 2025 tax burden is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-c)
   - A. 0.665.
   - B. 0.750.
   - C. 1.319.
5. Which of Haugen's statements is correct? (LOS: los-03-06-b)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both statements.
6. The answer to Solberg's key question is most likely that from 2024 to 2025: (LOS: los-03-06-a)
   - A. core profitability improved, because asset turnover excluding the associate rose.
   - B. the rise in reported ROE came from the associate, because ROE excluding the associate fell.
   - C. the rise in reported ROE came mainly from higher financial leverage.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Removing the equity-method investee and recomputing DuPont ratios converts collected data into analytically useful form, which is the processing phase; in practice the curriculum treats processing and interpretation together.
> A is wrong because collecting input data is Step 1, gathering the annual reports.
> C is wrong because developing and communicating conclusions is Step 3, the written recommendation.
> 2. **A.** Profit excluding associates $= 4{,}985 - 2{,}150 = 2{,}835$.
> Margin $= 2{,}835 / 42{,}600 = 0.0665 = 6.65\%$.
> B is wrong because it removes only the 1,300 remeasurement gain and leaves the recurring associate income in: $(4{,}985 - 1{,}300) / 42{,}600 = 0.0865$.
> C is wrong because it is the reported margin with the associate left in: $4{,}985 / 42{,}600 = 0.1170$.
> 3. **B.** Average total assets excluding associates $= [(61{,}800 - 9{,}900) + (57{,}400 - 7{,}600)] / 2 = (51{,}900 + 49{,}800) / 2 = 50{,}850$.
> Asset turnover excluding associates $= 42{,}600 / 50{,}850 = 0.8378$.
> Leverage (unadjusted) $= [(61{,}800 + 57{,}400) / 2] / [(29{,}300 + 26{,}900) / 2] = 59{,}600 / 28{,}100 = 2.1210$.
> ROE excluding associates $= 0.06655 \times 0.8378 \times 2.1210 = 0.1183 = 11.83\%$.
> Reported ROE is $4{,}985 / 28{,}100 = 17.74\%$, so the associate contributed $17.7402\% - 11.8250\% = 5.92$ percentage points.
> A is wrong because it adjusts the numerator but leaves the associate in the asset base, using reported turnover $42{,}600 / 59{,}600 = 0.7148$: $0.06655 \times 0.7148 \times 2.1210 = 0.1009$.
> C is wrong because it also strips the investment out of equity and recomputes leverage as $50{,}850 / 19{,}350 = 2.6279$, double counting the asset removal: $0.06655 \times 0.8378 \times 2.6279 = 0.1465$.
> 4. **B.** Tax burden excluding associates $= (NI - \text{Income from associates}) / EBT = 2{,}835 / 3{,}780 = 0.7500$.
> Check: $0.7500 \times (3{,}780 / 4{,}260) \times (4{,}260 / 42{,}600) = 0.7500 \times 0.8873 \times 0.1000 = 0.0665$, the margin from Question 2.
> A is wrong because it divides adjusted profit by EBIT instead of EBT, $2{,}835 / 4{,}260 = 0.665$, which merges the tax and interest burdens.
> C is wrong because it uses reported profit including associates over EBT, $4{,}985 / 3{,}780 = 1.3188$, a tax burden above 1 that signals the commingling problem.
> 5. **A.** Statement 1 is correct: associate income arrives after tax with no matching sales, so it inflates the margin, and the investment sits in total assets without generating sales, so it depresses turnover.
> Statement 2 is incorrect: two of nine board seats give significant influence, not control, and the associate's returns are not under Solvang management's control, which is why the curriculum separates them from the core business.
> B is wrong because it accepts the control claim and rejects the correct mechanics.
> C is wrong because Statement 2 confuses significant influence with control.
> 6. **B.** Reported ROE rose from $3{,}602 / 26{,}200 = 13.75\%$ in 2024 to $17.74\%$ in 2025.
> ROE excluding the associate fell from $0.0746 \times 0.8192 \times 2.1489 = 13.13\%$ in 2024 to $11.83\%$ in 2025, so the associate's contribution rose from $0.61$ to $5.92$ percentage points, most of it the non-recurring 1,300 remeasurement gain.
> A is wrong because it reads one factor: turnover excluding associates did rise from 0.8192 to 0.8378, but the margin excluding associates fell from 7.46% to 6.65%, and the product fell.
> C is wrong because leverage fell from 2.1489 to 2.1210.

## Distractor design

1. A: Step 1 activity attributed to Step 2 (phase confusion); C: phase 5 reporting confused with processing.
2. B: only the one-off gain removed (partial normalization); C: associate income left in the margin (commingled numerator).
3. A: numerator adjusted but not the asset base (turnover not adjusted); C: leverage adjusted as well (double counting the asset removal).
4. A: EBIT used in place of EBT (wrong formula variant); C: reported profit including associates used (commingled numerator).
5. B: significant influence treated as control; C: accepts both, missing the control error.
6. A: single-factor reading of turnover; C: misread exhibit, leverage actually fell.
