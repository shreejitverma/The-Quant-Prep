# Meridian Offshore Board Currency Decision

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Meridian Offshore Services Inc. is a US provider of offshore support vessels that reports under US GAAP with the US dollar (USD) as its presentation currency.
Its UK subsidiary, Meridian Offshore Services UK Ltd (MOS-UK), was formed on 1 January 2021 when it issued common stock to the parent and bought its fleet of vessels.
MOS-UK charters vessels to North Sea operators under contracts priced in USD, and it buys fuel and vessels in USD.
Its crew salaries and local administration costs are paid in pounds sterling (GBP), and it carries a GBP bank loan from a UK lender.
Meridian designates the USD as MOS-UK's functional currency.
Meridian's other foreign subsidiaries use their local currencies as functional currencies.

At a board meeting on 10 February 2026, director Fiona Halloran questions the designation.

Halloran: "MOS-UK is located in the UK, keeps its books in pounds and borrows in pounds, so its functional currency ought to be the pound."

Chief financial officer Rafael Ortiz lists three MOS-UK items from December 2025 to illustrate its exposures: a USD4.1 million charter invoice to a Norwegian operator on 30-day terms, a GBP5.2 million repair invoice from a Scottish shipyard payable in 60 days, and GBP1.8 million of crew salaries paid in GBP on the dates they were earned.

Ortiz proposes that on 1 January 2026 Meridian inject equity equal to GBP64,000 thousand, which MOS-UK would use to repay the bank loan in full.
Halloran supports the plan: "Repaying the loan removes our currency exposure, and with the pound expected to weaken to USD1.18 by 31 December 2026, it will also help 2026 earnings."

Ortiz also compares Meridian with Caspian Marine Corp., a US peer that designates local currencies as functional for all of its foreign subsidiaries.

## Questions

1. The most appropriate response to Halloran's comment on the functional currency is that it is: (LOS: los-03-03-a)
   - A. correct, because the currency of the country of location and of the bank loan determines the functional currency.
   - B. incorrect, because a subsidiary's functional currency is always its parent's presentation currency.
   - C. incorrect, because the currency that mainly influences sales prices and costs of vessels and fuel is the USD, and those indicators take priority.
2. Of the three items Ortiz lists, the one that creates foreign currency transaction exposure for MOS-UK is the: (LOS: los-03-03-b)
   - A. charter invoice to the Norwegian operator.
   - B. repair invoice from the Scottish shipyard.
   - C. crew salaries.
3. MOS-UK's balance sheet exposure at 31 December 2025 under its current designation, and if the GBP were designated as functional currency, is closest to (GBP thousands): (LOS: los-03-03-d)
   - A. -31,300 and 111,000
   - B. -40,000 and 111,000
   - C. -31,300 and 196,000
4. Under the current designation, MOS-UK's retained earnings in Meridian's consolidated statements at 31 December 2025 are closest to (USD thousands): (LOS: los-03-03-e)
   - A. 38,750
   - B. 41,026
   - C. 44,866
5. Assuming MOS-UK's other monetary balances stay at their 31 December 2025 levels and the GBP moves to USD1.18 by 31 December 2026, Ortiz's refinancing would change MOS-UK's 2026 remeasurement result by approximately (USD thousands): (LOS: los-03-03-e)
   - A. -4,480
   - B. -2,191
   - C. +4,480
6. On a clean-surplus basis (net income plus the period's translation adjustment), the 2025 growth rates of adjusted net income for Meridian and Caspian, respectively, are closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-d)
   - A. 15.0% and 24.7%
   - B. 13.5% and 63.3%
   - C. 16.5% and -8.8%

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** The functional currency is the currency of the primary economic environment; the currency that mainly influences sales prices, and the currency of costs, are the leading indicators (V3 p.135, p.136).
> MOS-UK prices its charters in USD and buys fuel and vessels in USD, so the USD designation is appropriate even though some costs and the loan are in GBP.
> A is wrong because location and bookkeeping currency define the local currency, not the functional currency, and financing currency is only a secondary indicator.
> B is wrong because many subsidiaries have their local currency as functional currency; Meridian's own other subsidiaries do.
> 2. **B.** Transaction exposure arises when a transaction is denominated in a currency other than the entity's functional currency and settlement comes after the transaction date (V3 p.118, p.193).
> For MOS-UK the GBP is a foreign currency, and the shipyard invoice is payable in 60 days, so it creates a liability exposure.
> A is wrong because the charter invoice is in USD, MOS-UK's functional currency; a foreign customer does not make it a foreign currency transaction.
> C is wrong because salaries paid in GBP on the date earned leave no interval for the rate to move.
> 3. **A.** Under the temporal method (USD functional) exposure is net monetary assets: $18{,}400 + 26{,}600 - 12{,}300 - 64{,}000 = -31{,}300$, a net monetary liability; deferred revenue is nonmonetary and excluded (V3 p.139, p.140).
> Under the current rate method (GBP functional) exposure is net assets: $196{,}000 - 12{,}300 - 8{,}700 - 64{,}000 = 111{,}000$ (V3 p.137).
> B is wrong because it treats deferred revenue as monetary: $-31{,}300 - 8{,}700 = -40{,}000$.
> C is wrong because it uses total assets rather than net assets for the current rate exposure.
> 4. **C.** Temporal method, balance sheet first, retained earnings as the plug (V3 p.138, p.140, p.145).
> Assets: $(18{,}400 + 26{,}600) \times 1.25 = 56{,}250$; inventory $9{,}000 \times 1.26 = 11{,}340$; vessels $142{,}000 \times 1.35 = 191{,}700$; total $259{,}290$.
> Liabilities: payables $12{,}300 \times 1.25 = 15{,}375$; deferred revenue (nonmonetary, historical) $8{,}700 \times 1.27 = 11{,}049$; loan $64{,}000 \times 1.25 = 80{,}000$.
> Common stock $80{,}000 \times 1.35 = 108{,}000$.
> Retained earnings $= 259{,}290 - 15{,}375 - 11{,}049 - 80{,}000 - 108{,}000 = 44{,}866$.
> A is wrong because it translates retained earnings directly at the closing rate: $31{,}000 \times 1.25 = 38{,}750$.
> B is wrong because it holds the bank loan at the rate when it was drawn, as if a monetary liability carried a historical rate: $64{,}000 \times 1.31 = 83{,}840$, giving $259{,}290 - 15{,}375 - 11{,}049 - 83{,}840 - 108{,}000 = 41{,}026$.
> 5. **A.** Before refinancing, exposure is $-31{,}300$; a GBP move from 1.25 to 1.18 on a net monetary liability is a gain of $31{,}300 \times 0.07 = 2{,}191$.
> After the equity injection and loan repayment, cash in and cash out cancel and the loan disappears, so exposure becomes $18{,}400 + 26{,}600 - 12{,}300 = +32{,}700$, a net monetary asset; the same move is a loss of $32{,}700 \times 0.07 = 2{,}289$.
> Change $= -2{,}289 - 2{,}191 = -4{,}480 = -64{,}000 \times 0.07$ (V3 p.151, p.152).
> Halloran is wrong twice: the refinancing flips the exposure rather than removing it, and with a weakening GBP it hurts 2026 earnings.
> B is wrong because it assumes the refinancing brings exposure to zero, so only the $2{,}191$ gain is lost.
> C is wrong because it has the sign backwards, assuming that removing a liability helps when the foreign currency weakens.
> 6. **C.** Meridian: $(184.0 + 1.5) / (160.0 - 0.8) - 1 = 185.5 / 159.2 - 1 = 16.52\%$.
> Caspian: $(212.0 - 46.0) / (170.0 + 12.0) - 1 = 166.0 / 182.0 - 1 = -8.79\%$ (V3 p.162, p.167, p.168).
> Caspian's current rate method sends its large translation losses to equity, so reported net income flatters it; Meridian's temporal subsidiaries already run remeasurement results through net income, so its adjustment is small.
> A is wrong because it compares reported growth: $184.0 / 160.0 - 1 = 15.0\%$ and $212.0 / 170.0 - 1 = 24.7\%$.
> B is wrong because it subtracts the translation adjustments instead of adding them: $182.5 / 160.8 - 1 = 13.5\%$ and $258.0 / 158.0 - 1 = 63.3\%$.

## Distractor design

- Q1: A confuses local currency and financing currency with functional currency; B claims the parent's currency is always functional.
- Q2: A treats a foreign customer as a foreign currency transaction when the invoice is in the functional currency; C ignores that same-day settlement has no exposure interval.
- Q3: B misclassifies deferred revenue as monetary; C uses total assets instead of net assets.
- Q4: A translates retained earnings directly instead of deriving it as the plug; B holds the bank loan at the 1.3100 rate at which it was drawn, treating a monetary liability as nonmonetary; the 31 December 2024 rate 1.2800 is a decoy.
- Q5: B assumes the refinancing zeroes the exposure instead of flipping it; C is a sign error on a weakening currency with a liability removed.
- Q6: A skips the clean-surplus adjustment; B subtracts the translation adjustment (sign error).
