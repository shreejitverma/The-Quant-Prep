# Castellan Cement: Restating the Clinker Joint Venture

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Lucia Ferrer is the group financial controller of Castellan Cement SA, a Spanish producer of cement and ready-mix concrete.
Castellan owns 50% of Iberia Clinker SL, a joint venture with a Portuguese partner, and the two owners share joint control.
Castellan's internal management reports have always presented Iberia Clinker using proportional consolidation.
Castellan's auditors have told Ferrer that the published IFRS statements must present Iberia Clinker using the equity method, with comparative figures restated.

Iberia Clinker operates a single kiln near Seville that supplies clinker to both owners' grinding plants and to third-party customers.
It is more profitable than Castellan's own ready-mix business, which operates on thinner margins and uses more working capital.
Castellan's bank covenants are measured on management-report figures, so the lending banks have asked Ferrer to show how the change of method will move the reported ratios.
Ferrer prepares the 2025 restatement for the audit committee.
The audit committee chair wants to know which ratios will change only because of the presentation method, so that the board does not mistake an accounting effect for an operating one.
Castellan's investment in Iberia Clinker has no excess of cost over its share of book value, and there were no transactions between Castellan and the joint venture in 2025.
Iberia Clinker paid no dividends in 2025.
Under the equity method Castellan will present its share of the joint venture's profit as a single line after finance costs, below operating profit.

Exhibit 1: 2025 data (EUR millions)

| Item | Castellan, proportional consolidation | Iberia Clinker, 100% |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 8,400 | 1,600 |
| Operating profit (EBIT) | 1,050 | 300 |
| Net income | 620 | 180 |
| Total assets | 12,800 | 2,600 |
| Total liabilities | 7,200 | 1,400 |
| Total equity | 5,600 | 1,200 |
| Employees | 6,900 | 1,150 |

Ferrer computes ratios on year-end balances for the audit committee pack.

Martin Roig, an equity analyst who covers Castellan, reviews the draft restatement and notes that Castellan's segment disclosures for 2023 are not being restated.
He asks Ferrer how he should treat Castellan's 2023 to 2025 history.
Ferrer replies with two statements.

Statement 1: "The segment revenue trend from 2023 to 2025 remains comparable, because the change of method does not alter Castellan's net income."

Statement 2: "When you compute 2024 ratios on average balances, you should first revise the 31 December 2023 balance sheet onto the equity method, because otherwise the averages mix two accounting methods."

## Questions

1. Castellan's total assets at 31 December 2025 under the equity method are closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-d)
   - A. EUR10,800 million.
   - B. EUR11,500 million.
   - C. EUR12,100 million.
2. Castellan's 2025 EBIT margin under the equity method is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-d)
   - A. 11.8%.
   - B. 13.0%.
   - C. 13.8%.
3. Relative to proportional consolidation, the equity method will cause Castellan's 2025 net profit margin and total asset turnover to, respectively: (LOS: los-03-06-d)
   - A. increase; decrease.
   - B. remain unchanged; decrease.
   - C. increase; increase.
4. Which of Ferrer's statements to Roig is correct? (LOS: los-03-06-c)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both statements.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** Proportional consolidation includes 50% of each joint venture line: assets $0.5 \times 2{,}600 = 1{,}300$ and liabilities $0.5 \times 1{,}400 = 700$.
> The equity method removes those lines and records one investment equal to 50% of net assets, $0.5 \times 1{,}200 = 600$.
> Total assets $= 12{,}800 - 1{,}300 + 600 = 12{,}100$; total liabilities $= 7{,}200 - 700 = 6{,}500$; equity is unchanged at $5{,}600$.
> A is wrong because it removes 100% of the joint venture's assets, $12{,}800 - 2{,}600 + 600 = 10{,}800$.
> B is wrong because it removes Castellan's share of the assets without recognizing the investment, $12{,}800 - 1{,}300 = 11{,}500$.
> 2. **A.** Revenue $= 8{,}400 - 0.5 \times 1{,}600 = 7{,}600$; EBIT $= 1{,}050 - 0.5 \times 300 = 900$.
> EBIT margin $= 900 / 7{,}600 = 0.1184 = 11.8\%$, down from $1{,}050 / 8{,}400 = 12.5\%$ because the joint venture's own margin, $300 / 1{,}600 = 18.75\%$, is above Castellan's.
> B is wrong because it adds the 90 share of the joint venture's profit to EBIT, $(900 + 90) / 7{,}600 = 0.1303$, although Castellan presents that line below operating profit.
> C is wrong because it removes the joint venture's revenue but not its EBIT, $1{,}050 / 7{,}600 = 0.1382$.
> 3. **A.** Net income is unchanged at 620 while revenue falls to 7,600, so net profit margin rises from $620 / 8{,}400 = 7.38\%$ to $620 / 7{,}600 = 8.16\%$.
> Turnover falls from $8{,}400 / 12{,}800 = 0.6562$ to $7{,}600 / 12{,}100 = 0.6281$, because revenue falls by 9.5% while assets fall by only 5.5%, the investment line having no revenue behind it.
> Leverage also falls, from $12{,}800 / 5{,}600 = 2.2857$ to $12{,}100 / 5{,}600 = 2.1607$, and ROE is unchanged at $620 / 5{,}600 = 11.07\%$.
> B is wrong because unchanged net income does not mean an unchanged margin when the denominator shrinks.
> C is wrong because it assumes assets fall proportionally more than revenue, ignoring the investment recognized in place of the removed assets.
> 4. **B.** Statement 2 is correct: averaging a restated year-end with an unrestated one mixes methods in the denominators, so the analyst revises the earlier balance sheet first, as the curriculum does with the 2011 Nestlé balance sheet.
> Statement 1 is incorrect: unchanged net income does not make segment revenue comparable, because unrestated 2023 segment revenue still includes 50% of the joint venture's revenue, and a trend drawn across the restatement date would show a fall caused only by the change of method.
> A is wrong because it treats unchanged net income as evidence of comparable line items.
> C is wrong because Statement 1 extends a trend across the restatement date.

## Distractor design

1. A: 100% rather than the 50% share removed; B: share of assets removed without recognizing the investment (incomplete reclassification).
2. B: share of joint venture profit placed in EBIT (misread presentation note); C: revenue adjusted but EBIT not (inconsistent numerator and denominator).
3. B: unchanged net income read as unchanged margin; C: direction error on turnover.
4. A: unchanged bottom line mistaken for comparable segment data; C: accepts both, extending a trend across the restatement.
