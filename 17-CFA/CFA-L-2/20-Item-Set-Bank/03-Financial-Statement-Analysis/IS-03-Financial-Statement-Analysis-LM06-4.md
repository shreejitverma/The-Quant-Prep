# Brightwater Leisure: Segments, Charges and a Listed Stake

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Oliver Pemberton, CFA, runs a UK equity income fund and is deciding whether to initiate a position in Brightwater Leisure Holdings plc, which operates theme parks, cruise ships and hotels and reports under IFRS.
He asks his analyst, Anika Sharma, CFA, three questions: is management allocating capital rationally, what is the underlying profitability of the group, and is the core business cheap relative to peers?

Exhibit 1: Brightwater segment data, 2025 (GBP millions)

| Segment | Revenue | Segment EBIT | D&A | Capex | Assets 31 Dec 2025 | Assets 31 Dec 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Theme Parks | 3,200 | 704 | 260 | 420 | 5,600 | 5,200 |
| Cruises | 2,400 | 336 | 520 | 610 | 4,800 | 4,300 |
| Hotels | 1,900 | 437 | 90 | 70 | 4,400 | 4,500 |
| Total | 7,500 | 1,477 | 870 | 1,100 | 14,800 | 14,000 |

Segment EBIT is stated before central costs, goodwill impairment and restructuring charges.
Most Hotels assets are goodwill and brands from a 2021 acquisition.
Management states that it allocates capital on the basis of cash generated per pound of assets employed.

Exhibit 2: Brightwater group income statement, 2025 (GBP millions)

| Item | 2025 |
|---|---|
| Revenue | 7,500 |
| Segment EBIT | 1,477 |
| Central costs | (97) |
| Goodwill impairment, Hotels | (200) |
| Restructuring provisions | (60) |
| Operating profit | 1,120 |
| Net finance costs | (120) |
| Profit before tax | 1,000 |
| Income tax | (300) |
| Share of profit of associate | 140 |
| Profit for the year | 840 |
| Attributable to non-controlling interests | 45 |

The goodwill impairment is not tax deductible; the restructuring provisions are deductible at 25%.
Brightwater recognized Hotels goodwill impairments of GBP150 million in 2023 and GBP120 million in 2024, and restructuring provisions in each of the last four years.

Brightwater owns 25% of TixNova Inc., a US-listed ticketing platform, and accounts for it using the equity method; the carrying amount is GBP1,450 million.
Brightwater holds 62.5 million TixNova shares, which closed 2025 at USD64.00; the exchange rate was USD1.25 per GBP.
TixNova is Brightwater's only associate.
Brightwater has 850 million shares outstanding, which closed 2025 at GBP14.20.
The median P/E of listed leisure peers without significant associates is 17.0.

Sharma's colleague, Rhys Fenwick, makes two statements about the charges in Exhibit 2.

Statement 1: "The goodwill impairment is a non-cash charge, so it should be excluded from the base-case earnings we use to value Brightwater."

Statement 2: "Adding the charges back helps us see the trend in underlying margins, but because impairments and provisions recur and result from management's acquisition and restructuring decisions, they should stay in our base-case assessment."

## Questions

1. The 2025 cash return on average assets for the Cruises segment is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-e)
   - A. 7.4%.
   - B. 17.8%.
   - C. 18.8%.
2. Sharma's most appropriate answer to Pemberton's first question is that management's capital allocation is: (LOS: los-03-06-a)
   - A. rational, because capex share relative to asset share ranks the segments in the same order as cash return on average assets.
   - B. irrational, because Hotels has the highest EBIT margin but receives the smallest share of capex relative to its assets.
   - C. indeterminate, because segment cash flow statements are not published.
3. Brightwater's 2025 net profit margin excluding the associate, adjusted to add back the goodwill impairment and restructuring provisions, is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-e)
   - A. 9.3%.
   - B. 11.9%.
   - C. 12.6%.
4. Which of Fenwick's statements is correct? (LOS: los-03-06-b)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both statements.
5. Brightwater's implied P/E excluding TixNova is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-a)
   - A. 11.2.
   - B. 12.3.
   - C. 13.5.
6. The discount of Brightwater's P/E excluding TixNova to the peer median is closest to: (LOS: los-03-06-c)
   - A. 10.7%.
   - B. 20.3%.
   - C. 25.5%.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** Cash generation proxy $= EBIT + D\&A = 336 + 520 = 856$.
> Average assets $= (4{,}800 + 4{,}300) / 2 = 4{,}550$.
> Cash return $= 856 / 4{,}550 = 0.1881 = 18.8\%$.
> A is wrong because it uses EBIT without adding back D&A: $336 / 4{,}550 = 0.0738$.
> B is wrong because it uses year-end rather than average assets: $856 / 4{,}800 = 0.1783$.
> 2. **A.** Asset shares at year-end 2025: Parks $5{,}600 / 14{,}800 = 37.84\%$, Cruises $32.43\%$, Hotels $29.73\%$.
> Capex shares: Parks $420 / 1{,}100 = 38.18\%$, Cruises $55.45\%$, Hotels $6.36\%$.
> Growth allocation ratios: Cruises $55.45 / 32.43 = 1.71$, Parks $1.01$, Hotels $0.21$.
> Cash returns on average assets: Cruises $18.8\%$, Parks $(704 + 260) / 5{,}400 = 17.9\%$, Hotels $(437 + 90) / 4{,}450 = 11.8\%$.
> The ranking is identical, so on the cash basis management says it uses, the allocation is rational; Cruises has the lowest EBIT margin, $336 / 2{,}400 = 14.0\%$, because of heavy depreciation, and Hotels the highest, $23.0\%$, on an asset base inflated by acquisition goodwill.
> B is wrong because it judges allocation on EBIT margin, which the curriculum rejects when management decides on a cash basis.
> C is wrong because EBIT plus D&A over average segment assets is the accepted proxy when segment cash flow statements are not disclosed.
> 3. **C.** Profit excluding the associate $= 840 - 140 = 700$.
> Add back the impairment in full, as it is not deductible, and the provisions net of tax: $700 + 200 + 60 \times (1 - 0.25) = 700 + 200 + 45 = 945$.
> Adjusted margin $= 945 / 7{,}500 = 0.1260 = 12.6\%$.
> A is wrong because it is the unadjusted margin excluding the associate, $700 / 7{,}500 = 0.0933$, and makes no normalization adjustment; the two charges consumed $3.27$ percentage points of margin.
> B is wrong because it also tax-effects the non-deductible impairment: $(700 + 150 + 45) / 7{,}500 = 0.1193$.
> 4. **B.** Statement 2 is correct: normalization is for insight into the trend, but the charges have recurred for three and four years, reflect management decisions and reduce shareholder returns, so they stay in the base case.
> Statement 1 is incorrect: a non-cash impairment is evidence that past acquisitions were overpaid, and the cash was spent when the acquisition was made.
> A is wrong because it excludes a recurring charge on the grounds that it is non-cash.
> C is wrong because the two statements reach opposite conclusions about the base case.
> 5. **C.** Market capitalisation $= 850 \times 14.20 = 12{,}070$.
> TixNova holding $= 62.5 \times 64.00 = \text{USD}4{,}000$ million, or $4{,}000 / 1.25 = \text{GBP}3{,}200$ million.
> Value excluding TixNova $= 12{,}070 - 3{,}200 = 8{,}870$.
> Earnings to Brightwater shareholders $= 840 - 45 = 795$; excluding TixNova $= 795 - 140 = 655$.
> P/E excluding TixNova $= 8{,}870 / 655 = 13.54$, against $12{,}070 / 795 = 15.18$ for the whole group and $3{,}200 / 140 = 22.86$ implied for the TixNova stake.
> A is wrong because it removes TixNova's market value but not its earnings: $8{,}870 / 795 = 11.16$.
> B is wrong because it subtracts the stake's US dollar value without converting it at USD1.25 per GBP: $(12{,}070 - 4{,}000) / 655 = 12.32$.
> 6. **B.** Discount $= 1 - 13.54 / 17.0 = 0.2034 = 20.3\%$.
> The whole-group multiple understates the discount because the market prices TixNova at a higher multiple than the core business; TixNova is $3{,}200 / 12{,}070 = 26.5\%$ of value but only $140 / 795 = 17.6\%$ of earnings.
> A is wrong because it uses the whole-group P/E, $1 - 15.18 / 17.0 = 0.1069$, which is not comparable with peers that hold no significant associates.
> C is wrong because it forgets to deduct non-controlling interests from core earnings, using $8{,}870 / 700 = 12.67$ and $1 - 12.67 / 17.0 = 0.2546$.

## Distractor design

1. A: D&A not added back (accrual EBIT used for cash); B: year-end rather than average assets (period error).
2. B: allocation judged on EBIT margin rather than cash return (wrong basis); C: ignores the accepted EBIT plus D&A proxy.
3. A: no normalization adjustment made, so both charges are left in profit; B: non-deductible impairment tax-effected (before-tax vs after-tax).
4. A: non-cash treated as irrelevant; C: accepts contradictory statements.
5. A: associate value removed but not its earnings; B: US dollar value of the stake not converted to pounds (spot conversion omitted).
6. A: unadjusted whole-group multiple compared with peers (comparability error); C: non-controlling interests left in core earnings.
