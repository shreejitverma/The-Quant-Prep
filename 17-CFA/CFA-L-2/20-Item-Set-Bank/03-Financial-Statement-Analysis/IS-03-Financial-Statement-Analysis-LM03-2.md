# Brightwater Health Audit Committee Review

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Brightwater Health plc is a UK-listed manufacturer of generic medicines that reports under IFRS.
Its functional and presentation currency is the pound sterling (GBP), and its fiscal year ends on 31 December.
Priya Ramaswamy, CFA, chairs the audit committee and is reviewing the 2025 statements before sign-off.

On 15 November 2025, Brightwater bought active ingredients from a Swiss supplier for CHF8,000,000, payable on 15 February 2026.
Brightwater did not hedge the purchase.
It reports foreign currency transaction gains and losses in other operating income, whereas its closest US peer reports them in finance costs.
Brightwater's 2026 budget shows revenue of GBP42,000,000 and operating profit of GBP5,250,000 before any foreign currency gain or loss.

**Exhibit 1: Swiss franc rates (CHF per GBP)**

| Date | Rate |
|---|---|
| 15 November 2025 | 1.1250 |
| 31 December 2025 | 1.1100 |
| Average 2025 | 1.1180 |
| 15 February 2026 (rate used in the 2026 budget) | 1.1400 |

Brightwater also owns Brightwater Veloria, whose functional currency is the Veloria dinar (VLD).
Cumulative inflation in Veloria over 2023 to 2025 was 253.6%, and both management and the auditors treat Veloria as hyperinflationary.
On 1 January 2024, Brightwater Veloria bought land for a distribution centre for VLD36,000,000.
Its 2025 revenue of VLD520,000,000 was earned evenly through the year.
A VLD90,000,000 bank loan, drawn in 2024, was outstanding throughout 2025.
The subsidiary's cash was VLD30,000,000 on 1 January 2025 and rose evenly during the year to VLD70,000,000.

**Exhibit 2: Veloria price index and exchange rates**

| Date or period | General price index | VLD per GBP |
|---|---|---|
| 1 January 2024 | 100 | 40 |
| 31 December 2024 | 160 | 58 |
| Average 2025 | 210 | 80 |
| 31 December 2025 | 272 | 104 |

**Exhibit 3: Effective tax rate reconciliation (GBP thousands)**

| Item | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Profit before tax | 4,800 | 5,600 |
| Tax at UK statutory rate of 25% | 1,200 | 1,400 |
| Effect of tax rates in foreign jurisdictions | 96 | 252 |
| Non-deductible expenses | 24 | 28 |
| Income tax expense | 1,320 | 1,680 |

At the committee meeting on 3 February 2026, finance director Tomas Weller comments on Veloria.

Weller: "Our US peer carries its hyperinflationary subsidiaries' land at historical cost and historical rates, so its land never shrinks. Under IFRS we restate for Veloria's inflation and then translate, which gives a different figure."

Ramaswamy asks Weller to quantify the CHF payable's effect on both years, the Veloria figures that will appear in the consolidated statements, and the reason the group's effective tax rate rose in 2025.

## Questions

1. The effect of the CHF payable on Brightwater's 2025 net income is closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-b)
   - A. a gain of GBP96,096.
   - B. a loss of GBP96,096.
   - C. a loss of GBP120,000.
2. If the 15 February 2026 rate in Exhibit 1 is realised, Brightwater's 2026 budgeted operating profit margin under its own placement policy is closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-b)
   - A. 12.50%
   - B. 12.72%
   - C. 12.95%
3. The land held by Brightwater Veloria will be reported in Brightwater's 31 December 2025 consolidated balance sheet at an amount closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-g)
   - A. GBP346,154
   - B. GBP900,000
   - C. GBP941,538
4. The amount of Brightwater Veloria's 2025 revenue included in Brightwater's consolidated revenue is closest to: (LOS: los-03-03-g)
   - A. GBP6,476,190
   - B. GBP6,500,000
   - C. GBP8,500,000
5. Brightwater Veloria's 2025 purchasing power result on its net monetary position is best described as: (LOS: los-03-03-g)
   - A. a gain of VLD14.00 million, recognised in net income.
   - B. a gain of VLD30.19 million, recognised in net income.
   - C. a gain of VLD30.19 million, recognised in other comprehensive income.
6. Brightwater's 2025 effective tax rate, and the most likely reason for its change from 2024, are: (LOS: los-03-03-h)
   - A. 30.0%, because the UK statutory rate applied to Brightwater increased.
   - B. 30.0%, because a larger share of profit was earned in jurisdictions taxed above 25%, or rates rose in those jurisdictions.
   - C. 29.5%, because a larger share of profit was earned in jurisdictions taxed above 25%, or rates rose in those jurisdictions.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** The rate is quoted in CHF per GBP, so divide to get the GBP value of the payable.
> Purchase date: $8{,}000{,}000 / 1.1250 = 7{,}111{,}111.11$; year end: $8{,}000{,}000 / 1.1100 = 7{,}207{,}207.21$.
> The quote fell, so the CHF strengthened; a liability in a strengthening foreign currency grows, a loss of $7{,}207{,}207.21 - 7{,}111{,}111.11 = 96{,}096.10$, recognised in 2025 net income even though unrealised (V3 p.119, p.120, p.121).
> A is wrong because it reads a falling CHF per GBP quote as a weakening franc, or applies the receivable sign to a payable.
> C is wrong because it multiplies instead of dividing: $8{,}000{,}000 \times (1.1250 - 1.1100) = 120{,}000$, which is in CHF-times-rate units, not GBP.
> 2. **C.** The 2026 result is $7{,}207{,}207.21 - 8{,}000{,}000 / 1.1400 = 7{,}207{,}207.21 - 7{,}017{,}543.86 = 189{,}663.35$, a gain because the CHF weakened after year end.
> Brightwater places the gain in operating income: $(5{,}250{,}000 + 189{,}663.35) / 42{,}000{,}000 = 12.95\%$ (V3 p.122, p.123).
> A is wrong because it is the peer's placement (below operating profit): $5{,}250{,}000 / 42{,}000{,}000 = 12.50\%$.
> B is wrong because it books the whole-life realised gain of $189{,}663.35 - 96{,}096.10 = 93{,}567.25$ in 2026, ignoring that the 2025 loss was already recognised: $(5{,}250{,}000 + 93{,}567.25) / 42{,}000{,}000 = 12.72\%$.
> 3. **C.** Under IFRS a hyperinflationary subsidiary is restated under IAS 29 and then translated at the closing rate (V3 p.141, p.142, p.143).
> Restated cost: $36{,}000{,}000 \times 272 / 100 = 97{,}920{,}000$ VLD; translated: $97{,}920{,}000 / 104 = 941{,}538.46$.
> For reference, the 2024 figure was $36{,}000{,}000 \times 1.60 / 58 = 993{,}103.45$, so 2025 carries a translation loss of $51{,}564.99$ and the cumulative result since purchase is a gain of $41{,}538.46$ against the original $900{,}000$.
> A is wrong because it translates unrestated historical cost at the closing rate, $36{,}000{,}000 / 104 = 346{,}153.85$, the disappearing-plant result neither standard allows.
> B is wrong because it is the US GAAP temporal answer, $36{,}000{,}000 / 40 = 900{,}000$, which Weller correctly attributes to the peer, not to an IFRS reporter.
> 4. **A.** Revenue earned evenly is restated from the average index: $520{,}000{,}000 \times 272 / 210 = 673{,}523{,}809.52$ VLD, then translated at the closing rate: $673{,}523{,}809.52 / 104 = 6{,}476{,}190.48$ (V3 p.158, p.194).
> B is wrong because it translates unrestated revenue at the average rate, $520{,}000{,}000 / 80 = 6{,}500{,}000$, the US GAAP temporal treatment.
> C is wrong because it restates from the opening index of 160 rather than the average index at which the revenue was recorded: $520{,}000{,}000 \times 272 / 160 / 104 = 8{,}500{,}000$.
> 5. **B.** Monetary items are not restated, so holding them during inflation creates a purchasing power result in net income; each balance is indexed from the date it arose (V3 p.158, p.160, p.161).
> Loan (liability, gain): $90 \times (272 - 160) / 160 = 63.0000$.
> Opening cash (asset, loss): $30 \times (272 - 160) / 160 = 21.0000$.
> Cash added evenly (asset, loss from the average index): $40 \times (272 - 210) / 210 = 11.8095$.
> Net gain: $63.0000 - 21.0000 - 11.8095 = 30.1905$ VLD million.
> A is wrong because it indexes the cash added during the year from the opening index: $63 - 21 - 40 \times 0.70 = 14.00$.
> C is wrong because IAS 29 puts the purchasing power gain or loss in net income, and restate-then-translate at the closing rate produces no separate translation adjustment for a hyperinflationary subsidiary.
> 6. **B.** Effective rate 2025: $1{,}680 / 5{,}600 = 30.0\%$ against $1{,}320 / 4{,}800 = 27.5\%$ in 2024 (V3 p.169, p.170, p.171).
> The domestic line is 25% of pretax profit in both years ($1{,}200 / 4{,}800$ and $1{,}400 / 5{,}600$) and non-deductible items are 0.5 points in both years, so the whole change comes from the foreign jurisdiction line: $96 / 4{,}800 = 2.0$ points rising to $252 / 5{,}600 = 4.5$ points.
> A positive and rising foreign effect means more profit earned where rates exceed 25%, or higher rates there.
> A is wrong because the statutory line is unchanged at 25%.
> C is wrong because it omits the non-deductible expenses: $(1{,}400 + 252) / 5{,}600 = 29.5\%$.

## Distractor design

- Q1: A is the sign error from reading an indirect quote (CHF per GBP) the wrong way; C multiplies by the rate change instead of dividing by each rate.
- Q2: A is the non-operating placement (peer's policy); B books the whole-life realised gain in the settlement year instead of splitting it across the balance sheet date; the 2025 average rate is a decoy.
- Q3: A translates unrestated historical cost at the closing rate (disappearing plant); B is the US GAAP temporal answer offered to an IFRS reporter.
- Q4: B is the US GAAP treatment (average rate, no restatement); C restates from the opening index instead of the average index.
- Q5: A indexes additions from the wrong date; C places the purchasing power result in OCI instead of net income.
- Q6: A blames the statutory rate, which the reconciliation shows is unchanged; C omits a reconciling item from the rate computation.
