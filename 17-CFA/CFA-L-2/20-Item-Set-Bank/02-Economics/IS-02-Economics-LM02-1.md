# Tidewater Pension Sovereign Review

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Ingrid Halvorsen, CFA, manages the sovereign debt sleeve of the Tidewater Municipal Employees' Pension Plan.
Ahead of the annual rebalancing, the investment committee has asked her to review two issuers: the Republic of Calvera, a middle-income economy whose local-currency government bonds the plan already holds, and the Kingdom of Nordhavn, a high-income economy the plan is considering adding.

Halvorsen begins with Calvera.
Her team's long-term projections are shown in Exhibit 1.
The consensus forecast for Calvera's actual real GDP growth is 3.4% in each of the next two years, and core inflation currently sits at the central bank's 3.0% target.

**Exhibit 1: Calvera, long-term growth accounting projections**

| Input | Projection (per year) |
|---|---|
| Total factor productivity (TFP) growth | 0.9% |
| Growth of the capital stock | 3.2% |
| Growth of labor input (hours worked) | 0.6% |
| Labor share of national income | 65% |
| Population growth | 1.1% |

**Exhibit 2: Labor productivity and TFP, annual averages 2014-2024**

| Measure | Calvera | Nordhavn |
|---|---|---|
| Labor productivity growth | 2.7% | 1.6% |
| TFP growth | 0.9% | 1.2% |
| Growth in hours worked | 1.3% | 0.2% |

Halvorsen also summarizes Calvera's institutional profile.
Primary school enrollment is near universal, and public health indicators have improved steadily for two decades.
Gross domestic saving is 14% of GDP, well below the 25% to 30% typical of fast-growing peers, and the domestic banking system is small relative to the economy.
Calvera caps foreign ownership of any domestic company at 20% and requires ministerial approval for every foreign direct investment project.
A multilateral lender rates Calvera's contract enforcement and property registration as adequate.

Halvorsen then turns to Nordhavn, whose labor market projections appear in Exhibit 3.

**Exhibit 3: Nordhavn, labor market projections**

| Item | Projection |
|---|---|
| Native working-age population growth | 0.3% per year |
| Net immigration, addition to the labor force | 0.5% per year, set by a legislated quota with no expiry |
| Labor force participation rate | 61.0% today, expected to reach 63.5% in five years and then stabilize |
| Unemployment rate | 7.2% |
| Long-term labor productivity growth | 1.6% per year |

Nordhavn is a major exporter of natural gas and has strong legal and political institutions.
Over the past decade its real effective exchange rate has appreciated by roughly 25%, and manufacturing's share of merchandise exports has fallen from 38% to 22%.
Real GDP growth outside the gas sector has averaged less than 1% a year over the same period.

At a committee meeting on 3 March 2026, Halvorsen comments on Nordhavn's outlook.
She states: "Nordhavn's gas reserves are a guarantee of long-run growth, because a country that owns its own energy supply cannot be held back by resource constraints."

Her colleague Tomas Reyes responds in a memo dated 5 March 2026.
He writes: "The gas boom is more likely a drag on growth. Resource exports have pushed the currency up and hollowed out manufacturing, the sector in which Nordhavn historically captured most of its productivity gains."

## Questions

1. Using the growth accounting equation and Exhibit 1, Halvorsen's estimate of Calvera's potential GDP growth rate is closest to: (LOS: los-02-02-e)
   - A. 2.41%.
   - B. 3.19%.
   - C. 4.70%.
2. Based on the consensus forecast and the answer to Question 1, the most likely implication for Calvera's local-currency government bonds over the next two years is that bond prices will: (LOS: los-02-02-c)
   - A. fall, because growth above potential creates upward inflation pressure and a likely tightening of monetary policy.
   - B. rise, because faster actual growth raises tax revenue and lowers Calvera's sovereign credit risk.
   - C. be unaffected, because potential GDP growth itself does not change.
3. Based on Exhibit 2, the contribution of capital deepening to labor productivity growth in Calvera exceeded that in Nordhavn by closest to: (LOS: los-02-02-d)
   - A. 0.3 percentage points.
   - B. 1.1 percentage points.
   - C. 1.4 percentage points.
4. Based on Exhibit 3, Nordhavn's potential GDP growth rate beyond the next five years is closest to: (LOS: los-02-02-g)
   - A. 1.90%.
   - B. 2.40%.
   - C. 3.21%.
5. Which policy change is most likely to raise Calvera's potential GDP growth by addressing the growth-limiting factor identified in Halvorsen's institutional profile? (LOS: los-02-02-a)
   - A. Removing the foreign ownership cap and the approval requirement for foreign direct investment.
   - B. Shifting public education spending from primary schools toward university research programs.
   - C. Raising import tariffs to protect domestic producers while domestic saving builds up.
6. Regarding the statements made by Halvorsen and Reyes about Nordhavn's natural gas, which is most likely correct? (LOS: los-02-02-f)
   - A. Halvorsen's statement only.
   - B. Reyes's statement only.
   - C. Both statements.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** The labor share is 65%, so capital's share is $\alpha = 1 - 0.65 = 0.35$.
> $\Delta Y/Y = \Delta A/A + \alpha\,\Delta K/K + (1-\alpha)\,\Delta L/L = 0.90\% + 0.35(3.20\%) + 0.65(0.60\%) = 0.9000\% + 1.1200\% + 0.3900\% = 2.4100\%$.
> B is wrong because it treats the 65% labor share as capital's share and swaps the weights: $0.90\% + 0.65(3.20\%) + 0.35(0.60\%) = 3.1900\%$.
> C is wrong because it adds the input growth rates without factor-share weights: $0.90\% + 3.20\% + 0.60\% = 4.7000\%$.
> Population growth (1.1%) is a decoy; labor input is hours worked.
> 2. **A.** Actual growth of 3.4% exceeds potential growth of 2.41% by $0.99$ percentage points a year, so a positive output gap opens with inflation already at target.
> That signals upward inflation pressure, a likely central bank tightening, higher nominal yields, and falling bond prices.
> B is wrong because rating agencies tie sovereign credit quality to potential GDP growth, not to a cyclical overshoot of actual growth above potential.
> C is wrong because the gap between actual and potential growth, not the level of potential growth alone, drives the inflation and monetary policy outlook for bonds.
> 3. **C.** Capital deepening's contribution equals labor productivity growth minus TFP growth.
> Calvera: $2.7\% - 0.9\% = 1.8000\%$; Nordhavn: $1.6\% - 1.2\% = 0.4000\%$; difference $1.8000\% - 0.4000\% = 1.4000$ percentage points.
> A is wrong because it compares TFP growth alone ($|0.9\% - 1.2\%| = 0.3000$ points), which measures the shift of the production function, not movement along it.
> B is wrong because it compares labor productivity growth alone ($2.7\% - 1.6\% = 1.1000$ points), which mixes TFP and capital deepening.
> 4. **B.** Potential GDP growth equals long-term labor force growth plus long-term labor productivity growth.
> Long-term labor force growth is native working-age growth plus net immigration, $0.3\% + 0.5\% = 0.8\%$, because the quota has no expiry; the participation rise ends after five years.
> Potential growth $= 0.8\% + 1.6\% = 2.4000\%$.
> C is wrong because it extrapolates the participation transition, $(63.5/61.0)^{1/5} - 1 = 0.8066\%$ a year, beyond year five: $2.4000\% + 0.8066\% = 3.2066\%$; a participation change is a move to a new level, not a permanent growth-rate change.
> A is wrong because it omits net migration from labor force growth: $0.3\% + 1.6\% = 1.9000\%$.
> The 7.2% unemployment rate is a decoy for the same level-versus-rate reason.
> 5. **A.** Calvera already has near-universal primary education, adequate health, and adequate property rights; its binding constraint is low saving and investment.
> Foreign direct and portfolio investment can fund domestic investment and break the low income, low saving, low investment cycle, and foreign firms bring technology and management know-how.
> B is wrong because a country behind the technology frontier gains more from primary and secondary education and imitation; university research spending suits economies at the frontier.
> C is wrong because inward-oriented tariff protection has historically produced inefficient industries and slower growth, and it does nothing to raise the saving pool.
> 6. **B.** Reyes describes Dutch disease: resource-export demand appreciates the currency, manufacturing becomes uncompetitive, and the economy loses the TFP gains that manufacturing typically delivers; with strong institutions, this is the relevant resource-curse channel.
> Halvorsen is wrong because owning natural resources is neither necessary nor sufficient for growth; access through trade substitutes for ownership, and resource wealth can depress growth.
> A and C are wrong because each endorses Halvorsen's claim that ownership guarantees growth.

## Distractor design

1. B swaps the factor-share weights by misreading the labor share as $\alpha$; C adds raw input growth rates with no weights.
2. B confuses a cyclical overshoot of actual growth with potential growth as the credit-quality driver; C ignores the output gap.
3. A measures capital deepening with TFP growth alone; B uses labor productivity growth alone.
4. C treats a participation-rate transition as permanent labor force growth; A omits net migration as a source of labor input.
5. B applies the frontier-economy education prescription to a catch-up economy; C prescribes inward orientation, which misreads the saving constraint.
6. A accepts resource ownership as sufficient for growth; C additionally misses that the two statements are mutually inconsistent about the sign of the resource effect.
