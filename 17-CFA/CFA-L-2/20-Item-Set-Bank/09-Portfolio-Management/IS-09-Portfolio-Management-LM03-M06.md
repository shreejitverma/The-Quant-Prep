# Aldmoor Assurance: Vetting a Euro High-Yield Bond ETF

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Sofia Brandauer, CFA, is an investment officer at Aldmoor Assurance, a property and casualty insurer based in Dublin.
Aldmoor's high-yield allocation is run by a single external manager, and the investment committee wants to raise the allocation before a second manager is hired.
Brandauer proposes to buy EUR 40 million of EHY, a UCITS ETF that tracks an index of 1,480 euro-denominated high-yield corporate bonds.
She reviews the fund with junior analyst Pieter Van Loon.

Van Loon first collects a week of closing data for EHY's Xetra listing.

Exhibit 1: EHY closing prices and NAVs for one week in June

| Day | Closing price (EUR) | NAV per share (EUR) |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | 52.84 | 52.70 |
| Tuesday | 52.91 | 52.78 |
| Wednesday | 52.40 | 52.95 |
| Thursday | 52.66 | 52.60 |
| Friday | 52.88 | 52.71 |

On Wednesday, a large issuer in the index was downgraded during the afternoon, and EHY traded heavily as it fell.

Exhibit 2: EHY fund data

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Expense ratio | 0.40% |
| Replication | Representative sampling: 640 of the 1,480 index bonds |
| Securities lending | None |
| 12-month total return at NAV | 6.33% |
| 12-month total return at market price | 6.61% |
| 12-month index total return | 6.85% |
| Annualized tracking error | 0.35% |
| Listings | Xetra, Borsa Italiana, Euronext Amsterdam |
| Median daily on-exchange volume on Xetra | EUR 3.2 million |

At 11:00 a.m. on Friday, EHY is quoted on Xetra at a bid of EUR 52.79 and an ask of EUR 52.97.
At the same time, EGV, a euro government bond ETF from the same sponsor, is quoted with a bid-ask spread of 0.04% of its midpoint.

Van Loon sends Brandauer three statements about how Aldmoor's order would trade.

Statement 1: "EHY's Xetra volume of EUR 3.2 million a day shows that the fund cannot absorb a EUR 40 million order, which is to be expected because European ETF markets are dominated by retail investors trading on exchange order books."

Statement 2: "Because EHY is listed on three exchanges and a large share of European ETF trading is negotiated over the counter, its Xetra volume understates the fund's total trading activity."

Statement 3: "As in the United States, all European ETF trades settle through a single central securities depository, so our purchase will settle the same way whichever listing we use."

Van Loon also drafts a paragraph on EHY's tracking for the investment committee.
Brandauer asks him to measure the fund's 12-month tracking difference the way the curriculum recommends and to explain any gap between the tracking difference and the expense ratio.

## Questions

1. EHY's median premium or discount to NAV over the week in Exhibit 1, and its most appropriate interpretation, are: (LOS: los-09-03-e)
   - A. a premium of 0.25%, indicating that buyers were generally in net demand and paid above fair value for access to the portfolio.
   - B. a discount of 0.02%, indicating that EHY traded at fair value on average over the week.
   - C. a premium of 0.25%, indicating that authorized participants were redeeming EHY shares through the week.
2. EHY's quoted Xetra spread as a percentage of the midpoint at 11:00 a.m. on Friday, and the most likely reason it is wider than EGV's, are: (LOS: los-09-03-d)
   - A. 0.17%, because the underlying high-yield bonds trade in an opaque dealer market with wide spreads and are hard for market makers to hedge.
   - B. 0.34%, because the underlying high-yield bonds trade in an opaque dealer market with wide spreads and are hard for market makers to hedge.
   - C. 0.34%, because more market makers compete to quote EHY than EGV, and greater competition widens spreads.
3. Which of Van Loon's statements about ETF trading is correct? (LOS: los-09-03-b)
   - A. Statement 1
   - B. Statement 2
   - C. Statement 3
4. EHY's 12-month tracking difference and the most appropriate conclusion are: (LOS: los-09-03-c)
   - A. -0.24%; EHY lagged its index by less than its expense ratio, so the other sources of tracking error added to its return.
   - B. -0.52%; the shortfall beyond the expense ratio reflects other sources of tracking error, such as representative sampling of 640 of the 1,480 index bonds.
   - C. -0.92%; EHY lagged its index by more than twice its expense ratio, which points to poor portfolio management.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Daily premium or discount $= (\text{price} - \text{NAV})/\text{NAV}$: Monday $0.14/52.70 = +0.2657\%$, Tuesday $0.13/52.78 = +0.2463\%$, Wednesday $-0.55/52.95 = -1.0387\%$, Thursday $0.06/52.60 = +0.1141\%$, Friday $0.17/52.71 = +0.3225\%$.
>    Ordered, the middle value is Tuesday's $+0.2463\%$, so the median is a premium of about 0.25%.
>    A persistent positive median premium means buyers were in net demand and typically paid above fair value for ETF access to a high-yield portfolio, as with the high-yield bond ETFs in the curriculum's comparison.
>    B is wrong because it reports the mean, $-0.0180\%$, which Wednesday's single large discount drags below zero; the median is the statistic that describes the typical day.
>    C is wrong because a premium invites creations, not redemptions: APs buy the basket, deliver it for new shares, and sell those shares; redemptions follow discounts.
> 2. **B.** Midpoint $= (52.79 + 52.97)/2 = 52.88$; spread $= 0.18/52.88 = 0.3404\%$.
>    Both funds come from the same sponsor, so the difference lies in the underlying markets: high-yield bonds trade in a dealer market with wide spreads and little continuous pricing and are hard to hedge, so the market maker's underlying-spread and hedging-risk components are much larger than for liquid government bonds.
>    A is wrong because 0.17% is the half spread, $0.09/52.88 = 0.1702\%$, which is the cost of one side of a trade, not the quoted spread.
>    C is wrong because market-maker competition is negatively related to spreads; more competition narrows them.
> 3. **B.** European ETFs are often listed on several exchanges, and much of their trading is negotiated over the counter, so the on-exchange volume of one listing understates total trading activity.
>    A is wrong because the European ETF market is mostly institutional, not retail, and one venue's screen volume does not measure the fund's capacity.
>    C is wrong because European settlement is fragmented across many central securities depositories, unlike the single US system, which is one reason European spreads tend to be wider.
> 4. **B.** Tracking difference is measured with the fund's return at NAV: $6.33\% - 6.85\% = -0.52\%$.
>    A clean index fund should lag by about its expense ratio, so the other sources account for $-0.52\% - (-0.40\%) = -0.12\%$.
>    EHY holds only 640 of the 1,480 index bonds, so representative sampling is a likely contributor, and with no securities lending there is no lending income to offset expenses.
>    A is wrong because it uses the market price return, $6.61\% - 6.85\% = -0.24\%$, which mixes the change in EHY's premium into the tracking measure.
>    C is wrong because it deducts the expense ratio again from a NAV return that is already net of fees: $-0.52\% - 0.40\% = -0.92\%$.
>    The 0.35% tracking error is a decoy; it measures the dispersion of daily return differences, not the cumulative gap.

## Distractor design

1. B: mean used instead of median, distorted by one outlier day; C: premium tied to redemptions (arbitrage direction reversed).
2. A: half spread reported as the quoted spread; C: sign error on market-maker competition.
3. A: European market described as retail and exchange-based; C: US single-depository settlement applied to Europe.
4. A: market price return used instead of NAV return; C: expense ratio deducted twice.
