# Ashgrove Capital: Annual ETF Product Oversight Report

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Priya Raman, CFA, heads product analytics at Ashgrove Capital, a sponsor of index ETFs.
She is drafting the annual product oversight report for Ashgrove's board, and she reviews the draft with Chief Investment Officer Daniel Okoro.
The report opens with the rolling 12-month tracking record of three equity funds.

Exhibit 1: Tracking record, 36 monthly rolling 12-month windows

| | Fund A | Fund B | Fund C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Benchmark | Emerging markets (1,150 stocks) | Emerging markets (2,300 stocks) | International developed (900 stocks) |
| Replication | Full | Representative sampling | Full |
| Holds ADRs in place of local shares | No | Yes | No |
| Securities lending program | Yes | No | Yes |
| Expense ratio | 0.55% | 0.49% | 0.20% |
| Median rolling tracking difference | -0.68% | -0.44% | -0.14% |
| Best rolling tracking difference | -0.41% | +1.02% | -0.08% |
| Worst rolling tracking difference | -0.90% | -1.23% | -0.22% |
| Annualized tracking error | 0.21% | 0.74% | 0.05% |

Okoro comments: "Fund B has the smallest shortfall of the two emerging markets funds, so the board should see that representative sampling has improved our tracking."

Raman adds a section on Fund A's securities lending program.
At year-end Fund A had USD 84.0 million of its emerging markets shares on loan.
Ashgrove's lending agent applies the industry collateral standards for domestic and international securities, and the agent holds the cash collateral in very short-term instruments.
Lending income last year was USD 0.9 million, and Fund A's net assets were USD 2.6 billion.

Next, Raman reviews Fund D, a commodity ETF that obtains its exposure through total return swaps with a single bank.
The swaps are reset weekly.
Three days after the last reset, Fund D had unrealized gains of USD 6.3 million on the swaps, and the bank's credit rating was placed on negative watch that afternoon.

Raman then turns to Fund E, a high-yield corporate bond ETF.
Fund E uses cash creations and redemptions.
Raman writes: "Because Fund E creates and redeems in cash, APs participate more readily, but the fund itself bears the trading costs and tax consequences of buying and selling bonds."
Okoro adds a sentence about Ashgrove's equity funds: "Because our equity funds redeem in kind, the portfolio manager can deliver the highest-cost-basis tax lots into each redemption basket, which minimizes the unrealized gains left in the fund."

Finally, the report covers a stressed trading day in March.
High-yield spreads widened sharply during the session and many bonds in Fund E's index did not trade.
Fund E's NAV was struck at USD 81.10 from dealer bid quotes collected during the day.
Fund E closed on the exchange at USD 78.40 on its highest volume of the year.
Its 12-month median premium had been +0.18%.

## Questions

1. Relative to its expense ratio, Fund C's median rolling tracking difference implies that the net effect of all other sources of tracking error is closest to: (LOS: los-09-03-c)
   - A. -0.34%.
   - B. -0.06%.
   - C. +0.06%.
2. The most appropriate response to Okoro's comment about Fund B is that: (LOS: los-09-03-c)
   - A. he is correct, because Fund B's median shortfall is smaller than its expense ratio.
   - B. he is incorrect, because sampling made Fund B's tracking regime dependent and widened its range of rolling outcomes to about 4.6 times Fund A's.
   - C. he is incorrect, because Fund B's tracking error shows that it underperformed its benchmark by 0.74% per year.
3. The minimum collateral the lending agent should hold against Fund A's loaned securities is closest to: (LOS: los-09-03-g)
   - A. USD 80.0 million.
   - B. USD 85.7 million.
   - C. USD 88.2 million.
4. The risk created by Fund D's position three days after the swap reset is best described as: (LOS: los-09-03-g)
   - A. settlement risk.
   - B. fund-closure risk.
   - C. expectation-related risk.
5. Regarding the statements by Raman and Okoro about creation and redemption, which is correct? (LOS: los-09-03-a)
   - A. Raman's statement only
   - B. Okoro's statement only
   - C. Both statements
6. Which sentence is most appropriate for the report's description of Fund E on the stressed trading day? (LOS: los-09-03-e)
   - A. "Fund E closed at a 3.33% discount to NAV; because the NAV rested on lagging dealer quotes, the exchange price was likely the better estimate of the portfolio's value."
   - B. "Fund E closed at a 3.44% discount to NAV; because the NAV rested on lagging dealer quotes, the exchange price was likely the better estimate of the portfolio's value."
   - C. "Fund E closed at a 3.33% discount to NAV, which shows that the arbitrage mechanism failed and that shareholders sold below fair value."

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** A clean index fund should lag by about its expense ratio, so the residual is $TD - (-\text{ER}) = -0.14\% - (-0.20\%) = +0.06\%$.
> Fund C lagged by less than its fee, so the other sources net to a gain, consistent with securities lending income acting as a negative cost that outweighs Fund C's small frictions from full replication in local shares.
> A is wrong because it treats the expense ratio as an addition to the shortfall: $-0.14 - 0.20 = -0.34\%$.
> B is wrong on sign: it reads a shortfall smaller than the fee as an extra drag.
> 2. **B.** Fund B's range of rolling outcomes is $1.02 - (-1.23) = 2.25\%$ against Fund A's $-0.41 - (-0.90) = 0.49\%$, a ratio of $2.25/0.49 = 4.5918$.
> Sampling from 2,300 stocks makes the tracking difference depend on whether the omitted names outperform or underperform, so its median is unstable across regimes and its dispersion is much wider; the 0.74% tracking error confirms the dispersion.
> The ADR holdings add further error from trading-hour mismatches, and Fund B forgoes lending income.
> A is wrong because a favorable median over one sample says nothing about the next regime and ignores the widened distribution.
> C is wrong because tracking error is a standard deviation of daily return differences; it has no sign and does not measure the cumulative return gap.
> 3. **C.** Loaned international securities are overcollateralized at 105%: $84.0 \times 1.05 = 88.2$ million.
> A is wrong because it divides by 1.05 instead of multiplying ($84.0/1.05 = 80.0$), treating the loan as if it were the grossed-up collateral.
> B is wrong because it applies the 102% domestic standard ($84.0 \times 1.02 = 85.68$) to emerging markets shares.
> 4. **A.** Between swap resets the fund is owed its accumulated mark-to-market gain, here USD 6.3 million, which is exposed to the counterparty's default until the next settlement; that is settlement risk.
> B is wrong because fund-closure risk arises from regulation, competition, and corporate actions affecting the fund's viability, not from a swap counterparty's credit.
> C is wrong because expectation-related risk concerns investors misunderstanding how a complex product behaves.
> 5. **A.** Raman is correct: cash creation makes it easier for APs to participate when the underlying bonds are hard to deliver in kind, but the fund must then trade the bonds itself, incurring trading costs and possible tax consequences.
> Okoro is wrong on the tax lots: managers deliver the lowest-cost-basis lots into redemption baskets, which raises the fund's average cost basis and minimizes the unrealized gains that could later be distributed.
> B is wrong because it accepts the reversed tax-lot logic; delivering the highest-basis lots would leave the largest embedded gains in the fund.
> C is wrong for the same reason.
> 6. **A.** Discount $= (78.40 - 81.10)/81.10 = -2.70/81.10 = -0.033292$, or $-3.33\%$.
> Underlying bonds trade in a dealer market without continuous prices, and on a stressed day many did not trade at all, so the NAV built from dealer quotes was stale; the heavily traded ETF price is the better reflection of current supply and demand for the portfolio.
> B is wrong because it divides by the ETF price: $-2.70/78.40 = -0.034439$, or $-3.44\%$.
> C is wrong because the discount reflects stale NAV inputs and price discovery, not an arbitrage failure; the prior +0.18% median premium is a decoy that has no bearing on the day's gap.

## Distractor design

1. A: expense ratio added to the shortfall (sign error); B: residual sign reversed.
2. A: median tracking difference accepted without regard to regime dependence and dispersion; C: tracking error misread as a signed annual shortfall.
3. A: division instead of multiplication by the collateral ratio; B: domestic 102% standard applied to international securities.
4. B: business-viability risk label substituted for swap exposure; C: investor-comprehension label substituted.
5. B and C: tax-lot direction reversed (highest-basis lots instead of lowest-basis lots).
6. B: wrong denominator (ETF price instead of NAV); C: stale-NAV discount misread as an arbitrage failure.
