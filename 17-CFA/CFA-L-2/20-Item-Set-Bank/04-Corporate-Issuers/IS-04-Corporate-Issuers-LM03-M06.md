# Tessaro Specialty Paper Recapitalization

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Katja Lindqvist, CFA, is a director in the corporate finance advisory group of Maren Hale Partners (Maren Hale).
The board of Tessaro Specialty Paper (Tessaro), a listed producer of labels, release liners and filtration papers, has hired Maren Hale to review a proposed leveraged recapitalization.
Tessaro has 80 million shares outstanding at 50.00 per share and 600 million of debt at market value.
Under the proposal, Tessaro would borrow 1,200 million of new long-term debt and use all of the proceeds to repurchase shares at 50.00.
For the analysis, Lindqvist assumes that the share price stays at 50.00 and that the total market value of Tessaro's debt and equity stays at 4,600 million.

**Exhibit 1: Market data**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Broad equity index level | 5,200 |
| Index dividends paid over the last 12 months | 114.40 |
| Consensus long-term earnings growth for index companies | 5.5% per year |
| 10-year government bond yield | 4.00% |
| 3-month government bill yield | 3.20% |

Maren Hale estimates the equity risk premium (ERP) with the constant growth dividend discount model applied to the broad equity index.
For all long-horizon estimates, it uses the 10-year government bond yield as the risk-free rate.

**Exhibit 2: Tessaro and peer data (currency in millions)**

| Item | Tessaro | Peer median |
|---|---|---|
| Equity beta (60 monthly returns) | 1.05 | 1.20 |
| Debt-to-equity at market values | 0.15 | 0.55 |
| EBIT | 560 | Not used |
| EBITDA | 790 | Not used |
| Interest expense | 33 | Not used |
| Marginal tax rate | 25% | 25% |
| Effective tax rate | 18% | 22% |
| Credit rating | AA | BBB |

Tessaro's beta regression has a high R-squared, so Lindqvist uses Tessaro's own beta as her starting point and adjusts it for the change in financial leverage.
Tessaro's lenders have indicated that total interest expense after the recapitalization would be 104 million per year, and Lindqvist expects EBIT to be unchanged.
Maren Hale assigns synthetic ratings with the schedule in Exhibit 3 and costs all of a company's debt at the spread for its rating.

**Exhibit 3: Maren Hale synthetic rating schedule**

Coverage is EBIT divided by interest expense; debt-to-equity (D/E) uses the market value of equity.

| Rating | EBIT/interest | D/E | Spread over the 10-year government yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| AA | above 12.0 | below 25% | 0.80% |
| A | 8.0 to 12.0 | 25% to 50% | 1.20% |
| BBB | 5.0 to 8.0 | 50% to 80% | 1.80% |
| BB | 3.0 to 5.0 | 80% to 120% | 2.90% |

Before the recapitalization, both of Tessaro's ratios fall in the AA band, so its pre-tax cost of debt is $4.00\% + 0.80\% = 4.80\%$.

On 22 April, board chair Oskar Brandvold tells Lindqvist: "Our after-tax cost of debt is less than half our cost of equity, so swapping 1,200 million of equity for debt must cut our WACC substantially and bring our capital structure closer to the peer median."

Lindqvist replies that the recapitalization changes every input to the WACC, not only the weights, and that she will quantify the net effect before the board meets.
She also notes that the recapitalization would move Tessaro's D/E above the peer median of 0.55 and its rating down to the peer median of BBB.

## Questions

1. Using Maren Hale's method, the ERP is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-c)
   - A. 3.70%
   - B. 3.82%
   - C. 4.62%
2. After the recapitalization, Tessaro's required return on equity is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-e)
   - A. 8.01%
   - B. 8.82%
   - C. 9.35%
3. After the recapitalization, Tessaro's after-tax cost of debt is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-b)
   - A. 3.60%
   - B. 4.35%
   - C. 4.76%
4. After the recapitalization, Tessaro's WACC is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-f)
   - A. 6.13%
   - B. 6.58%
   - C. 7.39%

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** The constant growth model needs next year's dividend: $D_1 = 114.40 \times 1.055 = 120.692$.
> Expected dividend yield $= 120.692/5{,}200 = 2.3210\%$.
> $\text{ERP} = 2.3210 + 5.50 - 4.00 = 3.8210\%$, or about 3.82%.
> A (3.70%) is wrong because it uses the trailing dividend yield, $114.40/5{,}200 = 2.2000\%$, instead of the forward yield: $2.2000 + 5.50 - 4.00 = 3.70\%$.
> C (4.62%) is wrong because it subtracts the 3-month bill yield, against Maren Hale's policy and the long horizon of the estimate: $2.3210 + 5.50 - 3.20 = 4.6210\%$.
> 2. **C.** Post-recapitalization debt $= 600 + 1{,}200 = 1{,}800$; equity $= 4{,}000 - 1{,}200 = 2{,}800$ (24 million shares repurchased at 50.00); D/E $= 1{,}800/2{,}800 = 0.6429$.
> Unlever Tessaro's own beta at its current D/E: $\beta_{Asset} = 1.05/[1 + (1 - 0.25)(0.15)] = 1.05/1.1125 = 0.9438$.
> Relever at the new D/E: $\beta = 0.9438 \times [1 + (1 - 0.25)(0.6429)] = 0.9438 \times 1.4821 = 1.3989$.
> $r_e = 4.00 + 1.3989(3.8210) = 4.00 + 5.3451 = 9.3451\%$, or about 9.35%.
> The peer beta of 1.20 is a decoy: Tessaro's own regression beta is reliable and already reflects its operating risk.
> A (8.01%) is wrong because it keeps the pre-recapitalization beta, ignoring that more debt raises the equity's financial risk: $4.00 + 1.05(3.8210) = 8.0120\%$.
> B (8.82%) is wrong because it measures post-recapitalization D/E against the pre-repurchase equity of 4,000: $1{,}800/4{,}000 = 0.45$, so $\beta = 0.9438 \times 1.3375 = 1.2624$ and $r_e = 4.00 + 1.2624(3.8210) = 8.8235\%$.
> 3. **B.** Post-recapitalization coverage $= 560/104 = 5.3846$, in the BBB band (5.0 to 8.0), and D/E of 64.29% is also in the BBB band (50% to 80%), so the ratios agree and the spread is 1.80%.
> Pre-tax cost of debt $= 4.00 + 1.80 = 5.80\%$; after tax at the marginal rate $= 5.80 \times (1 - 0.25) = 4.3500\%$.
> A (3.60%) is wrong because it keeps the current AA rating, $(4.00 + 0.80)(1 - 0.25) = 3.60\%$, although the WACC for the new structure needs the cost of debt at that structure.
> C (4.76%) is wrong because it tax-adjusts at the 18% effective rate instead of the marginal rate: $5.80 \times (1 - 0.18) = 4.7560\%$.
> 4. **C.** Weights at the new structure: $w_d = 1{,}800/4{,}600 = 0.3913$ and $w_e = 2{,}800/4{,}600 = 0.6087$.
> $\text{WACC} = 0.3913(4.35) + 0.6087(9.3451) = 1.7022 + 5.6883 = 7.3905\%$, or about 7.39%.
> Before the recapitalization, $\text{WACC} = 0.1304(3.60) + 0.8696(8.0120) = 0.4696 + 6.9670 = 7.4366\%$, so the WACC falls by only about 0.05 percentage points.
> Brandvold's argument fails because both the cost of equity and the cost of debt rise with leverage, offsetting almost all of the shift toward cheaper debt; the remaining small decline reflects the added interest tax shield.
> A (6.13%) is wrong because it uses the D/E ratio as the debt weight: $0.6429(4.35) + 0.3571(9.3451) = 2.7964 + 3.3375 = 6.1340\%$.
> B (6.58%) is wrong because it applies the new weights but keeps the pre-recapitalization cost of equity, which is Brandvold's error: $0.3913(4.35) + 0.6087(8.0120) = 1.7022 + 4.8769 = 6.5791\%$.

## Distractor design

- Q1: A uses the trailing instead of the forward dividend yield (period error, $D_0$ vs $D_1$); C subtracts the bill yield instead of the 10-year yield (spot short rate vs long-horizon rate).
- Q2: A ignores the effect of leverage on beta (skips the relevering step); B divides new debt by pre-repurchase equity (misread capital structure).
- Q3: A keeps the pre-recapitalization rating (current vs marginal cost of debt); C uses the effective instead of the marginal tax rate (wrong tax rate).
- Q4: A treats D/E as the debt weight (wrong formula variant); B holds the cost of equity constant as leverage rises (ignores the leverage effect on equity risk).
