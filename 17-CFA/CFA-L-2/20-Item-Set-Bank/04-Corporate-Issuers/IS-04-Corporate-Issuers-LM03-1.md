# Tarrant Foodservice Equity Research Update

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Priya Okafor, CFA, covers food distribution companies for Brightwater Securities, an independent research firm.
She is updating her valuation of Tarrant Foodservice Holdings (Tarrant), a listed distributor that supplies restaurants, hospitals and schools.
Tarrant's revenue is moderately cyclical, and the company finances itself at a target structure of 30% debt, 10% preferred stock and 60% common equity at market values.
Tarrant's marginal tax rate is 25%, and it has ample taxable income to absorb its interest deductions.

Tarrant has one senior unsecured bond outstanding: a 6.5% coupon, semiannual-pay issue rated A that matures in exactly six years.
The bond trades only a few times a month, so Okafor does not trust its last quoted price.
Exhibit 1 lists four liquid A-rated bonds of other consumer staples issuers, all semiannual-pay with a par value of 100.

**Exhibit 1: Liquid A-rated comparable bonds**

| Bond | Coupon | Years to maturity | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| W | 4.0% | 5 | 98.20 |
| X | 6.0% | 5 | 106.90 |
| Y | 5.0% | 9 | 99.00 |
| Z | 7.0% | 9 | 112.00 |

Tarrant's preferred shares are cumulative, perpetual and non-callable, with a 5.75% dividend rate on a par value of 25.00, and they trade at 23.40.
The common shares trade at 38.00.
Tarrant paid a dividend of 0.40 per share last year, a token payout that management calls symbolic because most free cash flow goes to warehouse automation and share repurchases.
Consensus expects the dividend to grow at 2.5% per year.

**Exhibit 2: Equity inputs**

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| 20-year government bond yield | 3.40% |
| 3-month government bill yield | 1.90% |
| Equity risk premium (geometric mean, over long bonds) | 5.20% |
| CAPM beta (60 monthly returns) | 0.92 |
| Fama-French market beta | 1.05 |
| SMB beta and SMB premium | 0.40 and 2.10% |
| HML beta and HML premium | -0.25 and 3.40% |
| Historical premium of equities over corporate bonds | 4.50% |

Brightwater uses the 20-year government bond yield as the risk-free rate for all long-horizon estimates.
For the bond yield plus risk premium (BYPRP) approach, the firm's policy is to use the analyst's estimate of the YTM on the company's own long-term debt.

Okafor's supervisor, Daniel Reyes, reviews her draft on 14 May.
He notes that her dividend discount model (DDM) estimate of Tarrant's cost of equity is far below her other estimates, and he asks her which estimate should be excluded from the range she publishes.

Tarrant's treasurer has told investors that the company will refinance the six-year bond next year.
She lists three structures under consideration.

Structure 1: a straight bond that Tarrant can call at par after three years.

Structure 2: replacing part of the debt with a new preferred issue on which unpaid dividends do not accumulate.

Structure 3: a straight bond that bondholders can put back to Tarrant at par if Tarrant's rating falls below investment grade.

Reyes asks Okafor to explain to clients how each feature is likely to affect the return investors require at issuance.

## Questions

1. Using matrix pricing, the estimated pre-tax YTM on Tarrant's six-year bond is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-b)
   - A. 2.31%
   - B. 4.62%
   - C. 4.82%
2. Okafor's estimate of Tarrant's cost of preferred equity is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-e)
   - A. 4.61%
   - B. 5.75%
   - C. 6.14%
3. Using the Fama-French three-factor model, Tarrant's required return on equity is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-d)
   - A. 7.35%
   - B. 8.85%
   - C. 10.55%
4. Consistent with Brightwater's policy, the BYPRP estimate of Tarrant's required return on equity is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-e)
   - A. 9.12%
   - B. 9.72%
   - C. 11.00%
5. In response to Reyes's request, the estimate Okafor should most likely exclude is the: (LOS: los-04-03-d)
   - A. CAPM estimate, because a single-factor model omits the size and value exposures shown in Exhibit 2.
   - B. DDM estimate, because it falls below Tarrant's pre-tax cost of debt and Tarrant's token dividend is not a stable basis for the model.
   - C. BYPRP estimate, because the historical equity-over-bond premium is arbitrary and Tarrant's bond is thinly traded.
6. Relative to an otherwise identical security without the feature, the structure most likely to carry a lower required return at issuance is: (LOS: los-04-03-a)
   - A. Structure 1.
   - B. Structure 2.
   - C. Structure 3.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Solve each comparable's semiannual yield, double it, average within maturity, then interpolate.
> W: N = 10, PMT = 2.0, PV = -98.20, FV = 100 gives 2.2025% per half-year, or 4.4050%.
> X: N = 10, PMT = 3.0, PV = -106.90 gives 2.2229%, or 4.4457%.
> Y: N = 18, PMT = 2.5, PV = -99.00 gives 2.5701%, or 5.1402%.
> Z: N = 18, PMT = 3.5, PV = -112.00 gives 2.6529%, or 5.3058%.
> Five-year average $= (4.4050 + 4.4457)/2 = 4.4254\%$; nine-year average $= (5.1402 + 5.3058)/2 = 5.2230\%$.
> Per-year increment $= (5.2230 - 4.4254)/4 = 0.1994\%$.
> Six-year YTM $= 4.4254 + 1 \times 0.1994 = 4.6248\%$, or about 4.62%, before any bump for Tarrant's extra illiquidity.
> A (2.31%) is wrong because it interpolates the semiannual yields without doubling them: $4.6248/2 = 2.3124\%$.
> C (4.82%) is wrong because it averages all four bonds into one number, $(4.4050 + 4.4457 + 5.1402 + 5.3058)/4 = 4.8242\%$, which is the seven-year point, not the six-year point.
> 2. **C.** The annual preferred dividend is $0.0575 \times 25.00 = 1.4375$, so $r_p = 1.4375/23.40 = 0.061432$, or about 6.14%.
> A (4.61%) is wrong because it applies the 25% tax shield to preferred dividends, which are not deductible: $6.1432 \times 0.75 = 4.6074\%$.
> B (5.75%) is wrong because it uses the stated dividend rate on par instead of the dividend over the current market price.
> 3. **B.** $r_e = 3.40 + 1.05(5.20) + 0.40(2.10) + (-0.25)(3.40) = 3.40 + 5.46 + 0.84 - 0.85 = 8.85\%$.
> The negative HML beta subtracts 0.85 points because Tarrant behaves like a growth stock.
> A (7.35%) is wrong because it uses the 3-month bill yield as the risk-free rate: $1.90 + 5.46 + 0.84 - 0.85 = 7.35\%$.
> C (10.55%) is wrong because it drops the sign on the HML beta: $3.40 + 5.46 + 0.84 + 0.85 = 10.55\%$.
> 4. **A.** The firm's policy uses the estimated YTM on Tarrant's own long-term debt, which is the 4.6248% matrix-pricing estimate from Question 1.
> $r_e = 4.6248 + 4.50 = 9.1248\%$, or about 9.12%.
> B (9.72%) is wrong because it uses the nine-year comparable average instead of Tarrant's six-year estimate: $5.2230 + 4.50 = 9.7230\%$.
> C (11.00%) is wrong because it uses the 6.5% coupon rate, which is not a current yield: $6.50 + 4.50 = 11.00\%$.
> 5. **B.** The DDM estimate is $r_e = 0.40(1.025)/38.00 + 0.025 = 0.010789 + 0.025 = 0.035789$, or about 3.58%.
> That is below Tarrant's 4.62% pre-tax cost of debt and its 6.14% preferred yield, which is impossible for the residual claim in the capital structure.
> The DDM also requires a stable, predictable dividend, and Tarrant's token dividend plus buybacks do not reflect its cash returned to shareholders.
> A is wrong because the CAPM estimate, $3.40 + 0.92(5.20) = 8.18\%$, is plausible and sits above both the debt and preferred costs; omitting factors is a reason estimates differ, not a reason to exclude one.
> C is wrong because the arbitrary premium is a general weakness of BYPRP, and the 9.12% estimate is anchored on a matrix-priced yield, not on the stale quote.
> 6. **C.** A put option benefits investors, who accept a lower yield at issuance in exchange for the right to sell the bond back at par if credit quality deteriorates.
> A is wrong because a call option benefits the issuer, so investors demand a higher yield.
> B is wrong because non-cumulative preferred is riskier for investors than cumulative preferred, so it carries a higher required return.

## Distractor design

- Q1: A forgets to double the semiannual yield (period error); C averages all four comparables instead of interpolating by maturity (wrong formula variant).
- Q2: A applies a tax shield to preferred dividends (before-tax vs after-tax error); B uses the stated rate on par instead of the market price.
- Q3: A uses the 3-month bill as the risk-free rate (misread exhibit); C drops the negative sign on the HML beta (sign error).
- Q4: B uses the nine-year comparable yield instead of Tarrant's own interpolated yield (misread exhibit); C uses the coupon rate instead of the YTM.
- Q5: A treats a model difference as a plausibility failure; C cites a general weakness instead of the sanity check against senior claims.
- Q6: A misreads who benefits from a call option; B reverses the cumulative versus non-cumulative preferred effect.
