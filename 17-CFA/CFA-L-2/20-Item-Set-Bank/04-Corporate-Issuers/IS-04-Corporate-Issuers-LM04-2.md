# Harrowgate Group Strategic Review

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Thandiwe Nkosi, CFA, is a portfolio manager at Ridgeway Asset Management, which owns 6% of Harrowgate Group plc, a UK-listed industrial conglomerate.
An activist fund holding 9% of the shares has called for a break-up, and on 2 June 2026 Harrowgate's board announced a strategic review.
Harrowgate reports three segments: Packaging, a mature and stable business; Specialty Chemicals; and Digital Labels, a fast-growing business that is not yet profitable.

**Exhibit 1: Segment data, trailing twelve months (GBP millions)**

| Segment | Revenue | Segment EBITDA | Revenue growth | Peer median multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Packaging | 2,400 | 420 | 2% | 8.0x EV/EBITDA |
| Specialty Chemicals | 1,600 | 300 | 4% | 10.0x EV/EBITDA |
| Digital Labels | 250 | (10) | 38% | 4.0x EV/sales |

Unallocated corporate costs are GBP80 million a year.
For her sum-of-the-parts valuation, Nkosi allocates corporate costs to Packaging and Specialty Chemicals in proportion to their revenue; Digital Labels is valued on sales and receives no allocation.
Harrowgate has 400 million shares outstanding at GBP9.50, total debt of GBP2,400 million, and cash of GBP250 million.

The activist proposes that Harrowgate sell Specialty Chemicals and spin off Digital Labels to its shareholders.
On 16 June 2026, a private equity firm offered GBP3,100 million, on a cash-free and debt-free basis, for Specialty Chemicals.
Nkosi gathers the comparable acquisitions in Exhibit 2.

**Exhibit 2: Completed acquisitions of specialty chemicals businesses (GBP millions)**

| Transaction | Year closed | Enterprise value paid | Target EBITDA | Target sales |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deal 1 | 2021 | 1,440 | 120 | 800 |
| Deal 2 | 2022 | 2,730 | 210 | 1,300 |
| Deal 3 | 2024 | 880 | 80 | 440 |

Nkosi weights the mean EV/EBITDA multiple at 60% and the mean EV/sales multiple at 40%.
She applies them to the segment's EBITDA before any corporate cost allocation, because an acquirer would absorb the business into its own corporate structure.

She then discusses her approach with a colleague, Owen Pryce.
Nkosi states: "I did not add a takeover premium to the value I derived from the comparable acquisitions, because the deal multiples already include one."
Pryce replies: "For Digital Labels, comparable company analysis is the right starting point, because the spun-off company's shares will trade without a control premium."

Harrowgate's CFO has disclosed the planned use of proceeds if the sale goes ahead.
After taxes and fees, net proceeds would be GBP2,850 million; GBP1,500 million would repay debt and GBP1,350 million would fund a share repurchase.
Only GBP12 million of corporate costs would disappear with the sale; the rest would remain with Harrowgate.
Nkosi measures leverage as net debt to EBITDA after all corporate costs, and she notes that the repurchase would consume the proceeds not used for debt repayment, leaving cash at GBP250 million.

## Questions

1. Which statement about the activist's two proposals is most accurate? (LOS: los-04-04-a)
   - A. Both are divestments, but only the sale of Specialty Chemicals raises cash for Harrowgate.
   - B. The spin off of Digital Labels is a restructuring action, because Harrowgate's shareholders retain the same economic exposure.
   - C. The sale of Specialty Chemicals is a balance sheet restructuring, because part of the proceeds would retire debt.
2. Based on Exhibit 1 and Nkosi's allocation method, Harrowgate's conglomerate discount is closest to: (LOS: los-04-04-f)
   - A. GBP706 million.
   - B. GBP1,410 million.
   - C. GBP2,856 million.
3. Using comparable transaction analysis, the estimated takeover value of Specialty Chemicals is closest to: (LOS: los-04-04-c)
   - A. GBP3,188 million.
   - B. GBP3,419 million.
   - C. GBP4,444 million.
4. Regarding the statements by Nkosi and Pryce, which is most likely correct? (LOS: los-04-04-c)
   - A. Nkosi's statement only.
   - B. Pryce's statement only.
   - C. Both statements.
5. Using Nkosi's sum-of-the-parts values for Packaging and Digital Labels, the value of Specialty Chemicals implied by Harrowgate's current enterprise value is closest to: (LOS: los-04-04-f)
   - A. GBP1,590 million.
   - B. GBP1,974 million.
   - C. GBP2,680 million.
6. If the sale closes and proceeds are used as planned, Harrowgate's pro forma net debt to EBITDA is closest to: (LOS: los-04-04-d)
   - A. 1.03x.
   - B. 1.80x.
   - C. 1.90x.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** A sale and a spin off both shrink Harrowgate's size and scope, so both are divestments.
> A sale transfers the business to a buyer for cash; a spin off distributes shares of a new independent company to existing shareholders as a stock dividend and raises no cash for the parent.
> B is wrong because a restructuring leaves size and scope unchanged, while a spin off removes Digital Labels from Harrowgate's perimeter.
> C is wrong because a balance sheet restructuring changes asset composition or capital structure without changing scope (for example, a sale leaseback or a dividend recapitalization); using proceeds to repay debt does not turn a business sale into one.
> 2. **A.** Allocation: Packaging receives $80 \times 2{,}400/4{,}000 = 48$ and Specialty Chemicals $80 \times 1{,}600/4{,}000 = 32$.
> Adjusted EBITDA: Packaging $420 - 48 = 372$; Specialty Chemicals $300 - 32 = 268$.
> $EV_{SOTP} = 372 \times 8.0 + 268 \times 10.0 + 250 \times 4.0 = 2{,}976 + 2{,}680 + 1{,}000 = 6{,}656$.
> Trading EV $= 400 \times 9.50 + 2{,}400 - 250 = 3{,}800 + 2{,}150 = 5{,}950$.
> Conglomerate discount $= 6{,}656 - 5{,}950 = 706$, so the market prices Harrowgate below the sum of its parts.
> B is wrong because it skips the corporate cost allocation: $420 \times 8.0 + 300 \times 10.0 + 1{,}000 - 5{,}950 = 7{,}360 - 5{,}950 = 1{,}410$.
> C is wrong because it compares an enterprise value with market capitalization: $6{,}656 - 3{,}800 = 2{,}856$.
> 3. **B.** Deal EV/EBITDA multiples are $1{,}440/120 = 12.0$, $2{,}730/210 = 13.0$, $880/80 = 11.0$, mean $12.0000$.
> Deal EV/sales multiples are $1{,}440/800 = 1.80$, $2{,}730/1{,}300 = 2.10$, $880/440 = 2.00$, mean $1.9667$.
> $V_0 = 0.60 \times (12.0000 \times 300) + 0.40 \times (1.9667 \times 1{,}600) = 2{,}160.0000 + 1{,}258.6667 = 3{,}418.6667$.
> The GBP3,100 million bid is $3{,}100/3{,}418.6667 - 1 = -9.32\%$ below this value, and it equals $3{,}100/300 = 10.33$x EBITDA against a 12.0x deal mean.
> A is wrong because it applies the EV/EBITDA multiple to the post-allocation EBITDA of 268, contrary to Nkosi's stated method: $0.60 \times 12.0 \times 268 + 1{,}258.6667 = 3{,}188.2667$.
> C is wrong because it adds a 30% takeover premium to a value that already embeds one: $3{,}418.6667 \times 1.30 = 4{,}444.2667$.
> 4. **C.** Nkosi is correct because comparable transaction multiples come from actual acquisition prices, so the takeover premium is already embedded and adding one double counts.
> Pryce is correct because a spun-off company trades without a control premium, so trading multiples of comparable listed companies are the natural valuation tool.
> A and B are wrong because each rejects one of two correct statements.
> 5. **B.** Implied value of Specialty Chemicals $=$ trading EV minus the SOTP values of the other segments $= 5{,}950 - 2{,}976 - 1{,}000 = 1{,}974$.
> The bid is $3{,}100/1{,}974 - 1 = 57.04\%$ above the value the market implicitly credits, even though it is 9.32% below the comparable transaction value; both comparisons are true at once, which is why the sale can create value for Harrowgate's shareholders.
> A is wrong because it values Packaging on unallocated EBITDA: $5{,}950 - 420 \times 8.0 - 1{,}000 = 1{,}590$.
> C is wrong because it is the segment's value at the peer trading multiple ($268 \times 10.0 = 2{,}680$), not the value implied by Harrowgate's current EV.
> 6. **C.** Pre-sale net debt $= 2{,}400 - 250 = 2{,}150$; pre-sale EBITDA $= 420 + 300 - 10 - 80 = 630$; ratio $3.4127$x.
> Post-sale net debt $= 2{,}150 - 1{,}500 = 650$, since the buyback uses the rest of the proceeds.
> Post-sale EBITDA $= 420 - 10 - (80 - 12) = 342$, because only GBP12 million of corporate costs disappear.
> Net debt to EBITDA $= 650/342 = 1.9006$x.
> A is wrong because it keeps Specialty Chemicals' EBITDA in the denominator: $650/630 = 1.0317$x.
> B is wrong because it assumes all of the segment's GBP32 million allocated corporate cost disappears (stranded costs ignored): $650/(420 - 10 - 48) = 650/362 = 1.7956$x.

## Distractor design

1. B calls a spin off a restructuring (scope change missed); C calls a business sale a balance sheet restructuring because of the use of proceeds.
2. B skips the corporate cost allocation; C compares SOTP enterprise value with market capitalization.
3. A misreads the exhibit instructions and applies the multiple to allocated EBITDA; C adds a premium to a transaction-based value (double count).
4. A and B each reject one correct method statement.
5. A values Packaging before corporate costs; C confuses the peer-multiple value with the value implied by the current EV.
6. A leaves the divested segment's EBITDA in the denominator; B ignores stranded corporate costs.
