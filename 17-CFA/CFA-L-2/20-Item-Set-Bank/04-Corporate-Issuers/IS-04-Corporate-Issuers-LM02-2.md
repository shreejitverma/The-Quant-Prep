# Kestrel Microsystems: Water Efficiency in an Equity Valuation

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Priya Raman, CFA, is an equity analyst at Harbourline Pension Fund.
She is updating her valuation of Kestrel Microsystems, a listed semiconductor foundry whose fabrication plants sit in a region that regulators classify as water stressed.
Chip fabrication consumes large volumes of ultrapure water, and Raman identifies water management as a material environmental factor for Kestrel.

Harbourline subscribes to two ESG data vendors.
One vendor ranks Kestrel in the top decile of its industry for environmental management, while the other ranks it only slightly above the industry median.
Raman therefore builds her own comparison from Kestrel's sustainability report and the reports of its peers, following the sector-specific factors in the SASB materiality map.

Exhibit 1: Water and output data, 2025

| Item | Kestrel | Peer average |
|---|---|---|
| Water withdrawn (megaliters) | 36,400 | 21,000 |
| Revenue (USD millions) | 9,100 | 4,200 |
| Wafers produced (thousands) | 1,560 | 980 |

Raman normalizes water use per USD 1 million of revenue to compare companies of different size.
She concludes that Kestrel's water recycling program is a competitive advantage that customers increasingly reward.

Exhibit 2: Raman's 2026 forecast before ESG adjustments (USD millions)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue | 10,000 |
| COGS as a percentage of revenue | 54.0% |
| Selling, general and administrative expense | 1,450 |

Raman makes two ESG adjustments to the 2026 forecast.
First, two large customers have begun allocating orders to suppliers with lower water and emissions footprints, which she expects to raise Kestrel's revenue by 1.2%.
Second, the expanded recycling program is expected to reduce COGS as a percentage of revenue by 0.9 percentage points; she assumes peers' ratios stay unchanged.

Raman values Kestrel's equity with a single-stage free cash flow to equity model.
The most recent FCFE is USD 2.40 per share.
Before ESG integration she used a required return on equity of 10.5% and a long-term growth rate of 4.0%.
After integration she lowers the required return to 10.0%, reflecting lower exposure to water-rationing risk, and raises long-term growth to 4.5%.

Raman's colleague Lukas Brandt makes three statements during the investment committee meeting.

Statement 1: "Kestrel's sustainability report devotes several pages to solar panels installed at its head office, so that initiative should carry significant weight in our materiality assessment."

Statement 2: "A water-withdrawal cap that regulators now expect after 2032 may be immaterial to a two-year holder of the stock but material to a long-term holder, and that horizon could shorten abruptly if a severe drought triggers emergency rationing."

Statement 3: "Governance best practice varies widely across industries, so for a semiconductor company we should focus only on environmental and social factors."

Brandt also asks what other adjustments are consistent with Raman's integration approach.

## Questions

1. The characteristic weakness of the approach that produced the conflicting vendor rankings of Kestrel is: (LOS: los-04-02-c)
   - A. inconsistent reporting and voluntary, variable disclosure of ESG information among companies.
   - B. the subjective assessments embedded in ESG scores and rankings.
   - C. that it sets disclosure standards rather than providing company-level assessments.
2. Using Raman's normalization, Kestrel's water intensity relative to the peer average is closest to: (LOS: los-04-02-c)
   - A. 20% lower.
   - B. 25% lower.
   - C. 73% higher.
3. Kestrel's ESG-adjusted 2026 gross profit, in USD millions, is closest to: (LOS: los-04-02-d)
   - A. 4,564.
   - B. 4,690.
   - C. 4,746.
4. Which of Brandt's statements is most likely correct? (LOS: los-04-02-c)
   - A. Statement 1.
   - B. Statement 2.
   - C. Statement 3.
5. Kestrel's ESG-adjusted equity value per share is closest to: (LOS: los-04-02-d)
   - A. USD 41.60.
   - B. USD 43.64.
   - C. USD 45.60.
6. Which additional adjustment is most consistent with Raman's ESG integration for Kestrel's equity? (LOS: los-04-02-d)
   - A. Raising the terminal-value multiple relative to a peer without a comparable water advantage.
   - B. Writing down Kestrel's fabrication plants as stranded assets.
   - C. Limiting the analysis to downside risk, because the recycling program's benefits are capped.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** ESG data vendors convert company disclosures into scores and rankings, and the characteristic weakness of that approach is the subjectivity of the assessments, which is why two vendors can rank the same company very differently.
> A is wrong because inconsistent, voluntary disclosure is the weakness of the proprietary approach that works directly from company reports, which is what Raman does after seeing the divergent rankings.
> C is wrong because standard setting describes the not-for-profit frameworks such as SASB, whose materiality map Raman uses as a guide.
> 2. **A.** Kestrel: $36{,}400 / 9{,}100 = 4.0000$ megaliters per USD 1 million of revenue.
> Peers: $21{,}000 / 4{,}200 = 5.0000$ megaliters per USD 1 million of revenue.
> Relative intensity $= 4.0000 / 5.0000 - 1 = -0.2000$, so Kestrel uses 20% less water per unit of revenue.
> B is wrong because it uses Kestrel as the base ($5.0000 / 4.0000 - 1 = 0.2500$), which says peers are 25% more intensive, not that Kestrel is 25% lower.
> C is wrong because it compares absolute withdrawals without normalizing for size ($36{,}400 / 21{,}000 - 1 = 0.7333$).
> 3. **C.** Adjusted revenue $= 10{,}000 \times 1.012 = 10{,}120$.
> Adjusted COGS ratio $= 54.0\% - 0.9\% = 53.1\%$, so gross margin $= 1 - 0.531 = 0.4690$.
> Gross profit $= 10{,}120 \times 0.4690 = 4{,}746.28$.
> A is wrong because it adds the 0.9-point cost improvement to the COGS ratio instead of subtracting it ($10{,}120 \times (1 - 0.549) = 4{,}564.12$).
> B is wrong because it applies the cost adjustment but omits the 1.2% revenue adjustment ($10{,}000 \times 0.4690 = 4{,}690.00$).
> 4. **B.** The investment horizon is central to materiality: a long-dated regulatory risk can be immaterial to a short-horizon investor yet material to a long-horizon one, and the horizon can shift suddenly with regulation or a controversy.
> A is wrong because companies may showcase positive but immaterial initiatives; head-office solar panels do not affect a foundry's operations, financial performance, or valuation the way water use does.
> C is wrong because governance is relevant in every industry and governance factors are reasonably consistent across companies; it is environmental and social best practice that varies by industry.
> 5. **C.** Before integration: $V_0 = 2.40 \times 1.040 / (0.105 - 0.040) = 2.4960 / 0.0650 = 38.40$.
> After integration: $V_0 = 2.40 \times 1.045 / (0.100 - 0.045) = 2.5080 / 0.0550 = 45.60$, an increase of $45.60 / 38.40 - 1 = 18.75\%$.
> A positive ESG exposure lowers the required return and raises growth, and both changes raise value.
> A is wrong because it adjusts only the required return and leaves growth at 4.0% ($2.4960 / 0.0600 = 41.60$).
> B is wrong because it divides the current FCFE rather than next year's FCFE by the new spread ($2.40 / 0.0550 = 43.64$).
> 6. **A.** In equity analysis, ESG integration identifies opportunities as well as mitigating downside risk, and adjustments can be made to forecast financials, the discount rate, or a price or terminal-value multiple; a durable water advantage supports a higher multiple.
> B is wrong because stranded assets are assets made obsolete or uneconomic by regulation or demand shifts; Raman's analysis finds Kestrel's fabs better positioned than peers', not impaired.
> C is wrong because a focus on downside risk with capped upside describes fixed-income analysis, where the bond redeems at par; an equity analyst also captures the opportunity.

## Distractor design

1. A: weakness of the proprietary company-data approach assigned to vendors; C: weakness of the framework approach assigned to vendors.
2. B: wrong base for the percentage difference; C: misread exhibit (absolute water use compared without normalizing per USD 1 million of revenue).
3. A: sign error on the COGS ratio adjustment; B: one of two forecast adjustments omitted.
4. A: immaterial positive disclosure treated as material; C: industry variation attributed to governance instead of environmental and social factors.
5. A: partial integration (discount rate adjusted, growth left unchanged); B: period error (CF0 used in place of CF1).
6. B: negative-exposure balance-sheet channel applied to a positive exposure; C: fixed-income focus applied to an equity analysis.
