# Vantor Cold Chain Buyout Hurdle Rate

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Halden Ridge Capital, a private equity firm, has signed exclusivity to acquire Vantor Cold Chain Logistics (Vantor), a privately held operator of refrigerated warehouses and truck fleets serving grocery retailers.
Associate Tomasz Brandt is preparing the cost of capital section of the investment committee memo, and partner Aisha Mensah will decide on 2 June whether to submit a binding bid.
Vantor's debt consists of a bank term loan and a lease, and the company has no traded securities and no credit rating.
Mensah instructs Brandt to treat Vantor's current capital structure as its target and to cost all of Vantor's debt, including the lease, at a synthetic-rating yield.

**Exhibit 1: Vantor data, fiscal 2025 (currency in millions)**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| EBITDA | 24.0 |
| Interest expense | 3.2 |
| Total debt, including lease liability | 60.0 |
| Total assets | 250.0 |
| Marginal tax rate | 21% |
| EBITDA/interest in 2023 and 2024 | 9.1 and 8.3 |
| Debt-to-equity (equity = assets minus debt) in 2023 and 2024 | 22% and 27% |
| Revenue from the two largest customers | 55% |
| Revenue under multi-year minimum-volume contracts | 80% |

**Exhibit 2: Public peer medians**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Equity beta | 1.30 |
| Debt-to-equity | 0.55 |
| Marginal tax rate | 25% |
| EBITDA/interest | 5.8 |
| Revenue from the two largest customers | 20% |

**Exhibit 3: Halden Ridge synthetic rating schedule**

| Rating | EBITDA/interest | Debt-to-equity | Spread over 10-year government yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| AA | above 10.0 | below 20% | 0.90% |
| A | 7.0 to 10.0 | 20% to 30% | 1.35% |
| BBB | 4.5 to 7.0 | 30% to 40% | 1.95% |
| BB | 3.0 to 4.5 | 40% to 55% | 2.90% |

The 10-year government bond yield is 4.00%.
Brandt's equity inputs are an ERP of 5.5%, a size premium of 3.0% taken from the lowest market capitalization decile after a downward adjustment for distressed companies, and a company-specific risk premium of 2.0%.
Brandt also proposes adding a 3.0% illiquidity premium to the required return on equity "because Halden Ridge cannot sell Vantor shares on an exchange."
Mensah prefers the expanded CAPM to the build-up approach because the peer group is large and closely comparable.

Vantor's largest distribution center is leased for 12 years at 3.8 million per year, paid at each year-end.
The lessor's records, obtained in due diligence, show a fair value of 36.0 million for the facility, direct lease initiation costs of 0.8 million, and an expected residual value of 5.0 million at the end of the lease term.
Brandt wants to compare the rate implicit in the lease with Vantor's synthetic unsecured borrowing cost.

Mensah raises two further points in her review.
First, she asks whether Vantor's credit ratios, which she suspects point in different directions, should be resolved mechanically or with judgment.
Second, she asks Brandt to identify which of Vantor's operating characteristics most justifies charging Vantor a company-specific risk premium that the peers do not bear.

## Questions

1. The credit spread Brandt should most appropriately apply to Vantor's debt is: (LOS: los-04-03-b)
   - A. 1.35%, because both credit ratios fall in the A band.
   - B. 1.65%, the midpoint of the A and BBB spreads, because the two ratios conflict.
   - C. 1.95%, because the ratios conflict and the trend in both ratios points toward BBB.
2. The rate implicit in Vantor's distribution center lease is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-b)
   - A. 3.46%
   - B. 4.85%
   - C. 5.21%
3. After adjusting the peer median beta for differences in financial leverage, Vantor's equity beta is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-f)
   - A. 0.92
   - B. 1.15
   - C. 1.62
4. Using the expanded CAPM, the most appropriate estimate of Vantor's required return on equity is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-e)
   - A. 14.06%
   - B. 15.32%
   - C. 18.32%
5. Using the credit spread Brandt should apply and the most appropriate expanded CAPM estimate, Vantor's WACC is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-f)
   - A. 11.97%
   - B. 12.77%
   - C. 13.07%
6. The characteristic that most justifies a company-specific risk premium for Vantor relative to the peers is Vantor's: (LOS: los-04-03-a)
   - A. concentration of revenue in its two largest customers.
   - B. share of revenue under multi-year minimum-volume contracts.
   - C. asset base of refrigerated warehouses and truck fleets.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** EBITDA/interest $= 24.0/3.2 = 7.50$, inside the A band (7.0 to 10.0).
> Debt-to-equity $= 60.0/(250.0 - 60.0) = 60.0/190.0 = 0.3158$, inside the BBB band (30% to 40%).
> The ratios conflict, so the synthetic rating is a matter of judgment informed by trends.
> Coverage has fallen from 9.1 to 8.3 to 7.5 and leverage has risen from 22% to 27% to 31.6%, so both ratios are deteriorating toward BBB, which supports the 1.95% BBB spread.
> A is wrong because it measures debt-to-equity against total assets, $60.0/250.0 = 24\%$, although the schedule defines equity as assets minus debt.
> B is wrong because splitting the spread mechanically ignores the trend analysis that the conflict requires.
> 2. **B.** Solve $36.0 + 0.8 = 36.8 = \sum_{t=1}^{12} 3.8/(1+r)^t + 5.0/(1+r)^{12}$ for $r$.
> Calculator: N = 12, PMT = 3.8, PV = -36.8, FV = 5.0, CPT I/Y gives 4.8498%, or about 4.85%.
> The lease is secured by the facility, so it costs less than Vantor's unsecured synthetic yield of $4.00 + 1.95 = 5.95\%$.
> A (3.46%) is wrong because it ignores the residual value, which the lessor recovers: FV = 0 gives 3.4634%.
> C (5.21%) is wrong because it omits the lessor's direct costs from the time-zero amount: PV = -36.0 gives 5.2115%.
> 3. **B.** Unlever at the peer tax rate and peer D/E: $\beta_{Asset} = 1.30/[1 + (1 - 0.25)(0.55)] = 1.30/1.4125 = 0.9204$.
> Relever at Vantor's tax rate and D/E: $\beta_{Vantor} = 0.9204 \times [1 + (1 - 0.21)(0.3158)] = 0.9204 \times 1.2495 = 1.1500$.
> Vantor carries less leverage than the peers, so its equity beta is below the peer median of 1.30.
> A (0.92) is wrong because it stops at the unlevered asset beta.
> C (1.62) is wrong because it relevers the peer's equity beta without first unlevering it: $1.30 \times 1.2495 = 1.6243$.
> 4. **B.** $r_e = 4.00 + 1.1500(5.5) + 3.0 + 2.0 = 4.00 + 6.3248 + 5.00 = 15.3248\%$, or about 15.32%.
> Illiquidity of a private stake is handled as a discount for lack of marketability applied to value, not as a premium in the required return.
> A (14.06%) is wrong because it uses the unlevered asset beta: $4.00 + 0.9204(5.5) + 5.00 = 14.0619\%$.
> C (18.32%) is wrong because it adds Brandt's 3.0% illiquidity premium: $15.3248 + 3.00 = 18.3248\%$.
> 5. **B.** Weights at the current (target) structure: $w_d = 60.0/250.0 = 0.24$ and $w_e = 190.0/250.0 = 0.76$.
> Pre-tax cost of debt $= 4.00 + 1.95 = 5.95\%$; after tax $= 5.95 \times (1 - 0.21) = 4.7005\%$.
> $\text{WACC} = 0.24(4.7005) + 0.76(15.3248) = 1.1281 + 11.6468 = 12.7749\%$, or about 12.77%.
> A (11.97%) is wrong because it uses the debt-to-equity ratio as the debt weight: $0.3158(4.7005) + 0.6842(15.3248) = 11.9697\%$.
> C (13.07%) is wrong because it omits the interest tax shield: $0.24(5.95) + 0.76(15.3248) = 13.0748\%$.
> 6. **A.** Vantor earns 55% of revenue from two customers against 20% for the peers, so the loss or weakening of one customer would hit its cash flows far harder; customer concentration is a classic company-specific risk.
> B is wrong because contracted, recurring revenue makes cash flows more predictable and lowers the cost of capital.
> C is wrong because tangible, fungible assets can be pledged or sold, which supports cheaper financing and lowers the cost of capital.

## Distractor design

- Q1: A computes D/E off total assets (misread exhibit definition); B resolves the ratio conflict mechanically instead of by trend analysis.
- Q2: A drops the residual value (wrong formula variant); C drops the lessor's direct costs from the time-zero amount.
- Q3: A stops at the asset beta; C skips the unlevering step.
- Q4: A uses the asset beta instead of the relevered beta; C adds an illiquidity premium that belongs in a discount for lack of marketability.
- Q5: A uses D/E as the capital weight (wrong formula variant); C uses the before-tax cost of debt (before-tax vs after-tax).
- Q6: B and C each name a bottom-up factor that lowers, rather than raises, the cost of capital (sign error on factor direction).
