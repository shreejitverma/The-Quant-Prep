# Aurelia Clinics Restructuring Program

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Camille Moreau, CFA, is a credit analyst at Banque Liserin covering Aurelia Clinics SA, a listed operator of 110 outpatient clinics in France and Belgium.
On 8 September 2026, Aurelia announced a three-part program.
First, it will move billing and medical transcription from its French clinics to a wholly owned service center in Morocco, with savings sized to close the gap to the peer median EBIT margin.
Second, it will sell 40 owned clinics to a healthcare REIT for EUR640 million and lease them back for 20 years at a combined annual rent of EUR36.8 million, with Aurelia paying all operating costs and property taxes.
Third, it will buy 25% of Medivo, a loss-making telehealth start-up, for EUR180 million in cash.
Aurelia's CEO described Medivo as "the first step toward a virtual-care model."
Aurelia shares fell 7% on the announcement day.

**Exhibit 1: Aurelia next-twelve-month forecast before the program (EUR millions)**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Revenue | 3,600 |
| EBIT | 252 |
| Depreciation and amortization (D&A) | 180 |
| Total debt (all at 7.0%) | 2,100 |
| Interest expense | 147 |
| Cash (earning 2.5%) | 300 |
| Interest income | 7.5 |
| Net income (tax rate 25%) | 84.375 |
| Shares outstanding (millions) | 150 |
| Share price (EUR) | 9.00 |

**Exhibit 2: EBIT margins of listed clinic operators**

| Peer 1 | Peer 2 | Peer 3 | Peer 4 | Peer 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9.5% | 11.0% | 8.6% | 12.4% | 10.2% |

The clinics being sold have a carrying amount of EUR400 million and generate EUR22 million of annual depreciation.
Aurelia's accounting policy defers the EUR240 million gain and amortizes it on a straight-line basis over the lease term as a credit within D&A.
After EUR40 million of taxes and fees, the remaining proceeds will retire 7.0% debt.
Moreau treats the full rent as an operating expense and excludes lease liabilities from the total debt she measures against her rating grid.

**Exhibit 3: Comparable sale leaseback capitalization rates**

| 25th percentile | Median | 75th percentile |
|---|---|---|
| 5.25% | 6.25% | 7.00% |

**Exhibit 4: Banque Liserin internal rating grid**

| Rating | Debt to EBITDA | EBIT interest coverage |
|---|---|---|
| BBB | 2.0x-3.0x | 4.0x-8.0x |
| BB | 3.0x-4.0x | 2.0x-4.0x |
| B | 4.0x-5.0x | 1.0x-2.0x |

Moreau expects Medivo to report a net loss of EUR64 million next year.
Aurelia will amortize EUR6 million a year of fair value adjustments on the investment.
The EUR180 million comes from cash that currently earns 2.5%.
Consistent with her firm's model, Moreau tax-effects both Aurelia's share of Medivo's loss and the forgone interest income at 25%.

## Questions

1. The Moroccan service center and the clinic transactions are best classified, respectively, as: (LOS: los-04-04-a)
   - A. a cost restructuring and a balance sheet restructuring.
   - B. a cost restructuring and a divestment.
   - C. a reorganization and a balance sheet restructuring.
2. The reduction in operating expenses needed for Aurelia to reach the peer median EBIT margin, expressed as a percentage of current operating expenses, is closest to: (LOS: los-04-04-g)
   - A. 3.20%.
   - B. 3.44%.
   - C. 3.59%.
3. The capitalization rate on the clinic transactions: (LOS: los-04-04-g)
   - A. is 5.75% and compares unfavorably with comparable transactions, because it is below the median.
   - B. is 5.75% and is supported by comparable transactions, because it lies between the 25th and 75th percentiles.
   - C. is 5.75% and compares favorably with comparable transactions, because it is below the median.
4. Considering only the clinic transactions, Aurelia's pro forma debt to EBITDA, EBIT interest coverage, and implied rating on Moreau's grid are closest to: (LOS: los-04-04-g)
   - A. 3.47x, 2.72x, and BB.
   - B. 3.80x, 2.37x, and BB.
   - C. 5.31x, 1.70x, and B.
5. Considering only the Medivo investment, Aurelia's pro forma EPS for next year is closest to: (LOS: los-04-04-e)
   - A. EUR0.39.
   - B. EUR0.43.
   - C. EUR0.46.
6. Which statement about Moreau's initial evaluation of the program is most accurate? (LOS: los-04-04-b)
   - A. The 7% share price decline on the announcement day indicates that the program is likely to destroy long-term shareholder value.
   - B. At about 13% of Aurelia's market capitalization, the Medivo investment is large by the size dimension of materiality.
   - C. Although the Medivo investment is small relative to Aurelia's enterprise value, it is likely material because it signals a change in strategy.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Relocating in-house processes to a cheaper country is offshoring, a form of cost restructuring aimed at restoring margins.
> Selling owned clinics and leasing them back is a sale leaseback, a balance sheet restructuring: Aurelia converts property into cash, keeps use of the clinics, and books a lease liability.
> B is wrong because a sale leaseback is not a divestment; Aurelia keeps operating the clinics, so its size and scope are unchanged.
> C is wrong because a reorganization is a court-supervised process for an insolvent issuer, and no court is involved.
> 2. **B.** Peer margins sorted are 8.6%, 9.5%, 10.2%, 11.0%, 12.4%, so the median is 10.2%.
> Current operating expenses $= 3{,}600 - 252 = 3{,}348$ (current margin $252/3{,}600 = 7.00\%$).
> Required cut $= 0.102 \times 3{,}600 - 252 = 367.2 - 252 = 115.2$; as a share of operating expenses, $115.2/3{,}348 = 3.44\%$.
> A is wrong because it expresses the cut as a share of revenue: $115.2/3{,}600 = 3.20\%$.
> C is wrong because it uses the mean peer margin of 10.34%: $(0.1034 \times 3{,}600 - 252)/3{,}348 = 120.24/3{,}348 = 3.59\%$.
> 3. **C.** Because Aurelia pays operating costs and taxes, rent approximates the buyer's NOI: cap rate $= 36.8/640 = 5.75\%$.
> The cap rate is the reciprocal of a valuation multiple, so a cap rate below the 6.25% median means a higher price for Aurelia as seller; the transaction compares favorably.
> A is wrong because it reads a low cap rate as a low price, reversing the direction.
> B is wrong because "supported" describes a cap rate at the median; being inside the interquartile range does not make a rate 50 bps below the median neutral.
> 4. **B.** Pre-program EBITDA $= 252 + 180 = 432$; debt to EBITDA $= 2{,}100/432 = 4.8611$x and coverage $= 252/147 = 1.7143$x, a B rating.
> Gain amortization $= 240/20 = 12$; D&A $= 180 - 22 - 12 = 146$.
> EBIT $= 252 - 36.8 + 22 + 12 = 249.2$; EBITDA $= 249.2 + 146 = 395.2$, so EBITDA falls by exactly the rent.
> Debt retired $= 640 - 40 = 600$; debt $= 1{,}500$; interest $= 147 - 0.07 \times 600 = 105$.
> Debt to EBITDA $= 1{,}500/395.2 = 3.7955$x and coverage $= 249.2/105 = 2.3733$x; both fall in the BB bands, a one-notch improvement.
> A is wrong because it forgets that rent is an operating expense: EBITDA stays at 432 ($1{,}500/432 = 3.4722$x) and EBIT becomes $252 + 22 + 12 = 286$ ($286/105 = 2.7238$x).
> C is wrong because it ignores the debt retirement: $2{,}100/395.2 = 5.3138$x and $249.2/147 = 1.6952$x.
> 5. **B.** Equity-method income $= 0.25 \times (-64) - 6 = -22.0000$; forgone interest income $= 0.025 \times 180 = 4.5000$.
> Pretax drag $= 22.0000 + 4.5000 = 26.5000$; after tax $= 26.5000 \times 0.75 = 19.8750$.
> Net income $= 84.375 - 19.875 = 64.500$; EPS $= 64.500/150 = 0.4300$, down from $84.375/150 = 0.5625$ with no change in the share count.
> A is wrong because it omits the tax effect Moreau's model applies: $(84.375 - 26.5)/150 = 0.3858$.
> C is wrong because it omits the amortization of fair value adjustments: $(84.375 - (16 + 4.5) \times 0.75)/150 = 69.000/150 = 0.4600$.
> 6. **C.** Aurelia's enterprise value $= 150 \times 9.00 + 2{,}100 - 300 = 3{,}150$, so the EUR180 million investment is $180/3{,}150 = 5.71\%$ of EV, below the 10% size threshold.
> Materiality also has a fit dimension, and a stated first step toward a virtual-care model is a strategy change that can make a small deal material.
> A is wrong because announcement-day returns have not been found to predict long-run value creation.
> B is wrong because size is judged against enterprise value, not market capitalization ($180/1{,}350 = 13.33\%$).

## Distractor design

1. B treats a sale leaseback as a divestment although the asset use is retained; C calls a voluntary program a reorganization without any court process.
2. A divides by revenue instead of operating expenses; C uses the mean instead of the median peer margin.
3. A reverses the cap rate to price relationship; B confuses "supported" (at the median) with "inside the range".
4. A omits the rent from EBITDA and EBIT; C omits the debt retirement from debt and interest.
5. A skips the tax effect required by the stated model; C forgets the fair value amortization in the equity-method line.
6. A trusts the announcement-day reaction; B uses market capitalization instead of enterprise value for the size test.
