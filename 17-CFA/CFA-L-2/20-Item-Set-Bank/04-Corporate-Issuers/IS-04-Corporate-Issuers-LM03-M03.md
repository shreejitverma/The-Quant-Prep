# Varenna Precision Castings Hurdle Rate Review

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Mireille Dubois, CFA, is an equity analyst at Coldharbour Global Investors (Coldharbour), which manages global equity portfolios for pension clients in several countries.
She covers Varenna Precision Castings (Varenna), a listed maker of turbine blades and structural castings for aircraft engines, headquartered in Country T, whose currency is the TKR.
About 70% of Varenna's revenue comes from exports invoiced in foreign currencies, and more than half of its shares are held by investors outside Country T.
The statutory corporate tax rate in Country T is 20%.
Dubois is estimating Varenna's cost of capital so that she can evaluate a proposed new casting plant.

**Exhibit 1: Varenna debt (par value 100, annual coupons)**

| Instrument | Coupon | Years to maturity | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bond V1, senior unsecured, straight | 4.60% | 9 | 101.30 | Trades daily in size |
| Bond V2, senior unsecured, convertible into common shares | 2.75% | 6 | 101.20 | Trades daily in size |
| Bank term loan | 3.10% fixed | 4 | Not traded | Arranged three years ago, before Varenna was downgraded from A to BBB |

Coldharbour's policy is to estimate the pre-tax cost of debt from the yield to maturity on the company's most liquid option-free bond whenever one exists.

**Exhibit 2: Varenna equity inputs (TKR terms)**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| 10-year Country T government bond yield | 3.25% |
| Expected return on the global equity index | 8.25% |
| Expected return on the wealth-weighted foreign currency index (expected currency appreciation plus foreign risk-free rates) | 4.85% |
| Varenna beta against the global equity index | 0.90 |
| Varenna beta against the foreign currency index | 0.55 |
| Varenna beta against the Country T equity index | 1.15 |
| Historical Country T equity risk premium | 5.20% |
| Current share price | 58.00 |
| Dividend paid last year | 1.48 |
| Forecast dividends for Years 1, 2 and 3 | 1.60, 1.75 and 1.90 |
| Forecast share price at the end of Year 3, just after the Year 3 dividend | 66.00 |
| Consensus long-term EPS growth | 5.50% |

For companies with a global shareholder base and significant foreign currency cash flows, Coldharbour's policy is to estimate the required return on equity with the international CAPM (ICAPM).
The policy uses the 10-year government bond yield as the risk-free rate for all long-horizon estimates.
Dubois also computes a multiyear dividend discount model (DDM) estimate from her own forecasts in Exhibit 2 as a cross-check.

Varenna completed a restructuring two years ago and has large tax loss carryforwards.
Dubois forecasts that the carryforwards will shelter all of Varenna's taxable income for the next five years, which covers the construction period of the new plant.

On 11 February, Dubois's colleague Anders Holm sends her two statements about how conditions in Country T will affect Varenna's cost of capital.

Statement 1: "The central bank's recent policy rate increases and the widening of domestic credit spreads as the economy slows will both raise Varenna's cost of debt, and the slowdown is also likely to raise the equity risk premium that investors demand."

Statement 2: "The government's increase in the corporate tax rate from 20% to 26%, effective next year, will lower Varenna's WACC because it makes the interest tax shield more valuable."

Dubois replies that she will address Holm's statements in her report after she finishes her debt and equity estimates.

## Questions

1. Consistent with Coldharbour's policy, Varenna's pre-tax cost of debt is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-b)
   - A. 2.53%
   - B. 3.10%
   - C. 4.42%
2. Consistent with Coldharbour's policy, Varenna's required return on equity is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-d)
   - A. 7.75%
   - B. 8.63%
   - C. 10.42%
3. Dubois's multiyear DDM estimate of Varenna's required return on equity is closest to: (LOS: los-04-03-e)
   - A. 6.30%
   - B. 7.28%
   - C. 8.26%
4. Which of Holm's statements is correct? (LOS: los-04-03-a)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both Statement 1 and Statement 2.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** V1 is the most liquid option-free bond, so its YTM is the pre-tax cost of debt.
> N = 9, PMT = 4.60, PV = -101.30, FV = 100, CPT I/Y gives 4.4218%, or about 4.42%.
> The bond trades above par, so its YTM is below its 4.60% coupon.
> A (2.53%) is wrong because it uses the convertible V2: N = 6, PMT = 2.75, PV = -101.20, FV = 100 gives 2.5319%, a yield held down by the conversion option that investors pay for, so it understates the cost of straight debt.
> B (3.10%) is wrong because it uses the rate on a bank loan arranged before the downgrade; a past borrowing rate is usable only if market conditions and the company's risk profile have not changed.
> 2. **B.** The ICAPM uses the premium on the global index and the premium on the currency index, each measured over the domestic risk-free rate.
> Global premium $= 8.25 - 3.25 = 5.00\%$; currency premium $= 4.85 - 3.25 = 1.60\%$.
> $r_e = 3.25 + 0.90(5.00) + 0.55(1.60) = 3.25 + 4.50 + 0.88 = 8.63\%$.
> The local CAPM figure, $3.25 + 1.15(5.20) = 9.23\%$, is a decoy that policy excludes for a globally held exporter.
> A (7.75%) is wrong because it drops the currency factor and applies the single-factor global CAPM: $3.25 + 4.50 = 7.75\%$.
> C (10.42%) is wrong because it multiplies the currency beta by the currency index's total expected return instead of its premium: $3.25 + 4.50 + 0.55(4.85) = 10.4175\%$.
> 3. **B.** The required return is the IRR that equates the price to the forecast dividends and the Year 3 price.
> Cash flows: $CF_0 = -58.00$, $CF_1 = 1.60$, $CF_2 = 1.75$, $CF_3 = 1.90 + 66.00 = 67.90$.
> CF worksheet: IRR CPT gives $r_e = 7.2836\%$.
> Check: $1.60/1.072836 + 1.75/1.072836^2 + 67.90/1.072836^3 = 1.4914 + 1.5204 + 54.9882 = 58.00$.
> The 1.48 dividend already paid is a decoy; the model uses forecast dividends.
> A (6.30%) is wrong because it omits the Year 3 dividend from the final cash flow, using $CF_3 = 66.00$, which gives an IRR of 6.3006%.
> C (8.26%) is wrong because it substitutes the constant growth model with the consensus EPS growth rate: $1.60/58.00 + 0.055 = 0.027586 + 0.055 = 8.2586\%$.
> That shortcut assumes the price grows at 5.50% a year, but Dubois's forecast price implies only $(66.00/58.00)^{1/3} - 1 = 4.4012\%$ a year.
> 4. **A.** Statement 1 is correct: tighter monetary policy raises the benchmark rate, a slowing economy widens credit spreads, and because $r_d = r_f + \text{spread}$ the cost of debt rises; recessions also raise the equity risk premium.
> Statement 2 is incorrect: a higher tax rate lowers the after-tax cost of debt only if the company has taxable income against which to deduct its interest.
> Varenna's carryforwards shelter all of its taxable income for the next five years, so its interest deductions produce no tax saving over that period and the tax increase does not lower its after-tax cost of debt or its WACC.
> B is wrong because it accepts the tax argument while ignoring that Varenna has no taxable income to shield.
> C is wrong for the same reason; only Statement 1 is correct.

## Distractor design

- Q1: A prices off the convertible, whose yield is depressed by the embedded option (wrong estimation route); B uses a stale bank loan rate set before the downgrade (misread exhibit).
- Q2: A omits the currency factor and reduces the ICAPM to the global CAPM (wrong formula variant); C multiplies the currency beta by the index's total return instead of its premium over the risk-free rate (wrong formula variant).
- Q3: A omits the final dividend from the terminal cash flow (period error); C substitutes the constant growth formula with the EPS growth rate for the multiyear IRR (wrong formula variant).
- Q4: B and C accept a tax shield benefit for a company with no taxable income to absorb its interest deductions (before-tax vs after-tax error).
