# Morava Energetika: Lignite Risk, Green Bonds, and a State Owner

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Hana Kovac, CFA, is a credit analyst at Adriatic Pension Fund, which runs a fixed-income mandate that may buy labeled green bonds only if their proceeds finance eligible environmental projects.
In January 2026 she is reviewing the fund's holdings of bonds issued by Morava Energetika, a listed electricity producer in central Europe.
The national government owns 58% of Morava's shares, and the remaining shares trade on the local stock exchange.
Morava sets its regional retail tariffs in consultation with the energy ministry, and it has kept two loss-making lignite mines open to preserve employment in the mining region.

Lignite plants provide 55% of Morava's generating capacity.
Parliament is debating a law that would ban lignite-fired generation from 2033.
If the law passes, Kovac expects much of Morava's lignite capacity to become uneconomic, and she wants her credit assessment to reflect that risk.

Exhibit 1: Morava bonds held by Adriatic Pension Fund

| Bond | Maturity | Price (per 100 par) | Spread duration | Current spread (bp) | Par held (EUR millions) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional notes | 2028 | 101.20 | 1.9 | 95 | 40 |
| Green bond | 2033 | 99.10 | 6.2 | 132 | 30 |
| Conventional notes | 2037 | 96.40 | 8.4 | 168 | 25 |

Morava's interpolated conventional spread at the 2033 maturity is 140 bp.
The green bond ranks pari passu with Morava's conventional notes and carries the same credit rating.
Its proceeds were designated for three onshore wind farms under the Green Bond Principles.

Kovac makes two statements to the fund's investment committee.

Statement 1: "The proposed lignite ban is less material to our 2028 notes than to our 2037 notes, because the 2028 notes mature before the ban would take effect."

Statement 2: "Because the ban is a long-dated risk, it will remain immaterial to our 2028 notes however events unfold before 2028."

To reflect the lignite risk, Kovac widens her spread assumption by 10 bp for the 2028 notes and by 75 bp for the 2037 notes.

Kovac then reads Morava's latest allocation report and a local press investigation.
The investigation shows that about 40% of the green bond proceeds financed a retrofit of a lignite plant rather than the wind farms.
Kovac expects that, once investors absorb this, the green bond will lose its demand-driven premium and trade at Morava's conventional 2033 spread, with Morava's conventional curve unchanged.

Kovac also reviews Morava's governance.

Exhibit 2: Morava governance features

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Board structure | Two-tier: supervisory board of 12 and management board of 5 |
| Employee representatives | 4 of the 12 supervisory board members, elected by staff |
| State-appointed supervisory board members | 5 |
| Audit committee of the supervisory board | 3 members; the chair was Morava's chief financial officer until last year and now receives an annual consulting retainer from Morava |
| Female supervisory board members | 4 |

## Questions

1. The governance risk most closely associated with the state's shareholding in Morava is: (LOS: los-04-02-a)
   - A. lower market scrutiny of management and the pursuit of public-policy goals at the expense of shareholder value.
   - B. pressure on Morava to take on large loans on unfavorable terms.
   - C. a principal-agent problem arising from dispersed ownership and dispersed voting power.
2. Which of Kovac's statements about the materiality of the lignite ban is correct? (LOS: los-04-02-c)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both statements.
3. Using Kovac's spread assumption, the approximate percentage price change on the 2037 notes is closest to: (LOS: los-04-02-d)
   - A. -8.25%.
   - B. -6.30%.
   - C. -0.84%.
4. Relative to Morava's conventional notes, Kovac's credit analysis of the green bond should most likely: (LOS: los-04-02-d)
   - A. be essentially the same, because the green bond carries the same rating and recourse.
   - B. assume a higher recovery rate, because proceeds are designated for specific wind farms.
   - C. assume that the green bond's spread premium will persist to maturity.
5. If Kovac's expectation about the green bond is correct, the approximate percentage price change on the green bond is closest to: (LOS: los-04-02-d)
   - A. -8.18%.
   - B. -0.50%.
   - C. +0.50%.
6. The feature in Exhibit 2 that most likely increases Morava's governance risk is the: (LOS: los-04-02-b)
   - A. two-tier board structure.
   - B. employee representatives on the supervisory board.
   - C. chair of the audit committee.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Morava is a listed state-owned enterprise under the mixed-ownership model, which tends to bring lower market scrutiny of management because of implicit or explicit state support, and SOEs may pursue social or public-policy goals, such as keeping loss-making mines open and consulting on tariffs, at the expense of maximizing shareholder value.
> B is wrong because pressure toward large loans on unfavorable terms is the signature conflict of a bank that is both lender and shareholder.
> C is wrong because a 58% state stake is concentrated ownership with a controlling shareholder, so the principal-agent problem of dispersed ownership does not describe Morava.
> 2. **A.** Statement 1 is correct: materiality depends on the investment horizon, and credit analysts must consider it bond by bond because maturities differ, so a risk that bites after 2033 matters far more to 2037 notes than to 2028 notes.
> Statement 2 is incorrect: the horizon over which an ESG issue bites can shift suddenly, for example if parliament brings the ban forward or a controversy accelerates regulation, so a long-dated risk cannot be declared permanently immaterial to short bonds.
> B is wrong because it accepts the permanent-immateriality claim and rejects the maturity-based reasoning.
> C is wrong because Statement 2 ignores that horizons can shift abruptly.
> 3. **B.** $\Delta P / P \approx -D_s \times \Delta s = -8.4 \times 0.0075 = -0.0630$, or -6.30%.
> A is wrong because it uses the 11 years remaining to maturity in place of spread duration ($-11 \times 0.0075 = -0.0825$).
> C is wrong because it applies the 10 bp widening for the 2028 notes to the 2037 notes ($-8.4 \times 0.0010 = -0.0084$).
> 4. **A.** Green bonds normally have the same credit ratings and bondholder recourse as the issuer's conventional bonds, so their analysis and valuation are essentially the same; the differences are the earmarked use of proceeds, a possible demand-driven premium, greenwashing risk, and liquidity risk.
> B is wrong because the green bond ranks pari passu with the conventional notes; designating proceeds does not give holders a claim on the wind farms or a higher recovery.
> C is wrong because a green premium is a possible, demand-driven effect, not a guaranteed one, and the investigation shows it can disappear.
> 5. **B.** The green bond trades at 132 bp versus a conventional 2033 spread of 140 bp, a premium of $140 - 132 = 8$ bp.
> Losing the premium widens its spread by 8 bp, so $\Delta P / P \approx -6.2 \times 0.0008 = -0.00496$, or about -0.50%.
> The misuse of proceeds is greenwashing, which both costs the investor the premium paid and may breach Adriatic's mandate.
> A is wrong because it applies the entire 132 bp spread rather than the 8 bp premium ($-6.2 \times 0.0132 = -0.0818$).
> C is wrong because it reverses the sign: a wider spread lowers the price.
> 6. **C.** Committees should be sufficiently independent, and an audit committee chaired by a recent chief financial officer who still receives a consulting retainer has both an employment and a remuneration relationship with the company, which taints the integrity of financial reporting oversight.
> A is wrong because a two-tier board, with a supervisory board overseeing a management board, is a recognized structure that is mandatory in several jurisdictions and is not in itself a weakness.
> B is wrong because seating key stakeholders such as labor on the supervisory board is a recognized feature of two-tier systems, not a governance defect.

## Distractor design

1. B: bank lender-shareholder conflict attributed to a state owner; C: wrong cell of the ownership-voting matrix (concentrated state stake read as dispersed).
2. B: horizon treated as fixed rather than subject to sudden shifts; C: accepts the fixed-horizon claim alongside the correct maturity reasoning.
3. A: maturity used in place of spread duration; C: misread exhibit (2028 spread change applied to the 2037 notes).
4. B: earmarked proceeds mistaken for a separate claim or seniority; C: possible demand premium treated as permanent.
5. A: whole spread used instead of the green premium; C: sign error on the price-spread relation.
6. A: recognized board structure read as a defect; B: stakeholder representation read as a defect.
