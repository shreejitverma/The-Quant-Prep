# Tessaro Environmental Dividend Recapitalization

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Rafael Quintero, CFA, is an analyst at Linden Row Partners covering Tessaro Environmental Inc., a US operator of municipal waste-collection contracts and landfills.
Most of Tessaro's revenue comes from ten-year municipal contracts with inflation-linked pricing, and its EBITDA has varied by less than 4% a year over the past decade.
On 9 March 2026, Tessaro announced that it will issue USD2,400 million of new senior notes and use the proceeds, together with USD400 million of cash on hand, to repurchase its own shares through an accelerated share repurchase at the current price of USD50.00.
Underwriters have committed to place the notes at a 7.0% coupon.
Tessaro's existing USD2,000 million of 5.0% debt will remain outstanding.
The CFO commented: "Our contracted revenue is highly predictable, so replacing part of our equity with lower-cost debt should raise the return our shareholders earn on their capital."

**Exhibit 1: Tessaro before the transaction, next-twelve-month forecast (USD millions, except per-share data)**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| EBITDA | 1,400 |
| Depreciation and amortization (D&A) | 350 |
| Total debt (5.0% coupon) | 2,000 |
| Cash and equivalents (earning 3.0%) | 500 |
| Shares outstanding (millions) | 200 |
| Share price (USD) | 50.00 |
| Dividend per share (USD) | 1.20 |
| Peer median EV/EBITDA | 10.0x |
| Tax rate | 25% |

Quintero maps Tessaro's credit profile to Linden Row's internal rating grid in Exhibit 2.
The grid uses total (gross) debt and EBIT interest coverage; when the two ratios indicate different ratings, the lower rating applies.

**Exhibit 2: Linden Row internal rating grid**

| Rating | Total debt to EBITDA | EBIT interest coverage | Before-tax cost of debt |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Below 1.5x | Above 9.0x | 5.0% |
| BBB | 1.5x to 2.5x | 5.0x to 9.0x | 5.8% |
| BB | 2.5x to 3.5x | 3.0x to 5.0x | 7.0% |
| B | 3.5x to 5.0x | 1.5x to 3.0x | 8.6% |

**Exhibit 3: Quintero's cost of equity estimates**

| | Before the transaction | After the transaction |
|---|---|---|
| Cost of equity | 8.5% | 9.6% |

For his pro forma analysis, Quintero assumes the transaction closes at the start of the forecast year and that operating results are unaffected.
He assumes Tessaro's share price remains USD50.00 after the repurchase and that the book value of debt equals its market value.
For the weighted average cost of capital, he assigns all of Tessaro's debt the before-tax cost of debt for its rating in Exhibit 2.

Quintero's colleague, Helen Marsh, notes that Tessaro's current ratios place it in the A row of the grid.
She asks Quintero to determine whether the transaction lowers Tessaro's cost of capital, as the CFO implies, once the rating consequences are included.

## Questions

1. Tessaro's planned transaction is best described as: (LOS: los-04-04-a)
   - A. a balance sheet restructuring undertaken opportunistically to improve returns on capital, an action suited to a company with stable cash flows.
   - B. a cost restructuring, because substituting lower-cost debt for equity reduces the company's cost structure.
   - C. a divestment, because the repurchase reduces the size of Tessaro's equity base.
2. Tessaro's pro forma EPS for the forecast year is closest to: (LOS: los-04-04-d)
   - A. USD3.87.
   - B. USD4.09.
   - C. USD4.15.
3. After the transaction, Tessaro's total debt to EBITDA, EBIT interest coverage, and rating on Linden Row's grid are closest to: (LOS: los-04-04-g)
   - A. 3.14x, 3.92x, and BB.
   - B. 3.43x, 3.55x, and BB.
   - C. 4.19x, 3.92x, and B.
4. Tessaro's weighted average cost of capital after the transaction is closest to: (LOS: los-04-04-d)
   - A. 7.27%.
   - B. 7.61%.
   - C. 7.95%.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** A dividend recapitalization, issuing debt to fund dividends or share repurchases, is a balance sheet restructuring: it changes the capital structure without changing Tessaro's size or scope.
> It is an opportunistic action aimed at improving returns on capital by swapping more expensive equity for cheaper debt, and it is sensible only for a company with stable cash flows, such as Tessaro's contracted revenue.
> B is wrong because a cost restructuring cuts operating costs to restore margins, for example through outsourcing or offshoring; financing changes do not qualify.
> C is wrong because a divestment sheds businesses and reduces size or scope; a share repurchase leaves every Tessaro operation in place.
> 2. **B.** Pre-transaction: EBIT $= 1{,}400 - 350 = 1{,}050$; interest expense $= 0.05 \times 2{,}000 = 100$; interest income $= 0.03 \times 500 = 15$; EBT $= 965$; net income $= 965 \times 0.75 = 723.75$; EPS $= 723.75/200 = 3.6188$.
> Shares repurchased $= (2{,}400 + 400)/50.00 = 56$ million, leaving $200 - 56 = 144$ million.
> Interest expense $= 100 + 0.07 \times 2{,}400 = 268$; interest income $= 0.03 \times (500 - 400) = 3$.
> EBT $= 1{,}050 - 268 + 3 = 785$; tax $= 0.25 \times 785 = 196.25$; net income $= 588.75$.
> Pro forma EPS $= 588.75/144 = 4.0885$, accretive by about USD0.47 because the after-tax cost of the new debt, $7.0\% \times 0.75 = 5.25\%$, is below Tessaro's earnings yield of $3.6188/50.00 = 7.24\%$.
> A is wrong because it retires only the shares funded by the notes ($2{,}400/50.00 = 48$ million, leaving 152 million): $588.75/152 = 3.8734$.
> C is wrong because it omits the interest income forgone on the USD400 million of cash used: $(1{,}050 - 268 + 15) \times 0.75 = 597.75$, and $597.75/144 = 4.1510$.
> 3. **A.** Before the transaction, total debt to EBITDA $= 2{,}000/1{,}400 = 1.4286$x and coverage $= 1{,}050/100 = 10.5000$x, both in the A row.
> After the transaction, total debt $= 2{,}000 + 2{,}400 = 4{,}400$, so total debt to EBITDA $= 4{,}400/1{,}400 = 3.1429$x.
> Coverage $= 1{,}050/268 = 3.9179$x.
> Both ratios fall in the BB row, so Tessaro drops from A to BB, a speculative-grade rating.
> B is wrong because it treats the entire USD2,800 million repurchase as debt financed, ignoring the cash used: $4{,}800/1{,}400 = 3.4286$x and $1{,}050/(100 + 0.07 \times 2{,}800) = 1{,}050/296 = 3.5473$x.
> C is wrong because it divides debt by EBIT instead of EBITDA: $4{,}400/1{,}050 = 4.1905$x, which falls in the B band.
> 4. **C.** Pre-transaction: $w_d = 2{,}000/(2{,}000 + 10{,}000) = 0.1667$, so $WACC = 0.1667 \times 5.0\% \times 0.75 + 0.8333 \times 8.5\% = 0.6250\% + 7.0833\% = 7.7083\%$.
> Post-transaction equity $= 144 \times 50.00 = 7{,}200$; $w_d = 4{,}400/11{,}600 = 0.3793$ and $w_e = 0.6207$.
> With the BB cost of debt of 7.0% from Question 3: $WACC = 0.3793 \times 7.0\% \times 0.75 + 0.6207 \times 9.6\% = 1.9914\% + 5.9586\% = 7.9500\%$.
> WACC rises by about 24 basis points: the drop to speculative grade raises both the cost of debt and the cost of equity by more than the shift toward cheaper debt saves, so the CFO's expectation does not hold at this size of recapitalization.
> A is wrong because it keeps the pre-transaction cost of equity of 8.5%, ignoring the higher financial risk borne by shareholders: $1.9914\% + 0.6207 \times 8.5\% = 1.9914\% + 5.2759\% = 7.2672\%$.
> B is wrong because it uses the BBB cost of debt of 5.8%, as if Tessaro kept an investment-grade rating: $0.3793 \times 5.8\% \times 0.75 + 5.9586\% = 1.6500\% + 5.9586\% = 7.6086\%$.

## Distractor design

1. B calls a capital structure change a cost restructuring; C calls a share repurchase a divestment although size and scope are unchanged.
2. A retires only the shares funded by the notes and forgets the cash-funded portion; C omits the forgone interest income on the cash used.
3. B treats the whole repurchase as debt financed, ignoring the cash used; C divides debt by EBIT instead of EBITDA.
4. A keeps the pre-transaction cost of equity despite higher leverage; B uses the BBB cost of debt, misreading the pro forma rating.
