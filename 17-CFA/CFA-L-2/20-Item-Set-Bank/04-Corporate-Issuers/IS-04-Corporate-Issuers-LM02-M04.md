# Solvenna Bank: Voting Caps and Credit Quality

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Tobias Renner, CFA, is a credit analyst at Kellbrook Insurance, which holds the senior unsecured bonds of Solvenna Bank, a listed commercial bank in continental Europe.
Kellbrook's investment committee has asked Renner to update his internal credit assessment of Solvenna after a year of changes in its shareholder register.

Solvenna has 500 million ordinary shares outstanding, each carrying one vote.
The country's banking ownership law, adopted to deter foreign control of strategically important banks, limits the votes that any single shareholder may cast to 10% of the shares outstanding.
Shares held above that limit remain fully entitled to dividends but cannot be voted.

Exhibit 1: Solvenna shareholder register

| Shareholder | Holding (% of shares outstanding) |
|---|---|
| Aurum Capital Group (foreign financial group) | 34% |
| Ostvik National Pension Fund | 8% |
| Other shareholders, none holding more than 1% | 58% |

Aurum acquired most of its stake over the past year and has publicly called for a new chief executive officer.
Renner assumes that every shareholder votes all the shares it is permitted to vote at the next general meeting.

Renner uses credit quality as a proxy for the quality of Solvenna's governance and risk oversight, because a weakly governed bank tends to underwrite loans poorly.
He compiles the data in Exhibit 2 for Solvenna and an aggregate of five domestic peer banks.

Exhibit 2: Selected balance sheet data, year-end 2025 (EUR millions)

| Item | Solvenna | Peer aggregate |
|---|---|---|
| Non-performing loans | 1,092 | 3,285 |
| Total loans outstanding | 52,000 | 219,000 |
| Total assets | 68,000 | 290,000 |
| Loan-loss allowance | 780 | 2,950 |

Last year Solvenna disclosed a cyber incident that exposed the account records of 1.2 million retail customers.
Renner regards data security and customer privacy as material factors for any bank.

Renner then reviews Solvenna's board, summarized in Exhibit 3.

Exhibit 3: Solvenna board of directors

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Board structure | Single tier, 13 directors |
| Chairperson | Independent non-executive director; the CEO is not a board member |
| Directors meeting the independence definition | 7 |
| Board committees | Audit, nomination, and remuneration; risk oversight is handled by the full board, whereas every peer bank has a dedicated board risk committee |
| Directors with banking or risk management experience | 1 |
| Directors with tenure over 10 years | 2 |
| Clawback policy | Allows recovery of executive pay after restatements or misconduct |
| Say-on-pay | Annual advisory shareholder vote on executive remuneration |

Renner plans to present one conclusion on Solvenna's control structure, one on its credit quality, and one on its board before recommending whether Kellbrook should keep the bonds.

## Questions

1. The percentage of the votes cast at the next general meeting that Aurum will control is closest to: (LOS: los-04-02-a)
   - A. 10.0%.
   - B. 13.2%.
   - C. 34.0%.
2. Solvenna's ownership and voting structure is best described as: (LOS: los-04-02-a)
   - A. concentrated ownership with dispersed voting power, which limits Aurum's ability to control Solvenna.
   - B. dispersed ownership with concentrated voting power, which lets Aurum control Solvenna without holding a majority of the cash flow rights.
   - C. concentrated ownership with concentrated voting power, which creates a principal-principal conflict between Aurum and minority shareholders.
3. The difference between Solvenna's non-performing loan ratio and the peer ratio, and the adjustment most consistent with Renner's role, are best described as: (LOS: los-04-02-d)
   - A. 47 bp above peers; widen the credit spread assumption relative to peers.
   - B. 60 bp above peers; raise the risk premium in the cost of equity and look for upside from Aurum's push for a new CEO.
   - C. 60 bp above peers; widen the credit spread assumption relative to peers.
4. The feature in Exhibit 3 that most likely increases Solvenna's governance risk is the: (LOS: los-04-02-b)
   - A. advisory rather than binding nature of the say-on-pay vote.
   - B. clawback policy's coverage of restatements and misconduct.
   - C. absence of a dedicated board risk committee, given that only one director has banking or risk management experience.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Aurum owns $0.34 \times 500 = 170$ million shares, but the cap lets it vote only $0.10 \times 500 = 50$ million.
> The 120 million capped shares cannot be voted, so the votes cast total $500 - 170 + 50 = 380$ million.
> Aurum's share of the votes cast is $50 / 380 = 0.1316$, or about 13.2%.
> A is wrong because it divides the capped votes by all 500 million shares ($50 / 500 = 0.1000$), forgetting that the unvotable shares also leave the denominator.
> C is wrong because it ignores the voting cap and treats shares owned as votes controlled.
> 2. **A.** Aurum's 34% block is a concentrated ownership position, but the legal voting cap reduces its votes to about 13.2% of the votes cast, so voting power stays dispersed.
> Voting caps are legal restrictions on the voting rights of large share positions, often imposed to deter foreign control of strategic companies, and they produce concentrated ownership with dispersed voting power.
> B is wrong because concentrated voting power without majority economics arises from dual-class shares or pyramids, and here the cap works in the opposite direction.
> C is wrong because a principal-principal conflict requires a controlling shareholder with concentrated voting power, which the cap denies Aurum.
> 3. **C.** Solvenna's NPL ratio $= 1{,}092 / 52{,}000 = 0.0210$, or 2.10%.
> Peer NPL ratio $= 3{,}285 / 219{,}000 = 0.0150$, or 1.50%.
> The gap is $2.10\% - 1.50\% = 0.60\%$, or 60 bp, which signals weaker underwriting and, by proxy, weaker governance.
> Renner is a credit analyst, so ESG integration focuses on mitigating downside risk, and the adjustment is a wider credit spread relative to peers.
> A is wrong because it uses total assets as the denominator ($1{,}092 / 68{,}000 = 0.016059$ and $3{,}285 / 290{,}000 = 0.011328$, a gap of about 47 bp) instead of total loans outstanding.
> B is wrong because raising the cost of equity and identifying opportunities are equity-analysis tools; a bondholder's upside is capped because the bond redeems at par.
> 4. **C.** Board committees, including risk and compliance, should exist and be sufficiently independent, and directors' skills should match the company's core operations.
> A bank that leaves risk oversight to a full board with only one director experienced in banking or risk management lags every peer on the function most central to its business, which is consistent with its elevated NPL ratio.
> A is wrong because say-on-pay is a provision allowing shareholders to vote on or give feedback about executive remuneration; an advisory vote still gives shareholders a voice and reduces rather than increases governance risk.
> B is wrong because a clawback policy that recovers pay after restatements or misconduct is a protective feature.
> The board's 7 of 13 independent directors ($7 / 13 = 0.5385$) and independent chair are also strengths, and 2 long-tenured directors are a minor concern.

## Distractor design

1. A: denominator error (capped votes divided by all shares, unvotable shares not removed); C: voting cap ignored (shares owned read as votes controlled).
2. B: voting-cap cell confused with the dual-class or pyramid cell of the ownership-voting matrix; C: large block read as control despite the cap.
3. A: wrong denominator (total assets instead of total loans); B: correct ratio with the equity-analysis tool and opportunity focus applied to a bond analysis.
4. A: shareholder-protective feature read as a risk because the vote is non-binding; B: protective clawback policy read as a risk.
