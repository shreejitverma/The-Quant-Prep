# Pellworth Capital: Pricing the Corvanne Self-Storage Portfolio

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Tomasz Wierzbicki, CFA, is an acquisitions analyst at Pellworth Capital, a closed-end private real estate fund that buys income-producing properties in the US Midwest.
The fund has agreed in principle to buy the Corvanne Self-Storage portfolio, three facilities around Columbus, Ohio, with 190,000 square feet of rentable space in total.
The agreed purchase price is USD29,000,000.

Every Corvanne tenant rents on a month-to-month agreement that renews automatically unless the tenant gives 30 days' notice.
Street rates for new tenants are reset every week by the seller's revenue management software.
Pellworth will keep the seller's on-site staff and management arrangements unchanged.

The seller, a taxable operating company, has provided the first-year pro forma in Exhibit 1.
Wierzbicki confirms that the budget is reasonable for the portfolio under Pellworth's ownership, and he treats every line as a first-year expectation.
The seller's existing mortgage will be repaid at closing.

Exhibit 1: Corvanne first-year pro forma prepared by the seller (USD)

| Line item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross potential rent (all units at street rates) | 3,600,000 |
| Vacancy and collection loss | 11% of gross potential rent |
| Concessions (first month free promotions) | 95,000 |
| Other income (tenant insurance, truck rental, box sales) | 310,000 |
| Property tax | 290,000 |
| Insurance | 85,000 |
| On-site payroll and management | 540,000 |
| Utilities and repairs | 260,000 |
| Marketing | 70,000 |
| Maintenance allowance (roof and door replacement reserve) | 120,000 |
| Interest on the seller's existing mortgage | 410,000 |
| Depreciation | 380,000 |
| Seller's income tax | 150,000 |

Pellworth will finance the purchase with a new mortgage equal to 65% of the purchase price.
The loan is fully amortizing over 25 years with annual payments at a fixed rate of 6.0%.
The balance of the price will be funded with fund equity.

Before the investment committee meeting, Wierzbicki cross-checks the price with the sales comparison approach.
He collects the recent transactions and one current listing in Exhibit 2, together with the percentage adjustments his firm's appraiser recommends to bring each comparable into line with Corvanne.
A positive adjustment means the comparable is inferior to Corvanne on that attribute.

Exhibit 2: Columbus-area self-storage comparables (prices in USD per rentable square foot)

| Comparable | Status | Price | Sale date adj. | Location adj. | Age and condition adj. | Size adj. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (Westerville) | Sold 20 months ago | 138 | +7% | +3% | +2% | 0% |
| B (Dublin) | Sold 5 months ago | 166 | +2% | -4% | +3% | +2% |
| C (Grove City) | Sold 11 months ago | 151 | +4% | +1% | -3% | 0% |
| D (Hilliard) | Listed for sale, no offer accepted | 184 | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% |

Imani Brooks, a portfolio manager on the investment committee, asks Wierzbicki how Corvanne's income is likely to behave over an economic cycle compared with the fund's existing suburban office buildings and its business hotel in Indianapolis.
Brooks notes that the office buildings are let on leases of five to ten years and that the hotel reprices its rooms every night.
Wierzbicki replies that he will address the question in his committee memo, drawing on the lease terms and rate-setting practice described above.

## Questions

1. Corvanne's first-year net operating income is closest to: (LOS: los-08-02-a)
   - A. USD1,644,000.
   - B. USD2,054,000.
   - C. USD2,174,000.
2. Pellworth's first-year debt service coverage ratio and equity dividend rate on Corvanne are closest to: (LOS: los-08-02-a)
   - A. 1.39 and 5.71%.
   - B. 1.82 and 9.09%.
   - C. 1.39 and 2.00%.
3. Wierzbicki's sales comparison value for Corvanne is closest to: (LOS: los-08-02-d)
   - A. USD28,816,667.
   - B. USD30,372,133.
   - C. USD31,519,100.
4. In his memo to Brooks, Wierzbicki's most accurate description of Corvanne's income characteristics is that: (LOS: los-08-02-c)
   - A. its rental income is insulated from a local downturn because the month-to-month agreements renew automatically.
   - B. its rents can be repriced quickly when local demand strengthens, but the same flexibility exposes its income to rapid declines when demand weakens.
   - C. like the business hotel, it is the most cyclical and management-intensive property type because its space is effectively re-leased every night.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Vacancy and collection loss: $0.11 \times 3{,}600{,}000 = 396{,}000$.
> Effective gross income: $3{,}600{,}000 - 396{,}000 - 95{,}000 + 310{,}000 = 3{,}419{,}000$.
> Operating expenses: $290{,}000 + 85{,}000 + 540{,}000 + 260{,}000 + 70{,}000 = 1{,}245{,}000$.
> NOI: $3{,}419{,}000 - 1{,}245{,}000 - 120{,}000 = 2{,}054{,}000$.
> Mortgage interest, depreciation, and income tax are excluded because NOI is measured before financing costs and income taxes, and depreciation is a non-cash accounting charge; the maintenance allowance is included because it funds the capex needed to keep the current economic use.
> A is wrong because it also deducts the seller's mortgage interest, $2{,}054{,}000 - 410{,}000 = 1{,}644{,}000$; that loan is repaid at closing, and financing costs never enter NOI in any case.
> C is wrong because it omits the maintenance allowance, $2{,}054{,}000 + 120{,}000 = 2{,}174{,}000$.
> 2. **A.** Loan: $0.65 \times 29{,}000{,}000 = 18{,}850{,}000$; equity: $29{,}000{,}000 - 18{,}850{,}000 = 10{,}150{,}000$.
> Annual payment: `25 N, 6 I/Y, 18850000 PV, 0 FV, CPT PMT` gives $-1{,}474{,}573.64$, of which first-year interest is $18{,}850{,}000 \times 0.06 = 1{,}131{,}000$ and principal is $343{,}573.64$.
> DSC: $2{,}054{,}000 / 1{,}474{,}573.64 = 1.3929$.
> Pre-tax cash flow: $2{,}054{,}000 - 1{,}474{,}573.64 = 579{,}426.36$.
> Equity dividend rate: $579{,}426.36 / 10{,}150{,}000 = 5.7086\%$.
> B is wrong because it treats interest alone as debt service, giving DSC of $2{,}054{,}000 / 1{,}131{,}000 = 1.8161$ and an equity dividend rate of $(2{,}054{,}000 - 1{,}131{,}000) / 10{,}150{,}000 = 9.0936\%$; the loan is fully amortizing, so debt service includes principal.
> C is wrong because it divides pre-tax cash flow by the purchase price instead of the initial equity, $579{,}426.36 / 29{,}000{,}000 = 1.9980\%$.
> 3. **B.** Net adjustments: A $+7\% + 3\% + 2\% = +12\%$; B $+2\% - 4\% + 3\% + 2\% = +3\%$; C $+4\% + 1\% - 3\% = +2\%$.
> Adjusted prices: $138 \times 1.12 = 154.56$; $166 \times 1.03 = 170.98$; $151 \times 1.02 = 154.02$.
> Average: $(154.56 + 170.98 + 154.02) / 3 = 159.8533$ per square foot.
> Value: $159.8533 \times 190{,}000 = 30{,}372{,}133$, which is above the USD29,000,000 price.
> A is wrong because it averages the unadjusted sale prices, $(138 + 166 + 151) / 3 = 151.6667$, and $151.6667 \times 190{,}000 = 28{,}816{,}667$.
> C is wrong because it adds the Hilliard listing as a fourth comparable, $(154.56 + 170.98 + 154.02 + 184) / 4 = 165.89$, and $165.89 \times 190{,}000 = 31{,}519{,}100$; an asking price on an unsold property is not a transaction price and does not belong in the sales comparison approach.
> 4. **B.** Self-storage tenants rent month to month, so rents reprice quickly, and Corvanne resets street rates weekly.
> That pricing flexibility lets income rise quickly when local demand strengthens, but it also exposes the owner to fast declines when demand falls, because tenants can leave on 30 days' notice and new tenants arrive only at the lower street rates.
> A is wrong because automatic renewal is not a commitment: a month-to-month tenant can leave on short notice, so the income has none of the protection of the office buildings' five to ten year leases.
> C is wrong because it attributes hospitality's characteristics to self-storage; the curriculum identifies hospitality as the most cyclical and management-intensive type, with high fixed costs and nightly repricing, and self-storage leases run month to month, not nightly.

## Distractor design

1. A: financing cost (seller's mortgage interest) deducted in NOI (NOI before financing vs after financing); C: maintenance allowance omitted (line item skipped); decoys: depreciation and seller's income tax.
2. B: interest used as debt service on an amortizing loan (principal omitted from debt service); C: pre-tax cash flow divided by price instead of equity (wrong denominator).
3. A: comparables left unadjusted (adjustments skipped); C: asking price on an unsold listing treated as a comparable sale (misread exhibit, listing vs transaction).
4. A: automatic month-to-month renewal mistaken for lease-term stability (lease structure misread); C: hospitality traits attributed to self-storage (property types swapped).
