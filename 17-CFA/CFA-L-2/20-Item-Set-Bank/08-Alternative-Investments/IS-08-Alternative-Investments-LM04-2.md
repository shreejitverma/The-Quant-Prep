# Ostrander Family Office Fund-of-Funds Review

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Tomas Lindqvist, CFA, prepares the annual hedge fund report for the Ostrander Family Office, a single-family office with USD 900 million under management.
The office's hedge fund exposure is held entirely through the Halcyon Diversified Fund of Funds, which charges a 1% management fee and a 10% incentive fee.
Halcyon invests in three underlying funds, each charging a 2% management fee and a 20% incentive fee.
At both levels, the management fee is charged on beginning assets, the incentive fee is charged on the return after the management fee, there is no hurdle rate, and all fees are paid at year-end without compounding.
Exhibit 1 reports the year's results.

**Exhibit 1: Halcyon's underlying funds, year just ended**

| Fund | Strategy | Weight in Halcyon | Gross return | Return in prior year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fund X | Distressed securities | 40% | 18.0% | 11.0% |
| Fund Y | Global macro | 35% | 9.0% | 4.0% |
| Fund Z | Volatility trading | 25% | -6.0% | 7.5% |

Most of Fund Z's loss came from one position.
At the start of the year, Fund Z sold a one-year variance swap on an equity index with a variance notional of USD 5,000 per variance point, struck at a volatility of 20.
Realized volatility over the year was 26.
The swap's implied volatility at inception was 20, and the index's implied volatility at year-end was 24.

Lindqvist writes the following about Fund X:
"Fund X buys the debt of companies in or near bankruptcy.
It is long-biased, runs leverage of roughly 4 to 5 times NAV financed through repo, and marks most holdings with Level 3 mark-to-model prices.
Its manager expects the strategy to be most rewarding in the early stages of an economic recovery after a period of market dislocation.
In one current reorganization, Fund X holds the senior unsecured notes, which are expected to be the fulcrum security."

The office's investment committee is considering replacing Halcyon with the Kestrel Multi-Strategy Fund, which runs teams covering the same three strategies under one roof.
Kestrel charges no management fee and instead uses a pass-through fee model, under which investors pay the costs of the individual strategy teams, including team bonuses, plus an incentive fee on the fund's total netted performance.
Lindqvist makes three statements in a memo dated 9 January.

Statement 1: "Moving to Kestrel would diversify our exposure to manager-specific operational risk, because Kestrel's strategy teams operate independently."

Statement 2: "Kestrel can move capital among its strategy teams faster than Halcyon can move capital among external managers, because it has full position-level transparency."

Statement 3: "Under Kestrel's pass-through model, we would bear none of the cost of paying winning teams in a year when other teams lose money."

The committee also asks whether a direct allocation to a life settlements fund, Arden Mortality Partners, would add diversification.
Arden is bidding on a pool of policies whose insureds have a medical condition that shortens life expectancy by about eight years relative to standard actuarial tables.
The pool was underwritten by an insurer known for low ongoing premiums and for surrender values that are high relative to its competitors.

## Questions

1. The net return to the Ostrander Family Office from its Halcyon investment for the year is closest to: (LOS: los-08-04-g)
   - A. 3.30%
   - B. 3.67%
   - C. 4.03%
2. Relative to a fee structure in which the underlying funds' incentive fees were charged on Halcyon's netted aggregate return after management fees, the extra incentive fees paid at the underlying fund level because of netting risk, as a percentage of Halcyon's assets, are closest to: (LOS: los-08-04-g)
   - A. 0.40%
   - B. 1.37%
   - C. 1.77%
3. The payoff to Fund Z on the variance swap at expiry is closest to: (LOS: los-08-04-f)
   - A. negative USD 1,380,000
   - B. negative USD 180,000
   - C. positive USD 1,380,000
4. Which of Lindqvist's statements about Kestrel is correct? (LOS: los-08-04-g)
   - A. Statement 1
   - B. Statement 2
   - C. Statement 3
5. Lindqvist's description of Fund X is most likely incorrect with respect to: (LOS: los-08-04-c)
   - A. leverage.
   - B. pricing of holdings.
   - C. the stage of the cycle at which the strategy is most attractive.
6. Which feature of the pool Arden is bidding on is least favorable to a life settlements investor? (LOS: los-08-04-f)
   - A. The insureds' shortened life expectancy relative to standard actuarial tables
   - B. The low ongoing premiums
   - C. The high surrender values

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Apply each underlying fund's fees, take the weighted average, then apply Halcyon's fees.
> Fund X: $18.0 - 2.0 - 0.20 \times (18.0 - 2.0) = 18.0 - 2.0 - 3.2 = 12.80\%$.
> Fund Y: $9.0 - 2.0 - 0.20 \times (9.0 - 2.0) = 9.0 - 2.0 - 1.4 = 5.60\%$.
> Fund Z: $-6.0 - 2.0 - 0 = -8.00\%$, because no incentive fee is charged on a loss.
> Halcyon's return before its own fees: $0.40 \times 12.80 + 0.35 \times 5.60 + 0.25 \times (-8.00) = 5.12 + 1.96 - 2.00 = 5.08\%$.
> Halcyon net: $5.08 - 1.00 - 0.10 \times (5.08 - 1.00) = 5.08 - 1.00 - 0.408 = 3.672\%$, or about 3.67%.
> The weighted gross return was $0.40 \times 18 + 0.35 \times 9 + 0.25 \times (-6) = 8.85\%$, so fees absorbed $8.85 - 3.672 = 5.178$ percentage points, or 58.51% of it.
> A is wrong because it charges the incentive fees on returns before the management fee at both levels: the underlying nets become 12.40%, 5.20%, and -8.00%, the weighted average is 4.78%, and $4.78 - 1.00 - 0.10 \times 4.78 = 3.302\%$.
> C is wrong because it nets performance across the three funds before charging underlying incentive fees, as a multi-strategy fund would: $8.85 - 2.00 - 0.20 \times 6.85 = 5.48\%$, then $5.48 - 1.00 - 0.10 \times 4.48 = 4.032\%$.
> 2. **A.** Underlying incentive fees actually paid, weighted by Halcyon's allocations: $0.40 \times 3.2 + 0.35 \times 1.4 + 0.25 \times 0 = 1.28 + 0.49 + 0 = 1.77\%$.
> Incentive fees on the netted aggregate return: $0.20 \times (8.85 - 2.00) = 1.37\%$.
> Extra cost of netting risk $= 1.77 - 1.37 = 0.40\%$, which equals Fund Z's weight times the incentive rate times its loss after management fees, $0.25 \times 0.20 \times 8.0 = 0.40\%$.
> B is wrong because 1.37% is the incentive fee that would be owed under netting, not the extra amount caused by the absence of netting.
> C is wrong because 1.77% is the total underlying incentive fee, most of which would be owed even with netting.
> 3. **A.** Fund Z sold the variance swap, so it pays realized variance and receives strike variance.
> The receiver of realized variance is owed $5{,}000 \times (26^2 - 20^2) = 5{,}000 \times (676 - 400) = 5{,}000 \times 276 = 1{,}380{,}000$, so Fund Z's payoff is negative USD 1,380,000.
> The implied volatilities at inception and year-end are decoys; the payoff depends only on realized volatility against the strike.
> B is wrong because it squares the volatility difference instead of taking the difference of the squares: $5{,}000 \times (26 - 20)^2 = 180{,}000$, with the correct sign.
> C is wrong because it takes the payoff of the buyer, the receiver of realized variance, while Fund Z is the seller.
> 4. **B.** A multi-strategy fund's teams work under one roof with full transparency, so capital can be reallocated among strategies faster and more efficiently than a fund of funds can move it among independent external managers.
> A is wrong because concentrating every team under one firm concentrates manager-specific operational risk; it is the fund of funds that diversifies operational risk across independent managers.
> C is wrong because the pass-through model shifts part of the netting cost back to investors: team payouts are passed through in place of a management fee, so investors do pay for winning teams in years when other teams lose.
> 5. **A.** Distressed securities investing is long-biased and only modestly levered, at about 1.2 to 1.7 times NAV; leverage of 4 to 5 times financed through repo is typical of fixed-income arbitrage, so the leverage claim is the error.
> The rest of the description is consistent with the strategy: holdings are illiquid and priced largely with Level 3 mark-to-model valuations that smooth reported returns, the strategy is most attractive early in a recovery after a period of market dislocation, and a fulcrum security is a partially in-the-money claim not expected to be repaid in full that converts into ownership of the reorganized company.
> B is wrong because Level 3 mark-to-model pricing is the norm for illiquid distressed holdings, which lack observable market prices.
> C is wrong because the strategy's cyclical sweet spot is the early recovery, when prices of distressed debt bought during the dislocation rise.
> 6. **C.** A life settlements investor buys the policy from the insured and must offer more than the surrender value, so a high surrender value raises the purchase price and lowers the investor's return.
> The investor wants low surrender values, low ongoing premiums, and a high probability that the insured dies earlier than standard actuarial tables predict.
> A is wrong because shortened life expectancy is the source of the return, not a drawback: the investor collects the death benefit sooner and stops paying premiums sooner.
> B is wrong because low ongoing premiums reduce the investor's cash outflows before the benefit is paid.

## Distractor design

- Q1: A charges incentive fees on gross returns before management fees (wrong formula variant); C nets winners against losers before the underlying incentive fees (multi-strategy fee model applied to a fund of funds).
- Q2: B reports the netted incentive fee instead of the excess; C reports the total underlying incentive fee instead of the excess.
- Q3: B squares the volatility difference instead of differencing the variances; C is a sign error (buyer's payoff reported for the seller).
- Q4: A gives the fund of funds' operational-risk advantage to the multi-strategy fund; C ignores that the pass-through model shifts netting cost back to investors.
- Q5: B treats Level 3 pricing as inconsistent with illiquid holdings; C misplaces the strategy's cyclical timing (the correct answer spots fixed-income arbitrage leverage imported into distressed investing).
- Q6: A and B flip a favorable life settlement characteristic into an unfavorable one.
