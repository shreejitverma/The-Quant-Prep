# Calder Rail Pension Plan Manager Review

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions (or 12 for 4). Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Priya Venkataraman, CFA, is a senior analyst at the Calder Rail Pension Plan, a corporate defined-benefit plan with USD 6.2 billion in assets.
The plan's board has approved a hedge fund sleeve, but its mandate restricts the sleeve to strategies classified as event-driven or relative value under the consultant's six-bucket, practice-based risk factor taxonomy.
Venkataraman has short-listed four managers and summarizes one current position for each.

Manager 1, Ashgrove Capital, runs a beta-neutral pairs book.
Ashgrove has allocated USD 2,400,000 to a long position in Harlow Foods, which it considers undervalued, and will short Pentland Brands, whose valuation multiples have stretched beyond their historical range.
Exhibit 1 gives the data Ashgrove uses.

**Exhibit 1: Ashgrove pair data**

| Stock | Market beta | Weight in broad equity index | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harlow Foods | 0.80 | 0.35% | Long USD 2,400,000 |
| Pentland Brands | 1.20 | 0.52% | Short, size to be set |

Manager 2, Brisk Event Partners, trades announced mergers.
Tavira Corp has announced a stock-for-stock offer of 3 Tavira shares for every 4 Lumen Inc shares.
Exhibit 2 shows the share prices.

**Exhibit 2: Tavira and Lumen share prices (USD)**

| Company | Before announcement | After announcement |
|---|---|---|
| Tavira Corp (acquirer) | 64.00 | 60.00 |
| Lumen Inc (target) | 40.00 | 42.50 |

Immediately after the announcement, Brisk bought 80,000 Lumen shares and shorted 60,000 Tavira shares at the post-announcement prices.
Brisk's founder tells Venkataraman that about 80% of the deals the firm has traded have closed.

Manager 3, Corvane Relative Value, runs two books.
In its convertible book, Corvane holds Meridel Group convertible bonds with a par value of USD 1,000, a price of 108% of par, a 4% annual coupon, a remaining term of five years, and a conversion ratio of 40 shares per bond.
Meridel shares trade at USD 31.50, and Meridel's price-to-earnings ratio is 45% above its industry average.
Corvane has bought the convertibles and shorted Meridel shares against them.
Over the next year, Corvane expects to pay a stock borrow cost of USD 0.90 per share and to owe the lender a dividend of USD 0.60 per share.
In its government book, Corvane buys off-the-run 10-year government bonds and shorts duration-matched on-the-run 10-year bonds of the same sovereign, financing the long position through repo.

Manager 4, Drumlin Systematic, trades futures in 24 commodity markets.
Each month, Drumlin ranks the 24 markets on trailing 12-month volatility-adjusted returns.
It then goes long the top 12 and short the bottom 12, with each position sized inversely to that market's volatility.
Drumlin describes its net holding as close to market neutral.

At a meeting on 14 May, Venkataraman makes two comments to the plan's chief investment officer, Graham Oduya.

Comment 1: "Corvane's government book is hedged for both interest rate and credit risk, so what remains is mainly the liquidity premium between the two bonds."

Comment 2: "Drumlin would still hold a meaningful net long position if nearly every commodity rallied strongly in the same quarter, because a strong absolute trend feeds straight into its signals."

Oduya asks her to confirm the payoffs of the Brisk and Corvane positions before the board meets.

## Questions

1. Which managers are permitted under the board's mandate? (LOS: los-08-04-a)
   - A. Managers 2 and 3 only
   - B. Managers 3 and 4 only
   - C. Manager 3 only
2. The value of Pentland Brands shares that Ashgrove should short is closest to: (LOS: los-08-04-b)
   - A. USD 1,600,000
   - B. USD 2,400,000
   - C. USD 3,600,000
3. Brisk's payoffs if the Tavira-Lumen merger closes and if it fails are closest to: (LOS: los-08-04-c)
   - A. USD 200,000 if it closes; negative USD 440,000 if it fails
   - B. USD 640,000 if it closes; negative USD 440,000 if it fails
   - C. USD 200,000 if it closes; negative USD 200,000 if it fails
4. Assuming the convertible trades near its conversion value, Corvane's expected profit per Meridel share over the next year, after carry, is closest to: (LOS: los-08-04-d)
   - A. USD 2.00
   - B. USD 4.00
   - C. USD 6.00
5. Regarding Comment 1, Corvane's government book is best described as a: (LOS: los-08-04-d)
   - A. carry trade whose main residual exposure is liquidity risk, as Venkataraman states.
   - B. yield curve trade whose main residual exposure is a steepening of the curve.
   - C. long/short credit trade whose main residual exposure is a change in the sovereign's credit spread.
6. Compared with a time-series momentum fund trading the same 24 markets, Drumlin's strategy most likely has: (LOS: los-08-04-e)
   - A. lower volatility and less sensitivity to strong sector-wide trends, so Comment 2 is incorrect.
   - B. higher volatility, because its positions are concentrated in the extreme-ranked markets, so Comment 2 is correct.
   - C. the same directional sensitivity, because both strategies use trailing-return signals, so Comment 2 is correct.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Merger arbitrage (Brisk) is event-driven, and both convertible bond arbitrage and the government bond carry trade (Corvane) are relative value strategies, so Managers 2 and 3 are permitted.
> Ashgrove's pairs book is equity market neutral, which sits in the equity bucket, and Drumlin's rules-based futures book is a managed futures strategy, which sits in the opportunistic bucket.
> B is wrong because it classifies Drumlin as relative value because its book ranks relative performance and is close to market neutral; cross-sectional momentum managed futures is still an opportunistic strategy.
> C is wrong because it excludes merger arbitrage for carrying equity market beta; event-driven strategies do carry some beta, but that does not move them into the equity bucket.
> 2. **A.** Beta neutrality requires equal beta-dollar exposures, $\beta_L V_L = \beta_S V_S$, so $V_S = \dfrac{V_L}{\beta_S / \beta_L} = \dfrac{2{,}400{,}000}{1.20 / 0.80} = \dfrac{2{,}400{,}000}{1.5000} = 1{,}600{,}000$.
> Check: $1{,}600{,}000 \times 1.20 = 1{,}920{,}000 = 2{,}400{,}000 \times 0.80$.
> The index weights in Exhibit 1 are irrelevant to the sizing.
> B is wrong because it is a dollar-neutral trade, which leaves a net beta-dollar exposure of $2{,}400{,}000 \times (0.80 - 1.20) = -960{,}000$.
> C is wrong because it inverts the beta ratio: $2{,}400{,}000 \times 1.20 / 0.80 = 3{,}600{,}000$.
> 3. **A.** The position is in the offer ratio: 80,000 Lumen shares convert into $80{,}000 \times 3/4 = 60{,}000$ Tavira shares, which deliver into the short.
> If the deal closes: short proceeds $= 60{,}000 \times 60.00 = 3{,}600{,}000$ and the Lumen shares cost $80{,}000 \times 42.50 = 3{,}400{,}000$, so the payoff is the spread, $3{,}600{,}000 - 3{,}400{,}000 = 200{,}000$.
> If the deal fails, both prices revert to pre-announcement levels.
> Short leg: $3{,}600{,}000 - 60{,}000 \times 64.00 = 3{,}600{,}000 - 3{,}840{,}000 = -240{,}000$.
> Long leg: $80{,}000 \times 40.00 - 3{,}400{,}000 = 3{,}200{,}000 - 3{,}400{,}000 = -200{,}000$.
> Total failure payoff $= -440{,}000$.
> B is wrong because its success payoff uses pre-announcement prices, $60{,}000 \times 64.00 - 80{,}000 \times 40.00 = 640{,}000$, which are not the prices at which Brisk set up the position.
> C is wrong because its failure payoff counts only the loss on the target and ignores the loss on the short Tavira shares as Tavira rebounds to 64.00.
> 4. **B.** Conversion price $= \dfrac{1{,}000 \times 1.08}{40} = \dfrac{1{,}080}{40} = 27.00$, so the gross locked-in profit is $31.50 - 27.00 = 4.50$ per share, whether the share price later rises or falls (at 27.00 or 36.00 the two legs still sum to 4.50).
> The coupon per share is $\dfrac{0.04 \times 1{,}000}{40} = 1.00$.
> Net profit $= 4.50 - 0.90 - 0.60 + 1.00 = 4.00$ per share.
> A is wrong because it subtracts the coupon instead of adding it: $4.50 - 0.90 - 0.60 - 1.00 = 2.00$; Corvane owns the bond and receives the coupon.
> C is wrong because it computes the conversion price from par rather than from the bond's price: $31.50 - 1{,}000/40 = 6.50$, then $6.50 - 0.90 - 0.60 + 1.00 = 6.00$.
> 5. **A.** Buying higher-yielding off-the-run bonds and shorting duration-matched on-the-run bonds of the same sovereign earns positive carry plus convergence.
> Duration matching hedges interest rate risk, and using the same issuer hedges credit risk, so liquidity risk remains; the payoff looks like a short put and suffers if the liquidity spread gaps wider and repo margin calls force sales.
> B is wrong because a yield curve trade takes positions at different points on the curve to bet on steepening or flattening, whereas both bonds here are 10-year bonds matched on duration.
> C is wrong because a long/short credit trade exploits differences in credit quality across issuers, whereas both legs here carry the same sovereign's credit.
> 6. **A.** Drumlin ranks the markets against each other and holds the top half long and the bottom half short, which is cross-sectional momentum; the two halves partly hedge each other, so the book stays roughly market neutral and runs lower volatility.
> In a quarter when nearly every commodity rallies, the bottom-ranked 12 are still held short, so Drumlin does not become meaningfully net long, and Comment 2 is incorrect.
> A time-series momentum fund judges each market alone on its absolute return and would go long nearly all 24, making it more volatile and far more sensitive to sector-wide trends.
> B is wrong because it reverses the volatility comparison and attributes to Drumlin a net long bias that belongs to time-series momentum.
> C is wrong because using trailing returns does not make the strategies equivalent; ranking assets against each other rather than judging each on its absolute return is what removes the directional exposure.

## Distractor design

- Q1: B classifies the strategy by its market-neutral construction rather than its family (managed futures is opportunistic); C classifies by equity beta exposure rather than by the event-driven family.
- Q2: B is the dollar-neutral versus beta-neutral error; C inverts the beta ratio.
- Q3: B enters the success payoff at pre-announcement prices (misread exhibit); C omits the short leg's loss on failure.
- Q4: A is a sign error on the coupon carry; C uses par instead of the bond price in the conversion price.
- Q5: B is the yield curve trade label triggered by two bonds on the same curve; C is the long/short credit label triggered by two different issues.
- Q6: B and C are the time-series versus cross-sectional momentum swap.
