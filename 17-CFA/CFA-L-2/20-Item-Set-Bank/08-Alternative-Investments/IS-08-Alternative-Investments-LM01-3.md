# Aurelia Foods: Hedging Policy Compliance Review

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Aurelia Foods is a listed bakery and confectionery manufacturer.
Its main inputs are wheat, sugar, natural gas for its ovens, and aluminum for its beverage cans.
Hannah Voss, CFA, is Aurelia's treasurer, and Leo Brandt is the internal auditor responsible for reviewing compliance with the board's hedging policy.

The hedging policy states that derivatives may be used only to reduce the price risk of inputs that Aurelia purchases in the ordinary course of business.
It prohibits positions whose profit depends on a view on price volatility, or on the price of a commodity that Aurelia does not purchase.

Brandt begins by asking which inputs are most exposed to weather.
Voss explains that a severe drought across major growing regions is the scenario the board worries about most.

Brandt then makes three statements about commodity life cycles:

- Statement 1: "Grain harvests in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres occur at the same time of year, so a Northern Hemisphere drought cannot be offset by the Southern harvest."
- Statement 2: "Industrial metals have the most flexible life cycle, because ore and refined metal can be stored for long periods without spoiling."
- Statement 3: "Natural gas, like crude oil, must be refined before it can be consumed, which is why gas supply responds slowly to demand."

Brandt reviews two trades from the past quarter.
In Trade 1, Aurelia bought wheat futures covering six months of forecast flour requirements.
In Trade 2, a trader in Aurelia's treasury sold sugar futures because he had obtained early information suggesting a larger-than-expected Brazilian crop; the position was not linked to any sugar sale by Aurelia, which only buys sugar.

In December, Aurelia entered a natural gas swap with a dealer.
The swap pays Aurelia, at each month-end from January through June, the excess, if any, of the monthly reference price over USD 3.40 per MMBtu on 200,000 MMBtu per month.
Aurelia makes no payment when the reference price is below USD 3.40.
Aurelia paid the dealer an upfront premium of USD 240,000.

Exhibit 1: Natural gas monthly reference prices (USD per MMBtu)

| Month | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reference price | 3.25 | 3.62 | 3.95 | 3.38 | 3.51 | 3.10 |

Voss has three new swap proposals for Brandt's review:

- Proposal 1: a basis swap in which Aurelia receives the Gulf Coast regional gas price, which its plants pay, and pays the Henry Hub price that underlies its natural gas hedges.
- Proposal 2: a long variance swap on wheat, because Voss expects realized wheat volatility to exceed the level implied in dealer quotes.
- Proposal 3: a long total return swap on a broad commodity index, to earn commodity returns on surplus cash.

Voss also sits on the investment committee of Aurelia's pension plan, which is choosing between two commodity indexes as a benchmark.
Index R weights commodities by production and liquidity, with sector caps and commodity floors, and holds natural gas at 8.5%.
Index S weights commodities by world production value and holds natural gas at 2.5%.
Both rebalance annually, and each index rolls monthly into the nearby contract.
The committee's consultant estimates natural gas's annualized roll return at -17.0% and its correlation with other commodities at 0.35.

## Questions

1. Among Aurelia's inputs, the price least likely to be affected in the short term by the drought scenario is that of: (LOS: los-08-01-a)
   - A. wheat.
   - B. sugar.
   - C. aluminum.
2. Which of Brandt's statements about commodity life cycles is correct? (LOS: los-08-01-b)
   - A. Statement 1
   - B. Statement 2
   - C. Statement 3
3. In Trade 2, Aurelia is acting most like: (LOS: los-08-01-d)
   - A. a hedger, because Aurelia is a commercial user of sugar.
   - B. an informed investor, because the position is a directional view based on an information advantage.
   - C. an arbitrageur, because Aurelia holds sugar inventory in its own warehouses.
4. Based on Exhibit 1, Aurelia's net gain or loss on the natural gas swap over the six months, after the premium, is closest to: (LOS: los-08-01-i)
   - A. -USD 158,000.
   - B. -USD 64,000.
   - C. +USD 176,000.
5. Which of Voss's swap proposals complies with the hedging policy? (LOS: los-08-01-i)
   - A. Proposal 1
   - B. Proposal 2
   - C. Proposal 3
6. Holding all else constant, the natural gas roll return makes Index R's annual return, relative to Index S's, closest to: (LOS: los-08-01-j)
   - A. 1.02 percentage points lower.
   - B. 1.02 percentage points higher.
   - C. 1.45 percentage points lower.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** Grains and softs are driven on the supply side by weather and disease, so a drought directly cuts wheat and sugar supply; industrial metals such as aluminum are driven mainly by GDP growth, industrial activity, and infrastructure demand, and refined metal is stored for long periods.
> A is wrong because grain yields fall directly in a drought.
> B is wrong because sugar is a soft commodity, grown as a cash crop and exposed to weather and disease.
> 2. **B.** Metals have the most flexible life cycle of the commodity sectors because ore and finished metal can be stored for months or years without spoilage.
> A is wrong because the hemispheres are out of phase: Northern and Southern harvests fall in different seasons, so one hemisphere's harvest can partly offset a shortfall in the other.
> C is wrong because natural gas is consumable almost immediately after extraction; it is crude oil that must be refined (cracked) before it is useful.
> 3. **B.** Hedging and long or short are independent: a commercial firm can also speculate on its own information, as an oil major may.
> Aurelia only buys sugar, so a short sugar position adds price risk rather than offsetting any exposure; it is a directional view based on an information advantage, the hallmark of an informed investor, and it breaches the policy.
> A is wrong because being a commercial user does not make every trade a hedge; a sugar buyer would hedge by going long, not short.
> C is wrong because the vignette mentions no storage capability or spot-futures mispricing, the conditions that define arbitrage.
> 4. **B.** Monthly receipts per MMBtu are the excess over 3.40: Jan 0, Feb 0.22, Mar 0.55, Apr 0, May 0.11, Jun 0, summing to 0.88.
> Total receipts $= 0.88 \times 200{,}000 = 176{,}000$; net result $= 176{,}000 - 240{,}000 = -64{,}000$.
> The swap acts like a strip of call options: the premium buys protection against a price spike and makes costs predictable.
> A is wrong because it treats months below 3.40 as payments by Aurelia (sum of all differences $= 0.41$, giving $0.41 \times 200{,}000 - 240{,}000 = -158{,}000$), contradicting the swap terms.
> C is wrong because it ignores the upfront premium.
> 5. **A.** A basis swap pays on the difference between two related but imperfectly correlated reference prices; receiving the Gulf Coast price Aurelia actually pays against the Henry Hub price underlying its hedges reduces the basis risk in its input hedge, so it complies.
> B is wrong because a variance swap pays on realized versus fixed variance, a view on volatility rather than on the level of an input price, which the policy prohibits.
> C is wrong because a total return swap on a broad index creates investment exposure to commodity prices, most of them commodities Aurelia does not purchase, rather than reducing input price risk.
> 6. **A.** Roll contribution of natural gas to Index R $= 0.085 \times (-17.0\%) = -1.4450\%$; to Index S $= 0.025 \times (-17.0\%) = -0.4250\%$.
> Difference $= -1.4450\% - (-0.4250\%) = -1.0200\%$, so Index R's return is about 1.02 percentage points lower from this source and must find more price or rebalance return to overcome it.
> B is wrong because it treats the roll return as a positive contribution; a negative roll return (contango) subtracts from index returns.
> C is wrong because it reports Index R's gas roll drag alone, ignoring that Index S also bears the drag on its 2.5% weight.
> The 0.35 correlation is a decoy that does not enter the roll attribution.

## Distractor design

1. A and B: weather-driven supply sectors (grains, softs) misclassified as weather-insensitive.
2. A: hemispheric seasonality reversed; C: crude oil's refining stage attributed to natural gas.
3. A: commercial user status equated with hedging regardless of position direction; C: arbitrage assumed without storage or mispricing evidence.
4. A: option-like swap misread as a two-way swap (months below strike charged); C: upfront premium omitted.
5. B: volatility view mistaken for a price hedge (variance swap payoff variable misread); C: investment exposure mistaken for input hedging.
6. B: sign of contango roll return reversed; C: gross contribution of one index used instead of the difference between the two (decoy correlation unused).
