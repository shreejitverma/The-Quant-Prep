# Northfield Brands Regional Sales Model

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Northfield Brands is a packaged snack manufacturer that sells through grocery chains in 52 sales regions.
The chief financial officer, Daniel Okafor, must decide by the end of the fiscal year how to allocate next year's marketing budget across regions.
He asks Sofia Marchetti, a senior analyst in financial planning and analysis, to model regional sales.
Okafor intends to use the model for two purposes.
The first is to forecast total company sales for the annual budget.
The second is to estimate the incremental sales generated by an additional USD 1 million of advertising, which will decide whether to shift spending from trade promotion to advertising.
The data cover fiscal year 2025 only; regional data for earlier years exist but have not yet been collected.

Marchetti regresses each region's annual sales (SALES, USD millions) on four variables: advertising spend (ADV, USD millions), trade promotion spend (PROMO, USD millions), the number of stores carrying the brand (STORES), and median household disposable income in the region (INCOME, USD thousands).

**Exhibit 1: Regression of SALES on four regressors, 52 regions**

| Variable | Coefficient | Standard error | t-statistic | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intercept | -9.335 | 7.159 | -1.30 | 0.200 |
| ADV | 3.596 | 1.944 | 1.85 | 0.071 |
| PROMO | 3.041 | 1.810 | 1.68 | 0.100 |
| STORES | 0.1411 | 0.0720 | 1.96 | 0.056 |
| INCOME | 0.4029 | 0.2238 | 1.80 | 0.078 |

$R^2 = 0.8233$, adjusted $R^2 = 0.8083$, $F = 54.75$ (p-value below 0.0001).
The two-tailed 5% critical t-value with 47 degrees of freedom is 2.012.

Marchetti then regresses each regressor on the other three and records the coefficient of determination of each auxiliary regression.

**Exhibit 2: Auxiliary regressions**

| Regressor | $R_j^2$ from regression on the other three regressors |
|---|---|
| ADV | 0.6953 |
| PROMO | 0.8141 |
| STORES | 0.9298 |
| INCOME | 0.7702 |

**Exhibit 3: Pairwise correlations**

| | ADV | PROMO | STORES | INCOME | SALES |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ADV | 1.00 | | | | |
| PROMO | 0.28 | 1.00 | | | |
| STORES | 0.68 | 0.68 | 1.00 | | |
| INCOME | 0.30 | 0.05 | 0.62 | 1.00 | |
| SALES | 0.65 | 0.62 | 0.90 | 0.59 | 1.00 |

Marchetti presents the results to Okafor and to the treasurer, Lena Varga, on 9 October 2026.
Okafor is surprised that a model explaining over 80% of the variation in regional sales contains no individually significant regressor.
Marchetti notes that no pairwise correlation among the regressors exceeds 0.70.

Marchetti also tests a smaller model that uses only ADV and STORES as regressors.

In the discussion that follows, Okafor and Varga make the following statements.

- Okafor: "For next year's forecast of total company sales, we can keep using the four-variable model even with these diagnostics, because we need a prediction of sales, not a reading of individual coefficients."
- Varga: "To measure the payoff to marketing, we should add total marketing spend, defined as ADV plus PROMO, as a fifth regressor so the model captures the joint effect of the two budgets."

## Questions

1. Based on Exhibits 2 and 3, the regressors that exhibit serious multicollinearity requiring correction are: (LOS: los-01-03-d)
   - A. none, because no pairwise correlation among the regressors exceeds 0.70.
   - B. PROMO and STORES.
   - C. STORES only.
2. In Marchetti's smaller model with ADV and STORES as the only regressors, the variance inflation factor of each regressor is closest to: (LOS: los-01-03-d)
   - A. 0.54.
   - B. 1.86.
   - C. 3.13.
3. The pattern in Exhibit 1 that surprised Okafor most likely indicates: (LOS: los-01-03-d)
   - A. multicollinearity, which inflates the standard errors and raises the probability of Type II errors.
   - B. conditional heteroskedasticity, which understates the standard errors and raises the probability of Type I errors.
   - C. multicollinearity, which understates the standard errors and makes the t-statistics unreliable.
4. Which of the statements made at the meeting is correct? (LOS: los-01-03-d)
   - A. Okafor's only.
   - B. Varga's only.
   - C. Both statements.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** $VIF_j = 1/(1 - R_j^2)$: ADV $1/(1 - 0.6953) = 3.2819$, PROMO $1/(1 - 0.8141) = 5.3792$, STORES $1/(1 - 0.9298) = 14.2450$, INCOME $1/(1 - 0.7702) = 4.3516$. A VIF above 10 indicates serious multicollinearity requiring correction, so only STORES qualifies; PROMO, between 5 and 10, warrants investigation but not correction. A is wrong because with more than two regressors low pairwise correlations do not rule out multicollinearity: STORES is almost a linear combination of the other three regressors ($R_j^2 = 0.9298$) even though no pairwise correlation exceeds 0.68. B is wrong because it applies the investigation threshold of 5 as the correction threshold.
> 2. **B.** With exactly two regressors, the auxiliary $R^2$ of each on the other equals the squared pairwise correlation, so $R_j^2 = 0.68^2 = 0.4624$ and $VIF = 1/(1 - 0.4624) = 1.8601$ for both ADV and STORES. A is wrong because it stops at $1 - r^2 = 0.5376$ without taking the reciprocal. C is wrong because it uses the correlation instead of its square: $1/(1 - 0.68) = 3.1250$.
> 3. **A.** A high $R^2$ (0.8233) and a highly significant F-statistic combined with no individually significant t-statistic (all below 2.012) is the classic symptom of multicollinearity. Multicollinearity leaves the coefficients consistent but inflates their standard errors, so t-statistics are too small, the tests have little power, and Type II errors (failing to reject false nulls) become more likely. B is wrong because conditional heteroskedasticity inflates t-statistics, the opposite of what Exhibit 1 shows. C is wrong because it names the right problem but reverses its effect on the standard errors.
> 4. **A.** Okafor is correct: if the only goal is to predict the dependent variable, multicollinearity may not be a major issue, because the regressors jointly explain SALES well even though their individual effects cannot be separated. Varga is wrong: total marketing spend is an exact linear combination of ADV and PROMO, which violates the assumption of no exact linear relation among the regressors and makes estimation impossible; the listed corrections are to drop a variable, use a different proxy, or increase the sample size. B is wrong because it accepts adding a redundant regressor, which worsens collinearity. C is wrong because Varga's statement is incorrect.

## Distractor design

1. A uses pairwise correlations as the detector; B collapses the 5 and 10 thresholds into one.
2. A forgets the reciprocal in the VIF formula; C uses $r$ instead of $r^2$ as the auxiliary $R^2$.
3. B misdiagnoses the violation and reverses the standard error direction; C diagnoses correctly but reverses the standard error direction.
4. B and C treat adding an exact linear combination of existing regressors as a remedy rather than the cause of perfect collinearity.
