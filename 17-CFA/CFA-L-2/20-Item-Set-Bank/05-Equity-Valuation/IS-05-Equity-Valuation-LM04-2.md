# Aldermoor Capital: Screening Two Logistics Targets

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Priya Raman, CFA, is a principal at Aldermoor Capital Partners, a private equity firm that acquires mid-sized transport businesses.
In March 2026 she is screening two listed freight logistics companies as possible take-private targets.
Tidewater Freight Inc. is a US company reporting under US GAAP; it financed a terminal expansion in 2024 with debt and is now highly levered.
Rheinhafen Logistik AG is a German company reporting under IFRS; it carries little debt, measures its warehouses under the revaluation model, and capitalizes the development costs of its routing software.
Rheinhafen's figures below have been translated into US dollars.

Exhibit 1: Selected data for the most recent fiscal year (USD millions, except per-share data)

| Item | Tidewater | Rheinhafen |
|---|---|---|
| Share price (USD) | 34.00 | 58.00 |
| Common shares outstanding (millions) | 150 | 80 |
| Debt, market value | 3,100 | 600 |
| Debt, book value | 3,250 | 620 |
| Preferred stock, market value | 200 | 0 |
| Noncontrolling interest | 120 | 0 |
| Cash and cash equivalents | 180 | 340 |
| Short-term investments | 90 | 0 |
| Net income from continuing operations | 290 | 300 |
| Income from discontinued operations | 35 | 0 |
| Interest expense | 210 | 30 |
| Income tax expense (continuing operations) | 95 | 130 |
| Depreciation and amortization | 405 | 180 |
| Cash flow from operations | 610 | 395 |

Exhibit 2: Dividends declared in the last 12 months (USD per share)

| Company | Payment pattern | Amounts, oldest to most recent |
|---|---|---|
| Tidewater | Quarterly | 0.20, 0.20, 0.22, 0.24 |
| Rheinhafen | Semiannual, uneven | Interim 0.70 (September 2025), final 1.30 (February 2026) |

Raman's associate, Leon Fairbanks, prepares the first comparison.
He reports that Tidewater's price-to-EBITDA multiple is far below Rheinhafen's and recommends focusing on Tidewater.
Raman replies: "The two companies have very different capital structures, so I want the multiple that matches the claim on the business we would be buying before we choose."

Fairbanks also notes that Tidewater's trade receivables rose sharply in the last fiscal year after it extended payment terms to win new shipping customers, and that Tidewater's capital expenditure is lumpy because terminal projects arrive every few years.
He proposes adding a price-to-cash-flow cross-check that defines cash flow as earnings plus noncash charges.

Finally, Raman asks Fairbanks to identify a multiple that would be least distorted by the differences between the two companies' accounting standards, noting Rheinhafen's revaluation surpluses, its capitalized development costs, and the differences in goodwill and deferred tax accounting that appear in its reconciliations.
Aldermoor's investment committee meets in two weeks and expects one recommended target with a supporting multiple, a dividend yield comparison for the income-oriented co-investors, and a view on which cash flow measure to trust.

## Questions

1. Tidewater's enterprise value to EBITDA multiple is closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-o)
   - A. 7.97.
   - B. 8.25.
   - C. 8.40.
2. Based on the multiple Raman describes, which target appears relatively cheaper? (LOS: los-05-04-o)
   - A. Rheinhafen, because its EV/EBITDA is lower and EBITDA is a flow to all providers of capital.
   - B. Tidewater, because its P/EBITDA is lower and equity investors pay the share price.
   - C. Tidewater, because its total invested capital to EBITDA is lower once cash is not deducted.
3. Rheinhafen's price-to-cash-flow ratio using the definition Fairbanks proposes is closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-d)
   - A. 7.25.
   - B. 9.67.
   - C. 11.75.
4. The trailing dividend yields of Tidewater and Rheinhafen are closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-d)
   - A. 2.82% and 3.45%.
   - B. 2.53% and 3.45%.
   - C. 2.82% and 4.48%.
5. For Tidewater, the limitation of Fairbanks's cash flow definition that is most relevant is that it: (LOS: los-05-04-n)
   - A. is more volatile and more frequently negative than other cash flow measures.
   - B. is a pre-interest flow and therefore mismatched with the share price.
   - C. ignores the increase in working capital and any noncash revenue.
6. The multiple that best meets Raman's final request is: (LOS: los-05-04-p)
   - A. price to book value.
   - B. EV to EBITDA.
   - C. price to free cash flow to equity.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Market value of equity $= 34.00 \times 150 = 5{,}100$.
> EV $= 5{,}100 + 200 \text{ (preferred)} + 120 \text{ (NCI)} + 3{,}100 \text{ (market debt)} - 180 \text{ (cash)} - 90 \text{ (short-term investments)} = 8{,}250$.
> EBITDA is built from net income from continuing operations: $290 + 210 + 95 + 405 = 1{,}000$; EV/EBITDA $= 8{,}250 / 1{,}000 = 8.2500$.
> A is wrong because it starts EBITDA from total net income including discontinued operations ($1{,}035$, $8{,}250/1{,}035 = 7.9710$).
> C is wrong because it uses book debt when market value is available ($8{,}400/1{,}000 = 8.4000$); omitting short-term investments would give a further wrong answer of $8{,}340/1{,}000 = 8.34$.
> 2. **A.** Rheinhafen: equity $58.00 \times 80 = 4{,}640$; EV $= 4{,}640 + 600 - 340 = 4{,}900$; EBITDA $= 300 + 30 + 130 + 180 = 640$; EV/EBITDA $= 4{,}900/640 = 7.6562$, below Tidewater's 8.2500.
> P/EBITDA points the other way (Tidewater $5{,}100/1{,}000 = 5.1000$, Rheinhafen $4{,}640/640 = 7.2500$) only because it ignores Tidewater's heavy debt; EBITDA is pre-interest, so it must sit under enterprise value, and that multiple governs when the two conflict.
> B is wrong because it pairs an equity numerator with a pre-interest denominator, exactly Fairbanks's error.
> C is wrong because TIC/EBITDA also favors Rheinhafen ($5{,}240/640 = 8.1875$ versus $8{,}520/1{,}000 = 8.5200$); rankings by TIC and EV differ only when cash relative to EBITDA differs materially.
> 3. **B.** EPS $= 300/80 = 3.7500$; depreciation and amortization per share $= 180/80 = 2.2500$; CF $= 3.7500 + 2.2500 = 6.0000$.
> P/CF $= 58.00 / 6.0000 = 9.6667$, or 9.67.
> A is wrong because it is P/EBITDA ($58.00/(640/80) = 7.2500$), a different and mismatched cash flow definition.
> C is wrong because it uses cash flow from operations ($4{,}640/395 = 11.7468$), not earnings plus noncash charges.
> 4. **A.** Tidewater pays quarterly, so the dividend rate is four times the most recent quarterly dividend: $4 \times 0.24 = 0.96$ and $0.96/34.00 = 2.82\%$.
> Rheinhafen pays uneven semiannual dividends, so the dividend rate is the most recent annual total: $0.70 + 1.30 = 2.00$ and $2.00/58.00 = 3.45\%$.
> B is wrong because it sums the last four Tidewater dividends ($0.86/34.00 = 2.53\%$) instead of annualizing the latest one.
> C is wrong because it annualizes Rheinhafen's larger final dividend ($2 \times 1.30 = 2.60$, $2.60/58.00 = 4.48\%$), which overstates the rate for an uneven payer.
> 5. **C.** Earnings plus noncash charges ignores changes in working capital and noncash revenue, so Tidewater's sharp rise in receivables from extended credit terms flatters this measure relative to cash actually collected.
> A is wrong because volatility and frequent negative values are the limitation of FCFE, which would be affected by the lumpy capital spending instead.
> B is wrong because the pre-interest mismatch with price is the limitation of EBITDA, not of earnings plus noncash charges.
> 6. **C.** Multiples built on cash flow measures that are well down the income and cash flow statements, P/CFO and P/FCFE, are the least affected by international accounting differences.
> A is wrong because book value is directly changed by revaluation surpluses, capitalized development costs and goodwill treatment, so P/B is among the most affected multiples.
> B is wrong because EBITDA-based multiples are also among the most affected; capitalizing development costs, for example, moves expense out of EBITDA and into amortization.

## Distractor design

1. A: EBITDA started from total net income including discontinued operations; C: book debt used when market debt is given.
2. B: equity numerator matched with a pre-interest denominator; C: TIC ranking assumed to reverse the EV ranking without checking cash.
3. A: EBITDA per share substituted for CF; C: CFO substituted for earnings plus noncash charges.
4. B: sum of the last four dividends instead of four times the latest; C: latest semiannual payment annualized for an uneven payer.
5. A: FCFE limitation attributed to CF; B: EBITDA limitation attributed to CF.
6. A and B: multiples most sensitive to accounting standards chosen as least sensitive (IFRS versus US GAAP).
