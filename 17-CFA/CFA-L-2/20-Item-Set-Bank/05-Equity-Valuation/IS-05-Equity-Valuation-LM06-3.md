# Solenne Foods: Fair Value of an Associate Stake

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Brightwater Holdings plc, a listed food distributor, owns a 30% non-controlling equity interest in Solenne Foods Ltd., a private Irish food manufacturer.
The remaining 70% is held by the founding Mulcahy family, who control the board.
Brightwater's auditors require an estimate of the fair value of the 30% interest for the annual financial statements.
Aoife Gallagher, CFA, a valuation manager at Brightwater, is responsible for the estimate.

Solenne has three business lines with different growth and margin profiles.
Gallagher assembles segment data and, for each segment, the median trading multiples of listed companies in that business.

Exhibit 1: Solenne segment data and guideline public company multiples (EUR millions)

| Segment | Revenue | EBITDA | Peer EV/Sales | Peer EV/EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chilled Meals | 180 | 27 | 1.2 | 8.5 |
| Snacks | 120 | 21 | 1.8 | 10.0 |
| Plant-Based | 40 | (4) | 3.5 | 25.0 |
| Total | 340 | 44 | | |

Plant-Based was launched two years ago, is growing rapidly, and has not yet broken even.
Solenne's statements are audited, and management has supplied five-year projections that Gallagher intends to use only as a cross-check.
Solenne has EUR 95.5 million of debt and negligible cash.
Solenne's net book value of tangible assets is EUR 210 million.

Gallagher first computes a sales-weighted composite EV/Sales multiple.
She then computes an EBITDA-weighted composite EV/EBITDA multiple, but is uneasy with the result.
Her final enterprise value instead applies each segment's own peer EV/EBITDA multiple to the Chilled Meals and Snacks segments and the peer EV/Sales multiple to the Plant-Based segment.

Gallagher notes that the guideline multiples come from minority trades in listed shares.
From a study of restricted stock transactions, she estimates a discount for lack of marketability of 15% for Solenne shares.
An acquisitions database shows an average control premium of 20% for food manufacturers.

Her colleague, Rory Finnegan, suggests two alternatives.
First, he proposes the guideline transactions method using three recent acquisitions of whole food companies by large strategic buyers, at a median EV/EBITDA of 12.4.
Second, he suggests that because Solenne has substantial factories and equipment, the asset-based approach would give "the most objective answer for an auditor."

Gallagher replies in an e-mail.

"Our purpose determines the standard of value, and the standard is set by the accounting rules, not by what a particular acquirer would pay us.
The strategic deals include what those buyers expected to save by combining operations, and each buyer bought 100%, which is not what we own."

## Questions

1. Solenne's sales-weighted composite EV/Sales multiple is closest to: (LOS: los-05-06-i)
   - A. 1.28.
   - B. 1.68.
   - C. 2.17.
2. Gallagher's final enterprise value for Solenne is closest to: (LOS: los-05-06-i)
   - A. EUR 339.5 million.
   - B. EUR 439.5 million.
   - C. EUR 579.5 million.
3. Using Gallagher's final enterprise value and discount data, the fair value of Brightwater's 30% interest is closest to: (LOS: los-05-06-f)
   - A. EUR 102.85 million.
   - B. EUR 123.42 million.
   - C. EUR 145.20 million.
4. The most valid criticism of Finnegan's proposal to use the guideline transactions method for the 30% interest is that the transaction multiples: (LOS: los-05-06-i)
   - A. reflect minority trading prices and must be increased by a control premium.
   - B. embed synergies and control and would overstate the value of a non-controlling interest.
   - C. cannot be used for private companies because acquisition data are never disclosed.
5. Finnegan's suggestion to use the asset-based approach for Solenne is best evaluated as: (LOS: los-05-06-g)
   - A. appropriate, because tangible assets can be verified by the auditors.
   - B. inappropriate, because the approach is generally unsuitable for an operating going concern whose value includes intangibles and growth.
   - C. inappropriate, because the asset-based approach cannot be used in any financial reporting valuation.
6. Brightwater's valuation of the Solenne interest is best classified by practice area and standard of value as: (LOS: los-05-06-b)
   - A. compliance; fair value.
   - B. transaction; investment value.
   - C. compliance; investment value.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Sales weights are $180/340 = 0.5294$, $120/340 = 0.3529$, and $40/340 = 0.1176$.
> Composite $= 0.5294 \times 1.2 + 0.3529 \times 1.8 + 0.1176 \times 3.5 = 572 / 340 = 1.6824$.
> Equivalently, the segment EVs $216 + 216 + 140 = 572$ divided by total revenue 340.
> A is wrong because it weights the EV/Sales multiples by EBITDA shares ($27/44 \times 1.2 + 21/44 \times 1.8 - 4/44 \times 3.5 = 1.2773$), the wrong fundamental for a sales multiple.
> C is wrong because it takes a simple average of the three multiples ($(1.2 + 1.8 + 3.5)/3 = 2.1667$), ignoring Solenne's mix.
> 2. **C.** $EV = 27 \times 8.5 + 21 \times 10.0 + 40 \times 3.5 = 229.5 + 210.0 + 140.0 = 579.5$ million.
> Pricing the loss-making segment on sales avoids the implication that Plant-Based has negative value.
> A is wrong because it is the composite EV/EBITDA result, $7.7159 \times 44 = 339.5$, in which Plant-Based enters with a negative EBITDA weight and a negative value of $-100$; that is the result Gallagher is uneasy with.
> B is wrong because it drops Plant-Based entirely ($229.5 + 210.0 = 439.5$), valuing a growing business at zero.
> 3. **B.** Equity value $= 579.5 - 95.5 = 484.0$ million; the 30% pro rata share is $145.20$ million.
> Guideline public company multiples come from minority trades, so the value is already on a non-controlling basis and no DLOC is needed.
> The shares are private, so the DLOM applies: $145.20 \times (1 - 0.15) = 123.42$ million.
> A is wrong because it also applies a DLOC derived from the 20% control premium ($145.20 \times (1/1.20) \times 0.85 = 102.85$), double counting lack of control.
> C is wrong because it omits the DLOM, treating private shares as if they were marketable.
> 4. **B.** Acquisition multiples of whole companies bought by strategic buyers include a control premium and the synergies those buyers expected, so applying them to a 30% non-controlling interest would overstate its value unless adjusted.
> A is wrong because it describes guideline public company multiples, not acquisition multiples.
> C is wrong because transaction data are available from filings and databases; the concern is their comparability, not their existence.
> 5. **B.** The asset-based approach suits holding companies and firms facing liquidation; Solenne is an operating going concern with a fast-growing segment, and its value depends on earnings power that tangible asset values miss.
> A is wrong because verifiability of tangible assets does not make them a measure of the value of an operating business.
> C is wrong because asset-based methods are used in some reporting contexts, such as holding companies; the objection is to the fit with Solenne, not a blanket prohibition.
> 6. **A.** A valuation for financial statements is a compliance valuation, and the relevant standard is fair value, the financial reporting standard.
> B is wrong because no sale is contemplated, and investment value is the value to a specific buyer, which Gallagher's e-mail explicitly rejects.
> C is wrong because it pairs the correct practice area with the buyer-specific standard of value.

## Distractor design

1. A: EBITDA weights applied to a sales multiple (wrong fundamental); C: equal weights (mix ignored).
2. A: negative-EBITDA segment left in the composite EV/EBITDA (negative segment value); B: loss-making segment dropped (valued at zero).
3. A: DLOC applied to a value already on a minority basis (basis error, double counting); C: DLOM omitted (marketability ignored).
4. A: acquisition multiples confused with trading multiples (GTM versus GPCM); C: overstated data limitation.
5. A: objectivity of inputs confused with relevance of the approach; C: overgeneralized prohibition.
6. B: wrong practice area and buyer-specific value; C: correct practice area with buyer-specific value.
