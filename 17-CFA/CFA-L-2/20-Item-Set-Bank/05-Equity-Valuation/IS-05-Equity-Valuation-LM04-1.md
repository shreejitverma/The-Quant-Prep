# Brennholt Specialty Steel: Valuing a Cyclical Producer

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Ingrid Castellano, CFA, covers European metals for Varden Securities, a Frankfurt broker.
On 2 March 2026 she is updating her report on Brennholt Specialty Steel AG, a producer of tool and stainless steels whose earnings track the European industrial cycle.
Brennholt's shares closed at EUR 42.60 that day.

For fiscal 2025, Brennholt reported basic EPS of EUR 2.64 and diluted EPS of EUR 2.52; the difference reflects convertible bonds issued in 2023.
The 2025 notes disclose three items, each stated per diluted share:

- a gain of EUR 0.36 on the sale of a rolling mill, which Castellano classifies as nonrecurring;
- a goodwill impairment of EUR 0.22 on a distribution subsidiary, also nonrecurring;
- an increase of EUR 0.08 in energy costs under a new ten-year supply contract, which will continue in future years.

Castellano treats 2021 to 2025 as one complete business cycle for Brennholt.
ROE in Exhibit 1 is computed on year-end book value per share.

Exhibit 1: Brennholt per-share history (EUR)

| Year | Diluted EPS | BVPS (year-end) | ROE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 3.10 | 16.40 | 18.9% |
| 2022 | 3.85 | 18.60 | 20.7% |
| 2023 | 1.20 | 19.10 | 6.3% |
| 2024 | 0.45 | 18.90 | 2.4% |
| 2025 | 2.52 | 20.30 | 12.4% |

Castellano's full model is a three-stage dividend discount model whose mature stage begins after 2029.
She estimates the terminal value at the end of 2029 with a benchmark multiple from mature peers and, as a cross-check, with the Gordon growth model.
She also runs a two-stage free cash flow to equity (FCFE) model as an independent estimate of intrinsic value.

Exhibit 2: Forecast and market inputs

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Consensus EPS, 2026 | EUR 3.05 |
| Castellano's two-stage FCFE value per share | EUR 39.80 |
| Long-run dividend payout ratio | 40% |
| Long-run sustainable ROE | 12.0% |
| Risk-free rate | 3.5% |
| Brennholt equity beta | 1.30 |
| Equity risk premium | 5.0% |
| Forecast EPS, 2029 | EUR 3.60 |
| Forecast EPS, 2030 | EUR 3.86 |
| Median trailing P/E, mature European specialty steel producers | 13.5 |
| Median forward P/E, mature European specialty steel producers | 12.4 |

Castellano uses the capital asset pricing model for Brennholt's required return on equity and assumes long-run growth equals the retention ratio times sustainable ROE.

Her colleague Mateo Ruiz, who covers North American steel, reviews the draft on 4 March 2026 and makes two statements.

Statement 1: "Your peer-median terminal multiple relies on the law of one price, whereas your justified multiples from the FCFE and Gordon models are grounded in discounted cash flow valuation."

Statement 2: "If the whole European specialty steel group is overvalued, any producer that trades below the peer median P/E is necessarily undervalued in absolute terms."

## Questions

1. Based on underlying earnings, Brennholt's trailing P/E is closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-e)
   - A. 16.9.
   - B. 17.3.
   - C. 17.9.
2. Using the method of average return on equity, Brennholt's P/E on normalized EPS is closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-e)
   - A. 17.3.
   - B. 19.2.
   - C. 21.4.
3. Brennholt's justified trailing P/E based on the Gordon growth model is closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-i)
   - A. 8.1.
   - B. 14.3.
   - C. 15.3.
4. Brennholt's justified forward P/E based on Castellano's FCFE value, and the valuation it implies relative to the market price, are closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-b)
   - A. 13.0; overvalued.
   - B. 13.0; undervalued.
   - C. 15.8; undervalued.
5. Using the appropriate peer benchmark multiple, Brennholt's terminal value per share at the end of 2029 is closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-k)
   - A. EUR 44.64.
   - B. EUR 48.60.
   - C. EUR 52.11.
6. Which of Ruiz's statements is correct? (LOS: los-05-04-a)
   - A. Statement 1 only.
   - B. Statement 2 only.
   - C. Both statements.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** Underlying EPS starts from diluted EPS, removes the nonrecurring gain, adds back the nonrecurring charge, and leaves the recurring energy cost in place.
> Underlying EPS $= 2.52 - 0.36 + 0.22 = 2.3800$; trailing P/E $= 42.60 / 2.3800 = 17.8992$, or 17.9.
> A is wrong because it uses unadjusted diluted EPS ($42.60 / 2.52 = 16.9048$).
> B is wrong because it also reverses the recurring EUR 0.08 energy cost ($2.38 + 0.08 = 2.46$, $42.60 / 2.46 = 17.3171$); a cost that will continue belongs in underlying earnings.
> 2. **A.** Average ROE over the 2021 to 2025 cycle $= (18.9 + 20.7 + 6.3 + 2.4 + 12.4)/5 = 60.7/5 = 12.14\%$.
> Normalized EPS $= 0.1214 \times 20.30 = 2.4644$, using the most recent (2025) BVPS; P/E $= 42.60 / 2.4644 = 17.2860$, or 17.3.
> B is wrong because it uses the method of historical average EPS ($11.12/5 = 2.2240$, $42.60/2.2240 = 19.1547$), which ignores the growth in the equity base from 16.40 to 20.30.
> C is wrong because it multiplies average ROE by the 2021 BVPS instead of the current BVPS ($0.1214 \times 16.40 = 1.9910$, $42.60/1.9910 = 21.3967$).
> 3. **C.** Required return $r = 3.5\% + 1.30 \times 5.0\% = 10.00\%$; retention $b = 1 - 0.40 = 0.60$; $g = 0.60 \times 0.12 = 7.20\%$.
> Justified trailing P/E $= (1-b)(1+g)/(r-g) = 0.40 \times 1.072 / (0.100 - 0.072) = 0.4288/0.0280 = 15.3143$, or 15.3.
> A is wrong because it computes growth with the payout ratio instead of the retention ratio ($g = 0.40 \times 0.12 = 4.80\%$, $0.40 \times 1.048/0.052 = 8.0615$).
> B is wrong because it is the justified forward P/E ($0.40/0.028 = 14.2857$); the trailing version needs the extra $(1+g)$.
> 4. **A.** A justified multiple from any DCF model is the model value divided by the forecast fundamental: $39.80 / 3.05 = 13.0492$, or 13.0.
> The market forward P/E is $42.60 / 3.05 = 13.9672$; the justified multiple is below the actual multiple, so the stock is overvalued, consistent with $V_0 = 39.80 < P_0 = 42.60$.
> B is wrong because it reverses the direction: a justified multiple below the market multiple signals overvaluation.
> C is wrong because it divides the value by trailing reported EPS ($39.80/2.52 = 15.7937$) and then compares a trailing-based multiple with the forward market multiple.
> 5. **B.** The terminal value at $n = 2029$ pairs a trailing benchmark with $E_n$ or a forward benchmark with $E_{n+1}$: $V_{2029} = 13.5 \times 3.60 = 48.60$.
> The forward pairing gives a similar $12.4 \times 3.86 = 47.86$, which is not offered; the Gordon cross-check $3.60 \times 0.40 \times 1.072 / 0.028 = 55.13$ is 13.4% higher, reflecting its sensitivity to $r$, payout and $g$.
> A is wrong because it applies the forward P/E to 2029 earnings ($12.4 \times 3.60 = 44.64$), mismatching the multiple and the earnings year.
> C is wrong because it applies the trailing P/E to 2030 earnings ($13.5 \times 3.86 = 52.11$), the opposite mismatch.
> 6. **A.** Statement 1 is correct: the method of comparables rests on the law of one price, and the method based on forecasted fundamentals rests on a DCF model.
> Statement 2 is incorrect: a comparables verdict is only relative; if the peers are overvalued, a stock trading below the peer median can still be overvalued in absolute terms.
> B and C are wrong because they accept Statement 2, confusing relative with absolute valuation.

## Distractor design

1. A: reported diluted EPS used without adjustment; B: a recurring cost reversed as if nonrecurring.
2. B: historical average EPS method substituted for average ROE; C: an old year's BVPS used instead of current BVPS.
3. A: payout used in place of retention in $g = b \times ROE$; B: forward formula when trailing was asked (missing $1+g$).
4. B: direction of misvaluation reversed; C: trailing EPS in the denominator compared with a forward market multiple.
5. A: forward P/E applied to $E_n$; C: trailing P/E applied to $E_{n+1}$ (period mismatch both ways).
6. B and C: relative valuation read as absolute valuation when the peer group itself is mispriced.
