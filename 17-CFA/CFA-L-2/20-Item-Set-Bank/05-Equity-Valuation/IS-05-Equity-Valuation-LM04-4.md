# Meridian Assurance: Supervisory Review of an Insurer Report

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Hannah Lindqvist, CFA, is the supervisory analyst at Castlegate Research, a London equity research boutique.
Castlegate's compliance policy requires her to confirm that every figure in a report can be reproduced from the stated inputs and that every valuation term is used correctly before the report is released.
On 20 May 2026 she reviews a draft by junior analyst Owen Mbeki on Meridian Assurance Group plc, a UK property-casualty and life insurer.

Exhibit 1: Meridian balance sheet and market data at 31 December 2025 (GBP millions unless stated)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Total shareholders' equity | 4,860 |
| Of which: preference shares | 400 |
| Goodwill and other intangible assets | 610 |
| Ordinary shares issued (millions) | 540 |
| Ordinary shares held in treasury (millions) | 20 |
| Share price, 20 May 2026 (GBP) | 10.20 |

In 2025 Meridian repurchased GBP 300 million of its ordinary shares at prices well above book value per share.
Mbeki's valuation assumes a sustainable ROE of 10.5% and a dividend payout ratio of 60%, with long-term growth equal to retention times ROE.
He estimates Meridian's cost of equity with the CAPM using a risk-free rate of 4.0%, a beta of 0.90 and an equity risk premium of 5.5%.

Exhibit 2: Meridian first-quarter 2026 earnings (GBP per share)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Consensus EPS forecast | 0.31 |
| Actual EPS | 0.37 |
| Standard deviation of analysts' Q1 forecasts | 0.02 |
| Standard deviation of Meridian's quarterly earnings surprises, prior 20 quarters | 0.08 |

Exhibit 3: Meridian share price and benchmark UK equity index

| Date | Meridian (GBP) | Index |
|---|---|---|
| 20 May 2025 | 8.50 | 4,000 |
| 20 November 2025 | 10.00 | 4,450 |
| 20 May 2026 | 10.20 | 4,600 |

Lindqvist marks the following passages from Mbeki's draft for review.

Passage 1: "We value Meridian primarily on price to book value. P/B is well suited to an insurer whose assets are mainly financial instruments carried at amounts close to market value. Meridian's 2025 buyback does not affect the multiple. We also prefer P/B to P/E because book value is a flow measure that captures current-year underwriting profitability."

Passage 2: "Meridian's first-quarter standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) of 3.0 signals an unusually strong positive surprise."

Passage 3: "Meridian's 20% share price gain over the past year, together with a relative-strength indicator above 1.0, shows that the stock is still outperforming the market."

Passage 4: "The Prudential Regulation Authority is consulting on higher capital requirements. If adopted, we expect Meridian's sustainable ROE to fall to 9.5%, with its payout ratio and cost of equity unchanged, and we will revisit our valuation multiples."

Lindqvist recomputes the multiples herself before signing off, using common shares outstanding and reported (not tangible) book value.

## Questions

1. Meridian's price-to-book ratio is closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-d)
   - A. 1.09.
   - B. 1.19.
   - C. 1.23.
2. Meridian's justified P/B based on Mbeki's inputs, and the valuation it implies, are closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-i)
   - A. 1.17; overvalued.
   - B. 1.33; undervalued.
   - C. 1.58; undervalued.
3. Which sentence in Passage 1 is accurate? (LOS: los-05-04-c)
   - A. P/B is well suited to an insurer whose assets are mainly financial instruments carried at amounts close to market value.
   - B. Meridian's 2025 buyback does not affect the multiple.
   - C. Book value is a flow measure that captures current-year underwriting profitability.
4. If the scenario in Passage 4 occurs, Meridian's justified P/B and justified trailing dividend yield will most likely: (LOS: los-05-04-g)
   - A. both decrease.
   - B. remain unchanged and increase, respectively.
   - C. decrease and increase, respectively.
5. Meridian's correctly computed first-quarter SUE is closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-q)
   - A. 0.75.
   - B. 3.00.
   - C. 19.4%.
6. Meridian's relative-strength indicator at 20 May 2026 (scaled to 1.0 at 20 May 2025) and its performance against the index over the most recent six months are best described as: (LOS: los-05-04-q)
   - A. 1.200; outperformed.
   - B. 1.043; outperformed.
   - C. 1.043; underperformed.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** Common equity $= 4{,}860 - 400 = 4{,}460$; common shares outstanding $= 540 - 20 = 520$ million.
> BVPS $= 4{,}460 / 520 = 8.5769$; P/B $= 10.20 / 8.5769 = 1.1892$, or 1.19.
> A is wrong because it does not deduct the preference shares ($4{,}860/520 = 9.3462$, $10.20/9.3462 = 1.0914$).
> C is wrong because it divides by issued shares including treasury shares ($4{,}460/540 = 8.2593$, $10.20/8.2593 = 1.2350$); treasury shares are not outstanding.
> 2. **B.** $r = 4.0\% + 0.90 \times 5.5\% = 8.95\%$; $g = (1 - 0.60) \times 0.105 = 4.20\%$.
> Justified P/B $= (ROE - g)/(r - g) = (0.105 - 0.042)/(0.0895 - 0.042) = 0.0630/0.0475 = 1.3263$.
> The actual P/B of 1.1892 is below 1.3263, so Meridian appears undervalued.
> A is wrong because it applies the zero-growth form $ROE/r = 0.105/0.0895 = 1.1732$ when growth is positive, which flips the conclusion.
> C is wrong because it computes growth with the payout ratio ($g = 0.60 \times 0.105 = 6.30\%$, $(0.105 - 0.063)/(0.0895 - 0.063) = 0.0420/0.0265 = 1.5849$).
> 3. **A.** Book value is a natural base for companies such as insurers and banks whose assets are largely financial instruments carried at or near market value.
> B is wrong because repurchases above book value reduce BVPS, which raises P/B and makes the stock look more expensive against its own history.
> C is wrong because book value is a stock (balance sheet) variable, which is why it is more stable than EPS; it is not a flow measure of current-year profitability.
> 4. **C.** New $g = 0.40 \times 0.095 = 3.80\%$; justified P/B falls from 1.3263 to $(0.095 - 0.038)/(0.0895 - 0.038) = 0.0570/0.0515 = 1.1068$.
> Justified trailing dividend yield $= (r - g)/(1 + g)$ rises from $0.0475/1.042 = 4.56\%$ to $0.0515/1.038 = 4.96\%$, because lower growth raises the yield investors require for the same $r$.
> A is wrong because it moves dividend yield in the same direction as value; yield falls with growth, so lower growth raises it.
> B is wrong because the ROE decline is not offset by lower growth: $ROE - g$ falls while $r - g$ widens, so P/B must decline.
> 5. **A.** Unexpected earnings $= 0.37 - 0.31 = 0.06$; SUE divides by the standard deviation of past surprises: $0.06 / 0.08 = 0.75$.
> B is wrong because $0.06/0.02 = 3.00$ is the scaled earnings surprise, which divides by the dispersion of analysts' forecasts; this is the error in Passage 2.
> C is wrong because $0.06/0.31 = 19.35\%$ is the percentage surprise, not a standardized measure.
> 6. **C.** Base ratio $= 8.50/4{,}000 = 0.002125$.
> RSTR at 20 November 2025 $= (10.00/4{,}450)/0.002125 = 1.0575$; RSTR at 20 May 2026 $= (10.20/4{,}600)/0.002125 = 1.0435$.
> Over the year Meridian outperformed by 4.35%, but over the latest six months $1.0435/1.0575 - 1 = -1.33\%$: the stock rose 2.00% while the index rose 3.37%, so it underperformed, and Passage 3 is misleading.
> A is wrong because 1.200 is Meridian's own price relative ($10.20/8.50$), which ignores the index's 15% rise.
> B is wrong because a cumulative RSTR above 1.0 shows outperformance since the base date, not in the most recent period; period performance is the ratio of consecutive RSTR values.

## Distractor design

1. A: preferred equity not deducted from total equity; C: treasury shares counted in the share base.
2. A: zero-growth $ROE/r$ used with positive growth; C: payout used in place of retention in $g$.
3. B: buyback effect on BVPS ignored; C: stock variable mislabeled as a flow variable.
4. A: dividend yield assumed to move with value rather than inversely with growth; B: ROE and growth effects assumed to offset.
5. B: analyst-dispersion denominator (scaled surprise) used for SUE; C: percentage surprise substituted.
6. A: own price return used with no index scaling; B: cumulative RSTR level read as recent-period performance.
