# Kessler Brands: Reviewing a Residual Income Model

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Ingrid Solberg works in the model validation team at Adler Rhee Asset Management in Frankfurt.
She is reviewing a residual income valuation of Kessler Brands SE prepared by analyst Wei Chen, CFA.
Kessler is a German consumer goods company that reports under IFRS and owns subsidiaries in Turkey, Brazil and South Africa.
It has reported currency translation losses in other comprehensive income (OCI) in each of the last five years, and Chen expects losses of EUR 0.60 per share in each of the next three years.
Kessler's beginning book value of EUR 20.00 per share includes an accumulated translation reserve of EUR -3.10 per share.

Chen uses a required return on equity of 10%.
He assumes that Kessler's share price at the end of Year 3 will be 1.5 times its book value per share at that date.
He rolled book value forward from the statement of changes in equity, so his book values include the forecast OCI, but he computed each year's residual income from net income alone.

Exhibit 1: Chen's forecasts for Kessler (EUR per share)

| Year | Net income | Dividends | OCI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.60 | 0.80 | -0.60 |
| 2 | 2.90 | 0.90 | -0.60 |
| 3 | 3.10 | 1.00 | -0.60 |

Chen documents three accounting adjustments in his model file.

Exhibit 2: Chen's accounting adjustments

| Adjustment | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Remove a one-time gain of EUR 0.35 per share on the sale of a distribution center from the base-year earnings used to anchor the return on equity forecast |
| 2 | Reduce beginning book value by the EUR 3.10 per share accumulated translation reserve, to reflect the persistence of the losses |
| 3 | Leave the internally generated Kessler brand unrecognized in book value |

The valuation Solberg is reviewing reflects Adjustments 1 and 3 but has not applied Adjustment 2, so it uses the reported beginning book value of EUR 20.00 per share.

Kessler also plans to acquire Almquist Naturals AB for cash equal to Almquist's standalone intrinsic value.
Chen's post-acquisition model deducts amortization of the acquired intangible from combined earnings and concludes that the deal destroys value.
Solberg tests that conclusion with the simplified steady-state illustration in Exhibit 3.

Exhibit 3: Steady-state acquisition illustration (EUR millions)

| Item | Kessler | Almquist |
|---|---|---|
| Book value of equity | 8,000 | 1,200 |
| Net income | 880 | 168 |

In the illustration, both companies pay out all earnings and do not grow, and the required return on equity is 10%.
Almquist's identifiable net assets are carried at fair value, and the excess of the purchase price over Almquist's book value will be allocated to a customer license with an eight-year life, amortized straight-line.

Solberg's memo notes three further characteristics of Kessler: dividends were cut in two of the last six years, free cash flow to equity will be negative for the next two years because of a factory modernization program, and the translation losses are large and persistent.

The memo closes with two statements.

Chen: "A strength of the residual income model for Kessler is that a smaller share of its value depends on the terminal value than in a dividend discount model."

Solberg: "A further strength is that Kessler's accounting choices will not affect our valuation, because aggressive accounting that raises book value today reduces future residual income by an exactly offsetting amount."

## Questions

1. Kessler's intrinsic value per share, with residual income computed consistently with the clean surplus relation, is closest to: (LOS: los-05-05-k)
   - A. EUR 29.38.
   - B. EUR 30.87.
   - C. EUR 47.49.
2. Solberg also values Kessler with a dividend discount model using the Exhibit 1 dividends and Chen's terminal price. The difference between Chen's original residual income value and Solberg's dividend discount model value is closest to: (LOS: los-05-05-i)
   - A. EUR 0.00.
   - B. EUR 0.55.
   - C. EUR 1.49.
3. In Solberg's Exhibit 3 illustration, the combined value of Kessler after the cash acquisition that correctly reflects a purchase at fair value is closest to: (LOS: los-05-05-k)
   - A. EUR 9,880 million.
   - B. EUR 10,480 million.
   - C. EUR 10,960 million.
4. Which of Chen's adjustments in Exhibit 2 is least appropriate? (LOS: los-05-05-k)
   - A. Adjustment 2.
   - B. Adjustment 1.
   - C. Adjustment 3.
5. Which characteristic in Solberg's memo most weakens the case for using a residual income model to value Kessler? (LOS: los-05-05-j)
   - A. The irregular dividend history.
   - B. The negative free cash flow to equity over the next two years.
   - C. The large and persistent translation losses.
6. Which of the closing statements is correct? (LOS: los-05-05-j)
   - A. Solberg's statement only.
   - B. Chen's statement only.
   - C. Both statements.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Book values including OCI: $B_1 = 20.00 + 2.60 - 0.60 - 0.80 = 21.20$; $B_2 = 21.20 + 2.90 - 0.60 - 0.90 = 22.60$; $B_3 = 22.60 + 3.10 - 0.60 - 1.00 = 24.10$.
> Clean-surplus-adjusted residual income, $(NI_t + OCI_t) - rB_{t-1}$: Year 1 $= 2.00 - 2.00 = 0.00$; Year 2 $= 2.30 - 2.12 = 0.18$; Year 3 $= 2.50 - 2.26 = 0.24$.
> Present values: $0.0000 + 0.18/1.10^2 + 0.24/1.10^3 = 0.1488 + 0.1803 = 0.3291$.
> Terminal premium $P_3 - B_3 = 1.5 \times 24.10 - 24.10 = 12.05$; present value $= 12.05/1.331 = 9.0533$.
> $V_0 = 20.00 + 0.3291 + 9.0533 = 29.3824$.
> B is wrong because it is Chen's original model, with residual income from net income alone: $0.60, 0.78, 0.84$ with present values $0.5455 + 0.6446 + 0.6311 = 1.8212$, so $20.00 + 1.8212 + 9.0533 = 30.8745$; it ignores a clean surplus violation that is not expected to net to zero.
> C is wrong because it discounts the full terminal price instead of the premium over book value: $20.00 + 0.3291 + 36.15/1.331 = 20.00 + 0.3291 + 27.1600 = 47.4891$.
> 2. **C.** DDM value $= 0.80/1.10 + 0.90/1.10^2 + 1.00/1.10^3 + 36.15/1.10^3 = 0.7273 + 0.7438 + 0.7513 + 27.1600 = 29.3824$, identical to the OCI-adjusted residual income value in Question 1.
> Chen's original value is 30.8745, so the difference is $30.8745 - 29.3824 = 1.4921$.
> The gap equals the present value of the omitted OCI: $0.60/1.10 + 0.60/1.10^2 + 0.60/1.10^3 = 0.5455 + 0.4959 + 0.4508 = 1.4921$.
> A is wrong because it assumes the two models always agree; they agree only when residual income is measured on a clean surplus basis, which Chen's net-income-based residual income is not.
> B is wrong because it counts only the Year 1 OCI, $0.60/1.10 = 0.5455$, as if the translation loss were a one-off.
> 3. **B.** Standalone values from the single-stage model with $g = 0$: Kessler $= 8{,}000 + (0.11 - 0.10)/0.10 \times 8{,}000 = 8{,}800$; Almquist $= 1{,}200 + (0.14 - 0.10)/0.10 \times 1{,}200 = 1{,}680$; total $10{,}480$.
> A fair-value purchase for cash of 1,680 should neither create nor destroy value.
> Kessler's equity stays at 8,000 (cash of 1,680 leaves; net assets of 1,200 and a license of 480 arrive), and amortization is $480/8 = 60$ a year.
> Adding the amortization back to earnings for valuation: $NI = 880 + 168 = 1{,}048$, $ROE = 1{,}048/8{,}000 = 0.1310$, $V = 8{,}000 + 0.031/0.10 \times 8{,}000 = 10{,}480$.
> A is wrong because it is Chen's value with amortization deducted: $NI = 988$, $ROE = 0.1235$, $V = 8{,}000 + 0.0235/0.10 \times 8{,}000 = 9{,}880$, an apparent loss of 600 that reflects accounting, not economics.
> C is wrong because it adds the recognized license on top of both standalone values, $8{,}800 + 1{,}680 + 480 = 10{,}960$, double counting the premium already inside Almquist's 1,680.
> 4. **A.** The accumulated translation losses are already in book value through the translation reserve, so reducing book value again double counts them; persistent OCI losses should instead be reflected by including forecast OCI in residual income, or by lowering the return on equity forecast, with book value left unchanged.
> B is wrong because removing a nonrecurring gain from the earnings used for forecasting is appropriate, and no book value adjustment is needed.
> C is wrong because internally generated brands are generally not recognized, and adding an omitted asset and then amortizing it has no net effect on residual income value, so leaving the brand out is acceptable.
> 5. **C.** Residual income models are least appropriate when there are significant departures from clean surplus accounting, and large persistent translation losses in OCI are exactly such a departure.
> A is wrong because irregular or unpredictable dividends are a reason to prefer a residual income model over a DDM.
> B is wrong because negative near-term free cash flow is also a reason to prefer a residual income model over a free cash flow model.
> 6. **B.** Chen is correct: residual income models recognize value earlier through current book value, so the terminal value is a smaller share of total value than in DDM or free cash flow models (compare 9.0533 of 29.3824 under residual income with 27.1600 of 29.3824 under the DDM).
> Solberg is incorrect: the offsetting argument holds only if clean surplus accounting holds and analysts do not extrapolate distorted earnings or return on equity; Kessler violates clean surplus, and reliance on accounting data that management can manipulate is a stated weakness of residual income models.
> A and C are wrong because they accept Solberg's claim that accounting choices cannot affect the valuation.

## Distractor design

1. B: residual income from net income despite persistent OCI (clean surplus violation ignored); C: full terminal price discounted instead of the premium over book value.
2. A: DDM and RI assumed to agree even though RI is not on a clean surplus basis; B: only the first year of OCI counted (period error).
3. A: amortization of the acquired intangible left in, so a fair-value purchase appears to destroy value; C: license added on top of standalone values (double count).
4. B: appropriate removal of a nonrecurring gain chosen; C: acceptable non-recognition of an internal brand chosen, instead of the double-counting book value cut.
5. A and B: reasons to prefer an RI model chosen as reasons against it (direction reversed).
6. A and C: Bauman's offsetting argument accepted without its clean surplus condition.
