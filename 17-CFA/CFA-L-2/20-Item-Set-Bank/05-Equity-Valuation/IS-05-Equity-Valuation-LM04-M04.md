# Tarrow Bay Provisions: A Price-to-Sales Review

> [!info] Timer
> 12 minutes for 4 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Delphine Arsenault, CFA, is an equity analyst in the internal management team of Laurentide Public Service Pension Trust in Montreal.
In February 2026 she is reviewing the trust's holding in Tarrow Bay Provisions Inc., a Canadian producer of packaged and frozen foods sold mainly through national grocery chains.
Tarrow Bay's shares close at CAD 15.00 on the review date, and 350 million common shares are outstanding.

Tarrow Bay's fiscal 2025 earnings were depressed by a nonrecurring write-down of a closed bakery plant, and the company expects further restructuring charges in 2026.
Arsenault therefore decides to anchor her valuation on price to sales rather than on price to earnings.
In 2024 Tarrow Bay borrowed heavily to acquire a frozen-foods business, so its debt is now well above the sector average.
A footnote also discloses that Tarrow Bay recognizes revenue on bill-and-hold arrangements with two grocery chains that ask it to store finished goods until they are called for.

Exhibit 1: Tarrow Bay selected fiscal 2025 data (CAD millions)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Gross sales | 7,450 |
| Sales returns and allowances | 210 |
| Trade discounts and promotional rebates | 240 |
| Net income | 336 |
| Dividends declared | 134.4 |
| Total debt | 1,900 |
| Cash and cash equivalents | 250 |

Exhibit 2: Long-run forecasts and market inputs

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Net profit margin | 4.8% |
| Dividend payout ratio | 40% |
| Asset turnover (sales / total assets) | 1.25 |
| Financial leverage (total assets / shareholders' equity) | 1.80 |
| Risk-free rate | 3.5% |
| Equity risk premium | 5.0% |
| Tarrow Bay beta, Arsenault's estimate | 1.10 |
| Tarrow Bay beta, vendor two-year regression | 0.95 |
| Consensus net sales per share, 2026 | CAD 21.40 |
| Median trailing P/S, Canadian packaged-food peers | 0.85 |

Arsenault uses her own beta estimate, which reflects the higher leverage since the acquisition, and the capital asset pricing model for the required return on equity.
She assumes that long-run growth equals the retention ratio times ROE, with ROE built from the three DuPont components in Exhibit 2.
Her policy is to derive the justified multiple from forecasted fundamentals first and to use peer multiples only as a cross-check.

At the review meeting, Tarrow Bay's chief financial officer describes a cost program aimed at raising the long-run net profit margin to 5.2%.
He states that the program will not change the payout ratio, asset turnover or financial leverage, and Arsenault agrees that it will not change the company's risk.
She wants to know how sensitive her justified multiple is to that margin target before she accepts it in her model.

Arsenault's portfolio manager, Gilles Thibodeau, questions whether price to sales is the right anchor for Tarrow Bay at all.
He asks her to add a paragraph to the report that states the single most defensible reason for preferring P/S to P/E for this company.
Her draft offers three candidate reasons, and Thibodeau asks her to keep only the one that is consistent with the curriculum's treatment of the multiple.

## Questions

1. Tarrow Bay's trailing price-to-sales ratio is closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-d)
   - A. 0.70.
   - B. 0.75.
   - C. 0.99.
2. Tarrow Bay's justified forward P/S based on forecasted fundamentals, and the valuation it implies, are closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-i)
   - A. 0.76; undervalued.
   - B. 0.81; undervalued.
   - C. 0.41; overvalued.
3. If the CFO's margin target is achieved and all other inputs are unchanged, Tarrow Bay's justified forward P/S is closest to: (LOS: los-05-04-g)
   - A. 0.83.
   - B. 0.97.
   - C. 1.05.
4. The reason for preferring P/S to P/E for Tarrow Bay that Arsenault should keep is: (LOS: los-05-04-c)
   - A. sales are positive and more stable than EPS, which in 2025 was depressed by a nonrecurring write-down.
   - B. P/S captures the effect of the 2024 acquisition debt on the value of Tarrow Bay's equity.
   - C. P/S is unaffected by the bill-and-hold arrangements, because revenue recognition choices affect only earnings.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **B.** P/S uses net sales, which are gross sales less returns and allowances and less discounts: $7{,}450 - 210 - 240 = 7{,}000$ million.
> Net sales per share $= 7{,}000 / 350 = 20.0000$; P/S $= 15.00 / 20.0000 = 0.7500$.
> A is wrong because it uses gross sales ($7{,}450/350 = 21.2857$, $15.00/21.2857 = 0.7047$), which overstates the denominator by the returns and discounts.
> C is wrong because it is EV/S, not P/S: EV $= 15.00 \times 350 + 1{,}900 - 250 = 6{,}900$ and $6{,}900/7{,}000 = 0.9857$.
> 2. **A.** Required return $r = 3.5\% + 1.10 \times 5.0\% = 9.00\%$.
> ROE by DuPont $= 0.048 \times 1.25 \times 1.80 = 10.80\%$; retention $b = 1 - 0.40 = 0.60$; $g = 0.60 \times 0.108 = 6.48\%$.
> Justified forward P/S $= (E_1/S_1)(1-b)/(r-g) = 0.048 \times 0.40 / (0.0900 - 0.0648) = 0.0192/0.0252 = 0.7619$.
> The actual forward P/S is $15.00/21.40 = 0.7009$, below 0.7619, so the stock is undervalued; equivalently, value $= 0.7619 \times 21.40 = 16.30$, about 8.7% above the price.
> B is wrong because it is the justified trailing P/S, which adds a $(1+g)$ factor ($0.7619 \times 1.0648 = 0.8113$) that belongs only when the multiple is set against trailing sales.
> C is wrong because it computes growth with the payout ratio instead of the retention ratio ($g = 0.40 \times 0.108 = 4.32\%$, $0.0192/0.0468 = 0.4103$), which flips the conclusion.
> The vendor beta of 0.95 and the peer median of 0.85 are not inputs to the fundamentals-based multiple.
> 3. **C.** Margin drives justified P/S twice: directly in the numerator and again through $g = b \times PM \times \text{turnover} \times \text{leverage}$.
> New $g = 0.60 \times 0.052 \times 1.25 \times 1.80 = 7.02\%$; $r - g = 0.0900 - 0.0702 = 0.0198$.
> Justified forward P/S $= 0.052 \times 0.40 / 0.0198 = 0.0208/0.0198 = 1.0505$, a rise of 37.9% for an 8.3% rise in margin.
> A is wrong because it scales the multiple only for the direct margin effect ($0.7619 \times 5.2/4.8 = 0.8254$) and ignores the higher growth.
> B is wrong because it raises growth but keeps the old 4.8% margin in the numerator ($0.0192/0.0198 = 0.9697$).
> The size of the jump, driven by a narrowing $r - g$, is the reason Arsenault is right to test the target before accepting it.
> 4. **A.** Sales are almost always positive and are more stable than EPS, so P/S remains meaningful when earnings are depressed or distorted by nonrecurring items, which is Tarrow Bay's situation.
> B is wrong because price is a claim of equity holders while sales are a pre-financing flow to all capital providers; P/S ignores the capital structure, which is why EV/S is preferred for a highly levered company.
> C is wrong because revenue recognition practices, including bill-and-hold sales, can distort reported sales; the footnote is a reason for caution with P/S, not a strength of it.

## Distractor design

1. A: gross sales used instead of net sales; C: enterprise value substituted for price (EV/S reported as P/S).
2. B: trailing justified formula with $(1+g)$ used when the forward multiple was asked; C: payout used in place of retention in $g = b \times ROE$.
3. A: margin effect on $g$ ignored (proportional scaling only); B: new growth paired with the old margin in the numerator.
4. B: pre-financing denominator treated as reflecting leverage; C: sales assumed immune to revenue recognition abuses.
