# Kestrel Marine Coatings: Pricing a Founder's Exit

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Tamsin Orr, CFA, is a director at Halyard Corporate Finance, a UK advisory boutique.
Desmond Kearns, the founder, sole shareholder, and chief executive of Kestrel Marine Coatings Ltd., has engaged Halyard to value 100% of Kestrel's equity ahead of a sale process.
Kestrel manufactures anti-fouling hull coatings and sells most of its output to four shipyards.
Its financial statements are reviewed, not audited.
Kestrel's articles of association require board approval of any transfer of shares, and the board consists of Kearns and his two adult children.

Orr begins with the most recent reported income statement.

Exhibit 1: Kestrel reported results, most recent year (GBP millions)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue | 42.00 |
| Cost of goods sold | 25.20 |
| SG&A | 6.80 |
| EBITDA | 10.00 |
| Depreciation and amortization | 1.60 |
| EBIT | 8.40 |
| Income tax rate | 25% |

In reviewing the accounts, Orr notes the following:

- Kearns received a salary of GBP 1.40 million; a search firm estimates that a chief executive of a comparable company would be paid GBP 0.45 million.
- SG&A includes GBP 0.25 million of running costs for a yacht used only by the Kearns family, and depreciation includes GBP 0.15 million on the yacht.
- Kestrel operates from a warehouse owned by the Kearns family trust and pays no rent; an equivalent warehouse would rent for GBP 0.60 million a year.
- Kearns also received a dividend of GBP 2.00 million during the year.

Orr estimates base-year free cash flow using capital expenditure of GBP 2.10 million, equal to the change in long-term operating assets, and an increase in working capital of GBP 0.35 million.
She uses the capitalized cash flow method with a perpetual FCFF growth rate of 3.0%.

Exhibit 2: Cost of capital inputs for Kestrel

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Cost of equity | 14.0% |
| Pre-tax cost of debt | 7.0% |
| Debt to total capital, Kestrel actual | 5% |
| Debt to total capital, optimal for Kestrel | 20% |
| Debt to total capital, public comparables | 35% |
| Market value of debt outstanding | GBP 4.0 million |

Orr computes Kestrel's WACC using the optimal capital structure, consistent with an acquirer establishing that structure after the sale.

The most likely bidder is Varnhold AG, a large listed coatings group with a WACC of 8.2%.
At a meeting, Kearns makes two remarks.

Remark 1: "Varnhold will finance any deal at its own cost of capital, so Kestrel's cash flows should be discounted at 8.2%. That is the rate that actually applies to the buyer."

Remark 2: "The dividend I took is really my pay. Since I already got GBP 2 million out of the company, you should treat that as compensation expense, and there is no need to make any other adjustment for my salary."

Orr's colleague Liam Achebe, who is new to private company work, asks her how this engagement differs from the equity research he did on listed companies.

## Questions

1. Kestrel's normalized EBITDA is closest to: (LOS: los-05-06-c)
   - A. GBP 10.60 million.
   - B. GBP 11.20 million.
   - C. GBP 11.80 million.
2. Kestrel's base-year FCFF, based on normalized figures and Orr's estimates, is closest to: (LOS: los-05-06-h)
   - A. GBP 5.45 million.
   - B. GBP 5.86 million.
   - C. GBP 6.21 million.
3. Regarding Remark 1, the most appropriate response from Orr is that discounting Kestrel's cash flows at 8.2%: (LOS: los-05-06-d)
   - A. is appropriate because the acquirer's financing cost is the relevant opportunity cost in a sale.
   - B. is inappropriate because it would transfer to the seller value that the buyer itself creates.
   - C. is inappropriate because Kestrel's WACC at its actual capital structure is lower than 8.2%.
4. Treating the result in Question 2 as the base-year FCFF, Kestrel's equity value using the capitalized cash flow method is closest to: (LOS: los-05-06-h)
   - A. GBP 59.38 million.
   - B. GBP 61.28 million.
   - C. GBP 65.28 million.
5. Orr's engagement, and the adjustments she makes for the yacht and the warehouse, are best described as: (LOS: los-05-06-b)
   - A. a transaction valuation; adjustments to the numerator of the valuation.
   - B. a compliance valuation; adjustments to the numerator of the valuation.
   - C. a transaction valuation; adjustments to the discount rate.
6. Which feature of Kestrel described in the vignette is a stock-specific, rather than a company-specific, factor that distinguishes it from a public company? (LOS: los-05-06-a)
   - A. Board approval is required for any transfer of shares.
   - B. Most output is sold to four shipyards.
   - C. The founder is both the sole owner and the chief executive.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **A.** Remove the excess compensation ($1.40 - 0.45 = 0.95$) and the non-operating yacht running costs (0.25) from SG&A, and insert the missing market rent (0.60) as an expense.
> Normalized EBITDA $= 10.00 + 0.95 + 0.25 - 0.60 = 10.60$ million.
> The dividend is a distribution to the owner, not an operating expense, and needs no adjustment.
> B is wrong because it omits the market rent charge ($10.00 + 0.95 + 0.25 = 11.20$), leaving Kestrel's cost of space understated.
> C is wrong because it adds the rent instead of deducting it ($10.00 + 0.95 + 0.25 + 0.60 = 11.80$), the wrong normalization direction for a missing expense.
> 2. **B.** Normalized depreciation $= 1.60 - 0.15 = 1.45$ million.
> $FCFF = EBITDA(1 - t) + Dep \times t - \Delta LT\ assets - \Delta WC = 10.60 \times 0.75 + 1.45 \times 0.25 - 2.10 - 0.35$.
> $= 7.9500 + 0.3625 - 2.4500 = 5.8625$ million.
> A is wrong because it uses the reported EBITDA and depreciation ($10.00 \times 0.75 + 1.60 \times 0.25 - 2.45 = 5.45$), skipping normalization.
> C is wrong because it omits the working capital investment ($7.9500 + 0.3625 - 2.10 = 6.2125$).
> 3. **B.** An acquisition should be priced at the target's own cost of capital, reflecting the risk of the target's cash flows.
> Discounting at the buyer's lower 8.2% hands the seller value that arises only from the buyer's lower cost of capital, so the buyer would overpay.
> A is wrong because it repeats Kearns's reasoning; the buyer's financing cost does not change the risk of Kestrel's cash flows.
> C is wrong because a small private firm with too little debt has a WACC above, not below, a large listed buyer's; at the actual structure Kestrel's WACC is $0.05 \times 5.25\% + 0.95 \times 14\% = 13.5625\%$.
> 4. **B.** After-tax cost of debt $= 7.0\% \times 0.75 = 5.25\%$.
> $WACC = 0.20 \times 5.25\% + 0.80 \times 14.0\% = 1.05\% + 11.20\% = 12.25\%$.
> $FCFF_1 = 5.8625 \times 1.03 = 6.0384$ million.
> Firm value $= 6.0384 / (0.1225 - 0.03) = 6.0384 / 0.0925 = 65.2797$ million.
> Equity value $= 65.2797 - 4.0 = 61.2797$, or about GBP 61.28 million.
> A is wrong because it capitalizes the base-year FCFF without growing it ($5.8625 / 0.0925 - 4.0 = 59.3784$).
> C is wrong because it reports firm value and omits the deduction of the market value of debt.
> 5. **A.** A valuation for the sale of a company is in the transaction practice area.
> The yacht and warehouse items are normalization adjustments to earnings and cash flow, which is the numerator of the valuation.
> B is wrong because compliance valuations serve financial reporting and tax purposes, not a sale.
> C is wrong because personal-use assets and related-party rent flow through the income statement; they do not change the required return.
> 6. **A.** Restrictions on the sale of shares are stock-specific, like illiquidity and concentrated control, and are generally negative for value.
> B is wrong because customer concentration reflects smaller size and fewer, less diversified customers, a company-specific factor.
> C is wrong because overlap between owners and managers is a company-specific factor.

## Distractor design

1. B: missing related-party rent not inserted (omitted adjustment); C: rent added instead of deducted (sign error on a missing expense).
2. A: reported instead of normalized EBITDA and depreciation (normalization skipped); C: change in working capital omitted (incomplete FCFF formula).
3. A: buyer's financing cost substituted for target risk (wrong discount rate owner); C: direction of the private-firm WACC bias reversed.
4. A: base-year cash flow capitalized without growth (period error in the CCM numerator); C: firm value reported as equity value (debt not subtracted).
5. B: wrong practice area; C: normalization confused with a discount rate adjustment (numerator versus denominator).
6. B and C: company-specific factors mislabeled as stock-specific.
