# Veldmark Water: A Pension Plan Reviews a Regulated Utility

> [!info] Timer
> 18 minutes for 6 questions. Record confidence 1-5 before opening the answer key.

## Vignette

Ingrid Solberg, CFA, is an equity analyst at Norrvik Pensjon, a Norwegian defined benefit pension plan that holds small, non-controlling positions in listed European utilities.
On 2 June 2026 she is reviewing Veldmark Water NV, a regulated Dutch water and wastewater utility.
Veldmark has raised its dividend for 18 consecutive years, and its payout ratio has stayed between 62% and 68% of earnings over the last decade.
The annual dividend of EUR 1.84 per share was paid last week.
The regulator's five-year tariff settlement and Veldmark's stated policy support dividend growth of 3.6% a year indefinitely.
Veldmark also repurchases about 1% of its shares each year under a formula the board has followed without exception since 2019, and Solberg's per-share growth forecast already reflects those repurchases.
Veldmark's free cash flow to equity was negative in 2023 because of a one-off network renewal program but has been positive in every other year of the last decade.
The ordinary shares trade at EUR 58.40.

Exhibit 1: Inputs gathered by Solberg

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Risk-free rate | 2.8% |
| Equity risk premium | 5.0% |
| Raw beta (five years, weekly returns) | 0.62 |
| Adjusted beta | 0.75 |
| Expected long-run real GDP growth, Netherlands | 1.4% |
| Expected long-run inflation, Netherlands | 2.5% |
| Trailing EPS | EUR 2.83 |

Solberg estimates the required return on Veldmark's common shares with the CAPM and the adjusted beta.
Norrvik's policy treats a share as fairly valued when its market price lies within plus or minus 10% of the analyst's intrinsic value estimate.

Veldmark also has two preferred share issues outstanding.

Exhibit 2: Veldmark preferred shares

| Issue | Dividend rate | Par value | Call feature | Market price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Series A | 5.2% | EUR 50 | Non-callable, perpetual | EUR 44.10 |
| Series B | 4.8% | EUR 50 | Callable at par from 2029 | EUR 47.30 |

Solberg's required capitalization rate for Veldmark's preferred shares is 6.1%.

Her draft report, dated 2 June 2026, contains three statements about her choice of model for the common shares.

Statement 1: "The Gordon growth model is appropriate for Veldmark because its expected dividend growth is below the economy's expected nominal growth rate and its payout policy has been stable for a decade."

Statement 2: "Because Veldmark repurchases shares every year, the Gordon growth model cannot be applied to its common shares."

Statement 3: "Because Veldmark's dividends are so stable, the Gordon growth value is relatively insensitive to small changes in the required return."

On 4 June 2026, Marten de Vries, who runs Norrvik's separate infrastructure fund, emails Solberg.
He writes: "Our fund is preparing a bid for 51% of Veldmark, which would give us control of the board and of dividend policy. I plan to reuse your dividend model to set our bid price."

## Questions

1. For de Vries's valuation of the proposed 51% stake, the most appropriate definition of cash flow is: (LOS: los-05-02-a)
   - A. dividends, because Veldmark's payout ratio has been stable for a decade.
   - B. residual income, because Veldmark's free cash flow to equity was negative in 2023.
   - C. free cash flow, because a controlling investor can redirect the company's cash.
2. Solberg's Gordon growth model estimate of the value of a Veldmark common share is closest to: (LOS: los-05-02-c)
   - A. EUR 64.62.
   - B. EUR 72.14.
   - C. EUR 82.88.
3. Which of Solberg's statements is most accurate? (LOS: los-05-02-e)
   - A. Statement 1.
   - B. Statement 2.
   - C. Statement 3.
4. Using Solberg's required return, the dividend growth rate implied by Veldmark's current share price is closest to: (LOS: los-05-02-f)
   - A. 2.86%.
   - B. 3.26%.
   - C. 3.30%.
5. Based on Norrvik's fair value policy and Solberg's Gordon growth estimate, Veldmark's common shares are most likely: (LOS: los-05-02-j)
   - A. undervalued.
   - B. fairly valued.
   - C. overvalued.
6. The capitalization rate implied by the market price of the Series A preferred shares, and Solberg's conclusion about that issue, are closest to: (LOS: los-05-02-d)
   - A. 5.20%; overvalued.
   - B. 5.90%; undervalued.
   - C. 5.90%; overvalued.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Answers and reasoning
> 1. **C.** A 51% stake gives de Vries a control perspective: he can change dividend policy, so the cash the company could distribute, free cash flow, is the relevant measure rather than the dividends a minority holder happens to receive.
> Solberg's dividend model is appropriate for Norrvik's non-controlling position, not for a control buyer.
> A is wrong because payout stability supports a DDM only for an investor without control; the bidder is not bound by the current policy.
> B is wrong because residual income is preferred when free cash flow is expected to be negative within the forecast horizon, whereas Veldmark's single negative year (2023) is historical and one-off.
> 2. **A.** $r = 2.8\% + 0.75 \times 5.0\% = 6.55\%$.
> $D_1 = 1.84 \times 1.036 = 1.9062$; $V_0 = 1.9062 / (0.0655 - 0.036) = 1.9062 / 0.0295 = 64.6183$, or EUR 64.62.
> B is wrong because it substitutes the economy's expected nominal growth rate, $1.4\% + 2.5\% = 3.9\%$, for Veldmark's own 3.6% dividend growth: $1.84 \times 1.039 / (0.0655 - 0.039) = 1.9118 / 0.0265 = 72.14$.
> C is wrong because it uses the raw beta, $r = 2.8\% + 0.62 \times 5.0\% = 5.90\%$ and $1.9062 / (0.0590 - 0.036) = 82.88$, contrary to Solberg's stated method.
> 3. **A.** Nominal GDP growth is about $1.4\% + 2.5\% = 3.9\%$, so a perpetual 3.6% is sustainable, and a stable 62% to 68% payout makes dividends a readable function of earnings; both support a single-stage model.
> B is wrong because the Gordon model remains valid for a repurchasing company when the analyst can forecast the buybacks and reflect them in per-share dividend growth, which Solberg has done.
> C is wrong because the Gordon value is highly sensitive to $r - g$; at a 2.95% spread, a 25 bp rise in $r$ alone lowers the value by roughly 8%, whatever the stability of the dividend.
> 4. **C.** $g = (P_0 r - D_0)/(P_0 + D_0) = (58.40 \times 0.0655 - 1.84)/(58.40 + 1.84) = (3.8252 - 1.84)/60.24 = 1.9852/60.24 = 0.03295$, or 3.30%.
> The market prices slower growth than Solberg's 3.6%, consistent with her value exceeding price.
> A is wrong because it uses Solberg's 6.1% preferred capitalization rate as the required return on the common shares, $(58.40 \times 0.061 - 1.84)/60.24 = 1.7224/60.24 = 2.86\%$.
> B is wrong because $1.9062/58.40 = 3.26\%$ is the forward dividend yield, not the growth rate.
> 5. **B.** The band is built around intrinsic value: $64.6183 \times 0.90 = 58.1565$ to $64.6183 \times 1.10 = 71.0801$.
> The price of EUR 58.40 lies inside the band, so the shares are fairly valued under Norrvik's policy.
> A is wrong because it either ignores the band (value above price) or builds the band around price ($58.40 \pm 5.84$ gives 52.56 to 64.24, which excludes 64.62).
> C is wrong because it reverses the comparison; price is below, not above, the value estimate.
> 6. **C.** Annual dividend $= 0.052 \times 50 = 2.60$; implied capitalization rate $= 2.60 / 44.10 = 0.05896$, or 5.90%.
> At Solberg's 6.1%, $V_0 = 2.60 / 0.061 = 42.62 < 44.10$; equivalently the market's 5.90% is below her required 6.1%, so the issue is overvalued.
> A is wrong because 5.20% is the dividend rate on par, which equals the capitalization rate only when price equals par.
> B is wrong because it reverses the direction: a market yield below the required rate means the price is too high.
> Series B cannot be valued with $D/r$ at all, because its call option must be valued separately.

## Distractor design

1. A: minority-investor logic applied to a control buyer; B: historical one-off negative FCFE confused with negative FCF within the forecast horizon.
2. B: the economy's expected nominal growth rate substituted for the company's dividend growth rate; C: raw beta substituted for the adjusted beta (misread exhibit).
3. B: repurchases wrongly treated as ruling out the Gordon model; C: sensitivity to $r - g$ reversed.
4. A: the preferred-share capitalization rate used as the required return on common (misread exhibit); B: forward dividend yield reported as growth.
5. A: band ignored or built around price instead of value; C: direction of the price-value comparison reversed.
6. A: dividend rate on par taken as the capitalization rate; B: direction of the yield comparison reversed.
